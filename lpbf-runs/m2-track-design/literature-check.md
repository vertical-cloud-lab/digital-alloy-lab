# Literature check for the M2 wall build (PR #243, 2026-11-19)

Eight Edison (PaperQA) reports on the wall build came back grounded in retrieved text
([`edison-trajectories/m2-walls-literature/`](../../edison-trajectories/m2-walls-literature/)). This file checks 68 of
their claims against the papers themselves (for the AM Module, against Thermo-Calc's own documentation). It adds
sources found by our own searches (OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, Zenodo, OSTI, HAL), and collects published
316L numbers to put the walls against. The full numbers are in
[`out/literature_316l_data.csv`](out/literature_316l_data.csv) (1264 rows).
- Spot and hatch are given wherever the source states them. Hatch is blank for single tracks.
- Paywalled papers were read through BYU's network where the publisher allowed it.
- The scatter sources already checked in [`replicates.md`](replicates.md) (Reijonen, King and Kamath on scatter,
  Lane, Shen) were not re-checked.

**The design this is checked against** is the final sheet for Friday (commit e094d8e, [`out/wall_sets.csv`](out/wall_sets.csv)):
- 2 squares × 3 parts × 7 walls, so 42 walls, all along the recoater;
- 32 scrambled-Sobol settings: 151–370 W, 305–2760 mm/s, spot 50–190 µm, trace spacing 0.06–0.14 mm, areal energy
  P/(v·h) 1.0–5.95 J/mm²;
- 8 settings printed on both squares;
- the 316L-hatch control at 370 W, 1350 mm/s, 130 µm and 0.09 mm.

The Edison prompts described the earlier 61-set design at a fixed 130 µm spot and 0.09 mm hatch.

## Summary

- **Spot size will set the pool shape more than anything else in this build.** Hofmann et al.'s 316L single tracks
  (Aconity Midi, spots set by defocusing) let the spot be compared at matched power and speed. On 30 µm of powder,
  pools at a 50 µm spot are a median 5.1 times as deep as at 140 µm (2.5 times on bare plate), and about 0.7 times as
  wide. The median depth/width is 0.78, 0.49, 0.38 and 0.28 at 50, 80, 110 and 140 µm.
  - King's keyhole threshold, normalized enthalpy ΔH/h_s ≈ 30 ± 4, is confirmed, and he found it at both 52 and
    130 µm D4σ on an M2. Taking our spots as D4σ diameters, 11 of the 32 settings reach 30 or more, all at 50–80 µm.
    The 10 at 130–190 µm stay at 6–20.
  - Weaver 2022 (IN625, not 316L) saw tracks switch from keyhole to conduction between 100 and 150 µm at 195 W and
    800 mm/s.
  - So expect keyhole-shaped top-layer pools, and possibly keyhole pores, in the small-spot walls. The sheet's
    Ke ≤ 15 cap doesn't keep them below King's threshold, and Gan's Ke bands were fitted at 44–70 µm anyway.
  - The large-spot walls should be conduction-mode. The one dissent is Staub 2018 (M2, 130 µm): deep pools
    (width/depth 0.64–0.95) at P ≥ 250 W and P/v ≥ 0.22 in cubes.
  - None of this is a reason to change settings: finding the keyhole side is part of the point.
- **Small spot with wide hatch is where lack of fusion between tracks should show first.**
  - Dong 2019 (316L, 75 µm spot, 200 W, 400 mm/s, 50 µm layers) kept the pool about 230 µm wide at every hatch. Its
    density went 99.4, 99.9, 98.6 and 95.4 % at 75, 100, 150 and 200 µm hatch. Lack of fusion began near
    hatch/width 0.65, before Tang's overlap rule says it should.
  - Smith 2024 (316L, 100 µm spot) found lack of fusion that the overlap rule had called safe, at all three hatches
    (60, 120 and 180 µm) at 0.3 m/s.
  - Sow 2020 (IN625) puts the best hatch near 1–1.2 times the spot for small beams and 0.5 times for large ones.
  - In our sheet, 16 of the 32 settings have a hatch wider than the spot, 9 of them above 1.2 times (all at spots of
    90 µm or less). 20 fall outside the hatch range of most published work (Thomas 2016).
  - Those walls test the overlap rule, and the AM Module's hatch criterion, hardest. Keep them.
- **Where the energy edge should fall, in areal energy P/(v·h):**

  | Source | Machine and spot | Layer | 99 % edge (J/mm²) | Our settings below it |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Kamath 2014 | M2, 54 µm D4σ | 30 µm | 1.4–1.5 | 7 of 32 (under 1.5) |
  | Staub 2018 | M2, 130 µm | 40 µm | about 1.7–2.0 | 12 (under 2.0) |
  | Tenbrock 2020 | 316L, top-hat 100–200 µm | 50 µm | 2.85 (57 J/mm³), for robustly above 99.95 % | 19 |

  Roughly a third to a half of the walls should show measurable lack-of-fusion porosity, more if the layer is
  50 µm. Thicker layers move the edge up. At P/v 0.182 and 80 µm hatch, Zhang 2020 measured 99.36 % at 30 µm layers
  and 97.62 % at 50 µm.
- **The layer is probably 50 µm.** Bateman et al. ran exactly our control (370 W, 1350 mm/s, 90 µm hatch, 130 µm
  spot) as the "standard" 316L set on a GE Concept M2, at 50 µm layers, with under 1 % porosity by XCT (claim 13).
  Still read LayerNo on the machine.
- **Before Friday, find out how WRX Control makes a 50 µm and a 190 µm spot, and what the number means.** Ask
  whether it moves the focus, in which direction, and whether the number is D4σ, 1/e² or something else.
  - Metelkova 2018 (316L) found that at the same beam diameter, a converging beam (focal plane below the powder) made
    pools up to 204 % deeper than a diverging one, and keyholed at lower enthalpy.
  - Tenbrock 2020 notes that large raw beams shorten the Rayleigh length, which makes large spots sensitive to where
    the focal plane sits.
  - Edison's "the M2 has limited defocus control" is not supported: the paper it cites used a Fraunhofer ILT lab
    machine.
  - The AM Module's Gaussian radius is the 1/e² radius, and King's ΔH/h_s uses σ = D4σ/4. The 33 sets need 33 radii,
    so a wrong convention shifts every prediction.
  - If someone can, burn or profile the beam at 50 and 190 µm, and measure the delivered power: King's M2 delivered
    7–14 % less than set.
- **Thin walls screen well, but read higher than bulk.**
  - At the standard set, 1 mm 316L walls with no contour matched bulk density (≥ 99.97 %, Pauzon 2020).
  - In Reijonen 2024 (316L, beam 76–138 µm set by focus offset), bulk porosity stayed at most 0.004 % at every focus
    setting, but thin walls ran about ten times higher, and higher still at +5 mm. Defocusing cut penetration by up
    to 64 % while width barely changed. ΔH/h_s predicted pool size; volumetric energy density did not (R² 0.86–0.91
    against 0.19–0.56).
  - In one of Vu 2022's two specimen sets, thinner walls ran about 45 % more porous.
  - The control walls, set against the bases, measure this offset for our build.
- **Change to the sectioning plan: record where each pore is, not only the area fraction.**
  - At the standard setting, Bateman found most pores within 500 µm of the sample edge. Pores also gather at scan
    turns (Martin 2019, Ti-6Al-4V) and at hatch-to-contour gaps (du Plessis 2019, Ti6Al4V).
  - In a 1.27 mm wall, all of the core is within 0.64 mm of a face. Log each pore's distance from the nearest face
    and its height, so the 0.2 mm exclusion band can be checked or chosen afterwards.
  - About 4.9 mm² of core per wall separates walls above about 0.1 % from dense ones (Weaver 2021 used about 4.6 mm²
    per condition). It won't rank dense walls, whose porosity sits in a few large pores. Report pore counts with
    every area fraction.
- **Last-layer pools: about 7–17 per wall, and only if the top hatch runs along the wall.**
  - The upskin now uses each wall's own spot and hatch. The section crosses about 1.05 mm / h of them, so 7–17
    tracks, when the hatch runs along the wall. When it runs across, the section shows one track lengthwise.
    Edison's "about 140 tracks per wall" is wrong for this section.
  - With a 67° rotation per layer, which Bateman's M2 standard set used, the tracks are cut at an angle: correct the
    width for the angle; depth is unaffected.
  - Where the hatch is wider than the pool, each pool shows whole, so its width can be measured too.
  - Etching precedents: Vallejo 2021 used HCl, acetic acid and HNO₃ (3:2:1) for 40–60 s on the top layer.
    Rønneberg 2020 used 10 % oxalic acid, electrolytic, at 5 V for 90 s, and imaged it in BSE.
  - Etch after the porosity images.
- **Also before Friday: export the exposure order and the layer time.** In Fowler 2024 (Sandia, Kovar), identical
  cubes came out very different when the slicer exposed them in a different order. Our 8 repeated settings sit at
  different positions on the two squares, so their order and the time between neighbouring walls may differ too.
  Otherwise nothing in Friday's settings or layout needs to change.
- **What the AM Module needs from the walls** (Thermo-Calc documentation, claims 29–35):
  - Its printability map uses one steady-state track at a set plate temperature. It calls lack of fusion when
    depth is under the powder layer, or under it at half the hatch, and keyholing when width ≤ depth.
  - In Thermo-Calc's own 316L example, the lack-of-fusion threshold had to be raised to D/t = 1.3, so treat the
    thresholds as fit parameters.
  - Its calibration fits absorptivity and beam radius to depth alone if need be. Fit an effective radius for each
    nominal spot: Hong 2026 found a beam 40 % narrower than nominal fit strongly defocused IN625 spots.
  - Its 2026b evaporation mode returns a lack-of-fusion porosity in per cent, which can be set against each wall's
    measured porosity.
  - Thermo-Calc's Euro PM2025 paper ([10.59499/EP256768103](https://doi.org/10.59499/EP256768103)) overlays measured
    316L porosity at 20–166 J/mm³ on the module's maps. Ask Thermo-Calc for it.
- **How Edison did.** Of 68 claims, 38 were confirmed, 26 partly, 2 not supported and 2 contradicted. Every DOI it
  gave resolves to the paper it names. Its usual faults:
  - conditions carried over to the wrong case: results in Ti-6Al-4V, IN625 or AlSi10Mg presented as 316L, and a
    Renishaw or a 3D Systems machine presented as a Concept Laser;
  - effects pinned to the wrong variable: Metelkova's +204 % is the sign of the defocus, not the spot size;
  - its own arithmetic: "near the keyhole threshold", "about 140 tracks per wall", and "≈6 vs ≈30 reflect different
    definitions".

## Claims checked

Verdicts:
- **confirmed**: the source says it, under the stated conditions;
- **partly**: right in substance, but the material, machine, number or scope differs;
- **not supported**;
- **contradicted**.

Report links go to the Edison files. Quotes are verbatim from the full text unless marked otherwise. Page numbers
are those of the copy read, which the source column names.

ΔH/h_s values for our walls use King's Eq. 2.6 with his Table 3 constants, A 0.4 and σ = D4σ/4, treating each set's
spot as a D4σ diameter. This reproduces King's own Fig. 6 values. Some comments were written for the earlier fixed-spot
design and have been updated to the final sheet.

### Datasets

- **Hofmann et al. 2026** (Zenodo, above): `MeltpoolGeometryData.csv`, 677 316L single tracks at four spot sizes
  and three powder layers. It is the only open 316L set we found that varies the spot at fixed P and v.
- **MeltpoolNet** ([github.com/BaratiLab/MeltpoolNet](https://github.com/BaratiLab/MeltpoolNet)): 639 316L rows.
  - At 80–150 µm spots and 150–400 W it holds only Staub 2018 (entered as 116 µm, with depths derived from areas)
    and Heeling 2017.
  - Three 11 × 11 grids in it look like model output; don't use them.
- **LPBF relative-density dataset** (Barrionuevo et al. 2025,
  [10.1038/s41597-025-04576-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04576-x); Harvard Dataverse
  [10.7910/DVN/VPBQK8](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/VPBQK8)): 358 316L rows with spot and hatch.
  - 64 of them are at 80–150 µm and 140–400 W.
  - The one row checked against its paper had the wrong spot, so check rows before using them.
- **LLNL keyhole-mode microscopy dataset** (Ahlquist et al. 2022, [10.2172/1878448](https://doi.org/10.2172/1878448)):
  316L single tracks on two Concept Laser M2s.
  - It covers 50–250 W, P/v 0.1–1.5 J/mm, powder layers of 50–200 µm, and focus offsets of 0 or 5 mm (King's 52 and
    130 µm beams).
  - It overlaps our window at 150–250 W, and it is the only dataset with the M2's own spot change.
  - The report doesn't say where the spreadsheets are, and we didn't find them. They are worth asking LLNL for.
- **NIST AM Bench** has no 316L: 2018, 2022 and 2025 are nickel alloys and Ti-6Al-4V. AMB2022-03 varied the spot
  diameter on IN718 bare-plate tracks, so it is a method model. **Melt-Pool-Kinetics** (Jabbour et al. 2025) is
  in-situ images only.

## What we couldn't access

- **ScienceDirect blocked automated access** (bot check), from the runner and through the BYU network alike:
  - Obeidi et al. 2021;
  - Mohr et al. 2020 (its 13–19 % deeper pools and 1.1–2.7 % porosity are unchecked; the trend is confirmed through
    Chaudry 2022);
  - du Plessis et al. 2019 and Trapp et al. 2017 (abstracts only);
  - Shahabi 2022;
  - Tang et al. 2017, the overlap rule (its formula was checked as quoted in Smith 2024 and Metelkova 2018);
  - Tucho et al. 2018 and Heeling et al. 2017 (their CSV rows come from compilations and are marked unchecked);
  - Sun et al. 2016.
- **Read in other versions:** Metelkova et al. 2018 in the KU Leuven accepted manuscript, and Reijonen et al. 2024 in
  Reijonen's thesis. In the thesis, the beam diameters listed for +0.5 to +5 mm focus conflict with other columns,
  so only the 0 to −5 mm diameters are used.
- **Springer blocked automated access** for Kamath et al. 2014, which was read in the authors' report version,
  LLNL-TR-648000 (OSTI 1116929), with its page numbers. Smith et al. 2024 was read in the open HTML.
- **Greco et al. 2020** ([10.1007/s00170-020-05510-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00170-020-05510-8); 316L, hatch 42–84 µm
  at constant VED on a Concept Laser Mlab): its densities are only in a figure, so the CSV has no rows from it.
- **Not opened:**
  - Kranz et al. 2015 (thin walls on a Concept Laser M2): the publisher blocked it;
  - Wróbel et al. 2024: no reachable full text;
  - Thermo-Calc's Euro PM2025 316L paper ([10.59499/EP256768103](https://doi.org/10.59499/EP256768103)): abstract

  - Bertoli et al. 2017 and Rubenchik et al. 2018 (scaling laws): not reached in the time;
