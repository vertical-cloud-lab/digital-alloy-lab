## 1. Heat carried by the charge

- Sensible (solid, 25-1040 C) 487 J/g + latent 230 J/g + liquid (1054-1300 C) 130 J/g = **848 J/g**
- For 816 g: **692 kJ** (about the energy to heat 2 L of water from 20 to 100 C)
- Of that, 294 kJ has to leave before the metal is fully solid.

## 2. How far each droplet size gets towards solid before it reaches the cone

Fall of 0.8 m from rest takes about **0.40 s** (drag ignored; it only lengthens the flight of
the smallest drops). Heat loss is convection (Ranz-Marshall, Nu = 2 + 0.6 Re^0.5 Pr^0.33, with the
velocity reached halfway down) plus radiation (emissivity 0.3). Energy to remove before fully solid:
360 J/g.

| Droplet diameter | Time to solidify | Fall time / time to solidify | Arrives as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 um | 0.0125 s | 32 | solid, then cools further |
| 100 um | 0.0439 s | 9.2 | solid, then cools further |
| 200 um | 0.147 s | 2.7 | solid, then cools further |
| 1 mm | 1.91 s | 0.21 | mostly liquid |
| 2 mm | 5.12 s | 0.079 | mostly liquid |
| 3 mm | 8.84 s | 0.046 | mostly liquid |

Fine powder (d50 around 100 um on the good runs) freezes within the first few centimetres and reaches
the cup cool. Shot of 1-3 mm, which is what the plate threw on Nov 13, reaches the cone still largely
liquid, sticks to whatever it lands on and keeps nearly all of its heat.

## 3. Surface temperature the moment hot bronze touches each material

Two semi-infinite bodies in contact settle at T_c = (e1 T1 + e2 T2)/(e1 + e2), with effusivity
e = sqrt(k rho c). Bronze at 1000 C (just solid) against parts at 25 C:

| Bronze at 1000 C touching | Contact temperature |
|---|---|
| 316 stainless (flange) | 604 C |
| Borosilicate 3.3 glass | 899 C |
| Silicone rubber seal | 956 C |
| FKM (Viton) seal | 943 C |

Silicone seals are rated to about 200-230 C continuous and FKM to about 200-250 C, so a seal that a
bead touches is overheated at once; it only survives if no hot metal reaches it.

## 4. Thermal-shock margin of the glass

Stress from a sudden surface temperature change: sigma = E alpha dT / (1 - nu). Taking 40 MPa as the
tensile stress an abraded glass surface fails at (assumed; design values are lower):

| Glass | E (GPa) | alpha (1/K) | dT that reaches 40 MPa |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soda-lime | 70 | 9e-06 | 50 K |
| Borosilicate 3.3 | 64 | 3.3e-06 | 152 K |
| Fused quartz | 72 | 5.5e-07 | 838 K |

Bronze at 1000 C puts the touched glass surface near 899 C: about 6x what borosilicate takes,
and close to the limit even for fused quartz.

## 6. What it takes to leave about 900 ppm O2 after a backfill

Backfilling with argon to +150 mbar gauge is about 999 mbar absolute. The O2 left equals the O2
partial pressure at the bottom of the pump-down divided by that:

| Air left at the bottom of the pump-down | O2 after backfill |
|---|---|
| 0.1 mbar | 21 ppm |
| 0.5 mbar | 105 ppm |
| 1.0 mbar | 209 ppm |
| 2.0 mbar | 418 ppm |
| 4.0 mbar | 837 ppm |
| 5.0 mbar | 1046 ppm |

Normal runs (low 20s ppm) leave about 0.1 mbar of air. The 873-1000 ppm on Nov 13 needs about 4 mbar of
air at the bottom of every pump-down, while the gauge reading barely changes.

A one-off dose of air (such as the chamber drawing in air when the machine lost power) is purged quickly:
- after 1 pump-down/backfill cycle(s) to 16 mbar absolute: 3346.7 ppm
- after 2 pump-down/backfill cycle(s) to 16 mbar absolute: 53.6 ppm
- after 3 pump-down/backfill cycle(s) to 16 mbar absolute: 0.9 ppm

Six extra purges should therefore have brought a one-off dose of air below 1 ppm. A reading that went
back to 1000 ppm after every purge means either a source that refills during each pump-down (a leak
open to the room) or a sensor fault.

The pump-down filmed in video 3 (t = 12-36 s) gives a chamber time constant V/S of about **9 s**.
Valve the pump off at full vacuum and watch the gauge (a rate-of-rise test). A leak that leaves 4 mbar of
air at the bottom of a pump-down raises the pressure by about 4/9 = 0.46 mbar/s, roughly
**28 mbar per minute**, while a tight chamber with ~0.1 mbar of air rises about 0.7 mbar/min plus outgassing.
A 5-minute rate-of-rise test therefore separates the two cases by more than the gauge's resolution.
