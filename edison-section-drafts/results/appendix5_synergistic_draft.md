\subsection*{Appendix 5: Synergistic Activities — Sterling G. Baird}

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Self-Driving Laboratory Leadership \& Community Building.}
Serving as Guest Editor for \textit{npj Computational Materials} for the ``Self-Driving Labs for Chemistry and Materials Science'' collection (2024--present) and lead author of the structural materials section of a comprehensive \textit{Chemical Reviews} review on self-driving laboratories (Tom et al., 2024). Lead author of a review on low-cost self-driving laboratories introducing the ``Frugal Twin'' concept (Lo et al., 2024, \textit{Digital Discovery}). Solo-organized the Acceleration Consortium Bayesian Optimization Hackathon (120 participants, 62 organizations, 14 countries) and presented ``An Autonomous Vertical Cloud Lab for Additively Manufactured Aerospace Alloys'' at MRS Fall 2025.

\item \textbf{Open-Source Software \& Community Infrastructure.}
Contributed over 17,000 open-source code artifacts (15K commits, 1.5K issues, 913 pull requests) spanning materials informatics, Bayesian optimization, and laboratory automation. Developer of Honegumi, an interactive Bayesian optimization script generator for experimental scientists (JOSS, 2025), and xtal2png, a crystal-structure-to-image encoding tool for generative modeling (JOSS, 2022). Served as Technical Consultant to Meta for the v1.0 release of the Ax/BoTorch Bayesian optimization platform (Nov 2024--Jan 2025). Over 350 self-driving lab demonstration kits have been deployed to researchers and educators worldwide.

\item \textbf{Training \& Workforce Development.}
Delivered approximately 5,000 person-hours of self-driving laboratory training to roughly 700 individuals across 62 organizations through workshops, microcredential courses, and hackathons. Built the Acceleration Consortium Development Lab from the ground up (\$600K in equipment, 500 line items, 24 months), establishing a dedicated teaching, prototyping, and research facility for autonomous experimentation. Developed over 200 hours of course content in robotics, data science, and AI-driven experimental design. Supervised 12 staff members and 8 undergraduate/graduate students on research and training projects spanning electrolysis, battery optimization, and laboratory automation.

\item \textbf{Standardization \& Open Science.}
Principal Investigator on an NSF Pathways to Open-Source Ecosystems (POSE) project developing a community-governed materials informatics software toolchain. Contributing to Acceleration Consortium community standards for self-driving laboratory interoperability and advancing FAIR data practices through schema.org metadata templates for SDL datasets. Presenter at the German-Canadian Materials Acceleration Centre (GCMAC) Summer School (2024, 2025) and serving as a Review Panelist for the NSF TIP Directorate (2026). Participating in IEC/ISO working groups on materials data standardization.

\item \textbf{Open Data Impact \& Domain Expertise.}
Creator of the MPTS-52 time-based dataset split, which has become a widely adopted benchmark in the crystal structure generative AI community, and developer of the Matbench-genmetrics framework for standardized evaluation of generative models (JOSS, 2024). Completed a \$34K industry research contract with Fehrmann Materials GmbH applying machine learning to joint strength and ductility prediction in aluminum alloys. Maintains curated open datasets on Zenodo and Figshare with documented community reuse, supporting reproducibility and accelerating downstream research in materials discovery.

\end{enumerate}
