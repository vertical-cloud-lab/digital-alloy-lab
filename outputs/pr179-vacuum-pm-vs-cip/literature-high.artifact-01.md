| Parameter | Route A — Vacuum uniaxial die pressing | Route B — Vacuum-degassed, evacuated-bag CIP |
|---|---|---|
| Powder preparation | Use clean gas-atomized Al and sieve gently without creating a dust cloud. Blend additions in a sealed conductive container by low-energy tumbling. Prefer two or three 20–30 mm slugs rather than one 60 mm slug to limit die-friction gradients. | Use the same powder preparation. Fill the mould incrementally with gentle vibration. Validate blending and filling because powders of different size or density can segregate. |
| Drying / degassing | Minimum: dynamic vacuum at 100–150°C for 2–4 h. Preferred qualified treatment: loose powder near 350°C (623 K) for 2–5 h, followed by cooling and transfer without air exposure. Physisorbed water evolves mainly at 100–200°C; hydroxide-related water and H₂ continue through roughly 200–450°C. (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000067, pqac-00000070) | Degas loose powder before bagging with the same qualified schedule. Cool under vacuum or dry Ar, transfer in a glovebox, then evacuate and seal the bag. Reject powder after suspected water or oil contact. |
| Pressing pressure | Start at 200–300 MPa; qualify higher pressure only if handling strength is inadequate. Use double-action pressing for tall slugs or divide them into shorter pieces. Al-alloy die compacts increased from about 2.14 g/cm³ at 100 MPa to 2.57 g/cm³ at 500 MPa, although that powder contained lubricant. (pqac-00000053) | Start at 150–250 MPa with a 60 s hold; increase toward 300–400 MPa only if needed. Typical CIP practice is 200–400 MPa, while direct Al work reached 98% theoretical density at 600 MPa. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000054) |
| Press force / vessel | A 19 mm punch has an area of 283.5 mm². Required force is 56.7 kN at 200 MPa, 70.9 kN at 250 MPa, 85.1 kN at 300 MPa, 113.4 kN at 400 MPa, and 141.8 kN at 500 MPa. Use at least a guarded 150 kN press for development through 500 MPa. | Use a certified vessel with at least 25–30 mm usable bore and 80–100 mm usable depth. Rating should be at least 250 MPa for the initial recipe or 400 MPa for development at conventional CIP pressures. The vessel, closure, intensifier, tubing, and relief system must share the certified rating. |
| Dwell time | Evacuate the filled die for 2–5 min, compact gradually, hold at peak pressure for 30–60 s, and unload slowly. A longer dwell improves pressure transfer and allows gas to escape while pores remain connected. (pqac-00000046) | Hold at peak pressure for 60 s. Direct Al/Al–Ni work found that immediate decompression caused a denser surface and lower-density core, whereas a 1 min hold improved homogeneity. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000005) |
| Evacuation level | Evacuate loose powder and the die cavity to ≤10 mbar absolute; ≤1 mbar is preferable with a filtered powder-rated system. Reduction from 1 bar to 10 mbar removes about 99% of equilibrated free pore gas but not oxide-bound water. Vent only with dry Ar. | Evacuate the filled bag to ≤10 mbar, preferably ≤1 mbar, while gently manipulating it to release pockets; seal under vacuum and use a second dry containment bag. A non-evacuated sealed bag compresses rather than removes the original gas. |
| Lubricant | Use no admixed lubricant or binder. If ejection requires it, use only the smallest validated external die-wall film and prevent contact with the powder. | Use no lubricant or binder. Bag motion largely eliminates internal lubrication. Avoid mould-release agents unless transfer and outgassing have been quantified. |
| Die / mould | Use a polished hardened-tool-steel or carbide-lined 19.0 mm die, preferably double-action. Include a sintered-metal powder trap, grounding, and seals outside the powder-contact region. | Use thin polyurethane, natural rubber, latex, or another compatible elastomer, preferably double-bagged. Begin with an internal former around 21–24 mm diameter and determine experimentally the oversize needed to obtain ≤19 mm after shrinkage. (pqac-00000054, pqac-00000055, pqac-00000057) |
| Target green density | Target 85–90% theoretical density, equivalent to 2.30–2.43 g/cm³ for pure Al, provided measurable open porosity remains. Do not maximize density before degassing. | Target 85–90% theoretical density. If handling requires 90–95%, use only pre-degassed powder in an evacuated bag and verify gas release independently. CIP can exceed 95% at high pressure. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002) |
| Expected density and mass | At 200–300 MPa, an initial development expectation is about 2.25–2.45 g/cm³, subject to powder and tooling. At 19 mm diameter this corresponds to approximately 13–14 g at 20 mm length, 26–28 g at 40 mm, and 39–42 g at 60 mm. | At 150–250 MPa, expect approximately 2.30–2.50 g/cm³, but establish a pressure–density curve for the actual powder. Pure Al achieved 85–95% density at 103.5–138 MPa in one study and 98% at 600 MPa in another. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000063) |
| Decompression / ejection | Reduce load over 30–60 s and pause at low load before full release. Eject slowly with a supported lower punch. Inspect for end-capping, circumferential laminations, and cracks at the middle or remote end. | Decompress over at least 1–3 min and lengthen the cycle if springback cracks occur. Keep the bag contained until external fluid has been removed, then strip it over a clean tray. Low-compressibility metals may require several minutes. (pqac-00000054) |
| Dimensional acceptance | Require diameter ≤19.0 mm everywhere using a go/no-go gauge. Prefer 20–30 mm lengths. In Cu–Al compacts, increasing H/D from 0.7 to 2.12 increased maximum relative-density variation from 0.0851 to 0.1206; application to pure Al is an extrapolation. (pqac-00000046) | Require diameter ≤19.0 mm everywhere after bag removal. Reject folds, necking, taper, and closure-end bulges. Flexible-tool shrinkage must be calibrated for each bag and filling procedure. (pqac-00000054, pqac-00000056) |
| Density and porosity check | Record mass, dimensions, and geometric density. Determine open porosity with a validated nonreactive immersion liquid followed by complete drying, or use helium pycnometry plus envelope density. Require batch density within ±2% relative and connected porosity if relying on in-crucible degassing. | Apply the same tests. Compare dimensions before and after CIP to detect abnormal bag restraint or leakage. During qualification, section slugs and compare top, middle, and bottom density. |
| Thermal qualification | Heat a sacrificial slug under the intended dynamic-vacuum ramp to 500°C while recording mass and pressure, preferably with H₂O/H₂ residual-gas analysis. Require reproducible mass loss and no pressure spike, swelling, cracking, spalling, or ejection. Al powders have shown about 13–25 ppm H, equivalent to 14.7–27.5 cm³ H₂ per 100 g. (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000066, pqac-00000069) | Use the same qualification. Reject a part if its bag lost vacuum, changed mass unexpectedly, showed wetting or discoloration, or either containment layer was damaged. A leak into water-based fluid is disqualifying and should not be corrected by redrying. |
| Key warnings | Fit vacuum lines with grounding, a metal-powder trap, an isolation valve, and pump protection. Never vent an evacuated powder-filled die rapidly to air. Avoid moist powder, internal lubricants, tall single-action compacts, and near-full densification before degassing. Al-powder MIE can be below 1–13 mJ. (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000029) | CIP adds high-pressure stored energy and a fluid-ingress failure mode. Treat either water or oil ingress as rejection. Freshly exposed Al can generate H₂ according to 2Al + 3H₂O → Al₂O₃ + 3H₂. (pqac-00000022) |


*Table: Safety-first starting recipes for producing 19 mm diameter aluminium-powder slugs by vacuum die pressing or evacuated-bag CIP. Values requiring qualification on the actual powder and equipment are presented as development ranges rather than validated production limits.*
