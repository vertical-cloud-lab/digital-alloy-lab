| Cold uniaxial pressing—low pressure | ~100 | ~85 | Particle rearrangement and initial plastic deformation create some oxide cracks, but extensive Al-to-Al contact is unlikely; porosity begins changing from interconnected to closed near this density. | Stronger and more conductive than loose powder, but still liable to retain gas, float, fragment, or leave oxide-bound agglomerates; not a reliable endpoint for a two-minute melt hold. | (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000047) |
| Cold uniaxial pressing—practical starting range | 200–250 | ~90–95 | Greater particle deformation enlarges contacts and mechanically disrupts parts of the oxide shells; compaction alone does not remove the oxide or guarantee metallurgical bonding. | Should heat, submerge, and coalesce substantially better than loose powder. Aluminium powder pressed at 245.6 MPa reached 94.5% density under load and 96.7% after the final hold, although those tests were at 80 °C. | (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000047) |
| Cold uniaxial pressing—high pressure | 300–400 | ~95–98 | Extensive particle flattening and contact enlargement produce more oxide rupture and fewer voids; at 386 MPa, aluminium powder reached 97.0% under load and 98.2% after the final hold in an 80 °C test. | High thermal conductivity and good mechanical integrity, but nearly closed porosity can trap gas; excessive density can impede melt infiltration, so this is not automatically superior for dissolution. | (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000043) |
| Warm uniaxial pressing | 246–386 at 80 °C | 94.5–98.2 under load; 96.7–98.2 final | Aluminium softening increases plastic deformation and oxide cracking at a given pressure; the constant-load stage adds modest densification, partly through gas expulsion. | More reproducible, stronger compacts than room-temperature pressing, but the compact must remain vented and dry because high final density promotes closed porosity. | (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000014) |
| Spark-plasma sintering (SPS) / pressure-assisted hot consolidation | 20–50 at 500 °C | 92.5 at 20 MPa; 95.2 at 30 MPa; 96.6 at 40 MPa; 98.4 at 50 MPa | Pressure-driven deformation, local Joule heating, and particle-contact heating disrupt or displace oxide; resistance falls near ~200 °C as metallic contacts develop. Inert-gas-atomized powder with ~3–4 nm oxide bonds more readily than air-atomized powder with ~7 nm oxide. | Produces a robust, highly conductive slug that should melt more like bulk alloy than loose powder. It minimizes open porosity but can retain closed gas unless vacuum-degassed; it also embeds fragmented oxide rather than eliminating it. | (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000042, pqac-00000045, pqac-00000046, pqac-00000048) |
| Foundry alloying tablet—deliberately porous compact | 25.5–29.8 | Not directly comparable; specified density 2.5–5.0 g/cm³ for Ti/flux tablets | Relatively low pressure preserves interconnected pores so molten aluminium can infiltrate; oxide disruption depends on infiltration, flux action, and tablet breakup rather than high-density Al-particle welding. | At 730–750 °C, reported dissolution was typically 25–40 min; fastest was 28 min. This is much longer than a two-minute hold. Overconsolidated tablets dissolved in ~35–40 min because melt could not infiltrate them; stirring accelerates saturation. | (pqac-00000032, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000034) |
| Foundry alloying tablet—underpressed | 9.8–19.6 | Not reported; intentionally low | Insufficient interlocking and deformation leave weak contacts and largely intact particle oxides. | Tablets crumble, lose more than 5–15% of their geometry, and may release floating powder or oxide-rich fragments before dissolution. | (pqac-00000033) |
