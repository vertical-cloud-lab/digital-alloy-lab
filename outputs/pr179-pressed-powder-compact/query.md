We make small batches of aluminium-alloy powder by ultrasonic atomization (AMAZEMET rePOWDER, induction-heated module) and want to know whether pressing our alloying powder into a compact before melting would fix a melting and mixing problem. Please answer from the literature, with citations (DOIs where possible), and give numbers wherever the sources have them.

**Set-up.** Graphite crucible (BN-coated) heated by induction, 10-11 cm deep, with a sealing (stopper) rod down the middle; the melt is bottom-poured through a ~0.7 mm hBN nozzle onto a 40 kHz vibrating molybdenum plate. Argon at a small overpressure (~0.2 bar), 13-20 ppm O2 in the chamber, melt set point 810-840 °C, held about 2 minutes before pouring. Charges are 170-350 g. There is no mechanical stirring; any stirring comes from the induction field.

**Current charge.** A 6063 aluminium cup (19 mm OD, 12.7 mm bore, 57 mm deep) is filled by weight with gas-atomized alloy powder, closed with a press-fit 6063 plug that has a 1 mm vent hole, and melted with solid 6063 rod. Pieces must stay under 20 mm across to fit between the stopper rod and the crucible wall. In one run the core was AlSi10Mg powder, and it did not melt into the bath: the cup melted, but the powder did not consolidate. In another run the core was 4047 (Al-12Si) powder, and it melted. Next we want composition spreads (varying how much 4047 powder goes into 6063, checked by SEM/EDS) and later blends with elemental or master-alloy powders (for example Si, Mg, Cu, Mn, Fe, Ni, Zr, Sc). The problem is that the powder struggles to melt, coalesce and mix into the rest of the melt in the time and temperature available. The equipment maker warned us that powder compressed inside an aluminium cup could eject molten metal when melted, presumably from trapped gas.

**Proposed fix to evaluate.** Use uniaxial die pressing (powder metallurgy) to turn the powder, alone or blended with aluminium powder, into a partly dense compact (a "semi-solid" pellet or slug under 20 mm in diameter), either as pressed (green) or after sintering, warm pressing, hot pressing or vacuum hot pressing, and melt that in the crucible instead of loose powder in a cup.

Please address:

1. **Why loose or lightly packed aluminium-alloy powder fails to melt into a bath** at 810-840 °C under argon: oxide shells (Al2O3, and MgO or MgAl2O4 spinel for Mg-bearing alloys), low thermal conductivity of a powder bed, entrapped gas, poor wetting, floating agglomerates. Does the Mg in AlSi10Mg plausibly explain why its powder failed while 4047 (no Mg) melted?
2. **Does uniaxial compaction fix it, and at what pressure and relative density?** Does particle deformation break the oxide films enough for metal-to-metal contact and coalescence on melting? Use evidence from remelting aluminium powder, chips or swarf (loose versus cold-compacted versus hot-compacted, metal yield or recovery), remelting PM green or sintered compacts, and aluminium foundry alloying tablets or compacts (Mn, Fe, Cr, Ti, Cu, Ni with aluminium powder, with and without flux): their dissolution times versus melt temperature, compact density and stirring. Also the dissolution of Si added as lump, powder or compact to aluminium melts.
3. **Safety and quality risks of melting a compact**: is the "ejects molten metal" warning justified, and under what conditions (closed porosity, hydrated oxide or moisture on the powder, die lubricants, a compact dropped into a melt versus heated up with the charge from cold)? How do these change from green to sintered to hot-pressed? Effects on oxygen and hydrogen in the melt, on oxide films and inclusions that could clog a 0.7 mm nozzle, and on contamination from lubricants or fluxes.
4. **Practical recipe if compaction is worthwhile**: pressure (MPa), target relative density, die-wall versus admixed lubricant, degassing or drying before pressing, vacuum pressing, sintering atmosphere and temperature, whether to blend with aluminium powder, compact size, where in the charge to put it, and how much extra superheat or hold time it needs. What press force does a 19 mm compact need at those pressures?
5. **How it compares with the alternatives** for 100-350 g induction melts aimed at a homogeneous target composition, low oxygen and no nozzle clogging: compacting the powder inside the cup with a vented sliding plug, a freestanding green compact, a sintered or hot-pressed compact, arc-melting a master alloy button first, buying commercial master alloys (for example Al-50Si, Al-20Si, Al-50Mg, Al-50Cu) or pre-alloyed powder, using a flux, or raising the melt temperature and hold time. Which would you recommend, and when would a pressed compact be the wrong choice?
6. **A quick test** that would tell us whether a compact melts in and mixes, before committing an atomizer run.

Please be explicit about which conclusions come from direct evidence for aluminium systems and which are extrapolated from other metals.
