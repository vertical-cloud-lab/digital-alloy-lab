> **Figure N.** Representative one-week Gantt schedule for the FLAIME alloy
> experiment pipeline.
>

> — Early 7–15 (light blue), Mid 11–19 (light orange), Late 14–22
> (light green) — stack side-by-side each weekday. Each shift is staffed by
> ≥2 undergraduates, so ≥1 person is on-site at all times within the

> top-to-bottom, one column per composition, one row per weekday — a
> steady-state rate of **3 compositions/weekday + 1 on Saturday** driven by
> the staffed atomizer window (7:00–22:00 weekdays) and the overlapping

> single-person-out redundancy. Bars are colored by

## Headline numbers (for narrative text, not embedded in the figure)

* **Throughput**: 3 compositions/weekday + 1/Saturday = **16 compositions/week**,
  steady-state. Over a 9-month Phase I this comfortably covers the
  100–200 composition Phase I target with margin for pipeline failures
  and ramp-up.
* **Equipment hours per representative week** (FTE = 40 h), split
  hands-on / hands-off:
  * LPBF printer — **80 h/wk** (24 h hands-on setup/changeout + 56 h
    hands-off print & cool; 200% FTE)
  * Atomizer — **64 h/wk** (32 h hands-on melt prep / sieve + 32 h
    hands-off atomization & cool; 160% FTE)
  * SEM facility — **32 h/wk** (all hands-on; 80% FTE)
  * AutoTrickler — **16 h/wk** (all hands-on; 40% FTE)
* **Staffing**: each of the three weekday shift bands is staffed by 2
  undergraduates (drawn from the 2 MS + 12 UG technician team — 4 FT + 8
  PT per PR #58), giving ≥2 people on-site at all times within 7:00–22:00
  and ≥4 concurrent during the 14:00–15:00 shift-overlap peak — satisfying
  the buddy rule (≥2 trained personnel whenever the atomizer or LPBF is
  in use) with cushion for sickness/unavailability.
* **Logistics**: LPBF is at U of Utah; atomized powder transfers BYU→U of U
  and printed specimens transfer back (≈45 min each way); VCL, workbench,
  and microscopy are all co-located in the BYU Clyde/Engineering Bldg so
  there is no intra-BYU transit on the critical path.
* **CALPHAD screening**: runs 24/7 on BYU HPC as a continuous batch job,
  never on the experimental critical path.

## Notes on figure usage

* The y-axis uses weekday names (Monday–Sunday) and no specific calendar
  dates; the rendered week is representative, not a literal schedule.
* At ~7 inch single-column width the figure is legible for full-page
  embedding in a DOE ARPA-E / Genesis-style proposal.
* See `generate_gantt.py` for the scheduling engine, invariants, and the
  FTE utilization computation.
* Each panel — (a) shift coverage, (b) main compositions, (c) zoomed
  inlay, (d) equipment hours, plus the category legend — is also
  exported as a standalone PNG in `subfigures/` so the panels can be
  re-combined ad-hoc in a slide or one-column proposal layout without
  regenerating the scheduler.
