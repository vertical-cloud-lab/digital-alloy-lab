Independent review of a physics-based "truth" for a synthetic copper-alloy benchmark, with one question at its centre: **does the evidence support a nickel floor (a minimum Ni content below which no alloy can pass 30-day seawater immersion "no worse than" a copper-nickel reference), and would it justify restricting the search space?**

Context. The benchmark (blown-powder DED of Cu-Ni-Al-Fe-Mn alloys, Ni 0-30, Al 0-9.5, Fe 0-4.5, Mn 0-4 wt%, Cu balance) scores an alloy by tensile strength counted only if it passes 30-day immersion no worse than a reference (dense 90-10 Cu-Ni, C70600, by default; 70-30 Cu-Ni, C71500, as the stricter bar). Its first, hand-written truth used an immersion formula in which nothing under about 6 wt% Ni passed and only 3.2% of the box passed at all. The physics-based truth replaces that formula with (a) a film corrosion model fitted only to same-test seawater data (ratios between alloys tested in the same study), and (b) a CALPHAD node from an 864-composition pycalphad grid (open MatCalc mc_ni database), and it applies the proposal's own search-space limits (liquidus <= 1,250 C, Cu >= 65 wt%, Ni >= Fe when Al > 7 wt%, Fe <= 2.3 wt% when Al < 1.5 wt%).

Uploaded files: `truth_phys.py` (the truth: read its docstring first), `corrosion_data.csv` (every rate with study, composition, test and the line it was taken from), `fit_corrosion.py` and its outputs `corrosion_fit.json` / `corrosion_fit.md` (base fit, alternative fits, floors), `calphad_grid.py` and `calphad_grid.csv` (the grid), `phys_tables.md` / `.json` (optimum, reference alloys, share passing, optimized floor in the full truth), and `bofn_alloy.py` (the hand-written truth used for the nodes the physics truth does not replace).
