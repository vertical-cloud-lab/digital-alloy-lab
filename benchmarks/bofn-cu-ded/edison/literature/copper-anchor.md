Question: I need SAME-TEST seawater corrosion data that compare unalloyed copper (or a copper alloy with less than 5 wt% Ni) against 90-10 copper-nickel (C70600) and/or 70-30 copper-nickel (C71500), measured in the same study under the same conditions (natural or synthetic seawater, or 3.5 wt% NaCl; quiet immersion preferred; mass loss or weight loss preferred over Tafel; 30 days or longer preferred). Also any same-study series of Cu-Ni alloys with several Ni contents between 0 and 30 wt% (for example Cu-2Ni, Cu-5Ni, Cu-10Ni, Cu-30Ni), and any same-study comparison of an aluminum bronze (Cu-5Al to Cu-9Al, with or without Fe) against a copper-nickel. For each, give: the paper (authors, year, DOI), each alloy's composition in wt%, product form, the test (medium, temperature, flow, duration, method), and each alloy's corrosion rate exactly as reported with units. List only numbers you can see in the source; say which table or figure each comes from. Prefer peer-reviewed papers and authoritative compilations (for example LaQue, Efird, the Copper Development Association, INCO/Nickel Institute), and say which are compilations.


## Same-test seawater comparisons

The clearest natural-seawater comparison is a 120-day field test of copper, 90–10 Cu–Ni and 70–30 Cu–Ni. A second field dataset compares copper with Cu–30Ni over several longer exposures. The tables below preserve the sources’ reported rates and units; an alloy designation or specification range is **not** a measured specimen composition. (ravindran1983coppernickelalloysfor pages 5-7, gdowski1991surveyofdegradation pages 44-46)

**Ravindran, K. (1983), “Copper-Nickel Alloys for Marine Aquacultural Engineering,” *Fishery Technology* 20, Table 5; DOI not identified.** Metal panels were exposed together for **120 days** in natural seawater at Cochin Harbour, with tidal flushing and water velocity ranging from still to **0.80 m s⁻¹**. The source does not give a test-water temperature or explicitly state how the corrosion rates were measured; these must **not** be called confirmed weight-loss rates. Copper is identified as CDA 110, and the Cu–Ni plates were supplied by International Nickel; measured specimen assays are not given. The 90–10 and 70–30 descriptions imply *nominal*, not analytically verified, **10 and 30 wt% Ni**, respectively. (ravindran1983coppernickelalloysfor pages 3-5, ravindran1983coppernickelalloysfor pages 5-7, ravindran1983coppernickelalloysfor media d2968613, ravindran1983coppernickelalloysfor pages 7-7)

| Table 5 material | Product form | Corrosion rate, exactly as tabulated |
|---|---|---:|
| Copper, CDA 110; Ni content not reported | Plate/panel | **61.0 micron year⁻¹** |
| 90–10 copper-nickel; nominal 10 wt% Ni | Plate/panel | **22.5 micron year⁻¹** |
| 70–30 copper-nickel; nominal 30 wt% Ni | Plate/panel | **22.5 micron year⁻¹** |

The rates and their shared exposure appear together in **Table 5**, not in the article’s separate biofouling table. (ravindran1983coppernickelalloysfor pages 5-7, ravindran1983coppernickelalloysfor media d2968613)

**Wheatfall (1967), as reproduced in G. E. Gdowski (1991), *Survey of Degradation Modes of Four Nickel-Chromium-Molybdenum Alloys*, DOI: [10.2172/138275](https://doi.org/10.2172/138275).** Gdowski is a **compilation**, not the original experiment; an original Wheatfall DOI was not identified. Its **Table 34** reports mass-loss rates in **mdd (mg dm⁻² day⁻¹)** for unalloyed copper and “Cu-Ni(30)” (nominal 30 wt% Ni); **Table 35** supplies their shared natural-seawater exposure conditions. Specimen product form and measured wt% assays are not stated. Deep-water exposures had currents **<0.5 knot**; the surface exposure had **1–2 knots**. (gdowski1991surveyofdegradation pages 44-46)

| Exposure: depth; duration; temperature — Table 35 | Unalloyed Cu — Table 34 | Cu–Ni(30) — Table 34 |
|---|---:|---:|
| **5,640 ft; 123 days; 2.78°C** | **12.1 mdd** | **6.27 mdd** |
| **5,640 ft; 751 days; 2.39°C** | **3.78 mdd** | **2.75 mdd** |
| **5,300 ft; 1,064 days; 2.50°C** | **2.65 mdd** | **3.08 mdd** |
| **Surface; 386 days; 17.61°C** | **4.50 mdd** | **1.42 mdd** |

These results do **not** establish that Cu–30Ni always has the lower rate: copper is lower in the **1,064-day** exposure. Table 34 flags the **4.50 mdd** copper result as weight loss attributed largely to localized corrosion, so it should not be interpreted as a uniform penetration rate. (gdowski1991surveyofdegradation pages 44-46)

## Same-test copper, Cu–Ni and specified aluminum bronzes

**Cohen, A., and A. L. Whitted (1971), “Copper and Its Alloys in the Desalting Environment,” *Corrosion/71*, Paper 57; original DOI not identified.** The numerical results and nominal alloy specifications are reproduced in **L. H. Banning and L. L. Oden (1973), *Corrosion Resistance of Metals in Hot Brines: A Literature Review*, U.S. Bureau of Mines Information Circular 8601, Tables 7–8**. Banning and Oden is a **government literature compilation**; no DOI was identified for it. This is a particularly useful *same-loop* copper/bronze/Cu–Ni comparison, but it represents **treated, hot, flowing seawater**, not quiet ambient immersion. (banning1973corrosionresistanceof pages 16-19, banning1973corrosionresistanceof pages 19-21, banning1973corrosionresistanceof media 97c8a396, banning1973corrosionresistanceof pages 1-4)

The brine-heater simulation used seawater at **250°F**, pH **7.4**, **83%** of normal seawater salinity, **3 ppm CO₂**, and **72 ppb dissolved oxygen in make-up water**. Table 8 separately reports **4, 8 and 16 ft s⁻¹** and **90, 170 and 697 days**. The directly matched **4-ft-s⁻¹, 697-day** column is transcribed below. The retrieved account does not establish the specimens’ product form or explicitly identify the rate calculation as weight loss. **Table 7 contains nominal composition limits, not measured analyses**, and its “Cu+Ag” entries cannot be read as 99.5 wt% copper *in addition to* the listed Ni or Al. (banning1973corrosionresistanceof pages 16-19, banning1973corrosionresistanceof pages 19-21)

| Material; Table 7 nominal composition, wt% | Table 8 corrosion rate: 4 ft s⁻¹, 697 days |
|---|---:|
| **C122**, phosphorus-deoxidized copper: Cu+Ag **≥99.9**, P **0.015–0.040** | **0.99 mpy** |
| **C608**, aluminum bronze: Al **5.0–6.5**, Fe **≤0.10** | **0.75 mpy** |
| **C613**, aluminum bronze: Al **6.0–8.0**, Fe **≤3.5**, Sn **0.2–0.5** | **0.73 mpy** |
| **C706**, 90–10 Cu–Ni: Ni **9–11**, Fe **1.0–1.8** | **0.70 mpy** |
| **C715**, 70–30 Cu–Ni: Ni **29–33**, Fe **0.4–0.7** | **0.46 mpy** |

All five numbers are from the **same Table 8 column**, rather than rates assembled from different tests. Table 7 is the source of the stated wt% *specifications*. (banning1973corrosionresistanceof pages 16-19, banning1973corrosionresistanceof pages 19-21, banning1973corrosionresistanceof media 97c8a396)

A further bronze comparison is **R. O. Lewis and F. LaQue (1981), *Evaluation of Copper-Nickel Alloys for OTEC Applications*, DOI: [10.2172/6255887](https://doi.org/10.2172/6255887), Table XIV**. This is a **DOE compilation of “Inco Unpublished Data.”** Its *uncoupled* specimens were exposed for **one year** in seawater flowing at **0.6 m s⁻¹**: aluminum bronze, **43.2 µm/y (1.7 mpy)**; C70600, **30.5 µm/y (1.2 mpy)**; C71500, **20.3 µm/y (0.8 mpy)**. Table XIV does **not** identify the aluminum-bronze grade or establish that it contains 5–9 wt% Al; nor does it give specimen form, temperature, measured compositions or an explicit mass-loss method. It is therefore a useful bronze/Cu–Ni comparison, but **not a verified Cu–5Al-to-Cu–9Al comparison**. These are the *uncoupled* rows, not the table’s galvanically coupled results. (lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 48-55, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel media 84e4a13c)

## Scope and remaining gap

An additional **peer-reviewed, weight-loss** Cu–Ni comparison—but **without copper or bronze**—is **S. A. Al-Fozan and A. U. Malik (2008), *Desalination* 228:61–67, DOI: [10.1016/j.desal.2007.08.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.desal.2007.08.007)**. Rectangular **50 × 20 × 2 mm** specimens were fully immersed in slowly moving (**12 L h⁻¹**) Arabian Gulf seawater at **25 ± 5°C**. **Table 1** gives measured compositions, wt%: 90/10 Cu–Ni **Cu 88.18, Ni 9.93, Fe 1.41, Mn 0.273**; 70/30 Cu–Ni **Cu 68.81, Ni 30, Fe 0.53, Mn 0.51**. In **Table 3**, fully submerged 90/10 specimens measured **0.145 mpy at 198 days** and **0.082 mpy at 401 days**; 70/30 specimens measured **0.067 mpy at 192 days** and **0.049 mpy at 365 days**. The exposure durations differ by alloy, so even these are **same-study, same-conditions results, not exact same-duration pairs**. (alfozan2008effectofseawater pages 3-5, alfozan2008effectofseawater pages 1-3)

**No verified same-test numerical series containing intermediate Cu–2Ni or Cu–5Ni alongside Cu–10Ni and Cu–30Ni was located.** Ravindran and the Cohen–Whitted compilation supply the narrower **Cu/10Ni/30Ni** progression, with the composition and method qualifications above. Neither a Cu–Ni-only quiet-water table nor a polarization curve has been substituted for the requested copper-versus-Cu–Ni mass-loss evidence. (ravindran1983coppernickelalloysfor pages 5-7, banning1973corrosionresistanceof pages 16-19, banning1973corrosionresistanceof pages 19-21)
