### Additional measurements and qualifications

- **Iron and film maturation in Cu–Ni.** Lewis and LaQue’s review, DOI **10.2172/6255887**, reproduces a separate 60-day test in quiescent **3.4% NaCl**: Cu–10Ni with **0, 0.3, and 1.5 wt% Fe** gave **38.4, 33.0, and 12.2 µm/year**, respectively. The review identifies the underlying study as Popplewell, Hart, and Ford, DOI **10.1016/0010-938X(73)90007-3**; its original full text was not available here. These numbers establish an Fe effect *at fixed nominal Ni*, not a minimum-Ni threshold. In a different, 62-day Wrightsville Beach untreated-seawater test at **0.152 m/s**, 70–30 Cu–Ni–0.4Fe gave **0.0011 in/year**, versus **0.0023** and **0.0027 in/year** for 90–10 Cu–Ni with **0.7** and **1.5% Fe**. Temperature and processing were not reported for that test. (lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 60-64, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 32-35, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 55-60)

- **Broader matched *short-time* compositions.** Yang’s dissertation, DOI **10.26190/unsworks/21760**, used arc-melted, cast, single-phase α alloys, homogenized for **3 h at 750°C** for Cu–Al or **850°C** for Cu–Ni and Cu–Ni–Al. In stagnant, naturally aerated **3.5 wt% NaCl, pH 7, 25°C**, polarization after **2 h** gave, in **µm/year of electrochemically estimated penetration**: Cu **92.4**; Cu–9.3Ni **55.0**; Cu–18.8Ni **44.2**; Cu–28.4Ni **29.0**; and nickel-free Cu–**0.4, 1.1, 2.2, 4.5, 7Al** **30.5, 34.8, 41.9, 52.6, 37.0**, respectively. These binary Cu–Al specimens contain **no Fe** and are not C61400 or CuAl7Fe2–3. The measured ranking is not monotonically improved by Al content. (yang2019theevolutionof pages 37-42, yang2019theevolutionof pages 54-61, yang2019theevolutionof pages 68-71, yang2019theevolutionof pages 71-76)

- **Cu–Ni–Al in that same two-hour test.** For **Cu–2.3Ni–1.1Al**, **Cu–4.8Ni–2.2Al**, **Cu–2.4Ni–3.3Al**, **Cu–2.5Ni–4.5Al**, **Cu–2.5Ni–5.7Al**, and **Cu–2.5Ni–7Al**, the respective estimated rates were **19.8, 46.4, 46.3, 52.4, 87.3, and 70.9 µm/year**. The measured high-Ni/low-Al specimens **Cu–26.3Ni–1.1Al** and **Cu–28.8Ni–1.1Al** gave **35.3** and **49.4 µm/year**. ### Answers to the benchmark questions

**Minimum nickel.** There is **no measured basis here for a hard approximately 6 wt% Ni cutoff** in a 30-day C71500-matched test: the reproduced 30-day test starts at nominal **10% Ni**, and its clean-seawater, **2.0 m/s** duplicate rates for C70600 (**93.9, 84.9 µm/year**) overlap those for C71500 (**95.1, 89.5 µm/year**). This is evidence that *nominal 10% Ni can be comparable under that particular flowing test*, not proof that 5% or less can pass; exact Fe/Mn composition, temperature and processing are unreported. Short-time low-Ni Cu–Ni–Al results also preclude treating a Ni-only step function as an observed electrochemical law, but do **not** demonstrate a 30-day pass. (lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 55-60, yang2019theevolutionof pages 79-83)

**Nickel-free bronze or beta-free NAB beating C71500.** **Not demonstrated in a matched 30-day test.** The closest matched multi-family cleaned-rate comparison is the review’s **unpublished Inco one-year** test at **0.6 m/s**: isolated C71500 **20.3**, C70600 **30.5**, aluminum bronze **43.2**, and NAB **150.0 µm/year**—so neither bronze beat C71500 *there*. But the bronze grades, composition, processing and retained-β/β′ status are **not specified**; this result cannot decide what an annealed, verified beta-free bronze would do at 30 days. It must not be misidentified as a published 30-day C61400/C95800 trial. (lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 48-55, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel media 3dcda885)

**One hour versus 30 days.** A **paired one-hour/30-day ranking for these families does not exist in the located evidence**, so a ranking reversal cannot be asserted. On freshly polished surfaces, Walsh *et al.* found **Cu < 90–10 Cu–Ni < NAB** in *corrosion current* under high-flow natural seawater—an early result, not a mature-film claim. They reported OCP equilibration in **300–1000 s**, not a 30-day coupon series. In Yang’s different, stagnant NaCl test, the **two-hour** Cu–28.4Ni and Cu–2.3Ni–1.1Al both ranked ahead of pure Cu. Within Cu–Ni, Yang observed rising impedance over **600–7200 s**; Lewis and LaQue additionally reproduce a **78-day** comparison in which the corrosion rate over the **last 62 days relative to the first five** was **0.7 for Cu, 0.36 for Cu–20Ni, and 0.18 for Cu–30Ni**. These observations support *time-dependent film protection*, but neither they nor the cross-study rankings provide measured 30-day rates for the missing bronze families. (walsh2024corrosionofcopper pages 4-6, walsh2024corrosionofcopper pages 10-10, yang2019theevolutionof pages 68-71, yang2019theevolutionof pages 95-98, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 55-60)

**Model implication.** Fit oxygen reduction, film maturation and Fe/Ni effects only to measurements with their recorded flow and exposure conditions; keep the Al-rich film and retained-β′ penalties as **uncalibrated for the proposed 30-day C71500 decision** until matched, cleaned-coupon measurements identify them. A polarization current, a corrosion-product-covered mass change, and an exposure-averaged cleaned mass loss are different observables and should not be pooled as interchangeable training labels. (walsh2024corrosionofcopper pages 3-4, walsh2024corrosionofcopper pages 10-10, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 60-64, zhao2022thelaserdeposited pages 7-10, zhao2022thelaserdeposited media fe6cd856)
