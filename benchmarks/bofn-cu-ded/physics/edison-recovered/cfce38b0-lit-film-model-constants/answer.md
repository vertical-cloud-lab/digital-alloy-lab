### 1. Oxygen-reduction limiting current

**No verified numerical limiting-current density** was obtained for a copper alloy in *quiescent, aerated* seawater or 3.5% NaCl at 20–25 °C. In particular, the reported corrosion currents below are mixed-potential or Tafel-extrapolated quantities, **not** oxygen-reduction diffusion plateaus. An EIS Warburg response is likewise not a measured limiting current. Consequently, assigning an oxygen-reduction ceiling in µA cm⁻² from these papers would be an unmeasured model assumption. (zhao2022thelaserdeposited pages 7-10, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 4-5, zhao2022thelaserdeposited pages 10-13)

### 2. Corrosion-current evolution through 30 days

**No defensible power-law exponent or measured set of currents at 1 h, 1 d, 10 d, and 30 d** was established for copper, wrought 90–10 Cu–Ni, wrought 70–30 Cu–Ni/C71500, aluminum bronze, or NAB under a common qualifying exposure. I therefore cannot supply those requested values from Yuan and Pehkonen (2007), Kear and colleagues, Wharton and colleagues (2005), or Schüssler and Exner (1993). Wharton’s relevant paper is identifiable as DOI **10.1016/j.corsci.2005.05.053**, but its primary numerical time series was not available for verification here. This is an evidence-access limitation, not evidence that each experiment was never performed. (king1992thehotisostatic pages 43-49, king1992thehotisostatica pages 55-60)

The measurements that *are* accessible illustrate why a universal decay exponent would be unsafe. Dutta et al. report **14.81 µA cm⁻²** and polarization resistance **2462 Ω·cm²** for *as-cast* Cu–9.9Al–5.6Ni–3.5Fe–1.2Mn (wt%) in 3.5 wt% NaCl; their 1- and 16-day observations are surface morphology, **not repeat measurements of current**. Temperature and aeration were not established in the cited passages. DOI **10.3390/ma15155401**. (dutta2022astudyof pages 2-4, dutta2022astudyof pages 14-17)

Huo et al. report **2.56 ± 0.04, 3.85 ± 0.11, 5.03 ± 0.08, and 7.52 ± 0.07 µA cm⁻²** across *four different heat inputs*, not four exposure times. Their Cu-balance–8.73Al–5.90Fe–4.52Ni–1.39Mn wire was cold-metal-transfer arc deposited and tested in **aerated 3.5 wt% NaCl at 25 ± 5 °C**, following short open-circuit conditioning. The wire exceeds the benchmark’s Fe limit. DOI **10.3390/ma18102205**. (huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 10-13, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 4-5)

Zhao et al. obtained initial polarization currents of **7.82, 4.65, and 4.09 µA cm⁻²** for three *different* laser-deposited NAB/stainless-steel assemblies after approximately **20 min** in 3.5 wt% NaCl at **25 ± 5 °C**. Their separate **0–21-day** immersion/EIS experiment is not a 1-h/1-d/10-d/30-d current series: fitted film resistance can rise and fall as products form and dissolve. The starting NAB powder was Cu–10.8Al–4.1Ni–4.0Fe (wt%), outside the Al limit; nickel-alloy interlayers and steel dilution further change the exposed deposits. Solution flow and aeration were not specified in the retrieved methods. DOI **10.3390/met12050781**. (zhao2022thelaserdeposited pages 7-10, zhao2022thelaserdeposited pages 2-4, zhao2022thelaserdeposited pages 13-14)

### 3. Selective-phase penetration

**No measured µm/day rate for retained β′ *and* for the α constituent of α+κIII was established separately for as-cast, welded, laser-deposited, and arc-deposited NAB.** The available observations must not be relabeled as those rates. Dobson’s plasma-welded cast NAB showed β′ attack near the weld transitioning to selective attack along κIII farther away. After **12 months** in natural Millbay Marina seawater, the **maximum κIII-associated selective-corrosion depth was 1331 µm**; no separate β′ penetration depth was reported. Nominal parent metal was Cu-balance–9.14Al–4.38Fe–4.84Ni–0.09Mn (wt%); seawater ranged **9.7–19.2 °C**, salinity **28.1–35.3 psu**, with uncontrolled field flow and biofouling. Dividing the *maximum observed path* by roughly 365 days gives **3.65 µm/day as an arithmetic exposure average only**—not a measured constant velocity, an α-phase rate, or a 20–25 °C laboratory parameter. A DOI for this thesis was not verified. (dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 143-147, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 92-95, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 130-135)

For Zhao’s laser deposits, the maximum **overall cross-sectional** depths after **21 days** were **3.4 ± 0.1, 2.0 ± 0.1, and 1.8 ± 0.1 µm** for direct-on-steel, Ni–Al-interlayer, and Ni–Cr-interlayer assemblies. The corresponding depth/time quotients are approximately **0.162, 0.095, and 0.086 µm/day**; these are *derived exposure averages*, not measured β′ or α+κIII rates. The first assembly preferentially attacked Fe-rich dendrites. Neither the as-cast current result nor Huo’s arc-deposit polarization series supplies phase-resolved penetration kinetics. (zhao2022thelaserdeposited pages 7-10, zhao2022thelaserdeposited media 9610eaec, dutta2022astudyof pages 14-17, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 13-15)

### 4. Ni, Fe, and Al effects on film transport

**No measured composition-series coefficient** relating Ni or Fe *in solid solution*, or Al content, to the **intrinsic ionic conductivity** of a seawater-grown Ni/Fe-doped Cu₂O or Al-rich film was verified. An EIS fitted film resistance has units **Ω·cm²**; converting it to film conductivity requires, among other things, a known film thickness and an appropriate transport interpretation. It is not an intrinsic conductivity measurement. Zhao’s fitted film resistances for the three chemically and microstructurally different deposits were **291/2766/1460 Ω·cm² at day 1** and **843/2196/938.3 Ω·cm² at day 21**. Their complete table includes nonmonotonic and unusually small fitted values—**2 Ω·cm²** for N-1 and **8 Ω·cm²** for N-2 at day 4—so even these data do not support a simple monotonic Ni-doping factor. DOI **10.3390/met12050781**. (zhao2022thelaserdeposited pages 13-14, zhao2022thelaserdeposited media cf931e62)

Huo’s four *heat-input* conditions give fitted film resistances of **1152, 1047, 947, and 185.8 Ω·cm²**, respectively, in the short aerated-NaCl test. Nominal wire composition was held fixed; heat input changed microstructure, not independently controlled Ni, Fe, or Al concentration. The third EIS-table row is printed “S2” a second time and corresponds in sequence to S3. DOI **10.3390/ma18102205**. These are useful checks on an *effective interfacial-resistance* prior, but neither they nor Zhao’s assemblies identify how much Ni, Fe, or Al individually reduces film ionic conductivity. (huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 4-5, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 13-15, huo2025modulatingheatinput media 2d2fd246)

**Benchmark implication.** Treat the oxygen transport limit, each alloy’s first-30-day decay law, β′/α phase velocities, and composition-to-film-conductivity coefficients as **unmeasured parameters**, rather than filling them with the cited short-test currents or depth/time quotients. None of the reported measurements is a same-test, **30-day cleaned immersion mass-loss comparison against wrought C71500**; they therefore cannot establish which low-Ni compositions pass that criterion. (zhao2022thelaserdeposited pages 7-10, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 4-5, dutta2022astudyof pages 14-17)
