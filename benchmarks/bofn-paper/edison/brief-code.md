Independent correctness review of a cost-aware Bayesian optimization benchmark: pKGFN (partial knowledge gradient for function networks, Buathong et al., ICML 2024) on a synthetic alloy function network. Please act as a skeptical reviewer who is also willing to run code.

The uploaded files are the complete benchmark: bofn_alloy.py (synthetic truth, GP, network model, the KG estimator), policies.py (pKGFN and all baselines), run_sweep.py, analyze.py, plot.py, README.md (the claims as written), settings.md (versions, environment variables, true optima), results.zip (every run as JSON), and the figure bofn_best_vs_spend_k1_partial_vs_full.png. Read settings.md first.

The claim under review (README, "Bottom line"): on this network, when the CALPHAD screen misses the inoculation effect (kappa = 1), the two partial-evaluation policies (pKGFN and BOFN with powder reuse) reach tiered screening's $100k level after about $26k, i.e. 3.8x less spend (90% bootstrap CI about 2.0 to 5.7), over 20 seeds.

Please check the implementation, in this order, and say for each item whether you inspected it, derived it, or executed a numerical test (install torch and botorch with pip if they are not present; if that is impossible, do everything you can with numpy, and say so):

1. Gaussian process posterior (bofn_alloy.TinyGP). post_std() and cov_std() must equal the exact GP posterior mean, variance and cross-covariance for an RBF kernel with unit outputscale, fixed noise 1e-6, constant mean and the fitted lengthscales. Verify numerically against GPyTorch or a direct numpy implementation. Check that the standardization (ym, ys with the 0.25 floor) is undone consistently everywhere it is used (Network._sample, Network.kg, policies.expected_improvement, pkgfn's gmean). Check whether the unit-outputscale assumption matches BoTorch 0.18.1's default SingleTaskGP covariance module.

2. GP fitting. fit_gpytorch_mll is wrapped in a bare try/except that silently keeps whatever hyperparameters the model has when fitting fails. How likely is that to happen here, and what would it do to the policy? Judge the fixed noise of 1e-6 with noise-free observations, the lengthscale prior and bounds, and the std floor of 0.25.

3. The knowledge-gradient estimator (Network.kg). Derive the conditional (fantasy) update for observing node output y_Z at candidate input Z with noise variance sigma^2: mean shift = cov(x, Z) / sqrt(var(Z) + sigma^2) * w with w standard normal, variance reduction = cov(x, Z)^2 / (var(Z) + sigma^2). Confirm the code's `shift` and `vplus` implement exactly this in standardized units, including the broadcast to shape [candidates, fantasies, inner-set points, base samples]. Check the Gauss-Hermite quadrature (10 nodes for one-output stages; a 2-point-per-output product rule, 8 points with equal weights, for the three CALPHAD outputs): are the nodes and weights those of the standard normal (probabilists' Hermite, weights normalized), and is the 2-point rule an acceptable approximation or a problem? Check that fantasized nodes are propagated to their children through node_input and that the children use common random numbers eps shared across fantasies. Check the "current value" term (`base`): it is max over the inner set of the fantasy-weighted average of the fantasized objective, so that KG >= 0 by Jensen's inequality. Is this a consistent estimator of max_x mu_n(x), the current posterior mean of the objective? Could it bias KG upward or downward relative to the textbook estimator, and does it matter for choosing between stages whose costs differ by 250x?

4. Shapes and indexing. node_input, the input order of every node (q takes (a, g); rho takes (P, v, f, g, q); U takes (s, rho)), COMP/IA/IPV indices, the mapping from a chosen LPBF candidate back to its powder lot (policies.run_pkgfn, group "L"), and untested[j] for tensile candidates. Write a small test that evaluates AlloyTruth.full on random designs and compares with Lab.full_eval's recorded node observations.

5. Fairness of the comparison in policies.py. Every policy starts from the same seed data (initial_data), and spend is counted from zero after the seed data. Confirm that the tiered policy's CALPHAD screen is charged ($10 x 224 compositions), that budgets are enforced identically (while loops), that no policy can observe truth values without paying, that pKGFN's partial purchases are costed correctly, and that the Recorder scores every policy with the same metric. Flag any way a policy could be advantaged by construction (for example access to the optimum, to kappa, or to unpaid information). Note that `random`'s and `ei`'s purchases are whole pipelines; is the eifn_reuse ablation implemented as described in the README?

6. Hyperparameters and Monte-Carlo settings. S_ACQ = 64 base samples, N_CAND = 16 candidates per stage, 512 Sobol points plus local perturbations as the pool, an inner-max set of about 48 anchors plus all candidate designs, 10 Gauss-Hermite nodes, the 2^3 product rule, and the EI-per-dollar fallback when KG is numerically zero. Were any of these reduced to a level that would cripple the method? Say what you would test to find out, and if you can run the code, run a check (for example re-score the candidates of one iteration with 64 versus 1024 base samples and see whether the chosen stage changes). The authors are separately running the same check and higher-fidelity re-runs; your independent opinion on what "sufficient" means here is wanted.
