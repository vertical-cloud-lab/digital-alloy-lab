| Diffusion couples / multiples (Zhao) | Join several pure metals or alloys, anneal them, and use interdiffusion to create continuous bulk composition gradients, phase layers, and ternary junctions. Local equilibrium at interfaces supplies tie-line compositions. | One five-component Cr–Pd–Pt–Rh–Ru multiple produced **10 ternary isothermal sections**; diffusion distances were approximately **100 µm**. | Equilibrium phases, phase boundaries and tie lines, interdiffusion coefficients, hardness, modulus, thermal conductivity, thermal expansion, and specific heat; electrical and magnetic properties are also possible. | Example anneal: **1200 °C for 40 h**. One multiple can replace hundreds of equilibrated alloys. TDTR can map **100 × 100 points in about 1 h** at 2–4 µm resolution and approximately ±8% accuracy. | Long anneals; gradients may be too narrow for coarse probes; EPMA or EDS registration and evidence of local equilibrium are required. Poor for liquidus or solidus, corrosion, and bulk tensile response. | Zhao, Zheng & Cahill, *Materials Today* (2005), [doi:10.1016/S1369-7021(05)71122-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1369-7021(05)71122-6); Zhao, *Chinese Science Bulletin* (2014), [doi:10.1007/s11434-014-0120-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11434-014-0120-1) (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000006) |
| Dual-anneal diffusion multiples | A first anneal generates broad solid-solution gradients. The multiple is quenched and sectioned, and slices are aged at different temperatures and times to survey composition–time–temperature space. | A reported Co–Cr–Fe–Mo–Ni multiple contained **10 binary couples and 8 diffusion triples**; every slice supplies many local compositions at one aging condition. | Solvus boundaries, stable and metastable precipitation, transformation kinetics, precipitate morphology, hardness and modulus evolution, and interfacial-energy estimation; suitable for calibrating KWN-type models. | One high-temperature interdiffusion treatment followed by parallel lower-temperature aging of several slices. No universal samples-per-hour rate was reported, but one multiple replaces a large alloy × temperature × time matrix. | Initial treatment can introduce grain-size or vacancy-history artifacts. Local composition must be measured, and spatial resolution limits precipitate characterization. It is not a direct liquid–solid-equilibrium experiment. | Zhao, *Metallurgical and Materials Transactions A* (2020), [doi:10.1007/s11661-020-05915-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11661-020-05915-w) (pqac-00000005) |
| Thin-film sputtered composition-spread libraries | Co-sputter from multiple elemental or alloy targets while using shutters, masks, or substrate motion to form continuous or discrete composition spreads on one wafer. | Commonly hundreds to thousands of addressable positions across binary or ternary spreads; sampled volumes are nanometres to micrometres thick rather than bulk. | Mapped XRD phase screening, resistivity or conductivity, nanoindentation hardness and modulus when substrate effects are controlled, oxidation, and localized corrosion. Phase equilibria are conditional on adequate annealing. | A wafer-scale library is deposited in one run. Automated XRD is approximately **15 positions h⁻¹**; localized electrochemistry ranges from tens to hundreds of positions per hour depending on protocol. | Texture, residual stress, substrate constraint, interdiffusion, oxidation, and suppressed nucleation may differ from bulk LPBF alloys. Unsuitable for bulk tensile properties or direct LPBF-printability assessment. | Wang et al., *Materials* (2023), [doi:10.3390/ma16206696](https://doi.org/10.3390/ma16206696); DeCost et al., *JOM* (2022), [doi:10.1007/s11837-022-05367-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11837-022-05367-0) (pqac-00000043, pqac-00000044) |
| Laser-DED compositionally graded samples | Independently meter multiple powders into a melt pool and vary feeder rates along a track, wall, or array to produce discrete or continuous bulk composition gradients. | Continuous gradients or discrete deposits; HT-READ demonstrated **16 compositions per 32 mm ring/build** using a computer-controlled 16-vial feeder. | Rapid-solidification phase selection, cracking and printability, segregation, hardness, modulus, mapped XRD, SEM/EDS/EBSD, corrosion, and thermal properties. Tensile testing is possible from sufficiently large deposits. | HT-READ characterized all **16 specimens by automated SEM/EDS/EBSD in less than 1 h**; the ring enables automated 22.5° indexing. Multiple libraries can be processed in parallel. | Actual composition can lag feeder commands; substrate dilution, remelting, porosity, evaporation, and thermal-history gradients must be quantified. DED cooling rates and melt-pool dimensions differ from LPBF. | Vecchio et al., *Acta Materialia* (2021), [doi:10.1016/j.actamat.2021.117352](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actamat.2021.117352) (pqac-00000037, pqac-00000038, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040) |
| In-situ alloyed LPBF from elemental or master-alloy blends | Blend elemental or pre-alloyed powders at target ratios and alloy them within each LPBF melt pool, avoiding atomization of every candidate composition. | Usually discrete blends rather than continuous gradients; tens of compositions can be screened if powder changeover, recoating, and contamination are controlled. | LPBF-specific printability, cracking, porosity, melt-pool geometry, rapid-solidification phases, segregation, hardness, conductivity, and aging response. Equilibrium phase data require subsequent homogenization or annealing. | Single tracks and small pads print in seconds to minutes, but blend preparation, loading, cleaning, and composition verification dominate hands-on time. No general peer-reviewed samples-per-hour benchmark is established. | Powder segregation; differences in particle density, size, morphology, and absorptivity; incomplete homogenization; Mg or Zn evaporation; oxygen pickup; and cross-contamination. Equilibrium and ideal Scheil models omit important LPBF nonequilibrium effects. | Aota et al., *Materials* (2020), [doi:10.3390/ma13183922](https://doi.org/10.3390/ma13183922); Bosio et al., *Materials & Design* (2021), [doi:10.1016/j.matdes.2021.110247](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matdes.2021.110247); Martucci et al., *Materials* (2023), [doi:10.3390/ma16031084](https://doi.org/10.3390/ma16031084) (pqac-00000048, pqac-00000049, pqac-00000050) |
| Arc-melted button arrays | Dispense weighed elemental charges, arc-melt under inert gas, remelt for mixing, and cast small bulk buttons that can share heat treatment, mounting, polishing, and automated mapping. | Commonly tens of discrete bulk compositions per batch; gram-scale buttons provide material for XRD, microscopy, hardness, conductivity, corrosion, and DSC specimens. | Bulk equilibrium phases after annealing, DSC or DTA solidus and liquidus, phase fractions, hardness, modulus, conductivity, corrosion, and small-scale mechanical testing. | Throughput depends on furnace automation and number of remelts; no universal peer-reviewed buttons-per-hour rate is established.
