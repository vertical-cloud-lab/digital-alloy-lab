**NETZSCH DSC 404 F1/F3/C Pegasus.** This is a dedicated high-temperature heat-flux DSC with a vertical furnace design that can reach up to 1500 °C. The measurement head is equipped with a type S (Pt/Pt-10 % Rh) thermocouple beneath each crucible position for the sample and reference (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 6-10). The furnace lifts vertically via a hoist mechanism to allow access to the sample carrier. The DSC 404 F1 is documented with both roughing and turbomolecular vacuum pumps, enabling evacuation and inert-gas backfilling (tadgell2019examiningthemechanisms pages 69-76). Argon purge flow rates of 50 mL/min have been used for Al-alloy solidification and Ti-alloy bonding studies (tadgell2019examiningthemechanisms pages 69-76, rawson2021suitabilityofaluminium pages 4-4). Zhang et al. used a NETZSCH 404 DSC in an argon-controlled environment with high-purity Al₂O₃ crucibles, ~20 mg Al-alloy specimens, heating/cooling at 3 K/min, and an argon flow of 35 mL/min (zhang2017differentialscanningcalorimetry pages 2-3). A DSC 404 C Pegasus configuration used for Al-Cu-Si alloy employed 20–60 mg samples in Pt crucibles with Al₂O₃ liners and perforated lids at 50 mL/min Ar and heating/cooling rates of 5–20 K/min (rawson2021suitabilityofaluminium pages 4-4). Typical purge gas flow rates for high-temperature heat-flux DSC systems are 20–100 mL/min at slightly above ambient pressure (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 6-10).

**NETZSCH STA 449 F3 Jupiter.** This simultaneous TG-DSC system uses a platinum/platinum-rhodium sensor holder (TG-DSC sensor type S) (xu2018basicpropertiesof pages 3-3). Two furnace configurations are available: a rhodium furnace rated to 1650 °C and a platinum furnace rated to 1500 °C (steau2021elevatedtemperaturethermal pages 4-8). The STA 449 F3 accepts Pt-Rh crucibles of approximately 6.8 mm outer diameter with vented Pt-Rh lids (xu2018basicpropertiesof pages 3-3), or alumina crucibles of 85 µL volume (teodorescu2026investigationofalsicu pages 3-6). The STA 449 F3 has been used for thermal analysis of Al-Cu-Si alloys, with specimens placed in 85 µL alumina crucibles, heated at 10 °C/min, thermally cycled three times between 500 and 900 °C under N₂ flow at 60 mL/min (teodorescu2026investigationofalsicu pages 3-6). For ASTM E1269 specific-heat measurements, Pt crucibles with Al₂O₃ liners and pin-holed Pt lids were used with ~20 mg sapphire reference discs (steau2021elevatedtemperaturethermal pages 4-8).

**NETZSCH STA 409 PG Luxx.** This interchangeable DSC/DTA-TG platform was used for nickel-aluminium bronze solidification studies, with specimens heated to 1100 °C at 5 K/min, held for 5 min, and cooled to room temperature at 5 K/min (dogan2018microstructuralandthermal pages 1-3).

**Setaram Labsys Evo 1600.** This high-temperature DSC employs two alumina crucibles (sample and reference) and a type S Pt/Pt-10 % Rh thermocouple for furnace regulation (hearn2019microstructuresolidificationmaps pages 1-4, bogno2018effectsofscandium pages 1-4). The instrument has been extensively used for Al-Si and Al-Cu alloy solidification studies under protective flowing argon, with heating and cooling rates of 0.01–0.8 °C/s (0.6–48 K/min) and temperature programs spanning 200–850 °C (hearn2019microstructuresolidificationmaps pages 1-4, bogno2018effectsofscandium pages 1-4).

**TA Instruments SDT 650 and SDT Q600.** The SDT 650 is a simultaneous DSC/TGA instrument used for high-temperature alloy studies up to 1400 °C (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 6-10). The SDT Q600 has a thermobalance precision of 0.0001 mg; a published Al-alloy study used 50 mL/min argon purge, ≤30 mg specimens, and 10 °C/min heating rate (harvey2021quantificationofthe pages 2-3).

### 1.2 Physical Architecture Summary

All instruments share a common heat-flux DSC architecture: a vertical cylindrical furnace surrounds a sample carrier that holds two positions—one for the sample crucible and one for an empty reference crucible. The furnace typically lifts via a motorised hoist to provide access. Thermocouples (type S for high-temperature work) are attached beneath the crucible contact faces on the probe holder, with platinum contact disks ensuring thermal contact. The connection rod is made of alumina for thermal insulation and thermocouple protection (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 6-10). Pan dimensions are typically 5–8 mm; alumina inserts and lids (slightly perforated for isobaric operation) are common (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 6-10). No commercial autosampler for high-temperature DSC above 750 °C was documented in the literature surveyed; autosamplers exist only for lower-temperature polymer-type DSC instruments.

## 2. Operator Handling Steps

### 2.1 Sub-Sampling and Weighing

For gas- or ultrasonically atomised powder (20–63 µm), sub-sampling is performed using a riffle splitter or rotary sample divider to obtain a representative aliquot. The operator weighs 10–25 mg of powder into an alumina crucible using a precision microbalance. Luisi used a Sartorius MC 5 microbalance with a standard measurement uncertainty of less than 10 µg (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 30-33). Published Al-alloy DSC studies used specimen masses around 20 mg (zhang2017differentialscanningcalorimetry pages 2-3) and ≤30 mg (harvey2021quantificationofthe pages 2-3). Conventional calibration is generally conducted with 10 mg samples (braga2011methodologyfordsc pages 6-8). Powder is lightly tapped or gently pressed to ensure good contact with the crucible base; for quantitative enthalpy measurements, good contact between the sample and crucible base is essential (chapman2019determiningtheinfluence pages 55-59).

### 2.2 Crucible Preparation and Lid Placement

Alumina (corundum, Al₂O₃) crucibles are universally recommended for DSC of aluminium alloys that will be melted. NIST recommends corundum and copper as crucible materials; platinum and zinc were disregarded because they did not meet all requirements (anmark2012acalorimetricanalysis pages 46-49, anmark2012acalorimetricanalysis pages 43-46). Alumina is located in the lower part of the Ellingham diagram, confirming the oxide's thermodynamic stability in contact with molten aluminium (anmark2012acalorimetricanalysis pages 46-49). Alumina crucibles showed no detectable impact from contact with molten Al-Cu-Si alloy, and the solidified pellet did not adhere to the alumina crucible (rawson2021suitabilityofaluminium pages 8-8). Lids are placed on crucibles and are slightly perforated for isobaric operation to allow pressure equilibration (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 6-10).

### 2.3 Why Platinum Crucibles Must Not Be Used with Molten Aluminium

Platinum crucibles must be avoided when measuring aluminium alloys above their melting point. Platinum did not satisfy all chemical compatibility requirements established by NIST for DSC crucible materials (anmark2012acalorimetricanalysis pages 46-49). The Al-Pt binary system contains several low-melting eutectic reactions (the Al-Pt eutectic lies at approximately 1527 °C, but Al-rich intermetallic compounds such as PtAl₂ form readily at the Al liquidus). Molten aluminium aggressively wets and dissolves platinum, forming intermetallic phases that irreversibly destroy the crucible and contaminate the sample, invalidating the thermal signal. While some investigators have used platinum crucibles (possibly due to platinum's higher thermal conductivity than alumina), this practice is not recommended (anmark2012acalorimetricanalysis pages 43-46). Where platinum crucibles must be used (e.g., for cp measurements per ASTM E1269), an alumina liner must be inserted to prevent contact between the melt and platinum (rawson2021suitabilityofaluminium pages 4-4, steau2021elevatedtemperaturethermal pages 4-8).

### 2.4 Furnace Loading, Evacuation, and Backfilling

The operator positions the loaded sample crucible and an identical empty reference crucible symmetrically on the sensor contact faces (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 6-10). The furnace is lowered (or the hoist raises it) and sealed. For the NETZSCH DSC 404 F1, the roughing pump and turbomolecular pump are engaged to evacuate the measurement chamber (tadgell2019examiningthemechanisms pages 69-76). The chamber is then backfilled with high-purity argon (≥99.999 %). The evacuate-backfill procedure is typically repeated 3 times to reduce residual oxygen below damaging levels. A continuous purge of argon is then established; published flow rates are 35 mL/min (zhang2017differentialscanningcalorimetry pages 2-3), 50 mL/min (rawson2021suitabilityofaluminium pages 4-4, tadgell2019examiningthemechanisms pages 69-76, harvey2021quantificationofthe pages 2-3), or 60 mL/min (teodorescu2026investigationofalsicu pages 3-6).

### 2.5 Temperature Program

A typical temperature program for Al-alloy solidification (e.g., AlSi10Mg, Al-Mg-Sc-Zr) involves: heating from room temperature at 3–20 K/min to 720–850 °C (approximately 50–100 °C above the alloy liquidus), holding isothermally for 5 min to achieve complete melting and thermal equilibration, then cooling at a controlled rate (3–20 K/min, or as low as 0.6 K/min for near-equilibrium solidification studies) back to room temperature (zhang2017differentialscanningcalorimetry pages 2-3, hearn2019microstructuresolidificationmaps pages 1-4, bogno2018effectsofscandium pages 1-4). Commonly, two or three consecutive heating-cooling cycles are performed; the second heating is considered more representative because the first cooling homogenises the microstructure (zhang2017differentialscanningcalorimetry pages 2-3). For Cu-alloy (NAB) work, the temperature program extends to 1100 °C, with heating and cooling at 5 K/min and a 5-minute isothermal hold at peak temperature (dogan2018microstructuralandthermal pages 1-3).

### 2.6 Post-Run Checks

After the run, the sample crucible is removed and weighed to check mass loss (which may indicate oxidation or melt spillage). The solidified bead is visually inspected: good wetting of the alumina crucible base and formation of a compact, shiny metallic bead indicates satisfactory measurement conditions. Any discolouration, adherence to the crucible walls, or visible oxide growth suggests oxygen contamination.

### 3.2 Sample Masses and Procedure

Calibration specimens are prepared with low masses, mostly 10–40 mg, to obtain sharper melting peaks and reduce uncertainty in the onset temperature determination (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 33-38). Conventional calibration is typically performed at 10 mg sample mass and 10 °C/min heating rate (braga2011methodologyfordsc pages 6-8). Calibration is performed in the same crucible type, atmosphere, and configuration as the actual measurements; heating rates of 2–20 K/min are used (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 33-38, anmark2012acalorimetricanalysis pages 46-49).

### 3.3 Onset and Enthalpy Determination

The extrapolated onset temperature of the melting peak is determined from the intersection of the pre-transition baseline with the tangent drawn on the leading (low-temperature) slope of the melting endotherm. This onset temperature is compared with the nominal melting point; the resulting temperature offset is interpolated across the full calibration range using a polynomial or piecewise-linear fit (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 30-33). For enthalpy calibration, the melting endotherm is integrated between baseline-corrected limits, and the calculated heat of fusion is compared with the certified value (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 21-23, anmark2012acalorimetricanalysis pages 43-46). A minimum of three calibration substances spanning the measurement temperature range is recommended, though more are preferred (anmark2012acalorimetricanalysis pages 43-46, braga2011methodologyfordsc pages 5-6).

### 3.4 Acceptable Deviations

ASTM E968 reports a heat-calibration repeatability of 0.94 % at the indium melting point and 1.4 % within 265 °C of that point, with performance degrading by approximately 0.7 percentage points per additional 100 °C. Reproducibility is 2.7 % within 265 °C, decreasing by 2.0 percentage points per additional 100 °C. Extrapolation of a single-point indium calibration to high temperatures can produce unacceptable reproducibility—up to 25 % (matteo2014characterizingthemeasurement pages 21-23). ASTM D3418 acceptance criteria specify ±4.2 °C for onset melting-temperature reproducibility and ±7.3 % for enthalpy (braga2011methodologyfordsc pages 6-8).

### 3.5 Thermal-Lag Correction

The thermal-lag contribution can be estimated from the temperature shift equation ΔT = T_max − T₀ = 2 ΔH_m R β + τ_lag β, where τ_lag represents the thermal delay, β is the heating rate, and R is the total thermal resistance (braga2011methodologyfordsc pages 6-8). The trailing edge (high-temperature side) of a pure-metal melting peak depends on the total thermal resistance and the sample heat capacity, while the leading slope reflects the inverse total thermal resistance (braga2011methodologyfordsc pages 2-3). By examining the return of the signal to baseline after the melting peak at several heating rates, the instrument time constant τ can be extracted. This time constant is then used to deconvolute measured DSC signals and correct for smearing effects, particularly important at higher heating rates or for samples with high thermal resistance (braga2011methodologyfordsc pages 6-8, braga2011methodologyfordsc pages 2-3). Calibration should be performed and verified approximately every two months (anmark2012acalorimetricanalysis pages 43-46).

**NETZSCH LFA 467 HyperFlash/HT.** A xenon-flash-lamp-based system capable of testing from room temperature to 1200 °C. It has four small individual furnaces on a motor-controlled base and a turbomolecular pump achieving vacuum levels below 10⁻⁴ torr. It can test oxygen-sensitive materials (tungsten, graphite) using ultra-high-purity argon with gettering (wang2025referencematerialsfor pages 15-23). The xenon flash lamp provides software-controlled pulse energy up to 10 J per pulse (costa2021thermalcharacterizationof pages 68-71).

Both systems follow ASTM E1461 for design and data analysis (wang2025referencematerialsfor pages 15-23).

### 4.2 Specimen Geometry and Preparation

Specimens are typically 10 × 10 × 2 mm squares (costa2021thermalcharacterizationof pages 36-40, steau2021elevatedtemperaturethermal pages 4-8, teodorescu2026investigationofalsicu pages 3-6) or round disks of 12.7 mm or 6.35 mm diameter (toney2022technicalguidanceon pages 12-17). Standard specimens are 10 mm diameter × 1–2 mm thick; miniature specimens of 3 mm diameter × 0.5 mm thick have been validated (akiyoshi2018validationofminiature pages 1-2). Recommended thickness depends on diffusivity: 1–1.5 mm for low, 1.5–2 mm for medium, and 2–3 mm for high diffusivity materials (toney2022technicalguidanceon pages 12-17). Specimens must have flat, parallel surfaces. For LFA of printed Al-alloy coupons, specimens are machined or wire-EDM cut to the required geometry and thickness.

**Graphite coating.** Both faces of each specimen are coated with a thin, uniform layer of graphite spray (e.g., Graphit-33 carbon spray) to increase absorptivity of the flash energy and improve emissivity for the infrared detector (toney2022technicalguidanceon pages 12-17, akiyoshi2018validationofminiature pages 1-2). Solvent-clean the specimen first, then apply several thin spray passes, allowing 2–5 minutes of drying between coats; three thin coats per face is a documented practice (steau2021elevatedtemperaturethermal pages 4-8, toney2022technicalguidanceon pages 12-17). For semi-transparent materials such as Pyroceram 9606, a metallic layer (Pt or Au) may be needed on each side to prevent light penetration (wang2025referencematerialsfor pages 15-23).

### 4.3 Sample Holder and IR Detector

Specimens are placed in holder plates within the sample tray and covered by a mask with an opening centred over each specimen (toney2022technicalguidanceon pages 6-12). The LFA 447 NanoFlash uses a liquid-nitrogen-cooled InSb (indium antimonide) infrared detector sensitive to 1–5 µm wavelengths (toney2022technicalguidanceon pages 12-17). Commercial laser-flash systems generally employ Nd:YAG pulsed lasers or xenon flash lamps with millisecond or microsecond pulse durations (sundar2024thermaltransportand pages 10-14).

### 4.4 Measurement Sequence

A typical measurement sequence involves:
1. Stabilise the specimen at the programmed temperature isothermally.
2. Fire the flash pulse; record the time-dependent temperature rise on the rear face with the IR detector.
3. Wait for the specimen to return to the furnace equilibrium temperature.
4. Repeat: three shots are taken at each temperature to ensure reproducibility (sundar2024thermaltransportand pages 20-22, costa2021thermalcharacterizationof pages 40-45, teodorescu2026investigationofalsicu pages 3-6).
5. Step to the next programmed temperature (e.g., at 20 °C intervals from 25 to 500 °C) and repeat (steau2021elevatedtemperaturethermal pages 4-8).

The measurement duration is set to approximately ten times the half-rise time (t₁/₂), and the maximum temperature rise is adjusted to approximately two-fifths of the horizontal measurement window (costa2021thermalcharacterizationof pages 40-45). Data are fitted using the Cowan model with pulse correction, the Cape-Lehman model, or the Parker model, depending on heat-loss conditions and pulse duration relative to t₁/₂ (toney2022technicalguidanceon pages 6-12, teodorescu2026investigationofalsicu pages 3-6). Reported measurement accuracy is ±3 % for thermal diffusivity and ±5 % for specific heat, with repeatability of ±2 % and ±3 %, respectively (costa2021thermalcharacterizationof pages 68-71).

### 4.5 Thermal Conductivity Computation

Thermal conductivity is computed from the relationship:

**k(T) = α(T) × ρ(T) × c_p(T)**

where α is the thermal diffusivity from the flash measurement, ρ is the specimen density (measured by Archimedes' method per ASTM D792 or geometric measurement), and c_p is the specific heat capacity measured independently by DSC per ASTM E1269 or by comparative measurement against a reference in the LFA itself (steau2021elevatedtemperaturethermal pages 4-8, teodorescu2026investigationofalsicu pages 3-6). The reference material for c_p via LFA is typically Pyroceram 9606, POCO graphite, or a copper standard of known properties, with matched dimensions, coating, and test conditions (costa2021thermalcharacterizationof pages 36-40, teodorescu2026investigationofalsicu pages 3-6, wang2025referencematerialsfor pages 15-23).

### 4.6 Reference Materials for LFA System Validation

The NETZSCH reference material set includes Pyroceram 9606, POCO graphite, and Inconel 600, with certified thermal diffusivity and thermal conductivity values up to 1000 °C. For moderate-to-high conductivity materials (such as Al alloys), Inconel 600 is the preferred reference material due to its consistency, durability, and simple sample preparation—it is opaque and requires only a graphite spray coating (wang2025referencematerialsfor pages 15-23).
