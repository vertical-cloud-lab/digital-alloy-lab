### 1. Vickers Microhardness (ASTM E384, ISO 6507)

**Tester mechanics.** A Vickers microhardness tester houses a motorised turret carrying a 136° square-based pyramidal diamond indenter and two or more objectives (typically 10× for locating and 40× for measuring). The load is applied through a dead-weight or closed-loop mechanism. A calibrated machine presses the indenter into the polished specimen surface under a predetermined force in the range 0.098–980.7 N; loads ≤0.980 N (≤100 gf) are classified as microhardness tests (kumar2025characterisationofin718 pages 25-29). Common LPBF test loads include HV0.1 (100 gf) with a 15 s dwell (amir2022studyonthe pages 2-3) and HV0.5 (500 gf) with a 10 s dwell (argyrou2025multiscalemechanicalanisotropy pages 4-7). Loads of HV0.025 have also been used for melt-pool-scale mapping of LPBF Scalmalloy® (ghio2025laserpowderbedfused pages 1-2). The standard dwell time range is 10–15 s (argyrou2025multiscalemechanicalanisotropy pages 4-7, amir2022studyonthe pages 2-3).

**Specimen preparation and levelling.** The LPBF coupon is sectioned in the XY (transverse to build) and XZ (parallel to build) planes using precision cutting, then hot-mounted in Bakelite or cold-mounted in epoxy, ground through successive SiC grits, and polished to a mirror finish (commonly to 1 µm diamond or colloidal silica). The mounted surface must be flat and level, perpendicular to the indenter axis, to prevent asymmetric indents (argyrou2025multiscalemechanicalanisotropy pages 4-7).

**Indent spacing rules.** Per ASTM E384, the centre-to-centre distance between adjacent Vickers indentations must be at least 2.5 times the mean diagonal length d (argyrou2025multiscalemechanicalanisotropy pages 4-7). More conservative practice uses ≥3d spacing, as reported for LPBF AlSi10Mg tested at HV0.1 (amir2022studyonthe pages 2-3). The distance from an indent centre to the nearest specimen edge must be at least 2.5d (ASTM E384-22).

**Measuring both diagonals.** After unloading, the turret rotates to the high-magnification objective. The operator focuses the four corners of the square impression and measures both surface-projected diagonals d₁ and d₂; the hardness is calculated from their mean (kumar2025characterisationofin718 pages 25-29).

**Automated stages and hardness maps.** For hardness mapping across LPBF cross-sections, a motorised XY stage is programmed with a grid pattern. Published examples include 200 µm centre-to-centre spacing at HV0.2 with 10 s dwell on maraging steel LPBF sections (doris2023effectofmultiple pages 36-40), and 50 µm spacing at HV0.025 with 25 gf and 15 s dwell on LPBF AlSi10Mg (argyrou2026lpbfalsi10mgat pages 9-10). The resulting array produces a colour-coded hardness map revealing melt-pool boundaries, heat-affected zones, and build-direction gradients.

**Tabor HV/3 relation.** The Tabor relation provides an approximate conversion between Vickers hardness and yield strength. For non-strain-hardening materials, HV ≈ 3σ, where HV and σ are both expressed consistently in MPa (hales2020hardnessstrengthcorrelationsin pages 8-11, hales2020hardnessstrengthcorrelationsin pages 11-16). For aluminium alloys, the conversion from kgf mm⁻² to MPa uses HV (MPa) = 9.807 × HV (kgf mm⁻²); the yield strength estimate is then σ_y ≈ HV(MPa)/3. As an example, an Al-6101 alloy with HV 125 kgf mm⁻² gives an estimated σ_y ≈ 1226/3 ≈ 409 MPa, reasonably close to the measured 375 MPa (tavakkolisaiej2025optimizationofmechanical pages 138-141). One Al–Mg–Si study used the inverse form Δσ ≈ 3 × ΔHV for incremental strengthening contributions (khangholi2021effectsofnatural pages 14-19). The constraint factor K is typically 2.7–3.0 and is material-dependent, so the relation should be treated as approximate (hales2020hardnessstrengthcorrelationsin pages 11-16, hales2020hardnessstrengthcorrelationsin pages 8-11).

### 2. Eddy-Current Electrical Conductivity (%IACS)

**Standards and reporting.** Eddy-current conductivity is measured per ASTM E1004 (also DIN EN 2004-1 and DIN EN 50994). Results are reported as %IACS, where 100% IACS corresponds to 58 MS/m at 20 °C (li2021effectofaging pages 2-4, czerwinski2024aluminumalloysfor pages 3-5).

**Instruments and probe.** Published aluminium-alloy studies have used the Fischer SIGMASCOPE SMP10 with an 11 mm diameter probe (shen2012theeffectsof pages 52-63) and the Fischer SIGMASCOPE SMP350 (shao2025newgenerationof pages 87-91). Multi-frequency instruments such as the Foerster SIGMATEST 2.069 and the Hocking AutoSigma 3000 offer selectable frequencies of 60, 120, 240, 480, and 960 kHz. A study on 7N01 aluminium alloy used 60 kHz per ASTM E1004 (li2021effectofaging pages 2-4).

**Standard depth of penetration.** The electromagnetic skin depth (standard depth of penetration) is δ = 1/√(π f μ₀ μ_r σ), where f is frequency, μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ H/m, μ_r ≈ 1 for aluminium, and σ is conductivity. At 60 kHz and 20 MS/m (≈ 34.5% IACS), δ ≈ 0.46 mm; at higher frequencies δ decreases, improving near-surface sensitivity.

**Minimum thickness and flat area.** The specimen must be thicker than approximately 2–3 δ at the selected frequency and the flat contact area must exceed the probe diameter. A reported specimen geometry was 15 × 15 × 10 mm for 60 kHz measurement (li2021effectofaging pages 2-4).

**Lift-off compensation and calibration.** The instrument is calibrated using certified conductivity reference blocks of known %IACS before each measurement session. Surface preparation is typically grinding through fine-grit metallographic papers (up to 3000#) to ensure a smooth, flat contact surface (li2021effectofaging pages 2-4, shen2012theeffectsof pages 52-63). Temperature compensation is applied by calibrating at room temperature and either correcting readings or ensuring the specimen, probe, and standards are at a common equilibrium temperature near 20 °C. At least three readings per location are taken; published aging studies averaged ≥15 measurements per condition (shao2025newgenerationof pages 47-54, shao2025newgenerationof pages 87-91).

**Use for aging/precipitation monitoring.** Eddy-current conductivity has been extensively used to follow precipitation kinetics in aluminium alloys:

- **Al–Sc–Zr conductor alloys:** Aging at 350 °C for up to 48 h produced conductivities of 57.4–59.9% IACS as Al₃(Sc,Zr) precipitates formed and depleted solute from the matrix (shao2025newgenerationof pages 67-72, shao2025newgenerationof pages 47-54).
- **LPBF Scalmalloy® (Al–Mg–Sc–Zr):** Electrical conductivity increased monotonically from approximately 11.7 MS/m (≈ 20.2% IACS) during direct aging at 275, 325, and 400 °C, reflecting solute depletion and Al₃(Sc,Zr) precipitate evolution (ghio2025laserpowderbedfused pages 1-2).
- **Al–Mg–Si (AA6201):** A Matthiessen's-rule framework was applied, where total resistivity is the sum of contributions from pure aluminium, dislocations, grain boundaries, precipitates, and solute atoms; conductivity in %IACS increases as precipitates remove solute from the matrix (khangholi2021effectsofnatural pages 19-23).

### 3. Isothermal Aging of Small Coupons

**Coupon geometry and furnace.** LPBF Scalmalloy® was aged as 15 × 15 × 5 mm plates extracted from bars built with a 15 × 15 mm base, using a muffle furnace (ghio2025laserpowderbedfused pages 2-4). Typical aging temperatures for Al–Mg–Sc–Zr alloys are 275, 325, and 400 °C, with soak times from 5 minutes to 256 hours; the reference manufacturer-recommended condition is 325 °C × 4 h (ghio2025laserpowderbedfused pages 2-4, cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 11-14). For other Al–Mg–Sc–Zr compositions, aging at 300, 350, 375, and 400 °C has been studied, with as-built hardness of ~105 HV increasing to ~160 HV after 24 h at 350 °C (belelli2022investigationandcharacterization pages 8-14). AlSi10Mg T6 treatments typically involve solution treatment at 520 °C for 1 h followed by water quenching and artificial aging at 160 °C for 6 h (bosio2021laserpowderbed pages 199-204).

**Thermocouple verification.** An AlSi10Mg study monitored the furnace chamber temperature using a thermocouple with reported fluctuations of ±1 °C throughout the heat treatment (bosio2021laserpowderbed pages 199-204). Best practice is to place a calibrated check thermocouple on the tray beside a representative coupon rather than against a furnace wall or heating element.

**Water quenching practice.** For solution-treated AlSi10Mg, specimens are transferred immediately into an agitated room-temperature water bath for rapid quenching. The interval between solution treatment and subsequent artificial aging is kept below 30 minutes to limit natural aging (bosio2021laserpowderbed pages 199-204). For Al–Mg–Sc–Zr direct aging (e.g., 325 °C × 4 h), air cooling is more commonly used (zhao2022laserdirectedenergy pages 8-9), although some two-stage protocols specify water cooling between stages (zhao2022laserdirectedenergy pages 8-9).

**Operator actions for animation.** Preheat the empty furnace to set point. Place cleaned, uniquely marked coupons in a single layer on a low-mass ceramic or stainless-steel tray with gaps for air circulation. Insert the tray rapidly and start soak timing only when the check thermocouple returns to the set temperature. At the scheduled time, remove each coupon with tongs and either air cool on a refractory surface (direct aging) or immediately immerse in the water-quench bath (solution treatment), ensuring full submersion and adequate agitation.

### 4. Chemical Analysis by ICP-OES

**Standards.** ASTM E3061 covers ICP-OES determination of elements in aluminium alloys; ASTM E1479 covers aluminium powder analysis. These reference validated dissolution and measurement protocols.

**Sample mass and digestion.** A representative protocol for aluminium alloy reference-material certification used 150 mg of alloy placed in a microwave digestion vessel with 3.0 mL HNO₃ and 5.0 mL HCl. After microwave heating, the solution was cooled, transferred quantitatively to a 100 mL volumetric flask, and diluted with deionised water (yamani2021preparationandcertification pages 2-3). An alternative wet-digestion route used 500 mg of sample with 20 mL of 1+1 HCl and dropwise addition of 30% H₂O₂ (yamani2021preparationandcertification pages 2-3). For Si-bearing alloys (e.g., AlSi10Mg), hydrofluoric acid may be required in PTFE/PFA closed vessels to dissolve refractory siliceous residues; boric acid is added to complex excess fluoride before aspiration (robinson2025ggrhandbookof pages 9-10). Alkaline (lithium-borate) fusion is an alternative for complete Si dissolution (robinson2025ggrhandbookof pages 9-10). A direct digestion approach for aluminium alloy pins used 5% HCl with ultrasonic agitation at <50 °C (brown1999rapidaluminumalloy pages 46-50).

**Instrument path.** The dissolved sample is pumped by a peristaltic pump through Teflon tubing to a concentric nebuliser, where the liquid is converted into a fine aerosol by high-velocity argon carrier gas (lotter2014analysisofzirconium pages 90-97, malefo2016quantificationofhafnium pages 95-100, lotter2014analysisofzirconium pages 107-112). The aerosol enters a spray chamber (cyclonic or Scott double-pass design), where large droplets are removed to drain and only fine mist proceeds (lotter2014analysisofzirconium pages 90-97, lotter2014analysisofzirconium pages 107-112). The aerosol is transported into the central channel of a quartz argon ICP torch, where the RF-sustained plasma operates at approximately 6000–10,000 K (malefo2016quantificationofhafnium pages 95-100, lotter2014analysisofzirconium pages 97-102). Analyte atoms and ions are excited and emit element-characteristic wavelengths. The torch can be viewed radially (side-on, favoring stability) or axially (end-on, improving detection limits) (lotter2014analysisofzirconium pages 90-97, uemoto2011instrumentalchemicalanalysis pages 3-5). The emitted light enters a high-resolution spectrometer; an echelle grating with a prism provides cross-dispersed wavelength separation (uemoto2011instrumentalchemicalanalysis pages 3-5, lotter2014analysisofzirconium pages 112-116), with modern instruments using a CCD or CID detector array for simultaneous multi-element measurement.

**Calibration and CRMs.** Multi-point calibration is performed using matrix-matched multi-element standards prepared from 1000 ppm single-element stock solutions, with aluminium held at a concentration matching the expected sample matrix. NIST aluminium alloy standard reference materials (SRMs) are prepared using the same digestion procedure to verify trueness (yamani2021preparationandcertification pages 2-3, brown1999rapidaluminumalloy pages 41-46). Reagent blanks, calibration verification standards, duplicate measurements, and spiked recoveries are run as quality-control measures.

**Detection limits.** Practical ICP-OES detection limits are instrument-, line-, view-, and matrix-dependent. For aluminium alloy solutions at typical dilution factors, representative LODs (3σ of blank) are on the order of: Mg 0.1–1 µg/L, Si 5–20 µg/L, Fe 0.5–5 µg/L, Mn 0.1–1 µg/L, Cr 0.5–5 µg/L, Sc 1–5 µg/L, and Zr 0.5–5 µg/L in solution. These translate to approximately 0.5–10 µg/g in the original solid, depending on the dilution factor employed. One study achieved sub-ppm detection limits in solution by axial viewing (lotter2008identificationandquantification pages 61-67). For Sc, expanded uncertainty of approximately 8% has been reported (yamani2021preparationandcertification pages 2-3).

### 5. Oxygen, Nitrogen, and Hydrogen by Inert-Gas Fusion

**Standards.** ASTM E1019 covers O/N in metals by inert-gas fusion, ASTM E1409 covers O in metallic materials, and ASTM E1447 covers H in metallic materials.

**Instruments.** Key commercial instruments include the LECO ONH836, ELTRA ONH-2000, and Bruker G8 GALILEO. The Bruker G8 GALILEO uses a programmable impulse/electrode furnace reaching up to 3000 °C, monitored by a contact-free optical sensor, and accepts solid samples up to 1 g including powders (paplewskiUnknownyearanalyticalsolutionsfor pages 4-7). The LECO ONH836 and ELTRA ONH-2000 have been compared in published FeSiAl powder studies (liu2025fastdeterminationof pages 1-4).

**Sample mass and capsules.** The recommended sample mass for powder analysis is 80–100 mg; masses above 120 mg can produce incomplete recovery due to incomplete melting and decomposition of refractory phases such as alumina and silica (liu2025fastdeterminationof pages 4-6). The powder is weighed into a tin capsule. Tin (melting point 231.9 °C) is the preferred flux because its low melting point improves melt fluidity and permits complete oxygen release; nickel flux (melting point 1453 °C) can increase melt viscosity and prevent complete oxygen evolution (liu2025fastdeterminationof pages 4-6). Nickel baskets or foil cones are listed among available accessories and may be used for specific matrix requirements (liu2025fastdeterminationof pages 1-4).

**Outgassing the graphite crucible.** A graphite external crucible and nested graphite crucible are installed between the electrodes of the impulse furnace. Before sample analysis, the empty crucible is purged with high-purity inert carrier gas (helium or argon) and outgassed at high power. Published settings: LECO ONH836 degasses at 5.5 kW for 20 s, then analyses at 5.0 kW for 45 s; ELTRA ONH-2000 degasses at 5.5 kW for 45 s, then analyses at 5.0 kW for 40 s (liu2025fastdeterminationof pages 1-4). The outgassing step is repeated until the instrument blank is stable and acceptably low.

**Detection of gases.** At high temperature, oxygen-containing compounds in the sample react with the excess carbon of the graphite crucible to form CO (and CO₂), which is swept by the carrier gas to selective non-dispersive infrared (NDIR) detectors. Nitrogen compounds decompose to N₂, and hydrogen is released as H₂; both are measured by a thermal conductivity detector (TCD) (paplewskiUnknownyearanalyticalsolutionsfor pages 4-7). The total analysis time per sample is approximately 4 minutes (liu2025fastdeterminationof pages 1-4). Calibration is performed using steel or matrix-matched certified reference materials with known O, N, and H contents, and the capsule/flux blank is subtracted (liu2025fastdeterminationof pages 1-4, liu2025fastdeterminationof pages 4-6). Reported oxygen limits of quantification (LOQ) were 0.010–0.012% (100–120 ppm), with limits of detection (LOD) of 0.003–0.004% (30–40 ppm) (liu2025fastdeterminationof pages 4-6).

**Typical oxygen content of Al powders.** Virgin gas-atomized AlSi10Mg powder typically carries a native Al₂O₃ film of approximately 2–5 nm thickness and an initial oxygen content of approximately 500–600 ppm (0.05–0.06 wt%) (rezaei2026powderdegradationand pages 27-29, rezaei2026powderdegradationand pages 32-33). Extended LPBF powder recycling increases oxygen from ~600 ppm to ~1100–1250 ppm due to progressive oxide thickening during repeated exposure to air during handling, sieving, storage, and thermal processing (rezaei2026powderdegradationand pages 27-29, rezaei2026powderdegradationand pages 32-33). For copper powder (EB-PBF grade), reported oxygen levels after hydrogen treatment were approximately 225 ppm in powder and ~50 ppm in solid parts (ledford2019characteristicsandprocessing; DOI: 10.3390/app9193993).

**Sealing powders under argon.** To avoid oxygen pickup before analysis, aluminium and copper powders must be stored in airtight containers, preferably under an argon atmosphere (paplewskiUnknownyearanalyticalsolutionsfora pages 2-4, paplewskiUnknownyearanalyticalsolutionsfor pages 1-2). Best practice includes transferring the powder in an argon-purged glovebox, sealing it in evacuated and argon-backfilled containers, and opening only immediately before weighing the analysis portion. For highly reactive fine aluminium powders (5–150 µm), the high specific surface area makes even brief air exposure a significant source of oxygen contamination (paplewskiUnknownyearanalyticalsolutionsfor pages 2-4).
