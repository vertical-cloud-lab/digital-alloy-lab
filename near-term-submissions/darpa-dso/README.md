## Exact submission steps

1. **Fill the red TODOs**: BYU logo on the cover, administrative POC (name,
   mailing address, telephone, email). Remove all red text.
2. **Check with BYU research administration** whether they or Sterling submit
   (see questions below). The executive summary has no budget, but BYU may
   still want to route it.
3. **Register in BAAT** at <https://baa.darpa.mil/> (General Terms,
   "Unclassified Submission Instructions"). It is a two-step registration:
   - Click "Create New Account" to request an Extranet account, then wait for
     two separate emails, one with the username and one with a temporary
     password.
   - Log in to the Extranet, then use "Register your Organization" (left side
     of the homepage) to create the BAAT account. Every person who submits must
     create their own Organization Registration, even if BYU is already
     registered.
   - Accounts are deactivated after 30 days of inactivity, so register close
     to the submission date or log in regularly.
4. **Make one zip file** containing `executive-summary.pdf` (PDF or Word are
   both accepted, Attachment A p. 1). BAAT accepts only `.zip` or `.zipx`
   archives, at most 100 MB, one archive per submission; a later upload to the
   same submission overwrites the earlier one (General Terms, "Unclassified
   Submission Instructions").
5. **Submit through BAAT** under HR001126S0015. Executive summaries must go
   through BAAT whatever award instrument is wanted later (BAA p. 6). Email
   submissions are not accepted (General Terms).
6. **Wait for DARPA's letter** from HR001126S0015@darpa.mil before submitting
   anything else to this BAA (BAA pp. 6 to 7). Keep the letter: it must be
   attached as Attachment Q to the abstract or full proposal (BAA pp. 6, 7, 8).

BAAT technical support: BAAT_Support@darpa.mil, with a copy to
HR001126S0015@darpa.mil, Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. ET (General
Terms). Questions on content, format, or deadlines go to
HR001126S0015@darpa.mil; the question deadline is Aug 6, 2027, 4:00 PM ET
(BAA p. 2).

## Timing and what follows

- Executive summaries are accepted on a rolling basis until **Aug 27, 2027,
  4:00 PM ET**; abstracts and full proposals have the same rolling deadline
  (BAA p. 2).
- DARPA "will strive to respond to all preproposal submissions within 30
  calendar days." If there is no response within 60 calendar days of the BAAT
  timestamp, the proposer may submit another executive summary or abstract
  (BAA p. 7).
- The response is "a brief statement either encouraging or discouraging
  submission of a follow-on abstract or full proposal," with brief feedback if
  discouraged (BAA p. 6). The proposer may submit an abstract or full
  proposal either way (BAA p. 6).
- A full proposal without a prior executive summary or abstract, or without
  the DARPA letter as Attachment Q, is non-conforming (BAA p. 7).
- Full proposal route depends on the instrument: a cooperative agreement goes
  through Grants.gov with the SF424 (R&R) budget; procurement contracts and
  other transactions go through BAAT (BAA pp. 7, 9). An accelerated award
  option exists for full proposals of at most $2,000,000, with award within 30
  days of selection (BAA p. 7).

## One pending pre-proposal rule

- "Proposing PIs may only submit one (1) submission of any type at a time to
  this solicitation" (BAA p. 2).
- Only one pre-proposal (executive summary or abstract) may be pending per PI.
  If several are sent, DARPA evaluates only the first by BAAT timestamp and may
  reject the rest (BAA p. 7).
- Once DARPA has encouraged an abstract, the PI may submit a new executive
  summary or abstract on a different effort (BAA p. 7).

## Drafting choices

- **Thrusts**: (1) Materials, Manufacturing, and Energy Systems and (4)
  Complex, Dynamic, and Intelligent Systems.
- **Bold claim**: a tenfold cut in the cost and time of finding and
  qualifying a printable alloy, stated as a target. The evidence is the
  simulation only: 63% of the optimum after $100,000 for screening, reached by
  the cost-aware search after about $26,500 (3.8 times less spend, 90% CI 2.0
  to 5.7, 20 campaigns), with the caveats that it is hand-built, noise-free,
  one objective, one experiment at a time, and that screening kept pace when
  its physics was right (a2c tex Section 5; `benchmarks/bofn-prelim/README.md`).
- **No figure**: the simulation plot would need about 3.6 in of width for its
  labels to reach 8 pt, which does not fit on the one technical page with all
  four answers.
- **No cost or schedule**: Attachment A does not ask for either.
- **Capabilities stated**: aluminum only in-house (induction module), four
  compositions per week, LPBF builds bought at $800 per build from a
  University of Utah user facility, BYU Microscopy Center, BYU computing
  cluster, CALPHAD workflow and powder-to-part demonstration in hand. The
  plasma module (about $150,000) and more printer capacity are named as what a
  DARPA-scale effort would add.
