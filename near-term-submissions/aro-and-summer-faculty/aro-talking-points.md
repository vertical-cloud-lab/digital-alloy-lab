# Talking points for the ARO program manager call

Sources: "BAA" means DEVCOM ARL BAA W911NF-23-S-0001, Amendment 4 (posted Jan 8, 2025), page numbers as printed (PDF page = printed + 4): https://files.simpler.grants.gov/opportunities/5e185327-5f25-4c09-889a-43615e1f54da/attachments/3da7da00-4aaa-4ee0-b51f-f7b62c4008aa/DEVCOM_ARL_BAA_W911NF23S0001_Amendment_4_JAN_8_2025.pdf . "Topics" means the topics document generated 12/09/2024, PDF pages: https://files.simpler.grants.gov/opportunities/5e185327-5f25-4c09-889a-43615e1f54da/attachments/e4621f8a-b19c-40ec-8c59-6611cc4fa276/Current_Research_Topics_for_DEVCOM_ARL_BAA_9_Dec2024.pdf

1. Opening question. Does a cost-aware search over the powder-to-part chain fit Synthesis and Processing of Materials, and what basic question should sit at its center? The topic seeks "the governing processing-microstructure-property relationships" and lists additive manufacturing and metals as keywords (Topics p. 62). One candidate question: what decides whether a high-strength aluminum alloy cracks in LPBF, learned with fewer builds than a fixed screening order.
2. Army interest, as the topic states it. The topic lists the Army Modernization Priorities "Next Generation Combat Vehicle; Soldier Lethality" and names "ultrasonic arrangement" among example directions for new processing methods (Topics p. 62). Ask whether in-house ultrasonic atomization of custom alloy powder counts under that thrust.
3. Eligibility. ECP is open to U.S. citizens, nationals, or permanent residents who have held a tenure-track position for fewer than five years at application (BAA p. 8). If BYU does not call the position "tenure track," the proposal must say so and the chair letter must state that the PI will be considered for a permanent appointment (BAA p. 8). Confirm how ARO reads a December 2025 start at BYU.
4. ECP or STIR. ECP pays up to $120,000 per year for 3 years and needs a supporting letter from the chair or dean (BAA p. 8 to 9). STIR pays $60,000 or less over 9 months, allows no capital equipment, and must not depend on facilities outside the applicant's direct control (BAA p. 7 to 8). Ask whether buying LPBF builds from the University of Utah user facility rules out a STIR, or whether a STIR should cover only atomization, CALPHAD, and the algorithm.
5. Research Instrumentation for the plasma module. RI funds instrumentation for research in ARO topics or "the development of new research capabilities," and ARO asks applicants to discuss the budget with the TPOC first (BAA p. 10). Ask whether a plasma atomization module, which would extend in-house powder beyond aluminum, fits RI, what budget is typical, and whether RI must tie to a current or proposed DoD project (BAA p. 11).
6. Overlap with AFOSR. Say up front that an AFOSR YIP white paper on the same method went in on Oct 9, 2026. The BAA asks proposals to name other agencies receiving them and says concurrent submission "will not prejudice its review by ARL if ARL is kept informed" (BAA p. 25). Ask what scope would make an ARO version distinct.
7. White paper logistics. Up to 5 pages, one PDF, emailed to the TPOC, with title, PI, organization, topic name and ID, TPOC name, objective and approach, what is new, a rough cost estimate, and a short bio (BAA p. 21 to 22). Ask about turnaround, timing relative to the fiscal year, and whether a draft review is welcome.
8. What Sterling wants to learn. The program's current thrusts and recent awards, since the posted topic text dates from December 2024. Whether to also contact ARL's in-house additive manufacturing topic (ARL-BAA-0075, TPOC Brandon A. McWilliams, Topics p. 67) or Modeling of Complex Systems (Dr. Robert S. Martin, Topics p. 40).
