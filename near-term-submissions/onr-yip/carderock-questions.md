before the proposal is due (Oct 30), so answers can still change the
proposal. The questions below are ordered by how much each answer would
change the draft. Each says what the answer would change.

## 1. Questions that would change the proposal

1. **Which printed aluminum parts would the Navy most like to have?**
   Shipboard (brackets, housings, HVAC and fluid-system parts) or aviation?
   Which alloys and tempers would they accept today (AlSi10Mg, F357,
   Scalmalloy, A20X, something else)? *Changes:* the target properties in
   Table 2 and the naval-relevance section, which now names ships and
   aircraft only in general terms.
2. **Which corrosion tests decide whether a printed aluminum part can go on
   a ship?** Intergranular (ASTM G110 or G67), exfoliation (G34 or G85),
   salt spray (B117), stress corrosion (G44 with G47 or G49), galvanic
   compatibility with steel? *Changes:* the long tests in Task 2. The draft
   uses G110/G67 and B117 and leaves stress corrosion to Navy labs.
3. **What does a qualification test matrix for one printed aluminum
   composition cost, and how long does it take?** Even a rough number (weeks,
   dollars, number of specimens) anchors the cost model and the claim that
   the method saves money. *Changes:* the cost per step in the simulated
   benchmark and Fig. 2.
4. **Do they have corrosion data on printed aluminum they could share** as a
   public-release dataset for the retrospective benchmark? If it is
   Distribution A or can be cleared, it could be named in the data
   management plan. *Changes:* Task 1 benchmark and the DMP "Existing data"
   paragraph.
5. **Would anyone be willing to be listed as an unfunded collaborator** (for
   example, advising on corrosion methods or running a stress corrosion test
   on the final candidates)? ONR allows collaborators if the proposal
   explains how they fit the single-PI structure (NOFO p. 19), and they must
   then appear in current and pending support (p. 38). Get name, title, and
   whether they would write a short letter. *Changes:* Management Approach,
   current and pending support, and possibly a Field 12 letter. Ask ONR
   (Email 1, question 3 in `onr-questions.md`) before relying on it.
6. **Which ONR program officers fund their printed-metal and corrosion
   work?** If they name someone other than Dr. Fisher, that may be a better
   home for Field 4b. *Changes:* SF-424 Field 4b and the PO email.

## 2. Questions that build the relationship (also YIP goals)

7. **Would a Carderock branch host a Summer Faculty Research Program visit
   in summer 2027?** Applications close Dec 14, 2026. The YIP lists SFRP
   engagement as one of its purposes (NOFO p. 8), and the proposal says the
   PI will apply. Ask which branch and who to list as the host.
8. **Do they take NREIP interns?** The proposal says students will be
   encouraged to apply (NOFO p. 8).
9. **Are they running, or planning, autonomous or high-throughput
   experiments** (corrosion, welding, AM process development)? What data
   infrastructure do they use, and what blocks sharing data with a
   university (controlled unclassified information, export control)?
10. **How is printed-part qualification handled for NAVSEA**, and where does
    printed aluminum stand today? Is there published guidance the proposal
    should cite?
11. **Is sensitization still the main aluminum corrosion problem in the
    fleet**, and would they expect printed Al-Mg alloys (for example
    scandium- or zirconium-modified) to sensitize the same way as 5456? A
    2023 study found that printed Al-Mg-Sc-Zr aged at 150 C became
    susceptible to intergranular corrosion while its polarization curves did
    not change (Cabrera-Correa et al., *Corros. Sci.* 218, 111166). Ask
    which test they would trust to catch that: ASTM G67 after a sensitizing
    heat treatment, or something else.
12. **Printed parts as "new material applications."** A 2021 NAVSEA and
    Carderock briefing said printed critical parts are treated as a "New
    Material Application" under T9074-AX-GIB-010/100. What does that mean in
    tests and time for a printed aluminum part, and what would make it
    cheaper?

## 3. Things to show or say in the talk

- Show the simulation result (Fig. 3 in the proposal; partial evaluation
  reached screening's $100k level after about $26.5k). It is already public
  through the MS&T26 talk.
- Mention that the method treats corrosion tests as steps with their own
  costs, and that a one-hour polarization screen could stand in for weeks
  of exposure only if it predicts them; the audit of screened-out
  candidates is how that gets checked. That invites the audience to say
  which tests they trust.
- Keep the talk to published or public material. Do not share the
  unsubmitted proposal text or budget.
