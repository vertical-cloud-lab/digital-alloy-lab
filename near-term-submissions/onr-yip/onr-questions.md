# Questions for ONR before the Oct 16 cutoff

The NOFO sends questions to three places (NOFO p. 8):

| Kind of question | Send to | Cutoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Programmatic or eligibility | Dr. Reginald G. Williams, YIP Program Manager, ONR Code 34, `ONRYIP@navy.mil` | Fri Oct 16, 5:00 PM ET (p. 7) |
| Research topic and naval priorities | The program officer, here Dr. Charles Fisher, Code 332 (see `po-email.md`) | before submission; same Oct 16 date is safest |
| Grants.gov submission | Veronica Lacey, Grants Officer, ONR Code 253, `veronica.y.lacey.civ@us.navy.mil` | Thu Oct 29, 5:00 PM ET (p. 7) |

The NOFO asks that questions be "concise and to the point" and cite the part
and paragraph of the NOFO, and warns that "questions submitted within 2 weeks
prior to a deadline may not be answered" (p. 8). Oct 16 is exactly two weeks
before Oct 30, so send on **Mon Oct 12**.

Page numbers below are the NOFO's own (file `FY27_YIP_NOFO_N0001426SF004__003.pdf`).

## Email 3 (optional): ONR's corrosion program officer

Corrosion is now a main objective, and Dr. David Shifler's Code 332 program
(Corrosion Science and Corrosion Control Technologies) asks for work on the
"corrosion mechanism in additive manufactured (AM) alloys" and says machine
learning can "accelerate materials discovery and predict material life". He
may be as good a home as Dr. Fisher. The ONRYIP cc is needed only on the
first email to ONR, so leave it off if Email 1 or `po-email.md` already went.
If both POs reply with interest, ask them which one should be named in
Field 4b; only one can be.

**To:** david.a.shifler.civ@us.navy.mil
**Subject:** N0001426SF004 FY27 YIP proposal: fit question for Corrosion Science and Corrosion Control

```text
Dr. Shifler,

I plan to submit an FY27 YIP proposal (N0001426SF004) by October 30 and would like to ask whether it fits your program. I have also written to Dr. Fisher in Additive Manufacturing.

The proposal is a cost-aware search for printable high-strength aluminum alloys in which corrosion resistance is one of the objectives. A one-hour polarization screen, intergranular tests, and salt-spray exposure are steps the method chooses among, based on expected information per dollar. Because a polarization screen can miss sensitization in printed Al-Mg-Sc-Zr, the method also tests a random share of the candidates the screen would reject, to measure how often it is wrong.

The plan is a single-PI effort at about $250K per year for three years. Would this fit Corrosion Science and Corrosion Control, and if so, should I name your program on the SF-424?

Thank you,
Sterling Baird
Assistant Professor, Mechanical Engineering, Brigham Young University
sterling.baird@byu.edu | (801) 422-5344
```
