\begin{center}\textbf{Equipment}\\
ONR FY2027 YIP, NOFO N0001426SF004. PI: Sterling G.\ Baird, Brigham Young University (BYU)\end{center}

\textbf{Equipment available.} The equipment below is in place and available
to the project at no cost, except where a facility charges hourly rates,
which are in the budget.
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}
\item AMAZEMET rePOWDER ultrasonic atomizer with an induction crucible module
(to 1,300\,\textdegree{}C), with the coated plates and boron nitride nozzles
that the manufacturer supplies for copper alloys (PI laboratory, BYU;
purchased with start-up funds in 2026).
\item Liquid-handling gantry (CubXL, Ursa Laboratories) with a pipette, a
vial capper and two cameras, run by our own scripts (PI laboratory). It runs
the electrochemical corrosion screen and samples the immersion solutions.
\item Powder handling, sieving and specimen preparation equipment in the PI
laboratory: an open-source auger powder doser built in the laboratory, a
sieve stack from 45 to 250~$\mu$m with a sieve shaker, a Hall flowmeter
funnel, an analytical balance, glove bags, dry powder storage and sectioning
and mounting equipment.
\item Scanning electron microscopes with EDS and EBSD (Apreo C, Helios 600
with a focused ion beam, Verios) and a Tecnai TF-20 transmission electron
microscope at the BYU Microscopy Center.
\item Agilent 8900 ICP-MS/MS and XPS at the BYU Spectroscopic Facility, and
X-ray diffraction at the BYU X-ray Diffraction Facility.
\item Metallographic polishers (PX500 automatic, PX300 manual and
vibratory), a Keyence VHX-7000 digital optical microscope, and CNC machining
at BYU.
\item Instron load frames in the materials testing laboratory of the
Department of Mechanical Engineering, for sub-size tensile tests
\todo{confirm}.
\item The BYU Office of Research Computing cluster (about 28,000 CPU cores
and 367 GPUs).
\item Blown-powder DED through purchased sessions: the InssTek MX-Lab (500~W,
six powder feeders) at the University of Utah for tracks and small deposits
from up to 0.5~kg of powder, and the FormAlloy X2 at the Colorado School of
Mines for 2~kg builds and repair coupons.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Equipment to be purchased with project funds.} One potentiostat with
corrosion software and a flat cell, Year 1, \$18,000. This is a budgetary
estimate for a research-grade single-channel instrument with electrochemical
impedance capability and corrosion software (for example, a Gamry Interface
1010E or a BioLogic SP-150e, each with a flat cell and reference
electrodes). A vendor quote accompanies the budget. The electrochemical
corrosion screen (open-circuit potential, linear polarization resistance,
potentiodynamic polarization and impedance in substitute ocean water) is one
step of the workflow that the cost-aware search schedules. The instrument
therefore has to be available every day and run by our own scripts on the
gantry described above. No potentiostat at BYU can be dedicated to the
project in this way. The shared instruments on campus are booked by the
hour, and the search cannot schedule them. Equipment is excluded from the
modified total direct cost base.

\textbf{Government-owned equipment.} None.
