\begin{center}\textbf{Equipment}\\
ONR FY2027 Young Investigator Program, NOFO N0001426SF004. PI: Sterling G.\ Baird, Brigham Young University (BYU)\\
{\small\color{gray}Text copy: the photographs are left out and their captions kept as plain text.}\end{center}

\textbf{Equipment available.} The equipment below is in place and available
to the project at no cost, except where a facility charges hourly rates,
which are in the budget.
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}
\item AMAZEMET rePOWDER ultrasonic atomizer with an induction crucible module
(to 1,400\,\textdegree{}C, typically run to 1,300\,\textdegree{}C;
Facilities, Fig.~1), with the coated plates and boron nitride nozzles that
the manufacturer supplies for copper alloys (PI laboratory, BYU; purchased
with start-up funds in 2026).
\item Liquid-handling gantry (CubXL, Ursa Laboratories; Fig.~1b) with a
pipette, a vial capper and two cameras, run by our own scripts (PI
laboratory). It runs the electrochemical corrosion screen and samples the
immersion solutions.
\item Powder handling, sieving and specimen preparation equipment in the PI
laboratory: an open-source auger powder doser built in the laboratory
(Fig.~1a), a sieve stack from 45 to 250~$\mu$m with a sieve shaker, a Hall
flowmeter funnel, an analytical balance, glove bags, dry powder storage and
sectioning and mounting equipment. The atomizer's steel-panelled enclosure
(exhaust, argon supply, dehumidifier, strip-curtain door) with an ATEX-rated
vacuum cleaner, a Class D extinguisher and full-face respirators.
\item NuVant EZstat Pro potentiostat, lent to the PI laboratory by a BYU
colleague and run by our own scripts on the gantry, for the electrochemical
corrosion screen (Fig.~2).
\item Scanning electron microscopes with EDS and EBSD (Apreo C, Helios 600
with a focused ion beam, Verios) and a Tecnai TF-20 transmission electron
microscope at the BYU Electron Microscopy Facility.
\item Agilent 8900 ICP-MS/MS and XPS at the BYU Spectroscopic Facility, and
X-ray diffraction at the BYU X-ray Diffraction Facility.
\item Metallographic polishers (PX500 automatic, PX300 manual and
vibratory), a Keyence VHX-7000 digital optical microscope, and CNC machining
at BYU.
\item Instron load frames in the materials testing laboratory of the
Department of Mechanical Engineering, for sub-size tensile tests
\todo{confirm}.
\item The BYU Office of Research Computing cluster (about 28,000 CPU cores
and 367 GPUs).
\item Blown-powder DED through purchased sessions: the InssTek MX-Lab (500~W,
six powder feeders) at the University of Utah for tracks and small deposits
from up to 0.5~kg of powder, and the FormAlloy X2 at the Colorado School of
Mines for 2~kg builds and repair coupons.
\item Gas atomization through one purchased run a year at Ames National
Laboratory (Dr.~Jordan~A.~Tiarks, unfunded collaborator), which turns the
year's discovered alloy into a gas-atomized lot.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Figure 1.} Equipment in the PI laboratory. (a) The open-source auger
powder doser over its analytical balance in a fume hood, dosing an atomizer
charge (September 2026). (b) The CubXL liquid-handling gantry with its vial
rack and pipette. Frames from the laboratory's videos.

\textbf{Equipment to be purchased with project funds.} None. The
electrochemical corrosion screen, one step of the workflow that the
cost-aware search schedules, runs every day on the potentiostat and gantry
listed above under our own scripts, so no instrument is requested.

\textbf{Figure 2.} The copper corrosion screen on the gantry, from the
laboratory's animation of the planned protocol: an O-ring cell on a
copper-nickel specimen during the open-circuit potential step, with the
simulated current through the spot. The potentiostat listed above drives
this step.

\textbf{Government-owned equipment.} None.
