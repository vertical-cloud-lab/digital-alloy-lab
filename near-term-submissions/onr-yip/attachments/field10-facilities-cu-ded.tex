\begin{center}\textbf{Facilities and Other Resources}\\
ONR FY2027 YIP, NOFO N0001426SF004. PI: Sterling G.\ Baird, Brigham Young University (BYU)\end{center}

\textbf{PI laboratory, BYU Department of Mechanical Engineering.} A 700
square foot powder metallurgy laboratory in the Clyde Building (CB 154),
assigned to the PI, holds an AMAZEMET rePOWDER ultrasonic atomizer with an
induction crucible module. We use the atomizer to make alloy powder from
elemental and master-alloy feedstock weighed into machined cups, about four
compositions per week and about 0.5~kg per run. For this project the cups
are oxygen-free copper and the powders are copper alloys. They are melted at
up to 1,300\,\textdegree{}C in graphite crucibles and atomized onto coated
carbon-carbon plates. For melts that contain nickel or iron, the crucible is
sprayed with boron nitride and the nozzle is boron nitride. A powder doser
built in the laboratory, an open-source auger doser that weighs each dose on
an analytical balance (0.1~mg), dispenses the powdered minor additions into
each charge and logs every dose. Sieves (ASTM B214), a Hall flowmeter funnel
(ASTM B212 and B213), glove bags and dry powder storage support the powder
checks. The room has a canopy hood, a fume hood, an industrial dehumidifier,
and electrical, gas, compressed air and chilled water services installed
with BYU Environmental Health and Safety. It connects directly to the BYU
Microscopy Center by a door with no public access.

\textbf{BYU Microscopy Center.} Scanning electron microscopes with
energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) and electron backscatter
diffraction (EBSD) (Thermo Fisher Apreo C, Helios 600 with a focused ion
beam, and Verios), and a Tecnai TF-20 transmission electron microscope. The
instruments are shared, have high availability, and are charged at internal
hourly rates (in the budget).

\textbf{Other BYU facilities.} Metallographic preparation uses PX500
automatic and PX300 manual polishers, vibratory polishers, an etching fume
hood and an electropolisher, with a Keyence VHX-7000 digital optical
microscope for inspection. We measure composition and structure at the BYU
X-ray Diffraction Facility and the BYU Spectroscopic Facility (Agilent 8900
ICP-MS/MS and XPS). The BYU Prototyping Lab machines charge cups and test
specimens by CNC. Sub-size tensile tests (ASTM E8/E8M) run on the Instron
load frames of the materials testing laboratory in the Department of
Mechanical Engineering \todo{confirm}. We measure particle size
distributions by sieve analysis in the PI laboratory and by image analysis
of SEM micrographs.

\textbf{Computing.} The BYU Office of Research Computing operates a shared
cluster of about 28,000 CPU cores and 367 GPUs on 616 nodes
(\url{https://rc.byu.edu}, 2026), shared among BYU researchers at no direct
cost. CALPHAD calculations use Thermo-Calc with its copper alloy database
\todo{confirm the BYU license includes it} and the open-source pycalphad;
heat-flow calculations and the search software run on the same cluster.

\textbf{Directed energy deposition.} Single-stream, blown-powder laser DED is
not available at BYU. The project buys machine time at two facilities, and
the search treats them as two steps of different cost. Neither facility is a
subawardee. \emph{The small step} is the InssTek MX-Lab in the laboratory of
Professor Z.~Zak Fang (Department of Metallurgical Engineering, University
of Utah, Salt Lake City, 45 miles from BYU), which his group has offered to
the project. It has a 500~W fiber laser, 150~mm of travel on each axis,
control of melt-pool height, and six powder feeders that can be run
separately. It makes pre-placed tracks from a few grams of powder and
blown-powder tracks, thin walls and small blocks from about 0.5~kg, on a day
trip from BYU. Its use is budgeted as hourly instrument time until the group
sets a rate \todo{confirm with Fang: the rate, the smallest powder charge
per feeder, the argon chamber and any preheat, and who runs it}. \emph{The
large step} is the FormAlloy X2 blown-powder laser DED system at the
Colorado School of Mines (ADAPT center and Shared Instrumentation Facility,
Golden, Colorado), bought on a purchase order. Facility staff run it at
posted external rates for machine time and labor (in the budget). A typical
session of four 25~mm cubes takes about 3 machine hours and 2 labor hours.
The X2 takes powder from 50 to 200~$\mu$m, needs about 2~kg of each powder
to fill its hoppers, catches about half of the powder fed in the melt pool,
and has deposited copper-nickel tracks. It builds the tensile and corrosion
blocks and the repair coupons on thick plate, which need more laser power
than the MX-Lab has \todo{the X2's laser}. The United States representative
of InssTek has also offered deposition within the InssTek network, which
lists nickel-aluminum bronze (UNS C95300) among its qualified materials.

\textbf{Corrosion testing.} The electrochemical screen runs on the
liquid-handling gantry in the PI laboratory (CubXL, Ursa Laboratories: a
pipette, a vial capper and two cameras on a scripted gantry with about
390 $\times$ 230 $\times$ 120~mm of travel). The gantry positions small
O-ring cells on polished specimens, with the potentiostat requested in the
Equipment section. Immersion tests in substitute ocean water (ASTM D1141) run
in capped vials on the deck of the gantry, which samples the solutions for
ICP-MS at the BYU Spectroscopic Facility. Oxygen and carbon analysis of
powder lots is purchased from a commercial laboratory (inert gas fusion for
oxygen and combustion for carbon).

\textbf{Government-owned facilities.} None are required. No additional
facilities will be acquired at the applicant's expense.
