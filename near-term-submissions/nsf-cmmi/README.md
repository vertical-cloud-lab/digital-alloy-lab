## Who to send it to, and how

- **To:** `cmmi-amdo@nsf.gov` (AMDO Program Team, (703) 292-8360). Neither the solicitation nor the AMDO program page names an individual program director for AMDO; both list only this team mailbox. So the email is addressed "Dear AMDO Program Director" and has no cc.
- **How:** from `sterling.baird@byu.edu`, with `one-page-summary.pdf` attached and the text of `email.md` as the body.
- **When:** now, well before any full proposal. The solicitation says it is "strongly encouraged and expected that investigators discuss their ideas and potential fit with a Program Director well in advance of proposal submission."
- If a named program director replies, send all follow-ups (and any call) to that person.

## What happens next

1. A program director replies with guidance on scope. The solicitation: "Communication with Program Directors does not constitute review or influence funding decisions."
2. If the fit is good, prepare a full proposal under PAPPG Chapter II.D.2 and submit through BYU's sponsored research office via Research.gov or Grants.gov. Proposals are accepted anytime, due by 5 p.m. submitting organization's local time. No letter of intent or preliminary proposal.
3. Review: an initial compliance check, then an NSF program officer, and in most cases three or more external experts (ad hoc, panel, or both), on Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts.

How the summary follows this guidance: the solicitation asks only for a one-page summary that lets the program director judge scope, so the page states the research question, approach, resources and preliminary results, the two review criteria (Intellectual Merit, Broader Impacts), and ends with the scope question. The "decision method is the subject of the research" sentence answers the "scientifically justified rather than incidental" test.
