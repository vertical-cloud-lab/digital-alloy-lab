Yes. # Small-batch ultrasonic atomization for LPBF alloy development

**Yes.** I found **four reports covering nine distinct, custom aluminium powder compositions** that explicitly connect ultrasonic atomization, LPBF of the resulting powder, and a measured mechanical property of the printed material. They represent **three research programmes**, because the two Al–Zr–Er–Ni reports concern related but different compositions. “Mechanical property” here includes indentation hardness or creep; **only the Al–Zr–Er–Ni programme reports printed tensile tests**. This is a count of studies identifiable and verifiable in the literature searched, not a guarantee that unpublished studies do not exist. (chiara2023anewalcu pages 5-6, taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 22-25, ge2025highstrengthadditivelymanufacturable pages 6-7, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 4-6)

**Important qualifications to the table:**

- The original Taheri-Mousavi paper is available as a 2024 preprint and subsequently as an *Advanced Materials* publication. Its supplemental methods label particle-size percentiles “%” although the values describe sizes and are treated here as **µm, with that source-unit error flagged**. Its measured Zr changes from **3.25 wt.% in powder to 2.90 wt.% in the print**; that observation alone does not establish whether evaporation, sampling variation, or measurement effects caused the difference. The Ge paper explicitly describes a *different*, lower-Zr/lower-Ni powder made using the 60 kHz rePowder graphite setup; its process specifications and recovery must **not** be retroactively assigned to the earlier AUS500 powder. (taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 22-25, ge2025highstrengthadditivelymanufacturable pages 4-6, ge2025highstrengthadditivelymanufacturable pages 8-9)
- Monti’s approximately **30% after-sieving yield** has no explicitly defined 15–45 µm cut. Ge’s **40–70%** is atomization-process recovery during optimization, and its **1,500 g final output** is not the product of one stated 400–500 g charge. Ekaputra reports a **20–100 µm sieve fraction without its mass**. None of these figures establishes a standardized yield of 15–45 µm powder across the studies. (chiara2023anewalcu pages 3-5, ge2025highstrengthadditivelymanufacturable pages 8-9, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 4-6)
- For Ekaputra, hardness values in **MPa are indentation hardness, not tensile yield strength**. Table 1 measures composition in selected, relatively clean LPBF regions, **not a mass-balanced feedstock→powder→part recovery**: conspicuous Zr-/solute-rich powder particles produce printed compositional “swirls,” and the TZM sonotrode contributes Mo. The six alloys constitute a genuine physical composition comparison, but neither Bayesian optimization nor a tensile-screening campaign. (ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 6-12, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 12-16, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 40-43)

## Aluminium near-misses and volatile-element evidence

- **Ukabhai et al. (2025), Al–10Cu:** rePowder induction atomization of a **100 g charge**, **1,100 °C**, **40 kHz**, reported D10/D50/D90 **36/65/78 µm**. The separate **50 g rod** was arc melted. Powder and rod were characterized, **but no LPBF specimen or printed mechanical test was made**. The article also says “90% below 70 µm,” inconsistent with its tabulated D90 of 78 µm; the table value should not be silently overwritten. (ukabhai2025investigationofalcu pages 1-3, ukabhai2025investigationofalcu pages 3-7, ukabhai2025investigationofalcu pages 7-10)
- **AlSi12 atomization-parameter study:** compares powder characteristics and process settings, **not printed mechanical properties**. The SiC/AlSi9Mg ATO Lab+ example is likewise **DED**, not LPBF: 35 kHz, 80% amplitude, 130 A, with powder D10/D50/D90 **63/80/92 µm**. (yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 2-3, pippig2023semifinishedpowderof pages 1-3, pippig2023semifinishedpowderof pages 3-6)
- **PA1 Al–5.5Ni–4.6Ce–1.84Mn–0.3Fe–0.3Zr, wt.%:** custom ultrasonic powder was examined in LPBF tracks, multilayers and defect-imaging experiments. The published tensile-strength improvement belongs to a **separate directed-energy-deposition (DED) experiment on PA1**, not mechanically tested LPBF coupons. Thus PA1 does **not** meet the combined criterion. (guo2026influenceofsubstrate pages 2-4, guo2026influenceofsubstrate pages 4-5, guo2026mechanicalandin pages 1-3, guo2026mechanicalandin pages 3-4)
- **Bugelnig et al. (2026 preprint):** an especially close *computational-screening* comparator: **1,500 simulated scrap-mixture designs**, narrowed to **12 candidate mixtures**; one selected Al–Si–Cu–Mg–Ni-rich composition was atomized on an **Indutherm AUS500** and printed in miniature LPBF experiments. The paper assesses defects and manufacturability, but **does not report a measured mechanical property of that LPBF material**; its strength estimates are model outputs. (bugelnig2026analsicumgnialloy pages 7-11, bugelnig2026analsicumgnialloy pages 11-14, bugelnig2026analsicumgnialloy pages 14-21)

**Mg and Zn loss in newly designed Al alloys remains an evidence gap for this exact workflow:** the nine verified printed-and-mechanically-tested Al compositions contain neither intentionally added Mg nor Zn. It would be incorrect to use their W/Mo pickup or Zr/Er/Ni deviations as measurements of Mg/Zn volatilization. A nearby *non-Al* demonstration, Mg–7.5Li–3Al–1Zn, retained measured Li and Zn approximately across powder and LPBF, while another induction-atomization study of an Ag-modified **Mg alloy** reports potential Zn evaporation at its high pouring temperature. These are not measurements on Al–Mg or Al–Zn powders. (chiara2023anewalcu pages 1-3, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 4-6, ge2025highstrengthadditivelymanufacturable pages 6-7, dobkowska2022acomparisonof pages 2-3, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6)

## Bayesian optimization and autonomy: precisely what has been demonstrated?

**Bayesian optimization coupled to the route: yes, but only for computational composition selection, not an autonomous physical feedback loop.** Taheri-Mousavi et al. trained ML surrogates on CALPHAD-derived descriptors, used Gaussian-process Bayesian optimization of the **computed** design landscape, and then checked **nine** approximately **30 g induction-melted, laser-scanned compositions**. **One** selected composition became ultrasonically atomized powder and was LPBF-printed and mechanically tested. Reported design-space figures include **250,000–500,000 virtual compositions**, approximately **40 simulated training compositions** sufficient for the neural-network descriptor surrogate, and **80 computational evaluations** for one BO/ICME convergence example: **none of these numbers is a count of independently atomized powders**. Ge et al. subsequently evaluated **one** changed Al–Zr–Er–Ni powder formulation guided by CALPHAD, not a newly automated physical BO campaign. Ekaputra printed **six** compositionally different powders without ML/BO steering. (taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 4-7, taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 9-12, taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 31-40, taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 22-25, ge2025highstrengthadditivelymanufacturable pages 4-6, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 4-6)

**No verified study in this set autonomously cycles** measured LPBF properties → updated model/acquisition function → next ultrasonically atomized Al composition → next LPBF test. “Closed-loop” in some atomization papers instead means **material recycling**, not closed-loop alloy optimization. Thus the supported conclusion is **four qualifying Al reports, nine distinct printed powder compositions, one computational BO-guided programme with one atomized-and-printed final composition, and zero demonstrated autonomous experimental composition-feedback loops**. (taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 9-12, taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 22-25, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 4-6)
