# Survey of Aluminium Alloys Designed for Laser Powder Bed Fusion (LPBF/SLM): Patent and Commercial Landscape

## 1. Introduction

This survey covers the patent and published-composition landscape for aluminium alloys specifically designed or adapted for laser powder bed fusion (LPBF / SLM) and related rapid-solidification powder routes. The objective is to identify claimed composition windows, worked example compositions, strengthening mechanisms, recommended heat treatments, and reported mechanical properties, so that starting compositions for a new alloy discovery campaign can be selected with knowledge of what is already claimed. The following sections are organized by alloy family, followed by an integrated analysis addressing white space, best Sc-free performers, recommended seed experiments, and atomization/processing guidance.

### 2.1 Airbus / APWorks — Scalmalloy® (Al–Mg–Sc–Zr–Mn)

The foundational Scalmalloy® patent family originates from Palm / EADS Deutschland GmbH, with the earliest priority around 2007 (WO2008/125092, DE102007018123). The atomization-related patent EP2766136B1 (priority 2011) claims nitrogen-rich atomization (50–100 vol% N₂) of Al–Sc alloys with the composition Mg 0.5–10, Sc 0.2–2.0, Zr 0.2–2.0 wt%, optionally with Mn 0.1–1.0, and a preferred sub-range of Mg 2.5–5.0, Sc 0.3–1.0, Zr 0.2–0.5, Mn 0.1–0.8 wt% (EP2766136B1 pages 3-5). The published Scalmalloy® composition used in research is Al–4.4Mg–0.8Sc–0.3Zr–0.5Mn wt% (schimback2022alloydesignstrategy pages 1-2). The composition range reported across studies is Mg 3.02–4.77, Sc 0.20–0.71, Mn 0.20–0.53, Zr 0.08–0.42 wt% (cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 2-5).

**Mechanism:** Primary Al₃(Sc,Zr) particles nucleate fine equiaxed grains at melt-pool boundaries, creating a bimodal microstructure that suppresses hot cracking. Direct aging precipitates coherent L1₂ Al₃(Sc,Zr) nanoprecipitates for strengthening (cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 1-2, cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 16-18).

**Properties (journal/review data):** As-built YS 256–278 MPa, UTS 349–350 MPa, El 19–20%; after direct aging at 325°C × 4 h: YS 456–469 MPa, UTS 512–521 MPa, El 12–13%; after HIP at 325°C/1000 bar: YS 482–493 MPa, UTS 523–547 MPa (cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 1-2, mehta2021laserpowderbed pages 7-10).

Airbus also developed **Scancromal®** (Al–2.6Cr–0.7Sc–0.3Zr, US20220186346A1) and **Scantital®** (Al–1.0Ti–1.0Sc–0.4Zr, US20220168811A1 with Ti 0.1–15, Sc 0.1–3.0, Zr 0.1–3.0 wt%) (schimback2022alloydesignstrategy pages 1-2, US20220168811A1 pages 4-5).

### 2.2 HRL Laboratories — Functionalized 7xxx/6xxx and 7A77-Type

HRL's core innovation is surface functionalization of conventional alloy powders with grain-refining nanoparticles. Key granted patents include US11053571B2, US11286543B2, and US11919085B2 (priority 2015–2017 family). The worked example uses Al 7075 powder (Al bal.–5.40Zn–2.25Mg–1.54Cu–0.19Cr–0.17Fe–0.13Si wt%) with ~1 vol% ZrH₂ nanoparticles (500–1500 nm), producing crack-free equiaxed microstructures via Al₃Zr heterogeneous nucleation (US11919085B2 pages 31-34, US20240082913A1 pages 64-66, US11053571B2 pages 32-33). T6 heat treatment: 480°C/2h, water quench, 120°C/18h (US11286543B2 pages 32-33).

The **7A77-type enriched feedstock** patent US11578389B2 (priority 2017, granted) claims a feedstock with Al 79.8–88.3, Cu 1.1–2.1, Mg 3.0–4.6, Zn 7.1–9.0, Zr 0.5–2.8 wt%, designed so that after ~25% Zn and ~30% Mg vaporization loss during LPBF, the printed part reaches Al 84.5–92.1, Cu 1.1–2.1, Mg 1.8–2.9, Zn 4.5–6.1, Zr 0.5–2.8 wt% (US11578389B2 pages 7-10, US11578389B2 pages 30-33).

### 2.3 NanoAl / Unity / Northwestern University

**Addalloy® Sc-free Al–Mg–Zr:** US11603583B2 (Vo et al., priority ~2017, granted) claims Al–1–10Mg–0.3–3Zr wt% (preferably 0.45–3 Zr), intentionally Sc-free (Sc ≤0.05 as impurity), optionally with 0.3–1.5 wt% V/Ti/Hf/Nb/Ta. Examples: Al–3.6Mg–1.2Zr and Al–4Mg–1.7Zr wt%. Strengthening via primary and nanoscale L1₂ Al₃Zr (3–50 nm), stable to ~425°C (US11603583B2 pages 18-20, US11603583B2 pages 6-9, US11603583B2 pages 1-4).

**NanoAl 7xxx-type:** US10941473B2 (Snyder, granted) claims Zn 3–9, Mg 2–6, Cu 0.5–2, Zr 0–1 wt%, optional Y/Er/Yb/Sc 0–2 each. Examples: Al–6.3Zn–3.5Mg–1.1Cu–0.1Zr and Al–7.5Zn–4.6Mg–1.5Cu–0.1Zr. T7 hardness: 183–184 HV; crack-free DMLS builds with <0.5% porosity (US10941473B2 pages 21-22).

### A. Composition Overlap and White Space

**Well-claimed regions (avoid as primary novelty):**

- Al–Mn–Sc: Monash/Amaero (US11976343B2 pages 7-9)
- Al–Mg–Zr (Sc-free): NanoAl (US11603583B2 pages 18-20)

**Apparent white space for Sc-free, little/no Zr compositions with Al ≥ 85 wt%:**
- **Al–Mn–Mg–Cr** (no Sc, no Zr): No retrieved LPBF-specific patent claims this four-element core. Approximately Mn 1.5–4, Mg 1–4, Cr 0.3–1 wt% appears open.
- **Al–Mn–Cr–Si** (no Sc, no Zr): Relatively open except for Farkoosh's nearby Al–Mn–Si–Sn claims at low atomic %.
- **Al–Cu–Mn–Cr–Mg** (no Sc, no Zr): Not covered by retrieved AM claims.
- **Al–Mg–Si–Cr–Ti** (no Sc, no Zr): Open, though Ti grain refinement effectiveness needs validation.
- **Al–Mn–Cr–Fe–Si** (no Sc, no Zr, no RE): Open for a low-cost thermally stable family.

### B. Highest Sc-Free Yield Strength at ≥8% Elongation

The best verified Sc-free result is **Al–6Mg–0.7Mn–0.4Er–1.2Zr** from Beijing University of Technology (journal data): aged YS 440±3 MPa, UTS 520±2 MPa, El 15.5% (guo2022microstructureandmechanical pages 10-11, guo2022microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2). However, this alloy contains 1.2 wt% Zr and 0.4 wt% Er. Among alloys with no Sc and no/low Zr, no commercial datasheet with verified tensile data meeting the ≥8% elongation criterion was recovered. The Rusal RS-553-type achieves only 5.3% elongation at room temperature (CA3162961C pages 8-9).

### C. Recommended Seed Experiments

Given feedstock on hand (Al, Si, Mg, Mn, Cr, Zn, Sn, Ti, small Sc and Er; Fe, Cu, Ni easy; Zr uncertain):

1. **Al–6Mg–0.7Mn–0.4Er–1.2Zr** — Benchmark Sc-free recipe (requires Zr); age 375°C/10h (guo2022microstructureandmechanical pages 10-11).
2. **Al–4Mg–0.5Mn–0.3Sc–0.2Zr** — Low-Sc Scalmalloy control; age 300–325°C/4h (EP2766136B1 pages 3-5, cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 1-2).
3. **Al–3Mn–1.5Mg–0.5Cr–0.1Ti** — **High-priority white-space seed**, Sc/Zr-free; Mn/Cr solid-solution + dispersoid strengthening with Ti grain control.
4. **Al–5Mg–0.8Mn–0.5Si–0.3Cr–0.05Sn** — Farkoosh-inspired Sc/Zr-free precipitation concept; run Sn-free and 0.02/0.05 Sn variants (US12104237B2 pages 1-4).
5. **Al–3Mn–1.5Mg–0.6Cr–0.4Si–0.05Sn** — Lead Zr-free candidate combining white-space motifs; factorially vary Cr 0.3/0.6, Si 0.2/0.4, Sn 0/0.05.
6. **Al–7Zn–3Mg–1Cu–0.1Ti** — High-risk 7xxx control without Zr; consider TiB₂ inoculation.

### D. Atomization and Processing Guidance from Patents

- **Nitrogen atomization:** Airbus EP2766136B1 claims 50–100 vol% N₂ in atomizing gas for Al–Mg–Sc–Zr powders, producing an AlN-rich surface passivation layer that dramatically reduces moisture uptake versus conventional Al₂O₃. Conventional gas must be ≥99.99% purity with H₂O ≤25 ppm (EP2766136B1 pages 2-3, EP2766136B1 pages 3-5).
- **Mg/Zn vaporization loss:** HRL experimentally measured ~25% Zn and ~30% Mg loss during LPBF of 7075; enriched feedstock composition is claimed to compensate (US11578389B2 pages 7-10, US11578389B2 pages 30-33). These losses are machine- and parameter-specific.
- **Melt temperatures:** Croteau discloses melting at ~700–1000°C for Al–Zn–Mg–Zr alloys (US20210254196A1 pages 1-10). For Zr/Cr/Mn-bearing melts, use master alloys and verify dissolution; superheat should be established from the specific alloy liquidus.
- **LPBF parameter windows:** Rusal used EOS M290 at 280–320W, 400–1200 mm/s, ≤45 µm layers (CA3162961C pages 8-9). Monash used 370W, 1000 mm/s, 0.1 mm hatch, 30 µm layers (US11976343B2 pages 14-16).
- **Build-plate preheat:** No specific build-plate preheat temperature was recovered from the aluminium AM patents surveyed, though the Monash patent notes that direct aging can be performed before or after removal from the build platform (US11976343B2 pages 1-4).
- **Crucible materials and ultrasonic atomization:** No aluminium-specific crucible recommendation or directly applicable ultrasonic atomization protocol was recovered from the patent literature surveyed.

## 4. Limitations and Recommended Follow-Up

This survey covers the major patent families identified through systematic searching but has the following limitations:

1. **Incomplete extraction** of some patent families (Constellium EP4149702A2 HT1 compositions, Elementum 3D RAM, Fehrmann AlMgty, Japanese and Chinese filings) due to retrieval and time constraints.
2. **White space assessment is provisional** — a formal freedom-to-operate search with claim charting is required before filing or committing to a composition.
3. **Commercial datasheets** (EOS, SLM Solutions, Uniformity Labs, Höganäs forAM, Equispheres) were not retrieved as full documents; published compositions from these sources should be obtained directly from vendors.
4. The A20X (Al–Cu–Ag–Mg–Ti–B) patent family was not retrieved; the known composition (~Al–4.5Cu–0.3Ag–0.3Mg–0.2Ti–0.02B) should be confirmed against the original Aeromet/AMT filings.

The core finding is that Sc-free, Zr-free or low-Zr aluminium alloys based on **Al–Mn–Mg–Cr(–Si)** combinations represent the most promising apparent white space for a discovery campaign using the available feedstocks, while compositions in the Al–Mg–Er–Zr system (if Zr is available) provide the strongest published Sc-free mechanical performance benchmarks.
