| Diagnostic | What to Compute | Warning Signal | Suggested Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leave-one-out cross-validation (LOO-CV) | For each of the six observations, refit without it and record the held-out posterior mean, standardized residual, interval coverage, RMSE, and predictive log density. Compare SAAS with a dimension-scaled Matérn GP and a constant-mean baseline. | Predictions are nearly constant; RMSE is no better than the baseline; extreme standardized residuals; poor interval coverage; or conclusions change when one point is omitted. Six-point LOO is high-variance, so treat it as a stress test rather than proof of accuracy. | Do not trust acquisition rankings. Add coverage points, enlarge the noise prior, check outcome standardization, and prefer the model with better calibrated LOO predictions. SAAS-MAP itself used a LOO predictive criterion to select shrinkage settings (pqac-00000055, pqac-00000056). |
| Posterior length-scale analysis | Plot posterior medians and 50%/90% intervals for each inverse squared length scale \(\rho_i\), the global shrinkage \(\tau\), and rank probabilities such as \(P(\rho_i>c\mid D)\). Refit under several seeds/chains and modestly different shrinkage hyperparameters. | Every \(\rho_i\) remains near its prior-shrunk value, or nearly all 15 dimensions escape shrinkage; rankings are unstable across chains or minor prior changes. SAAS is designed to keep most \(\rho_i\lesssim\tau\), so “off” dimensions at tiny \(n\) may reflect absent evidence rather than irrelevance (pqac-00000055, pqac-00000070). | If all are off, add deliberately contrasting experiments before optimizing. If all are on, inspect scaling, collinearity, noise, and prior sensitivity; compare against a dimension-scaled Matérn GP rather than forcing a sparse interpretation. |
| Effective dimensionality | Predefine an activation rule—for example, count dimensions whose posterior median \(\rho_i\) exceeds a practical-effect threshold—and report the full posterior distribution of that count, not only one estimate. | Effective dimension is 0, exceeds about 10 of 15, has a very wide interval, or changes sharply under leave-one-out refits. The 0 or >10 thresholds are campaign triage rules, not published universal cutoffs. | With 0 active dimensions, spend the next batch on contrasts/coverage. With >10 or unstable dimensionality, use a less sparsity-dependent GP and revisit the representation. SAAS effective dimension can legitimately grow as data accumulate, as observed from roughly 2 to 10 on MOPTA (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000055). |
| Proposed-point distance from data | In the actual feasible composition representation, calculate each candidate’s nearest-neighbor distance to observed and pending points, pairwise distances within the batch, distance to the incumbent, and proximity to composition/cardinality boundaries. Compare these with distances from a large feasible Sobol or maximin candidate set. | All three candidates lie in the lowest tail of feasible nearest-neighbor distances, cluster around one incumbent, duplicate pending points, or sit on boundaries without a credible posterior reason. Conversely, all being maximally distant can indicate indiscriminate exploration. | Reject or penalize collapsed batches; condition on pending points; require one coverage-oriented candidate or a minimum separation; and inspect alternatives from posterior Thompson samples. Fully Bayesian EI can reduce boundary selection relative to point-estimated hyperparameters, but an uninformative high-complexity GP may instead query very near the incumbent (pqac-00000069, pqac-00000067). |
| Acquisition-function landscape | Evaluate posterior-averaged EI or qLogNEI on a large feasible Sobol/maximin set and along feasible paths connecting seeds, the incumbent, and boundaries. Plot acquisition values against distance from data and repeat across hyperparameter posterior draws. | Acquisition is numerically flat; its maximizer changes arbitrarily across posterior draws; almost all mass lies near one seed; or only extreme boundary points score well. | Do not rely on one numerical maximizer. Check scaling and Monte Carlo precision, increase multistart optimization, use pending-point fantasies, and allocate one batch member by Thompson sampling or coverage. SAASBO averages EI over NUTS hyperparameter samples and initializes acquisition optimization from Sobol candidates (pqac-00000056, pqac-00000073). |
| NUTS convergence | Run multiple chains and inspect rank-normalized \(\hat R\), bulk/tail effective sample size, divergences, tree-depth saturation, energy/BFMI diagnostics, trace plots, and agreement of posterior predictive/acquisition rankings across chains. | Any material divergences; \(\hat R\) appreciably above 1.01; very low effective sample size; chains occupying different modes without mixing; or candidate rankings changing by chain. | Reparameterize and rescale inputs/outputs, raise target acceptance, increase warm-up and samples, simplify the noise model, and do not present unstable active-dimension probabilities as evidence. NUTS is valuable precisely because posterior averaging can cover different hyperparameter modes, but its latent space is challenging (pqac-00000056, pqac-00000069). |
| Input coverage | Compute pairwise distances and per-element ranges among the six seeds in the feasible composition geometry; tabulate element-presence counts, cardinalities (2–5), total-addition levels, and correlations/condition number. Repeat after projecting onto each plausible active-coordinate subset. | Seeds occupy one compositional neighborhood; candidate elements are never or only jointly varied; columns are constant or collinear; cardinalities/subsystems are poorly represented; or benchmarks/literature alloys dominate one family. | Use the next batch for constrained maximin or k-medoids coverage, emphasizing unrepresented element families and deconfounding contrasts rather than unconstrained ambient-space corners. Geometry alone may still miss chemically informative diversity, so include subsystem/chemistry descriptors (pqac-00000043, pqac-00000046, pqac-00000054). |
