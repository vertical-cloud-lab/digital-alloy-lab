We are about to start a small-budget Bayesian optimization campaign for aluminium alloy design: about 25 expensive experiments in total, a composition space of about 15 candidate alloying elements (each alloy uses only 2-5 of them, total additions at most 15 wt%), evaluated in batches of 3, with results arriving weeks after a batch is chosen. The proposal is to run only SIX hand-picked initial experiments (2 commercial benchmark alloys, 3 literature-derived alloys, 1 exploratory) and then go straight to SAASBO (sparse axis-aligned subspace Bayesian optimization; Eriksson and Jankowiak 2021, fully Bayesian GP with a half-Cauchy / horseshoe-like prior on inverse length scales, fitted by NUTS), instead of adding Sobol or other space-filling points first. The argument is that SAASBO "will do a decent job of exploration on its own".

Please review the evidence and answer:
1. What initial design sizes do the SAASBO paper and follow-up benchmarks use (e.g. 10-20 Sobol points in 30-388 dimensions), and is there any evidence on SAASBO started from very few (5-10) NON-space-filling, expert-chosen points? How does a SAAS prior behave with n much smaller than d: do most length scales stay at the prior (dimension "off"), and what does the acquisition function then do (does it explore along few dimensions, pick boundary points, or collapse near the incumbent)?
2. Known failure modes of SAASBO and other sparse or high-dimensional BO methods at tiny n: over-shrinkage, missing relevant dimensions, boundary-seeking behaviour, sensitivity to clustered or collinear initial points, sensitivity to outcome scaling and noise. Compare with: standard GP with dimension-scaled lognormal length-scale priors (Hvarfner et al. 2024, "Vanilla Bayesian optimization performs great in high dimensions"), Xu et al. 2024 on standard GPs in high dimensions, MPD / local methods, trust-region methods (TuRBO), random embeddings (ALEBO, BAxUS), and additive / compositional kernels.
3. Hand-picked, clustered or biased initial points: published results on expert / literature / LLM warm starts versus Sobol initial designs in BO (materials, chemistry, hyperparameter tuning), including cases where warm starts hurt. Does mixing a few space-filling points into an expert seed set help model calibration in high dimensions?
4. Mixture / composition constraints and sparsity: best practice for BO over alloy compositions where most components are zero (cardinality constraints, hierarchical or conditional search spaces, element on/off indicators, simplex or isometric log-ratio transforms). How do SAAS priors interact with such sparse inputs? Any alloy or materials campaigns that used SAASBO or fully Bayesian GPs (e.g. in Ax / BoTorch, Honegumi) with fewer than 30 experiments?
5. Batch selection with delayed feedback (batch of 3, results 2-6 weeks later, earlier batches still pending): qLogNEI / qNEHVI with pending points under a fully Bayesian model, Thompson sampling, and risks of batch collapse at small n.
6. Using literature data or physics-model outputs as extra training rows or as prior means when real n is 6: multi-task / multi-fidelity SAAS models, transfer learning for BO, and the risk that biased literature rows dominate.
7. Concretely: with 25 total runs and batches of 3, would you start SAASBO after 6 hand-picked seeds, or spend 3 of the next runs on space-filling points first (9 seeds), or use another model? Give a recommendation with the evidence for and against, and simple diagnostics we can compute after the six seeds (leave-one-out calibration, posterior on which dimensions are active, distance of proposed points from the data) to decide whether the optimizer is ready.
