| Pitfall ID | Pitfall Description | Severity | Recommended Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Nine seeds consume **36% of the 25-run decision budget**, leaving only 12 adaptive BO runs before finalist validation. This exceeds Bossek et al.’s best tested Halton allocation of about 30%, although 30% is an empirical benchmark—not a universal optimum. Large initial fractions often underperform because they sacrifice sequential learning. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001) | High | Use about **7 common seed runs** (28%): three baselines, two knowledge-based candidates, and two constrained space-filling augmentations. Move the saved runs into adaptive BO rounds. |
| P2 | Loeppky et al.’s approximately **10d rule** would require roughly 140–150 observations for a 14–15-dimensional continuous composition representation—impossible here. Moreover, that rule concerns accurate GP emulation of deterministic computer codes, not necessarily optimization under a 25-run physical budget. (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009) | High | Reduce effective dimension before atomization: encode element-set choice categorically, use at most four active additions, exploit CALPHAD descriptors and metallurgy-informed screening, and fit sparse or hierarchical kernels rather than pretending nine runs identify 15 independent length scales. |
| P3 | Expert-, literature-, CALPHAD-, and LLM-selected seeds are likely to cluster around familiar Al–Si–Mg and Al–Mg–Sc–Zr families. Warm starts can improve early performance, but LLAMBO generated less-diverse, correlated points; knowledge-based proposals can reinforce confirmation bias and make the GP overconfident in known regions. (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019) | High | Limit knowledge-based physical seeds to about two, require distinct strengthening hypotheses and element sets, label provenance, and reserve at least two seeds for deliberately dissimilar coverage points. Compare against a model using only unbiased common seeds. |
| P4 | A scrambled Sobol design in the nominal 15-element simplex is geometrically mismatched to the feasible set. Almost all generic points violate the “at most four additions” rule or occupy irrelevant high-order mixtures; projection or repair can create clustering and destroy Sobol balance. Mixture spaces require simplex-aware designs. (pqac-00000038, pqac-00000042) | High | Generate a large candidate pool **directly on the union of feasible sparse simplexes**: first sample admissible element subsets, then sample bounded compositions within each selected face. Perform augmentation against all fixed baseline and knowledge-based points. |
| P5 | “At most k non-zero additions” is a combinatorial **NChooseK constraint**, but the plan specifies no metric, subset-allocation rule, or kernel for the resulting disconnected union of simplex faces. Ordinary Euclidean maximin can regard alloys with different element identities as misleadingly close or far. | High | Pre-register a mixed distance combining element-set dissimilarity, such as weighted Jaccard or Hamming distance, with an Aitchison or range-scaled distance within a simplex face. Ensure coverage across several metallurgically admissible element subsets before optimizing continuous concentrations. |
| P6 | Composition-level space filling alone does not initialize every BOFN node well. The print GP needs coverage in powder properties, CALPHAD descriptors, and LPBF parameters; property GPs need coverage in achieved microstructure, density, cracking, and heat-treatment states. BOFN studies commonly begin with complete network observations, while node-specific network designs target each node’s own feasible input domain. (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000028) | High | Obtain several complete end-to-end seed trajectories, then add cheap node-specific data: exhaustive CALPHAD evaluations, commercial-powder LPBF plates, and hardness measurements. Design LPBF conditions in the **print node’s input space**, conditional on observed powder and descriptor outputs, rather than only in nominal composition space. |
| P7 | The proposed “fantasy modelling” conflates two different operations. Synthetic or literature pseudo-observations alter the fitted model and can bias its posterior; BoTorch `fantasize` instead draws possible outcomes at genuinely pending evaluation locations and conditions temporary posterior models for asynchronous or batch acquisition calculations. | Medium | Rename the pre-campaign exercise a **synthetic digital-twin dry run**. Keep pseudo-data in a separate source or fidelity table and exclude it from the physical-data posterior by default. Use BoTorch fantasies only for pending real evaluations, integrating over multiple posterior samples rather than retaining fantasies as observations. |
| P8 | Pooling literature, database, simulation, and in-house measurements as exchangeable observations ignores systematic source bias and heterogeneous noise, inviting negative transfer and false confidence. Multi-fidelity work shows that source-specific noise and discrepancy matter, especially when simulations and experiments differ. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000050, pqac-00000051, pqac-00000052) | High | Add source or fidelity indicators, source-specific bias or discrepancy functions, and inflated heteroscedastic noise based on provenance. Prefer multi-task or multi-fidelity GPs, or RGPE-style source models whose influence can fall as target data accumulate. Never silently replace synthetic values with real ones in the same row. |
| P9 | A fair replay comparison requires every policy to start from the same information state. The current three-way seed allocation embeds agent knowledge only in the proposed policy’s initial dataset, so apparent “AI acceleration” could merely measure a better warm start; sparse real trajectories also make exact counterfactual replay impossible away from observed points. | High | Pre-register one **shared common seed set**, objectives, stopping rules, costs, failure handling, and random seeds for all policies. Report warm-start and sequential-policy effects separately. Use retrospective replay only when a policy selects an observed candidate; otherwise use a clearly labelled cross-validated simulator with uncertainty and avoid causal acceleration claims. |
| P10 | The five 6063 repeat atomizations, benchmark repeats, dosing checks, and baseline alloys are not merely overhead. They inform atomizer noise, composition fidelity, calibration, process transfer, and incumbent performance. Excluding them from GP training wastes scarce information; counting duplicates as independent coverage, however, exaggerates design size. | High | Include compatible runs in node-level likelihoods with explicit alloy, source, and run identifiers. Count a unique baseline composition once for **coverage**, but count all independent runs for **noise estimation**. Use the 6063 repeats primarily to estimate run-level variance and drift, not as five distinct composition seeds. |
