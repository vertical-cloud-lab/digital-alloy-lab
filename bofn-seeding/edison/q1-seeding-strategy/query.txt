We are choosing the seed (initial) experiments for a closed-loop Bayesian optimization (BO) campaign that looks for new aluminium alloys for laser powder bed fusion (LPBF). Please give a critical, literature-grounded review of our seeding plan. Act as a sceptical rubber duck: say where the plan is wrong, not only where it is right.

SETTING
- Optimizer: BO over a function network (BOFN; Astudillo and Frazier, 2021) with cost-aware partial evaluations (p-KGFN; Buathong, Frazier and co-authors, 2024). Each node of a directed acyclic graph has its own Gaussian process (GP):
  composition (Al balance, at least 85 at.% Al; additions chosen from Mn, Cr, Zr, Mg, Si, Cu, Ti, Fe, Ni, Ce, Sc, Li, Er, Zn, Sn; at most about 4 additions per alloy)
  -> CALPHAD/Scheil descriptors (freezing range, hot-cracking index, L1_2 and intermetallic fractions; seconds per evaluation)
  -> ultrasonic atomization of a 250 g charge (outputs: particle size distribution, yield in the 15-63 um LPBF cut, oxygen, composition fidelity by SEM-EDS)
  -> LPBF on a research machine (single tracks, then density cubes; inputs laser power, scan speed, hatch, layer thickness; outputs melt-pool depth and width, relative density, crack density)
  -> Vickers hardness (fast strength screen), sub-size tensile (yield strength, UTS, elongation) and corrosion.
  A mineral-burden objective (import-reliance or supply-risk weighted wt%) is analytic.
- Goal: an alloy stronger than LPBF AlSi10Mg and/or as strong as Scalmalloy (Al-Mg-Sc-Zr) with much less critical-mineral (Sc) content.
- Budget: 36 atomizer runs by late February 2027, 3-4 per week. 5 are fixed-setting repeat runs on a 6063 base (to measure run-to-run noise) and 6 are held back for failed runs, which leaves about 25 runs where we choose what to make. Those 25 currently are: 3 benchmark runs (AlSi10Mg-type, Scalmalloy-type, and a repeat of the first), 2 dosing checks at known Si, 4 CALPHAD-chosen seeds, 4 optimizer rounds of 3 runs, and 2 finalists made twice. Single tracks and small cubes are cheap next to atomization (many compositions and parameter sets fit on one build plate); tensile bars are expensive.

PROPOSED SEEDING PLAN (please critique)
About 9 of the 25 runs as seeds, 3 from each of:
 (1) agent-chosen: compositions proposed from the literature, databases, LLM agents and CALPHAD screening;
 (2) known baselines: AlSi10Mg, Scalmalloy and similar commercial or literature alloys;
 (3) space-filling exploration: scrambled Sobol in the design space, possibly made more model-based by discarding Sobol points too close to existing (baseline, agent or literature) experiments, by maximin augmentation, or by maximum-variance / IMSE designs from a GP fitted to the existing data.
In parallel, "fantasy modelling": fill each node with synthetic or literature-derived pseudo-observations so that the whole loop can run before real data exist, then replace them with real data as experiments come in.

QUESTIONS (answer each with evidence and DOIs)
1. Initial design size. What does the literature say about the share of a small BO budget (30-50 evaluations or fewer) to spend on the initial design? For example: Bossek et al. (2020) on initial design strategies for sequential model-based optimization; 2d versus 10d rules; Loeppky, Sacks and Welch (2009) for GP emulators; Ax and BoTorch defaults; self-driving-lab case studies. With about 25 decisions, 4 batch rounds of 3, and a design space that is high-dimensional unless reduced, is 9 seeds too many, too few, or about right? How should repeat and baseline runs that are already planned count toward the initial design?
2. Expert, literature and LLM-chosen seeds versus space-filling. What is the evidence from materials and chemistry BO (e.g., LLAMBO warm-starting, expert-initialised campaigns, transfer learning) on whether knowledge-based seeds help or hurt, on the risk of biasing the surrogate toward known regions, and on how to mix them with space-filling points? What is known about including the incumbent or benchmark alloys as seeds?
3. Space-filling that accounts for existing points. Compare methods for augmenting a design around fixed existing points (maximin augmentation, sequential or nested space-filling, Sobol with an exclusion radius, coverage designs, maximum-entropy / variance / IMSE designs) in constrained mixture (simplex) spaces with sparsity (at most k non-zero additions, NChooseK constraints). Which is most practical and defensible for adding only 3-6 points?
4. Seeding a function network. Each node is its own GP whose inputs are upstream outputs plus node-local parameters. Is there literature on initial designs for networks of GPs, multi-stage or cascade processes, or partial evaluations, e.g., seeding downstream nodes from cheaper sources (commercial powders for the print-parameter node, literature tensile data for the property node, CALPHAD for descriptors)? Should space-filling be done in each node's own input space (that is, in the upstream output or descriptor space) rather than in composition space?
5. Literature and synthetic ("fantasy") data as priors. How should literature or database data and simulation outputs enter the GP without biasing it: multi-task or multi-fidelity GPs with a source indicator, prior-mean functions, inflated or heteroscedastic noise for literature points, transfer learning such as RGPE, power-prior down-weighting? How can pseudo-observations be swapped cleanly for real data? Please also clarify how this differs from BoTorch's "fantasize" (conditioning on sampled outcomes at pending points).
6. Making the seeds count for the paper. We will pre-register an "AI acceleration factor" benchmark against random, expert design-of-experiments and standard BO baselines, and replay the policies on our real data. How should the seed design be chosen so that this comparison is fair and possible with so few experiments?

End with (a) a recommended allocation of the 25 runs (baselines, agent-chosen, space-filling, optimizer rounds, finalists), (b) the recommended space-filling method, and (c) the main pitfalls, as a table, plus a reference list with DOIs.
