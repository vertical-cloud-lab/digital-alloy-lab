# Critical Review of the Seeding Plan for a Closed-Loop BOFN Campaign on LPBF Aluminium Alloys

## 1. Initial Design Size: Is 9 of 25 Too Many?

**Verdict: Slightly too many; reduce to about 7.**

Bossek et al. (2020) systematically benchmarked 40 initial-design strategies for EGO (a canonical SMBO method) on the BBOB suite. Their virtual-best strategy allocated roughly 30% of the total budget to a Halton initial design; small initial designs were generally preferable, and allocating ≥50% of the budget to the initial design left little room for the surrogate to improve on the best initial point (bossek2020initialdesignstrategies pages 6-7, bossek2020initialdesignstrategies pages 1-2). With 25 decision runs, 9 seeds represent 36%—above that 30% sweet spot—leaving only 12 runs for adaptive BO plus finalists. This is problematic because Bossek et al. also observed that their finding is function- and dimension-dependent: all 40 tested combinations were best on at least one of 720 problems (bossek2020initialdesignstrategies pages 8-8).

The separate question is whether the GP can even be meaningfully fitted from 9 points. Loeppky, Sacks, and Welch (2009) recommend n ≈ 10d observations for a GP emulator, where d is the input dimension (loeppky2009choosingthesample pages 1-2, loeppky2009choosingthesample pages 10-11, loeppky2009choosingthesample pages 8-10). With 15 candidate elements, the nominal d is at least 14 continuous proportions. Even taking the "at most 4 additions" sparsity into account, the effective d is at least 4–8 continuous proportions plus a combinatorial element-selection variable, so 10d ≈ 40–80, far exceeding your entire budget. This means the 10d rule is inapplicable in its original emulation sense; you must rely on dimensionality reduction (CALPHAD descriptors, sparse kernels, element-subset categoricals) rather than hoping that 9 or even 25 runs adequately cover a 15-element simplex (loeppky2009choosingthesample pages 4-5). The CArBO framework defaults to τ/8 ≈ 12.5% of the cost budget for initialization (lee2020costawarebayesianoptimization pages 2-3), but that is specific to cost-aware BO; it at least reinforces the direction of using fewer initial points.

**How should baseline and repeat runs count?** The 5 fixed 6063 repeat runs and 2 dosing checks are not wasted—they should be included as node-level observations in the BOFN GPs (especially for atomizer noise estimation and LPBF parameter coverage). But they should not be counted as distinct *composition* seeds for the design-coverage criterion, since they represent a single alloy or known calibration. The 3 benchmark alloy runs (AlSi10Mg, Scalmalloy-type, repeat), however, are genuinely informative compositions and should be counted as part of the initial design.

## 2. Expert, Literature, and LLM-Chosen Seeds versus Space-Filling

**Verdict: Knowledge-based seeds help early but carry bias risk; limit to 2 and pair with 2 space-filling points.**

LLAMBO (Liu et al., 2024) demonstrated that zero-shot LLM-generated initial hyperparameters outperformed random, Sobol, and Latin Hypercube initialization on 74 benchmarks, with gains concentrated in early iterations (liu2024largelanguagemodels pages 2-4, liu2024largelanguagemodels pages 4-6). However, the LLM-proposed points showed stronger inter-parameter correlations and lower generalized variance than space-filling alternatives—meaning less diversity and potential concentration around the LLM's training-data modes (liu2024largelanguagemodels pages 4-6). BORA (Cissé et al., 2025) showed that LLM-generated hypotheses improved optimization in real chemistry and materials tasks, but explicitly warned that such context-aware suggestions can introduce confirmation bias and cause models to overlook promising but underrepresented regions (cisse2025languagebasedbayesianoptimization pages 1-2). Senthilkumar and Menzies (2026) found that LLM warm-starts helped in low- and medium-dimensional tasks but degraded performance in high-dimensional problems, where GP-based methods with UCB acquisition performed best (senthilkumar2026canlargelanguage pages 16-18).

For your 15-element alloy space, the effective dimensionality is high enough that the high-dimensional caution applies. Expert-chosen compositions will likely cluster around Al-Si-Mg and Al-Mg-Sc-Zr families. CALPHAD screening will further concentrate on alloys with low hot-cracking indices and large L₁₂ fractions, biasing the GP posterior toward compositions that look like known printable alloys. The plan's three "agent-chosen" seeds are therefore at risk of simply reinforcing what is already known.

**Recommendation:** Limit agent/CALPHAD-chosen *physical* seeds to 2, each representing a distinct strengthening mechanism and element set (e.g., one Mn-Cr-Zr candidate, one Ce-Ti-Cu candidate). The incumbent alloys (AlSi10Mg, Scalmalloy) already serve as the "known region" anchors.

## 3. Space-Filling Design in a Constrained Sparse Mixture Space

**Verdict: Scrambled Sobol in the full 15-d simplex is wrong; use constrained maximin augmentation on feasible sparse-mixture candidate pools.**

The plan's proposal to use scrambled Sobol with an exclusion radius around existing points is conceptually reasonable but geometrically inappropriate for this problem. Standard Sobol sequences are defined on the unit hypercube; projecting onto the simplex or repairing infeasible points creates clustering and destroys the low-discrepancy balance (gomes2018spacefillingdesignsfor pages 3-5, gomes2018spacefillingdesignsfor pages 1-3). The "at most 4 non-zero additions" constraint further restricts the feasible set to a union of low-dimensional simplex faces, not a single connected convex body.

Gomes et al. (2018) showed that mixture-specific space-filling designs should normalize component ranges relative to their bounds and evaluate distance-based criteria (Mindist, MeanMin, coverage RMS) directly on the feasible mixture region (gomes2018spacefillingdesignsfor pages 3-5, gomes2018spacefillingdesignsfor pages 1-3). The Kennard–Stone sequential selection algorithm naturally supports augmentation: treat existing points as fixed and greedily add the candidate farthest from the current design (gomes2018spacefillingdesignsfor pages 3-5). Perrin and Cannamela (2017) demonstrated a repulsion-based method for enriching designs in bounded convex domains including the simplex, with good minimax and mindist properties, explicitly supporting design augmentation around fixed points (perrin2017arepulsionbasedmethod pages 29-31, perrin2017arepulsionbasedmethod pages 1-3, perrin2017arepulsionbasedmethod pages 23-24). Huang et al. (2021) proposed Constrained Minimum Energy Designs (CoMinED) using soft probabilistic constraint relaxation; their greedy candidate-selection framework is equivalent to maximin spacing under a uniform target and works for non-rectangular constrained regions (huang2021constrainedminimumenergy pages 7-10, huang2021constrainedminimumenergy pages 4-7).

**Recommended method for adding 2–3 space-filling points:** (i) Enumerate admissible element subsets (e.g., all C(15,3) and C(15,4) combinations satisfying metallurgical pre-screens). (ii) Within each selected subset, generate bounded compositions on the corresponding simplex face using a Dirichlet or uniform mixture sampler. (iii) Pool all feasible candidates with the fixed baseline and agent-chosen points. (iv) Greedily select 2–3 candidates that maximize the minimum distance (Kennard–Stone augmentation) using a mixed distance that combines element-set dissimilarity (Jaccard) with composition-space distance (Aitchison or range-normalized Euclidean). This handles the NChooseK constraint explicitly, which no standard space-filling method does out of the box. IMSE or maximum-variance designs from a fitted GP are more principled but require a GP that is already reasonably fitted—a chicken-and-egg problem with ≤5 prior points.

## 4. Seeding a Function Network (BOFN / p-KGFN)

**Verdict: The plan is correct to think about node-level seeding, but current BOFN literature only prescribes full-network initialization. The plan's intuition to seed downstream nodes from cheaper sources is ahead of published practice—pursue it, but carefully.**

Astudillo and Frazier (2021) modeled BOFN by placing independent GP priors on each node function and observing intermediate outputs during full-network evaluations (astudillo2021bayesianoptimizationof pages 2-4). Buathong et al. (2023, 2025) initialized p-KGFN with 3–15 full-network evaluations and then allowed cost-aware partial evaluations of individual nodes; crucially, their later formulation removes the restriction that upstream nodes must be evaluated before downstream ones, implicitly supporting the injection of external downstream data (buathong2023bayesianoptimizationof pages 3-4, buathong2506fastbayesianoptimization pages 2-4, buathong2023bayesianoptimizationof pages 4-5, buathong2023bayesianoptimizationof pages 22-23). Kudva et al. (2026) described MOBONS initialization as node-specific: for each node k, generate n initial points in that node's input domain Z_k using space-filling sampling (kudva2026multi‐objectivebayesianoptimization pages 7-9). Torresi and Friederich (2026) introduced MSBO for multi-stage self-driving labs, maintaining an inventory of samples at each stage and deciding whether to advance existing samples or start new ones (torresi2026multistagebayesianoptimisation pages 1-2, torresi2026multistagebayesianoptimisation pages 3-4, torresi2026multistagebayesianoptimisation pages 5-6).

**What this means for your campaign:** Space-filling should be done *both* in composition space (for the CALPHAD/Scheil node) *and* in each downstream node's input space. The print-parameter node's inputs are powder properties, CALPHAD descriptors, laser power, scan speed, hatch, and layer thickness—a space that can be populated cheaply by printing many parameter sets from a single atomized composition on one build plate. Literature LPBF data for AlSi10Mg already provides extensive process-parameter-to-density maps (liu2020machinelearningassistedlaser pages 1-5); these can seed the print GP node directly. Similarly, CALPHAD descriptors can be computed for thousands of compositions in seconds, providing abundant cheap data for the CALPHAD node GP. The plan's "fantasy modelling" should be reframed as node-specific pre-training from heterogeneous cheaper sources.

## 5. Literature and Synthetic ("Fantasy") Data as Priors

**Verdict: The plan conflates two distinct mechanisms. Literature data should enter through multi-fidelity or multi-task GPs with source-specific noise—not as bare pseudo-observations. "Fantasy modelling" should be renamed and separated from BoTorch's `fantasize`.**

**How literature data should enter the GP.** The transfer-learning BO survey by Bai et al. (2023) catalogued four integration points: initialization, search-space design, surrogate model, and acquisition function (bai2023transferlearningfor pages 3-5). Within the surrogate, the principal options are:

- **RGPE (Ranking-Weighted GP Ensemble):** Fit separate GPs on each source dataset; weight them by ranking-loss agreement with target observations (bai2023transferlearningfor pages 17-19, mahboubi2025enhancedbayesianoptimization pages 34-38). This is robust to negative transfer because poorly ranked sources receive near-zero weight, but it does not model joint uncertainty correctly and can produce overconfident predictions (mahboubi2025enhancedbayesianoptimization pages 34-38).
- **Env-GP / adaptive noise:** Source observations receive an inflated noise variance that increases as source–target dissimilarity grows, effectively down-weighting unreliable literature data (bai2023transferlearningfor pages 13-15).
- **Multi-fidelity GP with source-specific noise (MFBOUQ):** Foumani et al. (2023) showed that assuming homogeneous noise across sources degrades MFBO performance; learning a separate noise model per source substantially improves optimization, especially when sources are biased (foumani2023ontheeffects pages 3-5).
- **Prior-mean function:** Use CALPHAD predictions or a physics-informed model as the GP prior mean, letting the GP residual capture discrepancy. This is conceptually clean but requires the prior mean to be reasonably accurate.

For your setting, the most practical approach is a multi-task or multi-fidelity GP with a source/fidelity indicator column, source-specific noise hyperparameters, and a discrepancy kernel. Literature LPBF property data, database alloy compositions, and CALPHAD predictions each get their own noise level. As real experimental data accumulate, the target-task GP weight grows and source influence diminishes (wakabayashi2608bayesianoptimizationfor pages 17-19, wakabayashi2608bayesianoptimizationfor pages 19-21).

**Fantasy modelling versus BoTorch `fantasize`.** BoTorch's `fantasize` is a specific mechanism for batch or asynchronous BO: it conditions the GP posterior on hypothetical outcomes sampled from the current posterior predictive distribution at pending evaluation points, enabling look-ahead acquisition functions like Knowledge Gradient. The fantasized outcomes are temporary, drawn fresh for each acquisition optimization, and are replaced by real data when evaluations complete. Your proposed "fantasy modelling"—filling every BOFN node with synthetic or literature values so the loop can run before real data exist—is a different operation: it is a *synthetic digital-twin dry run* that tests the pipeline's software and logic. If those synthetic values are retained in the GP training set alongside real data, they will bias the posterior. The correct procedure is to tag them as a separate source or fidelity and exclude them from the physical-data GP once real observations arrive.

## 6. Making the Seeds Count for the Paper

**Verdict: The current plan cannot support a fair "AI acceleration factor" benchmark because the agent-chosen seeds embed policy-specific knowledge into the initial dataset.**

For a fair comparison, every policy (random, expert DoE, standard BO, BOFN) must start from the same information state. If the BOFN policy alone receives agent-chosen compositions while the random baseline starts from the same AlSi10Mg and Scalmalloy baselines but random additional seeds, apparent advantages could reflect warm-start quality rather than adaptive-policy quality. The Bayesian Optimization Hackathon for Chemistry and Materials (Baird et al., 2025) standardized benchmarking by defining common tasks, evaluation budgets, and initial conditions.

**Recommendation:** Pre-register a *shared common seed set* (the 3 baselines + 2 space-filling points = 5 common seeds) that every policy uses identically. Any additional agent- or policy-specific seeds should be charged against that policy's adaptive budget. Report the "acceleration factor" as the ratio of evaluations needed by each policy to reach a target objective value, starting from the shared seeds. For offline replay, use only the actually-observed (composition, property) pairs; do not interpolate from the GP to simulate unobserved compositions, as this conflates surrogate accuracy with policy quality.

## (b) Recommended Space-Filling Method

**Constrained maximin augmentation via Kennard–Stone greedy selection on a pre-generated pool of feasible sparse-mixture candidates.** The procedure is: (1) enumerate metallurgically admissible element subsets of size 2–4 from the 15 candidate elements, applying CALPHAD pre-screens to discard thermodynamically implausible families; (2) within each admissible subset, sample bounded compositions uniformly on the corresponding simplex face; (3) pool all feasible candidates; (4) treat the fixed baseline and agent-chosen compositions as already-selected points; (5) greedily add 2 candidates that maximize the minimum mixed distance (Jaccard on element sets + Aitchison on compositions) to the current design (huang2021constrainedminimumenergy pages 7-10, gomes2018spacefillingdesignsfor pages 3-5, perrin2017arepulsionbasedmethod pages 29-31). This is more defensible than Sobol in the full 15-d hypercube because it respects the mixture constraint, the sparsity (NChooseK) constraint, and the existing fixed points simultaneously. It is simpler and more practical for 2–3 points than IMSE or maximum-variance designs, which require a pre-fitted GP.
