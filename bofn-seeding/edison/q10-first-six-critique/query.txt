Please act as a sceptical reviewer with expertise in aluminium physical metallurgy, powder atomization, laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) and Bayesian optimization. The uploaded files are a plan for the first six alloy compositions (plus three optional ones) of a 25-run aluminium alloy discovery campaign, with atomizer settings, a sieve cut, LPBF settings for an Aconity MIDI, expected microstructures, and a recommendation to hand over to SAASBO after six seeds. The plan will be acted on by students within two weeks, so errors matter more than style.

Files:
- README.md: the plan (the main thing to review).
- seeds.csv: one row per experiment with every recommended setting.
- named.csv: CALPHAD screen (liquidus from two open databases, Scheil freezing path, cracking indices) for about 60 alloys on a pure-Al base and on a 6063 base.
- liquidus_vs_content.csv: liquidus against content for Zr, Ti, Cr, Mn, Fe from the COST 507 database.
- atomization.csv, lpbf_window.csv, recipes.csv: outputs of process_models.py.
- summary.csv: results of the toy SAASBO hand-off simulation.
- process_models.py and saasbo_handoff_sim.py: the code behind those outputs.

Please check, and be specific (quote the line or number you object to):

1. METALLURGY. For each of the nine experiments (D1, D2, S1, S2, S3, X1, O1, O2, O3): is the composition sensible and correctly attributed to its source? Are the published strengths quoted consistent with what you can find in the literature? Is anything about the expected microstructure wrong? Is the choice of the six (two benchmarks, three literature picks with different strengthening routes, one exploratory Ti-refined alloy) defensible, and which one would you swap out, for what, and why?
2. THE 6063 BASE. The plan builds charges on 6063 (about 0.55 Mg, 0.40 Si, 0.20 Fe). Is that acceptable for each seed? In particular S1 (Al-4.8Mn-0.9Cr-1.1Zr-1.2Mg, which then carries 0.33 Si) and S2 (Al-1.9Fe-1.0Mg-1.0Zr with 0.35 Si): what does the extra Si do (Al-Fe-Si and Al-Mn-Si phases, (Al,Si)3Zr, loss of Mn supersaturation), and is it likely to change printability or strength?
3. MELT TEMPERATURES. The melt setpoints are the highest liquidus from two open databases plus 100 C (820 to about 1060 C). The MatCalc open database shows no Al3Zr liquidus at all, so Zr liquidus values come from COST 507 (about 835 C at 0.6 wt% Zr, about 900 C at 1.0 wt%). Are these liquidus values consistent with the assessed Al-Zr, Al-Ti, Al-Cr, Al-Mn and Al-Sc diagrams? Is +100 C enough for an Al-10Zr master alloy to dissolve in a 250 g induction-melted charge in 15-20 min? For X1 (Al-6Mg-2.5Si-0.7Ti) the two databases give 755 C and 1009 C (the latter for a Ti-Si compound); the plan uses 950 C. What is the best estimate of the liquidus of a Ti-bearing phase in Al-6Mg-2.5Si-0.7Ti, and what setpoint would you use? Are there crucible (graphite), nozzle or Mo-plate concerns at 1000-1060 C with 1-6 wt% Mg?
4. EVAPORATION. process_models.py estimates in-flight Mg loss from a 60 um droplet (0.5 % at 820 C, 5-8 % at 1010-1060 C) and a negligible crucible loss under 1 atm argon. Check the physics and the vapour-pressure and activity inputs. Is the conclusion that most Mg loss happens in LPBF rather than in the atomizer reasonable?
5. DROPLET SIZE. The plan uses Lang's relation with 0.34 x capillary wavelength and gets about 60 um for Al at 40 kHz (capillary wavelength 179 um with sigma 0.87 N/m, rho 2375 kg/m3). An earlier answer quoted 46 um and a wavelength of 135 um from the same formula. Please recompute and say which is right, and whether a 20-63 um cut at about 57 % of the atomized mass is a fair expectation.
6. LPBF SETTINGS. The start points come from matching the Eagar-Tsai melt-pool depth of each published parameter set (about 105 um) at 350 W on an 80 um spot with 50 um layers. Are the resulting settings in seeds.csv (speed, hatch, energy density, plate temperature, direct ageing) reasonable starting centres? Flag any that you expect to give lack of fusion, keyhole porosity or cracking, and any plate temperature or ageing schedule that contradicts the literature.
7. THE SAASBO RECOMMENDATION. Read saasbo_handoff_sim.py. Is the toy response biased toward the conclusion (six seeds then SAASBO is as good as nine seeds)? If you can run it, repeat a few instances with changes that would stress the conclusion (for example a response with sharper cliffs, no hidden synergy, or a penalty for adding an element not in any seed) and report what changes. Is the recommendation (six seeds, then SAASBO with one coverage pick in each of the first two batches, candidates restricted to feedstock on hand) sound?
8. WHAT IS MISSING. What would an experienced alloy developer or atomizer operator add or warn about that the plan does not mention (safety with fine Al-Mg powder and Mn/Cr fume, hydrogen, oxide skins, cross-contamination between runs, Sc and Er recovery, tensile specimen size effects, anything else)?
9. Give a short list of corrections ranked by how much damage each error would do if left in.

Separate what you verified from what you infer. If a claim in the README cannot be checked from the uploaded files, say so rather than assuming it is right.
