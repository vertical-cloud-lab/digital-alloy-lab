For a 15-week laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) aluminium alloy discovery campaign we need (a) a short list of known alloys to use as baselines and as literature-chosen seed compositions, and (b) published data and cheap models to seed each stage of a Bayesian-optimization pipeline before our own experiments exist.

CONTEXT
Our alloys are made by ultrasonic atomization of 250 g charges from a 6063 or high-purity Al base with additions from Mn, Cr, Zr, Mg, Si, Cu, Ti, Fe, Ni, Ce, Sc, Li, Er, Zn and Sn (at least 85 at.% Al, a few additions per alloy, dosed as master alloys or elemental powders). They are then printed as single tracks, small cubes and sub-size tensile bars on a research LPBF machine (Aconity MIDI class, 400 W-class fibre laser), with and without a heated plate. Goal: beat LPBF AlSi10Mg on yield strength and/or approach Scalmalloy (Al-Mg-Sc-Zr) with much less Sc or other critical minerals. Benchmarks: AlSi10Mg and Scalmalloy.

Please review the peer-reviewed literature and give:
1. A table of LPBF aluminium alloys that are Sc-free or Sc-lean and print crack-free, with nominal composition (wt% and at.%), how cracking was suppressed (eutectic fraction, Zr/Ti/Er/TiB2 inoculation, etc.), the process window (laser power, scan speed, hatch, layer thickness, plate preheat, volumetric energy density), as-built and heat-treated yield strength, UTS and elongation, relative density, and DOIs. Cover at least: AlSi10Mg, AlSi7Mg and AlSi12; Scalmalloy and lower-Sc Al-Mg-Sc-Zr; Al-Mg-Zr (Addalloy-type); Al-Mn-Sc and Al-Mn-Cr-Zr; Al-Zr and Al-Zr-Er/Yb; Al-Ce(-Mn, -Ni, -Mg); Al-Fe and Al-Fe-Ni / Al-Ni-Zr; Al-Cu-Mg with Zr, Ti or TiB2 (A20X-type, 2024+Zr); 6061 and 7075 with Zr or Ti/TiB2 inoculation; Al-Mg-Si with Zr. Flag which ones use critical minerals (Sc, Er and other rare earths, Li, etc.).
2. Which 3-4 of these make the best literature-chosen seeds for a campaign whose search space covers compositions near these systems, given that they should (i) spread out over the space rather than cluster, (ii) be dosable from master alloys (Al-Zr, Al-Mn, Al-Cr, Al-Ce, Al-Er, Al-Ti) and elemental Si, Mg, Cu, Fe and Ni, (iii) melt fully in a small crucible at moderate superheat (consider liquidus rise from Zr and Cr, primary Al3Zr at high Zr, Ce/Fe/Ni intermetallics), and (iv) be likely printable without a heated plate. Give specific compositions and the reasons.
3. Known problems when atomizing these compositions in small batches (Mg and Zn evaporation, oxide films, primary intermetallics of Zr or Cr, segregation) and the composition fidelity to expect.
4. Open datasets or databases of LPBF aluminium data that could seed (a) a print-parameter stage (laser power, speed, hatch, layer -> relative density, melt-pool size, cracking) and (b) a property stage (composition, process and heat treatment -> hardness, yield strength, UTS, elongation). For example: compiled LPBF Al process-density datasets, NIST AM-Bench, the Barrionuevo et al. data descriptor, Materials Data Facility, Citrine, Zenodo or Figshare datasets, review articles with tabulated LPBF Al tensile data, and aluminium alloy property databases used for machine learning. For each give the size, the variables, the licence or access model, and the DOI or URL.
5. Physics-based or semi-empirical models that can generate synthetic ("fantasy") pre-experiment data for each stage at low cost: Eagar-Tsai or Rosenthal melt-pool models with lack-of-fusion and keyhole criteria for print windows; Scheil-based hot-cracking indices (Kou index, CSC) from CALPHAD; solid-solution, precipitation (L1_2, Orowan) and grain-boundary strengthening models for yield strength; and droplet-size relations for ultrasonic atomization (e.g., Lang's relation). How accurate are they for LPBF aluminium, and how have they been used as priors or low-fidelity sources in Bayesian optimization?

Give a reference list with DOIs.
