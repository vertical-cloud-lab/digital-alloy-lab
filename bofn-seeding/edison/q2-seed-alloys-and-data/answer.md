**Key observations.** AlSi10Mg remains the volumetric baseline at approximately 250–350 MPa yield strength as-built, with UTS of 380–450 MPa and elongation of 5–8% (he2021designanddevelopment pages 30-35, li2026designinnovationsand pages 5-7). Scalmalloy® achieves 456–469 MPa yield strength after direct aging at 325 °C for 4 h, but relies on ~0.7 wt% Sc (cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 1-2). Among Sc-free systems, the Al–Mn–Ti–Zr alloy by Mair et al. achieved 284 MPa yield strength and 16.9% elongation as-built through metastable Mn supersaturation and coherent L1₂ Al₃(Ti,Zr) grain refinement (mair2022microstructuralfeaturesand pages 1-2). The A20X-type Al–Cu–TiB₂ alloy reached 401 MPa UTS as-built with 17.7% elongation and 99.5% density, with TiB₂ providing heterogeneous nucleation and sub-micron grain size (mair2021microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2). Near-eutectic Al–10Ce delivered 222 MPa yield strength and 319 MPa UTS with 10.8% elongation, exploiting the thermally stable Al₁₁Ce₃ eutectic (hesselmann2022effectofprecipitationforming pages 1-3). Al–Fe–Mg–Zr eutectic alloys reached 376 MPa yield strength after aging at 400 °C for 4 h (li2025precipitationevolutionaging pages 1-3), and Al–Fe–Cr quasicrystal-reinforced composites reached 395 MPa yield strength and 531 MPa UTS, though with limited ductility (kang2023laserpowderbed pages 1-3). For inoculated wrought alloys, AA7075 + 1 wt% Zr achieved crack-free builds with 325–373 MPa yield strength (yu2022processingandcharacterization pages 1-2, hussain2026metallurgicalaspectsof pages 7-8), and AA6061 + Zr-based nanoparticles reached 267 MPa UTS with 14.2% elongation and 99.94% density (hussain2026metallurgicalaspectsof pages 7-8).

## 2. Recommended Literature-Chosen Seed Compositions

For a campaign whose search space covers Al with additions from {Mn, Cr, Zr, Mg, Si, Cu, Ti, Fe, Ni, Ce}, four seed compositions are recommended that (i) spread across the composition space, (ii) are dosable from available master alloys and elemental additions, (iii) melt fully at moderate superheat, and (iv) are likely printable without a heated plate:

**Seed 1: Al–10Si–0.35Mg (AlSi10Mg baseline).** This eutectic system is universally printable, requires no heated plate, melts at ~600 °C with minimal superheat, and is dosable from elemental Si and Mg. It anchors the Si-rich corner and serves as the density and cracking benchmark. Extensive literature data exist for model validation (he2021designanddevelopment pages 30-35, li2026designinnovationsand pages 5-7).

**Seed 2: Al–4Mn–0.5Ti–0.5Zr (~Mair-type).** Dosable from Al–10Mn, Al–10Ti and Al–10Zr master alloys. The liquidus is only moderately elevated by Mn, Ti and Zr additions (Mn has ~2 wt% equilibrium solubility at ~660 °C, and Ti/Zr additions are small). The alloy prints crack-free without preheat at 380 W / 1900 mm/s with 284 MPa yield strength and excellent 16.9% elongation (mair2022microstructuralfeaturesand pages 1-2). This anchors the Mn-rich, Sc-free transition-metal corner.

**Seed 3: Al–10Ce (near-eutectic).** Dosable from Al–10Ce master alloy. The Al–Ce eutectic at ~10 wt% Ce melts near 640 °C with high fluidity and a narrow freezing range, ensuring crack-free printing without preheat (hesselmann2022effectofprecipitationforming pages 1-3). The composition anchors the rare-earth/eutectic corner and provides thermal stability data. Ce is a rare-earth element but substantially more abundant and less supply-critical than Sc or Er.

**Seed 4: Al–4.5Cu–0.3Mg–0.3Ti + 1 vol% TiB₂ (A20X-type).** Dosable from elemental Cu, Mg and Al–Ti–B master alloy or pre-alloyed A20X powder. This anchors the Cu-rich, age-hardenable corner. The TiB₂ grain-refinement strategy produces crack-free, dense parts with 401 MPa UTS and 17.7% elongation as-built (mair2021microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2). The liquidus is below ~650 °C and the alloy prints without preheat.

These four seeds cover the Si, Mn/Cr/Zr, Ce/Fe/Ni and Cu corners of the accessible search space. They avoid clustering, are all achievable by ultrasonic atomization from a 6063 or high-purity Al base with master-alloy dosing, and have demonstrated printability without heated plates.

## 3. Atomization Problems and Composition Fidelity

**Droplet size prediction.** For ultrasonic atomization of aluminium alloys at typical operating frequencies of 20–60 kHz, the characteristic droplet diameter can be estimated via the Lang-type relation D₃₂ ≈ 0.34·[8πγ/(ρf²)]^(1/3), where γ is the melt surface tension, ρ is the density and f is the ultrasonic frequency. Corrections based on Weber, Ohnesorge and intensity numbers refine the estimate. Doubling frequency is predicted to reduce powder size by approximately 40% (scientific2023comparisonofultrasonic pages 4-5). Accuracy is typically ±20–30% for metals when thermophysical properties are well known.

**Mg and Zn evaporation.** Magnesium (boiling point ~1091 °C) and zinc (boiling point ~907 °C) are volatile under the temperatures needed to melt aluminium alloys for atomization and during LPBF processing. Mg loss of 0.5–2 wt% is commonly observed during both gas atomization and LPBF, reducing the effectiveness of Mg₂Si and MgZn₂ precipitation strengthening (li2024additivemanufacturingof pages 38-42). For alloys targeting >4 wt% Mg (Addalloy-type or Scalmalloy compositions), significant process-stability problems arise from Mg evaporation and spatter during LPBF, which Mair et al. noted as a key reason to favor the Al–Mn system over Al–Mg for Sc-free designs (mair2022microstructuralfeaturesand pages 1-2). Zn is even more volatile and losses can exceed 1–3 wt% during LPBF, making 7xxx-type compositions especially challenging for composition fidelity.

**Oxide films.** Aluminium readily forms Al₂O₃ films. In small-batch ultrasonic atomization, maintaining inert-atmosphere purity (<100 ppm O₂) is essential. Oxide films on powders increase laser reflectivity, reduce flowability and can create oxide inclusions in printed parts. Charge sizes of 250 g are small enough that oxide-to-volume ratio may be higher than in industrial-scale atomization.

**Primary intermetallics.** High Zr content (>~0.5 wt%) risks formation of primary Al₃Zr during slow cooling of the melt before atomization, which can clog nozzles and produce inhomogeneous powder. The peritectic nature of Al₃Zr (formation at ~660 °C) means that at >1 wt% Zr, significant primary particles form unless extremely rapid quenching occurs. Similarly, Cr has very limited solubility in Al (~0.4 wt% at 660 °C) and can form primary Al₇Cr or Al₁₃Cr₂ intermetallics. Ce and Ni form intermetallics (Al₁₁Ce₃, Al₃Ni) at eutectic compositions, which are actually beneficial for printability but may settle or segregate in the crucible before atomization if the melt superheat is insufficient.

**Composition fidelity expectations.** For the recommended seed compositions atomized in 250 g batches: (i) Si, Cu, Fe, Ni and Ce are expected to be retained within ±0.2 wt% of target; (ii) Mn, Ti, Zr and Cr within ±0.3 wt% if well-dissolved; (iii) Mg within ±0.5–1.0 wt% due to evaporation; (iv) Zn may lose 1–3 wt%. ICP-OES verification of every powder batch is essential.

**Process-stage seeding.** The NIST AM-Bench 2022 aluminium challenge data provides single-track absorptance and X-ray imaging data for laser melting of bare aluminium plate, useful for calibrating melt-pool models (benoit2026provenancetrackedexperimentallyvalidated pages 61-63). The Barrionuevo et al. AlSi10Mg compilation reportedly aggregates ~270 records spanning laser power, speed, hatch, layer thickness, preheat, density, hardness and tensile properties from multiple studies, providing a useful structured dataset for process–density and process–property surrogate models. Machine-learning-optimized process parameter data for AlSi10Mg (200–355 W, 1307–2320 mm/s, relative densities of 99.2–99.4%) have been reported with Gaussian process regression achieving high accuracy (liu2021machinelearningassistedprocess pages 45-51).

**Property-stage seeding.** Review articles by Aversa et al. (2019) and Liu et al. (2024) contain tabulated tensile data for 10–30+ LPBF Al alloys covering compositions, process conditions, microstructures and mechanical properties, which can be manually digitized for training (he2021designanddevelopment pages 30-35). CALPHAD databases (Thermo-Calc TCAL, Pandat PanAl) can generate arbitrarily large synthetic composition screens of Scheil curves, phase fractions and thermophysical properties for use as low-fidelity training data (hyer2021effectsofalloy pages 1-2, dreano2022computationaldesignof pages 6-9, dreano2022computationaldesignof pages 4-6).

**Melt-pool models (Eagar–Tsai, Rosenthal).** The Eagar–Tsai model treats the laser as a moving Gaussian heat source on a semi-infinite body and predicts melt-pool width, depth and temperature field. It is typically accurate to ±20–30% for conduction-mode LPBF after alloy-specific absorptivity calibration, but fails in the keyhole regime because it neglects recoil pressure, Marangoni convection and free-surface depression. McElfresh et al. (2024) used the Eagar–Tsai model as a fast surrogate within a cellular-automaton framework for LPBF microstructure prediction. Lack-of-fusion criteria (pool depth ≥ layer thickness with overlap) and keyhole thresholds (normalized enthalpy or aspect ratio) can be applied to the model outputs to delineate admissible process windows. For LPBF 6061 aluminium, Sonawane (2021) coupled Rosenthal thermal simulations with the RDG hot-cracking criterion and found qualitative agreement between predicted cracking-sensitivity maps and experimental observations (sonawane2021structuralaluminumalloy pages 1-12).

**Scheil-based hot-cracking indices.** The Kou index, defined as |dT/d(f_s^{1/2})| near complete solidification, computed from Scheil–Gulliver curves using CALPHAD databases, has shown good qualitative correlation with experimental LPBF cracking behaviour for binary Al–Si, Al–Mg and Al–Cu alloys (hyer2021effectsofalloy pages 1-2). Hyer et al. (2020) demonstrated that including solid-state diffusion in the Scheil simulation substantially reduces predicted susceptibility and shifts the composition of maximum susceptibility. The Clyne–Davies CSC and Scheil-based brittle-temperature-range (BTR) criteria provide complementary metrics. Dreano et al. (2022) used a genetic algorithm with Scheil-BTR minimization and solid-solution strengthening maximization to computationally design a crack-free Al–6.7Mg–3Si–1.4Ni–1Zn–0.3Fe alloy with a BTR of only 9 °C (dreano2022computationaldesignof pages 22-24, dreano2022computationaldesignof pages 1-4, dreano2022computationaldesignof pages 4-6). Uba et al. (2025) used Kou's HSI criterion and Clyne–Davies CSC in Thermo-Calc to screen modified Al 7xxx compositions for LPBF printability (uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 4-5).

**Strengthening models.** Solid-solution strengthening using an atomic-size-misfit power law (Δσ_ss ∝ K·ε^{4/3}·c^{2/3}) was applied by Dreano et al. for alloy optimization (dreano2022computationaldesignof pages 6-9). For the Al–Mn–Ti–Zr system, Mair et al. measured that 3.7 wt% Mn in supersaturated solid solution contributed approximately 104 MPa (37% of the 284 MPa yield strength), consistent with the ~30 MPa per wt% Mn literature value (mair2022microstructuralfeaturesand pages 1-2). Orowan precipitation strengthening has been used to predict the aging response of L1₂ Al₃(Sc,Zr) precipitates in Scalmalloy-type alloys and of quasicrystalline particles in Al–Fe–Cr composites (kang2023laserpowderbed pages 1-3). Taheri-Mousavi et al. (2024) used a hybrid ICME + Bayesian-optimization workflow with CALPHAD-derived descriptors including L1₂ phase fraction, coarsening metrics and cracking susceptibility as inputs to a neural-network surrogate, achieving ~3% normalized prediction error with 40 training compositions and using expected-improvement Bayesian optimization to identify an optimized Al–Er–Zr–Ni alloy with 40% higher L1₂ fraction than the initial design (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 1-4, taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 7-9).

**Lang's equation for ultrasonic atomization.** The simplified Lang relation D ≈ 0.34·[8πγ/(ρf²)]^{1/3} predicts the Sauter mean droplet diameter from melt surface tension γ, density ρ and ultrasonic frequency f, with corrections for Weber, Ohnesorge and intensity numbers (scientific2023comparisonofultrasonic pages 4-5). Accuracy is approximately ±20–30% for metals. This can seed an atomization-stage BO model predicting sieve yield and particle size distribution as a function of frequency, temperature and composition.
