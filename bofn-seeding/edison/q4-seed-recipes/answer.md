### Family (a): Al–Mn–Cr–Zr and Al–Mn–Ti–Zr

**Al–Mn–Cr–Zr (Mehta / Chalmers).** Mehta et al. developed a family of Al–Mn–Cr–Zr alloys specifically for LPBF, with compositions determined by ICP-OES at Höganäs AB (mehta2023advancingnovelalmncrzr pages 1-3). Alloy C (Al–5.0Mn–0.8Cr–0.6Zr–0.17Fe–0.24Si, measured) was processed on an EOS M100 (170 W, 1500 mm/s, 0.1 mm hatch, 30 µm layers) and later an EOS M290 (370 W, 1300 mm/s, 0.13 mm hatch, 30 µm layers, 100 µm spot), achieving 99.5–99.8% relative density and inherent crack-freedom across a broad DOE window (mehta2022al–mn–cr–zrbasedalloystailored pages 3-4, mehta2022al–mn–cr–zrbasedalloystailored pages 4-5). As-built tensile properties averaged across XZ/XY orientations were YS 250 MPa, UTS 310 MPa, and 25% elongation; after direct ageing at 648 K for 24 h these rose to YS 350 MPa, UTS 440 MPa, and 8% elongation, with hardness increasing from ~102 to ~130 HV (mehta2023developmentofhigh pages 66-69).

Alloy F (Al–4.8Mn–0.9Cr–1.1Zr–1.2Mg, measured ICP-OES) introduced both increased Zr (beyond solubility, triggering grain refinement via primary L1₂-Al₃Zr) and Mg for solid-solution strengthening (mehta2023advancingnovelalmncrzr pages 1-3, mehta2023advancingnovelalmncrzr pages 3-5). It achieved 99.7–99.8% density and was crack-free, in contrast to Alloy E (Al–4.7Mn–0.8Cr–0.7Zr–1.0Mg) which cracked because its lower Zr content was insufficient for heterogeneous nucleation (mehta2023advancingnovelalmncrzr pages 3-5). Alloy F as-built: YS 390 MPa, UTS 450 MPa, 17% elongation; after ageing at 648 K for 8 h: YS 500 MPa, UTS 560 MPa, 5% elongation, hardness 132→172 HV (mehta2023advancingnovelalmncrzr pages 9-12, mehta2023developmentofhigh pages 66-69). The Al-HS1 industrial variant of this family was qualified at component level by Arena et al. (2024); after 375 °C/14 h ageing, horizontal specimens achieved YS 356 MPa, UTS 441 MPa, and 10.4% elongation (arena2024novelaluminumalloy pages 6-8).

**Al–Mn–Ti–Zr (Mair et al.).** A novel Al–4.8Mn alloy modified with Ti and Zr was processed at 380 W, 1900 mm/s, 0.1 mm hatch, 30 µm layers on an unheated substrate (mair2022microstructuralfeaturesand pages 1-2). The as-built alloy achieved 99.9 ± 0.1% density, was crack-free, and had YS 284 ± 3 MPa, UTS 320 ± 1 MPa, and 16.9 ± 0.2% elongation with 108 ± 1 HV10, primarily from Mn supersaturation (3.7 ± 0.5 wt% retained in matrix) and fine-grain strengthening via primary L1₂-Al₃(Ti,Zr) nucleants (mair2022microstructuralfeaturesand pages 4-6, mair2022microstructuralfeaturesand pages 7-8).

### Family (b): Al–Mg–Zr (Addalloy)

Griffiths, Croteau, Dunand and coworkers studied Addalloy™ with measured ICP-OES compositions of Al–3.60Mg–1.18Zr (Batch 1) and Al–3.60Mg–1.12Zr (Batch 2) (griffiths2020coarseningandcreep pages 2-3, griffiths2018effectoflaser pages 1-2). Processing was on a Concept Laser M2 (200 W CW, 1070 nm, 90 µm spot) at 200 mm/s, 0.135 mm hatch, and 30–40 µm layers. As-fabricated YS ranged from ~220 MPa (Batch 1, lower Zr) to ~290 MPa (Batch 2, higher Zr) depending on batch and orientation. After peak ageing at 400 °C for 2–8 h, YS reached 345–359 MPa with ~20% elongation (griffiths2020additivemanufacturingof pages 109-114, griffiths2020coarseningandcreep pages 2-3). The strengthening was dominated by coherent 2–5 nm L1₂-Al₃Zr precipitates formed during ageing, with Mg contributing solid-solution hardening.

### Family (c): Al–Fe–Zr (Aheadd CP1)

Pauzon et al. (2022) reported the Constellium Aheadd CP1 alloy with ICP-measured powder composition of Al–1.02Fe–1.05Zr (O 0.098, N <0.005, H 0.0019 wt%) (pauzon2022anovellaser pages 1-4). LPBF was performed on an EOS M290 at 370 W, 1400 mm/s, 0.13 mm hatch, 60 µm layers, with 100 °C platform preheating. Relative density exceeded 99.7%. After stress relief at 300 °C/2 h and direct ageing at 400 °C/4 h, the alloy reached YS 310 MPa with thermal conductivity of 180 W/m·K (pauzon2022anovellaser pages 1-4, pauzon2022anovellaser pages 8-12). The alloy exploits primary Al₃Zr for crack prevention and nanoscale Al₃Zr + Al₁₃Fe₄ precipitation for strengthening.

### Family (d): Al–Mg–Si Eutectic-Type Alloys (without Zr/Sc/Ti grain refiners)

Yang et al. (2023) processed a pseudo-binary Al–9.6Mg–4.9Si alloy (nominally Al–13.3 wt% Mg₂Si) by LPBF at VED 129.6 J/mm³, achieving 99.3% density and as-built YS 439 MPa, UTS 557 MPa, but only 2.9% elongation—far below the 8% target (yang2023microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2). A related Al–5.39Mg–3.08Zn–1.96Si–0.75Mn alloy (measured ICP) processed on a Farsoon FS271M at VED 129.1 J/mm³ achieved 403 MPa YS, 548 MPa UTS, and 6.7% elongation (yang2022developinganovel pages 1-5, yang2022developinganovel pages 18-21). These Zr-free, Si-free alloys demonstrate that high-Mg + Si eutectic compositions can reach >400 MPa YS as-built, but ductility and density are on the edge of the campaign requirements.

### Family (e): Modified Al–Si–Mg

The Al–Mg–Si–Zr alloy Al–7Mg–2Si–1.2Zr (nominal) by Yang et al. (2022) was processed at 170 W, 350 mm/s, 0.15 mm hatch, 30 µm layers, 150 °C plate, achieving as-built YS 447.9 ± 3.6 MPa, UTS 493.4 ± 6.7 MPa, and 9.6 ± 1.1% elongation (yang2022microstructureandmechanical pages 2-4, yang2022microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2). After HT2 (high-temperature ageing), YS further increased to 467 ± 1.3 MPa. This alloy requires Zr for grain refinement.

### Family (f): Er as Sc Substitute

Guo et al. (2022) demonstrated Al–Mg–Mn–Er–Zr alloys. The best performer, specimen 3# with measured composition Al–6.40Mg–0.72Mn–0.40Er–1.10Zr (guo2022microstructureandmechanical pages 2-3), was processed at 350 W, 1000 mm/s, 0.13 mm hatch, 30 µm layers (guo2022microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2). As-built: YS 325 ± 4 MPa, UTS 438 ± 3 MPa, 18.0 ± 1.8% elongation. After direct ageing at 375 °C/10 h: YS 440 ± 3 MPa, UTS 520 ± 2 MPa, 15.5 ± 1.0% elongation (guo2022microstructureandmechanical pages 4-8, guo2022microstructureandmechanical pages 3-4). The strengthening mechanism involves Al₃Er primary nucleation followed by Al₃(Er,Zr) core–shell precipitation. **No crack-free Er-bearing alloy without Zr was identified** in the retrieved literature.

**Al–Ni–Er–Zr (Taheri-Mousavi et al.).** The design composition was Al–2.73Ni–3.19Zr–2.33Er (wt%), ultrasonically atomized and LPBF-processed into crack-free specimens (taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 9-12, taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 12-15). The alloy exploits sub-micron metastable Al₂₃Ni₆M₄ ternary phases formed during rapid solidification as reservoirs for 1–5 nm coherent L1₂-Al₃(Er,Zr) nanoprecipitates upon ageing at 400 °C/8 h. As-built hardness was 180 HV; after ageing, peak tensile strength reached ~395 MPa, described as ~50% above the benchmark printable Al alloy and comparable to wrought Al 7075 (taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 12-15). The design is thermally stable through 48 h at 400 °C.

### Family (g): Low-Sc Al–Mg–Sc–Zr and Al–Mn–Sc

Bayoumy et al. (2023) studied an Al–Mn–Sc alloy with measured composition Al–4.58Mn–1.24Mg–0.91Sc–0.42Zr (wt%, converted from at%) (bayoumy2023effectiveplatformheating pages 2-3). Processed on an EOS M290 at 350 W, 1600 mm/s, 0.1 mm hatch, 30 µm layers, with 200 °C platform heating, the alloy achieved >99.5% density. The heated platform triggered in-situ ageing, precipitating 1.5–2 nm Sc-rich nanoclusters at 1.75 × 10²⁴ m⁻³ density, yielding YS 522 MPa with up to 18% elongation—without any post-build heat treatment (bayoumy2023effectiveplatformheating pages 1-2, bayoumy2023effectiveplatformheating pages 6-8). A related Al–3.04Mn–1.60Mg–0.78Sc–0.22Zr alloy (Zhang et al. 2023, measured) processed at large 120 µm layer thickness and heat-treated at 300 °C/5 h achieved YS 472 MPa and ~10% fracture strain (zhang2023strongandductile pages 1-3, zhang2023strongandductile pages 5-8). These results establish that ~0.8 wt% Sc with 0.2–0.4 wt% Zr is sufficient for fully equiaxed grains and excellent properties; reducing Sc below ~0.3 wt% risks incomplete refinement.

## 2. Zr-Free Compositions from Available Feedstock Exceeding ~300 MPa YS

The question restricts elements to Al, Si, Mg, Mn, Cr, Zn, Sn, Ti, Fe, Cu, Ni, Sc, and Er (no Zr, Ce, Li, Ag). Among compositions with published LPBF data:

1. **Al–9.6Mg–4.9Si (pseudo-binary Al–Mg₂Si eutectic):** YS 439 MPa, UTS 557 MPa, 2.9% elongation—**fails the ≥8% ductility criterion** (yang2023microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2).
2. **Al–5.39Mg–3.08Zn–1.96Si–0.75Mn (measured):** YS 403 MPa, UTS 548 MPa, 6.7% elongation, 98.9% density—marginal on ductility and density (yang2022developinganovel pages 1-5).
3. **Standard AlSi10Mg:** As-built YS ~240–270 MPa—**does not reach 300 MPa** (mehta2023developmentofhigh pages 66-69).
4. **Al–Mn–Cr (Zr-free ternary from the Mehta family):** Projected as-built YS ~200–250 MPa; aged YS may reach ~300 MPa—not directly demonstrated without Zr (mehta2022al–mn–cr–zrbasedalloystailored pages 2-3, mehta2022al–mn–cr–zrbasedalloystailored pages 3-4).
5. **Al–Mg–Si–Ti (Wen et al. 2024 concept, 0.77% Ti):** UTS 447.8 MPa, elongation 9.1% as-built—promising but Ti is the nucleant former, and the exact composition was not retrieved in full detail.
6. **AlSi11CuMn:** YS ~350 MPa as-built reported in a review (hussain2026metallurgicalaspectsof pages 5-7).

**Ranking by evidence strength:** The Al–Mg–Zn–Si–Mn composition has the strongest single-paper evidence for a Zr-free alloy exceeding 300 MPa, though its elongation and density are borderline. The pseudo-binary Al–Mg₂Si eutectic has the highest YS but fails ductility. Ti-containing Al–Mg–Si–Ti is the most mechanistically promising Zr-free route toward >400 MPa with ≥8% elongation, but requires validation of the specific composition. No Zr-free, Sc-free alloy in the retrieved evidence simultaneously meets YS >300 MPa, elongation ≥8%, and density ≥99.5%.

## 3. Microstructure Expectations for the Campaign's Route

The campaign uses ultrasonically atomized powder (D50 ~55–65 µm, cut to 20–63 µm), an Aconity MIDI (400 W-class, ~80 µm spot), and 30–50 µm layers. Key differences from the literature conditions:

**Coarser powder** (D50 ~40 vs. 15–37 µm in references): Coarser powder produces larger inter-particle voids and lower packing density, requiring higher VED to achieve full densification. Literature alloys like Addalloy used D50 14–37 µm (griffiths2020coarseningandcreep pages 2-3, griffiths2018effectoflaser pages 1-2), and CP1 used 60 µm layers with standard fine powder (pauzon2022anovellaser pages 1-4). Coarser feedstock may also have different chemistry (less surface oxide per unit mass but possibly different solidification history inside the particles).

**80 µm spot vs. 40–100 µm in references:** The 80 µm spot is similar to the EOS M290 (100 µm) used for most Mehta alloys and Bayoumy's alloy. The cooling rate will be on the order of 10⁵–10⁶ K/s, adequate for Mn/Cr/Mg supersaturation and primary Al₃Zr/Al₃Ti nucleation.

**Critical microstructural features by family:**
- **Al–Mn–Cr:** Success depends on Mn/Cr remaining in supersaturated solid solution (>3 wt% Mn metastably dissolved) (mair2022microstructuralfeaturesand pages 4-6). The coarser powder should not critically affect this, as supersaturation is determined by melt-pool cooling rate, not powder size.
- **Al–Mg–Si eutectic:** The α-Al/Mg₂Si eutectic spacing controls strength. Coarser powder and potentially lower cooling rate could coarsen the eutectic, reducing YS.
- **Al–Fe–Ni eutectic:** Similar eutectic-spacing sensitivity. The 80 µm spot should provide adequate refinement.
- **Mg loss:** At 810–840 °C atomization and during LPBF, Mg evaporation is significant for alloys with ≥5 wt% Mg. Add 0.5–1.0% excess Mg to the charge and verify by ICP-OES of both powder and printed material.

**25 °C vs. 150–200 °C plate preheat:** An unheated plate increases residual stress and cracking risk but preserves the supersaturated state. For Sc-containing alloys, a 200 °C plate can provide in-situ ageing equivalent to a post-build treatment (bayoumy2023effectiveplatformheating pages 1-2). For Al–Mn–Cr alloys, the ageing window is 350–400 °C, so a 150–200 °C plate should not cause premature precipitation. Using 150–200 °C for initial builds is recommended.

**Families that lose strength on a heated plate or during stress relief:** AlSi10Mg is most sensitive—holding at 200+ °C coarsens the Si network and reduces as-built UTS. The Al–Mn–Cr system is robust through ~250 °C but loses hardness above 350 °C over long holds.

### Design Rationale

**Mechanistic diversity.** The six seeds cover: (1) eutectic Si network hardening (AlSi10Mg); (2) Mn/Cr supersaturated solid solution (Zr-free Al–Mn–Cr); (3) eutectic Mg₂Si + Al₃Ti nucleation (Al–Mg–Si–Ti); (4) Mg + Mn + Cr solution strengthening with deliberate cracking-boundary probing (Al–Mn–Cr–Mg); (5) eutectic Al–Fe–Ni intermetallic network (Al–Fe–Ni); (6) Er-based L1₂ precipitation without Zr (Al–Mg–Mn–Er). Each probes a different strengthening mechanism and different elements, maximising SAASBO information content across 15 candidate elements.

**Feedstock compatibility.** Seeds 1–4 and 6 use only elements on hand (Al from 6063/Al-12Si bar, Mg, Mn, Cr, Ti, Si, Er). Seed 5 requires purchasing Fe and Ni (described as quick to buy). The three optional seeds (7–9) all require purchasing Al–10Zr master alloy.

**Meltability.** All six primary seeds have estimated liquidus temperatures below ~700 °C, well within the 1300 °C induction-furnace capacity. The practical concern is not furnace temperature but rather primary intermetallic formation:
- **Al–Mn–Cr:** At ≥5 wt% Mn, the Mn-rich sludge region (Al₆Mn) may form if the melt is held below ~700 °C for extended periods. Rapid melting and immediate pouring into the atomizer is recommended.
- **Ti-containing alloys (Seed 3):** Al₃Ti has a peritectic temperature of ~665 °C. Ti should ideally be added as an Al–5Ti–1B rod or similar master alloy and dissolved at ≥750 °C, though the charge is small enough that dissolution should be rapid.
- **Er-containing alloys (Seed 6):** Al₃Er forms at ~640 °C. With only 0.3 wt% Er and a 350 g charge, dissolution at 810–840 °C should be achievable.

### What Is Known vs. Unknown for Each Seed

**Seed 1 (AlSi10Mg):** Well-established LPBF alloy with hundreds of publications. Expected to print without issue. Known: as-built YS ~230–270 MPa (mehta2023developmentofhigh pages 66-69). Unknown: exact performance with ultrasonically atomized coarser powder.

**Seed 2 (Zr-free Al–5Mn–0.8Cr):** The ternary without Zr has not been independently published as a tensile specimen—Mehta's Alloy A (Al–4.7Mn, no Cr, no Zr) and Alloy B (Al–0.8Mn–0.14Cr, no Zr) were ternaries tested only for hardness (~80–90 HV) (mehta2022al–mn–cr–zrbasedalloystailored pages 2-3). Crack-freedom is expected based on the Scheil design principle, but the ageing response without Zr precipitation hardening is uncertain.

**Seed 3 (Al–5Mg–2Si–0.7Ti):** Inspired by Wen et al.'s Al–Mg–Si–Ti (UTS 447.8 MPa, 9.1% elongation as-built) and Yang et al.'s high-Mg–Si alloys (yang2022developinganovel pages 1-5, yang2023microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2). The specific composition is *not a direct reproduction* of any single published alloy. Mg₂Si eutectic backfilling + Al₃Ti nucleation is the mechanism being tested.

**Seed 4 (Al–4.7Mn–0.8Cr–1.0Mg):** The closest literature alloy (Mehta Alloy E, with 0.7 wt% Zr) **cracked** (mehta2023advancingnovelalmncrzr pages 1-3, mehta2023advancingnovelalmncrzr pages 3-5). This seed deliberately probes whether the alloy cracks without Zr, providing essential boundary information for the optimizer.

**Seed 5 (Al–1.5Fe–1.5Ni):** An exploratory eutectic composition. Al–Fe–Ni eutectic alloys have been investigated for thermal stability but tensile data at this composition are sparse in the retrieved evidence. The narrow freezing range should favour printability.

**Seed 6 (Al–5Mg–0.7Mn–0.3Er):** No Zr-free Er alloy was found to be published as crack-free. This is a high-information exploratory seed to determine whether Al₃Er alone provides sufficient nucleation.

**Optional Seed 7 (Mehta Alloy F reproduction):** Directly reproduces the strongest documented result in the Al–Mn–Cr–Zr family: 500/560 MPa YS/UTS (mehta2023developmentofhigh pages 66-69). Requires Al–10Zr master alloy purchase.

**Optional Seed 8 (Guo Al–6Mg–0.7Mn–0.4Er–1.1Zr):** Reproduces the best Er-bearing result: 440/520 MPa YS/UTS with 15.5% elongation after 375 °C/10 h (guo2022microstructureandmechanical pages 4-8, guo2022microstructureandmechanical pages 3-4). Requires Zr and uses limited Er stock.

**Optional Seed 9 (Low-Sc Al–4Mg–0.3Sc–0.4Zr):** Tests whether 0.3% Sc with 0.4% Zr provides a useful fraction of Scalmalloy performance (~460–520 MPa) (hussain2026metallurgicalaspectsof pages 36-38, he2021designanddevelopment pages 40-45). Requires both Zr and uses limited Sc stock.
