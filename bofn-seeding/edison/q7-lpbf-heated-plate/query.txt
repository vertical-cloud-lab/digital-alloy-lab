We will print nine different aluminium alloys, each as single tracks, density cubes and sub-size tensile bars, on an Aconity MIDI laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) machine (single-mode fibre laser, 400 W class with up to 1 kW optional, spot about 80 um, argon, build-plate heating available; a high-temperature heating module may reach 500 C or more). Powder is ultrasonically atomized in-house, sieved to about 20-63 um (D50 about 45-55 um), 100-300 g per alloy, so layers of 30-50 um and a reduced build platform. Please review the literature and give starting process windows and what build-plate heating does, per alloy family, quantitatively.

Alloy families: (1) AlSi10Mg; (2) Scalmalloy-type Al-Mg-Sc-Zr; (3) Al-Mn-Cr-Zr and Al-Mn-Ti-Zr; (4) Al-Mg-Zr (Addalloy-type); (5) Al-Fe-Zr (Aheadd CP1-type) and Al-Fe-Mg-Zr; (6) high-Mg Al-Mg-Si(-Mn) eutectic-type; (7) Al-Mg-Er-Zr; (8) Zr-modified 7xxx (Al-Zn-Mg-Cu-Zr) and 2xxx; (9) Al-Ce and Al-Ni eutectic alloys.

1. Published parameter sets on Aconity machines (MIDI, MIDI+, MINI, LAB, TWO) for aluminium alloys: laser power, scan speed, hatch, layer thickness, spot size, scan strategy, preheat, gas flow, resulting density. Include AlSi10Mg, Scalmalloy, 6061, 7075, 2024, Al-Mn-Sc, Al-Ce and any novel alloys. Which universities run Al on Aconity machines?
2. For each family above: the published crack-free, >99.5 % dense windows on any 200-400 W machine with a 70-100 um spot (power, speed, hatch, layer, volumetric energy density, plate temperature), and how the window shifts for 50 um layers and coarser powder.
3. Build-plate heating: for each family, the measured effect of 25 C vs 100-200 C vs 300-500 C plate temperature on (a) solidification and liquation cracking, (b) density and hydrogen porosity, (c) residual stress and distortion, (d) as-built strength (in-situ ageing or over-ageing: Si network coarsening in AlSi10Mg, Al3(Sc,Zr) precipitation in Scalmalloy at 200 C, Mn/Cr/Zr supersaturation loss), (e) Mg and Zn evaporation, (f) powder caking / sintering and reuse. Which families need a heated plate, which are indifferent, and which are harmed? Give the best plate temperature for each. Include the 6061 at 500 C result (Uddin et al.) and similar high-preheat studies for 7075 and 2024.
4. How to set a first print window for an alloy nobody has printed: normalized-enthalpy and keyhole-threshold criteria, lack-of-fusion criteria from melt-pool width/depth, Eagar-Tsai / Rosenthal estimates and their accuracy for Al (absorptivity values to use for Al alloys with powder, about 0.2-0.6 depending on mode), balling / humping limits at high speed, and how alloy thermal conductivity (e.g. Mn/Cr/Fe-rich alloys with low conductivity versus high-conductivity dilute alloys) and Mg/Zn vapour pressure move the window.
5. Efficient experimental designs when powder is scarce (100-300 g per alloy): single tracks on bare plate or on a thin powder layer, number of tracks per composition, small cubes (5-10 mm), mini tensile geometries (ASTM E8 sub-size, SS-J3, miniature dog-bones) and how their strength and elongation compare with full-size bars; reduced build-volume inserts; multi-material / multi-powder plates; in-situ alloying shortcuts. What is the minimum powder to get density + hardness + three tensile values?
6. Scan strategy and other settings that matter for novel Al alloys: 67 degree rotation, stripes vs chessboard, remelting, contour scans, oxygen level in the chamber (below 100-500 ppm), gas flow, recoater type for coarser / less flowable powder, powder drying (humidity; hydrogen porosity), and the effect of oxygen content of the powder.
7. Direct-ageing schedules after printing for each family and expected hardness / yield response; whether to stress relieve.
8. A recommended starting table for the nine families on an Aconity MIDI with a 20-63 um powder: layer thickness, power, speed, hatch, plate temperature, expected melt-pool depth/width, expected density, main failure mode to watch for, and a compact 12-20 condition design of experiments around that centre.

Give DOIs. Separate measured data from extrapolation.
