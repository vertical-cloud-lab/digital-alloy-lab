Context: we are designing a lab-built desktop laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) machine for small-batch screening of aluminium alloys (AlSi10Mg, 6061, 7075 and grain-refined variants with Zr, Sc or TiB2). Laser: 500 W single-mode CW fibre laser at 1070 nm (IPG YLR-500 class) into a SCANLAB basiCube 20 galvo (marking speed up to about 1.1 m/s at f = 160 mm) and a Thorlabs FTH160-1064 f-theta lens (f = 160 mm), giving a ~96 um 1/e2 spot diameter (48-75 um spots are options). Expected process window: 200-350 W, 0.4-1.1 m/s, 30 um layers, ~0.1 mm hatch. Build plates are 25, 50 or 75 mm across, heated to 200 C (500 C reserved). Chamber: ~280 x 190 x 120 mm inside, vacuum-purged then argon at +5 to +20 mbar, with an argon cross-flow of 2.5-3.3 m/s over the plate (140-200 L/min) from a slot nozzle on the back wall to an outlet slot at the front; a cover slide sits under a lid window ~130 mm above the plate.

We plan in-situ monitoring through windows in the chamber walls: a side high-speed camera through a ~20 mm fused-silica viewport in a side wall at ~14 degrees above the plate (~160-200 mm from the plate centre), a steep second camera through the lid at ~54 degrees, and a low raking-light window in the back wall at ~10 degrees.

1. How do commercial machines (EOS M 290/M 300, SLM, Trumpf TruPrint, Renishaw, Aconity, Additive Industries) and research rigs integrate off-axis cameras, high-speed cameras and pyrometers: window positions and elevation angles, window materials (fused silica, sapphire, BK7), protective cover glasses, argon purge curtains for viewports, and how often they are cleaned or changed?
2. Window and cover-slide fouling: measured transmission loss against build time for Al alloys; condensate deposition on windows at different positions relative to the cross-flow (upstream, downstream, side, above); how well purge jets or gas knives keep windows clean.
3. Side-view high-speed imaging of spatter and plume in LPBF: published set-ups (frame rate, exposure, field of view, um per pixel, illumination wavelength and power, filters), especially at low elevation angles (10-20 degrees above the bed), and the quantitative spatter metrics they extracted.
4. Viewport design for vacuum and argon chambers: window thickness against diameter for a 1 bar pressure difference (safety factors used), O-ring versus bonded window seals, clamp rings, and thermal limits near a 200 C build plate.
5. Laser safety of viewports on a Class 1 enclosure with a 500 W 1070 nm laser: filter OD requirements, diffuse against specular reflection hazards at low and steep angles, and whether a camera window needs a laser-safety filter rated for an exposure time.

Give quantitative values and sources.
