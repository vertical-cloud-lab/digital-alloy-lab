Context: we are designing a lab-built desktop laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) machine for small-batch screening of aluminium alloys (AlSi10Mg, 6061, 7075 and grain-refined variants with Zr, Sc or TiB2). Laser: 500 W single-mode CW fibre laser at 1070 nm (IPG YLR-500 class) into a SCANLAB basiCube 20 galvo (marking speed up to about 1.1 m/s at f = 160 mm) and a Thorlabs FTH160-1064 f-theta lens (f = 160 mm), giving a ~96 um 1/e2 spot diameter (48-75 um spots are options). Expected process window: 200-350 W, 0.4-1.1 m/s, 30 um layers, ~0.1 mm hatch. Build plates are 25, 50 or 75 mm across, heated to 200 C (500 C reserved). Chamber: ~280 x 190 x 120 mm inside, vacuum-purged then argon at +5 to +20 mbar, with an argon cross-flow of 2.5-3.3 m/s over the plate (140-200 L/min) from a slot nozzle on the back wall to an outlet slot at the front; a cover slide sits under a lid window ~130 mm above the plate.

We plan coaxial melt-pool sensing through the galvo and f-theta (the returning light passes a short-pass dichroic that reflects 1070 nm into the galvo): a Si photodiode (Thorlabs PDA36A2), a second photodiode for two-colour (ratio) pyrometry, a monochrome high-speed camera at ~8 um per pixel in an 850 nm band like NIST's AMMT, and possibly an InGaAs (1.3-1.7 um) channel. For aluminium alloys specifically:

1. Spectral emissivity of solid and liquid aluminium and of Al-Si and Al-Mg alloys between 0.5 and 1.7 um, its temperature dependence, and how oxide films and powder change it. How large is the error of two-colour (grey-body) pyrometry on liquid Al, and which band pairs minimise it?
2. Published coaxial pyrometry or imaging on AlSi10Mg or other Al alloys in LPBF: bands, detectors, sampling rates, signal levels, calibration methods (for example Hooper 2018's integrating sphere), and what they could and could not resolve.
3. Radiometric signal levels: photon counts or photocurrents from an Al melt pool through an NA ~0.05 coaxial path; whether a 10 us exposure at 850 +/- 10 nm on a CMOS camera gives usable SNR; the exposures and bandwidths published systems needed.
4. Spectral transmission of galvo mirror coatings designed for 1064 nm, and of f-theta lenses, outside 1064 nm (Kozjek et al. found strong blocking at 750-875 nm): typical curves, and how vendors (SCANLAB, Raylase) specify monitoring or dual-wavelength coatings.
5. Plume and condensate emission and absorption between 500 and 1700 nm for Al alloys: atomic lines (Al, Mg, Si, Zn, Na, K), continuum, and how to choose filter bands that avoid them.
6. Chromatic focus shift of f-theta lenses between 1064 nm and 600-1600 nm: typical magnitudes, and how coaxial camera systems compensate (refocusing, achromatic correctors, telecentric relays).

Quantitative answers with sources, please.
