| Electrochemical/galvanic cell | Oxygen diffuses through a membrane and is reduced electrochemically; generated current is proportional to O₂ concentration. (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020) | Preferred primary process sensor for roughly 10–1000 ppm O₂ when specified for Ar service. Use pressure/flow regulation, filtered sampling, zero/span checks and a second independent sensor for critical builds. |
| Amperometric zirconia, diffusion-limited | A voltage pumps O₂ through heated zirconia while a diffusion barrier limits supply; limiting current is approximately proportional to O₂ concentration. Dual-cell wide-range devices use feedback to hold a fixed Nernst potential. | Broader and more quantitative than a conventional lambda probe, potentially extending from trace oxygen to percent levels; actual low-ppm accuracy is device-specific. | Heated planar versions may reach operating temperature in under 10 s; concentration response also depends on barrier diffusion and sample transport. | Porous diffusion barriers can clog with LPBF dust or condensate. Catalytic electrodes remain vulnerable to reactive gases and surface poisons; temperature control is essential. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000021) | Potentially strong for fast, wide-range control, provided the sensor has a filtered bypass, stable sample pressure, automatic temperature control and calibration data specifically covering 1–1000 ppm in argon. |
| Optical luminescence-quenching sensor | O₂ collisionally quenches the intensity or lifetime of an excited luminophore; lifetime or phase-shift measurement is generally more stable than intensity alone. | Probe-dependent; trace-ppm capability is possible with specialized films, but many commercial probes target percent O₂ or dissolved oxygen.
