Please review, with sources, in-situ process monitoring for a small lab-built laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) machine used to screen aluminium alloys, and rank what to add first. The machine: 500 W single-mode CW Yb fibre laser at 1070 nm, SCANLAB basiCube 20 galvo (20 mm aperture, mirrors coated for 1064 nm, no camera port), f = 160 mm fused-silica f-theta lens, a 2 in. fused-silica chamber window with a replaceable cover slide, a sealed 280 x 190 x 120 mm aluminium chamber in argon at +5 to +20 mbar with a 2.5-3 m/s cross-flow, interchangeable heated build plates of 25, 50 and 75 mm diameter at up to 200 C, 30-60 um layers, ~75-96 um spot, scan speed up to ~1.1 m/s. Powders: AlSi10Mg, Al-Mg-Sc-Zr, and crack-prone 6xxx, 7xxx and 2xxx alloys with and without grain refiners (Zr, Sc, TiB2), so detecting solidification (hot) cracking matters. Monitoring already planned: (a) a 12 MP layer camera looking through a lid viewport 33 degrees off vertical, about 26 um per pixel over the plate, imaging after each spread and after lasing with a low raking light; (b) a coaxial silicon photodiode picking off 520-985 nm melt-pool emission with a dichroic between the shutter and the galvo, sampled at >= 100 kHz. We want to record what happens in the chamber as fully as possible (high resolution, high frame rate, high magnification), with extensive synchronised metadata, and learn about powder spreading, the melt pool, and when cracking happens during solidification.

Questions:
1. Solidification cracking: which in-situ methods have actually detected hot cracking during LPBF (or laser welding or DED) of crack-prone alloys (Al 6xxx/7xxx/2xxx, Ni superalloys such as CM247LC or IN738LC, others)? For each: sensor type (structure-borne acoustic emission under the plate, airborne microphones, fibre Bragg gratings, thermography, pyrometry, optical imaging, synchrotron X-ray), frequency band or wavelengths, sensor placement, how detections were validated (CT, metallography, operando X-ray), and detection rates or false-alarm rates. When does the crack form relative to the melt pool passing (time and distance), and is it ever visible optically at the surface? Does acoustic emission still work through a heated plate with a ceramic thermal break and bolted or foil interfaces; what sensor temperature limits and waveguides are used?
2. Coaxial melt-pool sensing through a galvo: photodiodes versus two-colour pyrometers versus coaxial high-speed cameras. Which wavelength bands and bandwidths are used, how much do 1064 nm scanner mirror coatings and f-theta chromatic aberration limit coaxial imaging (resolution, field, focus shift), and how are they calibrated (blackbody, tungsten lamp, melting-point references)? What melt-pool temperatures and cooling rates have been measured for aluminium alloys in LPBF, and how reliable is two-colour pyrometry on aluminium given its low and changing emissivity?
3. Off-axis high-speed imaging of the melt pool and spatter in research LPBF testbeds (for example Imperial College/Hooper, LLNL, NIST AMMT, Sandia, MIT, EMPA, PSI): frame rates, exposure, magnification and working distance, long-distance microscopes, illumination lasers (for example Cavitar Cavilux) and filters, viewing angle, and how cameras are mounted and windows protected from metal condensate. What is the cheapest setup that still resolves melt-pool and spatter dynamics usefully?
4. Powder spreading and layer quality: what image resolution and lighting detect streaks, short feed, particle-scale defects and super-elevated edges; what in-situ height mapping (fringe projection, laser-line profilometry, low-coherence or inline coherent imaging, triangulation with the scanner's pilot laser) has measured actual layer thickness, and to what accuracy? Has anyone imaged the spreading itself (the powder heap ahead of the blade) at high speed inside an LPBF chamber?
5. Thermal imaging in a small chamber: NIR, SWIR and MWIR cameras, window materials (sapphire, CaF2, ZnSe, germanium), emissivity problems for aluminium, frame rates achievable, and what thermography has shown about cooling, heat accumulation and cracking.
6. Data and synchronisation: which signals to record (scan-controller position and laser gate, commanded and actual laser power, photodiode, acoustic, cameras, plate temperatures, O2, dew point, gas flow, recoater), how to put them on one clock with a SCANLAB RTC-class controller, typical data rates and storage per build, data standards or schemas used (for example NIST, ASTM/ISO), and documented cases where monitoring data misled.
7. Priorities: for a lab machine with a monitoring budget of roughly 20,000-40,000 USD, rank the additions by what they teach about spreading, melt pool and cracking per dollar, and name specific commercial instruments and their approximate prices where you can.
