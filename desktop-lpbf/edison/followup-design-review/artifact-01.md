| Fused-silica chamber window plus 3 mm fused-silica sacrificial slide | Contamination makes fused silica strongly thermo-optically sensitive. At 400 W and 0.5% added absorption, modeling gave a −21.6 mm focus shift; contaminated-window experiments measured approximately −22 to −30 mm and beam-waist growth from 35 to 53 µm. | Use a replaceable, AR-coated sacrificial shield and gas knife, monitor transmitted power/focus, and clean or replace between builds. N-FK51A produced only about −1.3 mm modeled shift under comparable conditions. Sapphire is defensible for the structural pressure window because of toughness and NIR transmission, but the cited literature does **not** establish it as thermally superior to N-FK51A. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000047, pqac-00000048, pqac-00000054) | **Critical** |
| Nominal cross-flow of about 2 m/s | About 2.0 m/s is a reported lower critical threshold, not a comfortable design point; local speed will be lower where inlet profiles decay or recirculation occurs. At 2.5–4.0 m/s, reported porosity remained below 0.1%; at ≤2.0 m/s, porosity, unstable melt pools, balling, necking, and swelling increased. | Design for a **measured local velocity of at least 2.5 m/s everywhere over the usable plate**, preferably with adjustment through roughly 2.5–4.0 m/s, while verifying that the upper setting does not pick up Al powder. Qualify using a 2-D velocity map rather than slot-area flow calculations. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) | **Critical** |
| Compact 6.4 L chamber with only the plate-level cross-flow/gas knife | Compact chambers have little vertical and lateral margin; recirculated soot can cross the beam, redeposit on the bed, or foul the window. Baffles, guide vanes, tapering, and a larger outlet merely shifted recirculation in one small-chamber study. | Add an independently adjustable **secondary upper nozzle/window sweep** and a generously captured outlet. Griggs et al. suppressed window-side recirculation with dual nozzles and achieved >1.4–1.5 m/s over a 10 × 60 mm area using 12–15 NL/min; the present LPBF process should instead meet the ≥2.5 m/s local target established above. Validate with CFD followed by smoke/PIV/hot-wire mapping. (pqac-00000052, pqac-00000054) | **Critical** |
| Rigid high-speed-steel recoater blade | A rigid blade transmits collision loads from adhered spatter, overheight tracks, and curled parts; a nick creates a repeatable longitudinal streak. Rigid ceramic blades produced collision/rebound marks, whereas compliant rubber imposed little stress even when clearance closed. | Provide interchangeable rigid and compliant cartridges. Use a compliant silicone/rubber or other proven polymer blade for early development and collision-prone alloys, with a rigid blade retained for controlled comparison. Operate initially near **50–100 mm/s**, below the proposed 150 mm/s maximum, and add blade-height adjustment, force/accelerometer monitoring, and automatic abort. (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000059, pqac-00000060) | **High** |
| Build-plate heating limited to 200 °C | This range is useful for AlSi10Mg stress management but should not be presented as sufficient for crack-prone wrought alloys. For 7075, 100–200 °C did not significantly reduce cracking; 400 °C caused columnar crack networks and lower hardness. For Zr-modified 6182, fully equiaxed, crack-free material with >99.0% density was demonstrated at 500 °C. | Either restrict the first machine’s claims to AlSi10Mg/Al-Mg-Sc-Zr and grain-refined alloys proven at ≤200 °C, or redesign the hot module for alloy-dependent operation up to about **500 °C**. Do not extrapolate the 6182 result to every 2xxx/6xxx/7xxx alloy; high-temperature preheating alone did not solve 7075 cracking. (pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000042) | **High** |
| Gravity-fed wedge hopper with an 8 mm outlet and fluted metering roller | Fine Al powder is cohesive; van der Waals, capillary, and electrostatic forces promote arching, ratholing, wall adhesion, and intermittent short feed. An 8 mm slot may work, but the literature does not supply a transferable repeatability value proving it will work for 15–45 µm Al. | Make outlet width adjustable and use steep, smooth mass-flow walls, active agitation or a controllable vibrator, load-cell dose verification, and a low-level/short-feed interlock. Calibrate delivered mass versus actuation for every alloy and fill level. The published vibrating-hopper testbed used pressure/time calibration over **20–60 psi** and **0.5–20 s**, with three repeats per condition; its dose calculation was explicitly only first-order. (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000056, pqac-00000058) | **Critical** |
| Bare 6061-T6 aluminium chamber used while screening multiple aluminium alloys | The retrieved LPBF literature does not establish bare 6061 as prohibited, but soft, uncoated aluminium walls are harder to clean without scratching and can retain powder/condensate, increasing cross-alloy carry-over. A blanket claim that stainless steel is mandatory would exceed the cited evidence. | Prefer removable electropolished **316L liners**, trays, ducts, and powder-contact modules—or construct the powder-contact interior from stainless steel—while retaining aluminium externally if weight and machining favor it. Establish swab-based cleanliness acceptance and dedicated removable parts for chemically sensitive Sc/Zr, Cu-, Zn-, or Mg-bearing campaigns. | **High; engineering control with evidence gap** |
| Single evacuation to about 1 mbar followed by argon backfill | Pressure alone does not verify dryness: water desorbs from powder, porous insulation, seals, and chamber surfaces after backfill and heating. Moisture can supply oxygen and hydrogen; hydrogen promotes porosity in molten Al and can embrittle high-strength Al alloys. | Use multiple evacuation/backfill cycles, dry-argon flow-through during warm-up, and—where materials permit—mild chamber bakeout. Measure rather than infer atmosphere quality: install low-range O₂ and dew-point sensors near the return and interlock laser operation to validated limits. Keep slight positive pressure during processing, but pressure must not substitute for O₂/H₂O measurement. (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000065) | **Critical** |
