We are designing a small, research-grade (desktop) laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) machine for screening new aluminium alloys from small powder batches: AlSi10Mg-type alloys, Al-Mg-Sc/Zr, and crack-prone high-strength 2xxx, 6xxx and 7xxx alloys modified with grain refiners. The base design has a Ø25-50 mm build plate on a servo Z piston, a ~6 L vacuum-purgeable argon chamber, a dose-from-above hopper cartridge of ~100 cm3 instead of a supply piston, a servo ball-screw recoater, and a fixed galvanometer scanner above a window in the lid. Please review the peer-reviewed literature (and machine-builder documentation where it is the only source), with citations and quantitative values, on:

1. Heated build plates in LPBF. Preheat temperatures used for aluminium alloys and their effects on cracking, porosity, residual stress, in-situ ageing or over-ageing, and microstructure (AlSi10Mg at 35-200 C and above; Al-Mg-Sc/Zr; crack suppression in 2xxx and 7xxx), and for other alloys (Ti-6Al-4V, Ni superalloys, intermetallics, tungsten) up to 500-1200 C. Heater technologies (resistive cartridge or film heaters, induction, infrared lamps, laser preheating) and the maximum temperatures each reaches. Thermal isolation of a heated plate from the piston, piston seals and Z guides; temperature uniformity and measurement; effects of a hot powder bed on the powder (sintering or caking, Mg and Zn evaporation, oxidation, reuse); and effects of heating on the chamber gas, the lid window and the O2 sensors.
2. Interchangeable build-plate sizes and reduced build volume (RBV) inserts: how research and commercial machines implement them (Renishaw RBV, Aconity, TRUMPF TruPrint 1000 at Ø50 mm, Sisma MYSINT100, EOS M 290 reduced-volume kits, university-built inserts); plate registration, clamping and levelling; piston sealing; powder containment; and whether builds on small plates give microstructures and properties comparable to large plates (thermal mass, heat sinking, part spacing), i.e. how well screening results transfer to production machines.
3. Powder supply variants: interchangeable hopper or cartridge sizes, dose-on-demand feeders (metering roller, vibratory, screw, slot), dosing accuracy and repeatability, powder segregation and residence time in hoppers, multi-material powder delivery, and the minimum powder needed per layer.
4. Automation-friendliness: automated build-plate exchange and removal (zero-point clamping, magnetic or kinematic couplings, build modules or cylinders that leave the machine, Additive Industries MetalFAB, EOS and TRUMPF exchangeable cylinders, robot loading), closed or automated depowdering and powder recovery, sieving, chamber cleaning (condensate and soot removal, cleaning or exchanging the window cover slide), safe handling of reactive condensate, and integration of LPBF into autonomous or self-driving labs (robot arms, gantries, APIs, sensors that confirm each step).

Please end with a ranked list of concrete design choices for a desktop alloy-screening LPBF machine that make the heated plate, the plate size and the hopper size swappable, and make plate removal and chamber cleaning automatable, with the temperature, tolerance and dimension targets the literature supports.
