| Rank | Design choice | Quantitative target/specification | Rationale with key evidence | Implementation notes |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Modular heated build-plate cassette | Routine module: ambient–200 °C; high-temperature module: ambient–500 °C plate, with heater capability ≥550 °C | AlSi10Mg undergoes useful in-situ ageing around 150–170 °C but can over-age during long exposure; crack-free, fully equiaxed EN AW-6182 was demonstrated at 500 °C and >99% density. AA7075 was not made crack-free even at 400 °C, so 500 °C capability is valuable but not universally sufficient. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000007) | Use replaceable heater cassettes rather than forcing one assembly to span every regime. A 3–6-cartridge symmetric pattern is appropriate for Ø50 mm; derate heater watt density in vacuum and provide independent over-temperature protection. |
| 2 | Interchangeable Ø25 and Ø50 mm build modules | Ø25 mm screening insert and Ø50 mm validation insert; common Z datum and coupling; recommended remount repeatability ≤10 µm in Z and ≤25 µm in X–Y; plate flatness ≤20 µm | A published modular research system used a Ø40 mm substrate and very small powder quantities. A MySINT100 plate used twelve Ø12 mm removable inserts with precision registration, showing that small, replaceable build interfaces are practical. (pqac-00000045, pqac-00000050) | Swap the complete plate, containment sleeve and upper piston seal together. Treat the dimensional tolerances as design targets requiring gauge-R&R validation, not as values directly established by the cited literature. |
| 3 | Thermally isolate heater from Z piston and guides | Ceramic break beneath heater; cooled radiation shield; keep ball screw, encoder, guide bearings and ordinary seals <70–100 °C at a 500 °C plate | A custom cartridge-heated module used a ceramic insert. The high-temperature induction system used three water-cooled exchangers and targeted vulnerable components below 100 °C; motor and transmission limits were about 70 °C. Poor insulation prevented a 3 kW ceramic-heater system from reaching its 600 °C target. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020) | Minimize metallic cross-section between hot cassette and piston, use reflective shields and water-cooled intercepts, and monitor both sides of the thermal break. Interlock Z motion and heating on coolant flow and return temperature. |
| 4 | Reproducible plate registration and clamping | Three-point kinematic or zero-point coupling; one axial clamp; no overconstraint; target ≤10 µm Z repeatability and ≤0.02° tilt | Zero-point systems provide rapid, reproducible fixation. The MySINT100 research plate used cylindrical pockets, precision pins and reverse-side screws to align removable inserts. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000045) | Use three hardened contacts or a cone–groove–flat arrangement outside the hottest zone. Avoid relying on magnets alone above about 200 °C; provide a positive mechanical lock and presence/lock sensors. |
| 5 | Closed Ø25/Ø50 powder-containment sleeves | Sleeve ID matched to plate with radial running clearance approximately 0.05–0.15 mm at room temperature; leakage gap after thermal expansion ≤0.10–0.20 mm | Circular platforms limit powder dispersion and simplify contained handling. Small modular systems obtain their powder savings by restricting the entire filled cross-section, not merely by placing a small plate in a large bed. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000050) | Make sleeve, plate and seal a removable build module. Calculate hot clearance using the actual sleeve and plate coefficients of expansion; add an annular overflow collection groove outside the seal. |
| 6 | Dual-zone temperature measurement and control | At least two embedded K- or N-type thermocouples under the Ø50 mm plate plus one cold-side safety sensor; control stability ±5 °C; independent hard trip | A cartridge-heated tungsten module controlled temperature within ±5 °C using two central K-type thermocouples. Inductive TiAl heating sustained 800 °C for 450 min with approximately ±10 °C oscillation. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000016) | Calibrate sensors in the assembled cassette. Place control sensors at center and near the edge; use a separate thermocouple and safety relay for over-temperature shutdown. An IR camera is useful for validation but requires emissivity correction. |
| 7 | Verify plate-temperature uniformity before every alloy campaign | Target center-to-edge variation ≤±5 °C over Ø50 mm after a ≥30 min soak; map at ambient, 170, 300 and 500 °C | A 30 min dwell was used to improve consistency in a custom heated bed, and ±5 °C was its switching criterion. High-temperature systems use IR-camera monitoring because a single underside thermocouple does not prove surface uniformity. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000023) | Validate with a temporary multi-thermocouple calibration plate or calibrated IR camera. Store the uniformity map and thermal-expansion correction with each cassette’s machine-readable ID. |
| 8 | Vacuum-purgeable, low-oxygen argon chamber | Demonstration minimum: O₂ <200 ppm; preferred aluminium screening target before exposure: <100 ppm and stable; trend dew point or moisture independently | The EOS high-temperature TiAl system maintained oxygen below 200 ppm with gas purification. Tungsten work showed that residual oxygen at 600 °C rapidly formed blue WO₃, causing poor wetting and balling. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040) | Position the O₂ sensor in a cooled bypass rather than above the hot plate. Interlock laser and heater against oxygen, flow and chamber-pressure limits; log peak O₂ during recoating and heating. |
| 9 | Quick-swap sealed hopper cartridges | Nominal useful capacities about 25, 50 and 100 cm³; residual powder after removal <1% of cartridge capacity; keyed identity and sealed valves | Research systems use accessible rotating reservoirs and can operate with as many as four powders; rapid reservoir replacement reduces contamination and material demand. Industrial powder changes can otherwise require up to two days. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000050) | Use identical docking geometry with interchangeable cartridge bodies. Include RFID or barcode identification, tare mass, lot number, alloy lockout and a normally closed bottom slot or metering valve. Validate repeatability gravimetrically for each powder. |
| 10 | Servo ball-screw recoater with programmable dosing gap | Layer settings 20–100 µm; Z/recoater positioning resolution ≤2–5 µm; gap repeatability ≤5 µm; programmable speed and torque/force trip | Powder delivery is volumetric and layer consistency depends strongly on the wiper-to-bed datum. A modular system deposited 50 µm layers and recoated in about 5 s. (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000050) | Provide interchangeable soft polymer, ceramic and hardened-metal blades. Record motor current or force to detect caking, protrusions and collisions, and use a bed image before and after every pass. |
| 11 | Right-size the dose per layer rather than dumping a fixed 1 cm³ | Ø50 mm × 50 µm geometric layer volume = 0.098 cm³; initial commanded dose approximately 0.2–0.4 cm³ including packing and overflow; 1 cm³ should be an upper calibration range, not the normal minimum | A Ø40 mm system required about 628 mm³ to fill a 0.5 mm-high bed, consistent with simple area × height geometry, and approximately 791 mm³ after accounting for consolidation into parts. (pqac-00000050) | Calibrate delivered mass versus actuator motion for every powder. Dose only the quantity needed to fill the layer plus a measured safety factor; collect and weigh overflow to close the powder mass balance. |
| 12 | Temperature-compatible piston sealing | Keep elastomeric dynamic seals on the cold side <100–150 °C; use PTFE-based seals only in cooled regions; use graphite, metal labyrinth or replaceable clearance seals adjacent to a 500 °C sleeve | High-temperature LPBF prototypes protect ordinary mechanics by cooling them below 100 °C rather than exposing polymer seals to the bed temperature. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020) | Do not interpret generic silicone or PTFE material limits as acceptable dynamic-seal temperatures in oxygen-sensitive powder service. Prefer a hot-side labyrinth/graphite wiper backed by a cooled, conventional vacuum seal. |
| 13 | Plate material matched to experiment | Aluminium-alloy plate for routine Al builds; plate thickness about 8–15 mm for Ø50 mm; optional Ti-6Al-4V or steel/susceptor cassette for >500 °C induction work | Using compatible Al substrates improves thermal and metallurgical similarity for Al screening. The induction prototype selected Ti-6Al-4V because its electrical and thermal behavior allowed effective high-temperature coupling; custom tungsten work used a 5 mm W plate. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000019) | Maintain separate, serialized plate families to prevent cross-contamination. Verify that the plate alloy does not bias grain-refiner chemistry or dilute first-layer composition. |
| 14 | Robot-compatible plate/build-module removal | Repeatable external grasp features; positive lock state; plate/module mass preferably <5 kg; presence confirmation by two independent sensors | Airlocks and rapid-transfer ports shorten plate exchange, while zero-point clamping and common robotic interfaces support automated LPBF-to-post-processing transfer. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000048) | Prefer a mechanically latched kinematic coupling over magnetic retention alone. Add fiducials, a temperature-permissive signal, load-cell confirmation of pickup and a sealed transport canister for reactive powder. |
| 15 | Closed powder recovery and sieving | Sealed recovery bottle; inert or enclosed sieve matched to feedstock, commonly 63 µm for nominal 15–45 µm powder; recovered, overflow and waste masses logged separately | Automated sieving and recirculation reduce operator exposure and handling time. Multi-material recovery may require separation by size, density or magnetic response and is not automatically contamination-free. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000050) | For novel alloys, default to quarantine rather than immediate reuse. Sample recovered powder for O, H, particle-size distribution and chemistry; never combine different experiments without analytical release. |
| 16 | Removable condensate liners and exchangeable window cover | Tool-less chamber liners; sacrificial optical cover slide or carousel with at least 3–10 indexed positions; transmission check before each build | High-temperature and laser processes create spatter, nanoparticles and volatile condensate; optical contamination threatens fixed-scanner repeatability, while fast-change interfaces reduce downtime. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025) | Use an angled, cooled cover slide and monitor laser transmission with a reference photodiode. Treat Al/Mg-rich condensate as reactive combustible waste; remove it using grounded tools or closed HEPA extraction, never dry compressed air. |
| 17 | Chamber geometry designed for automated cleaning | Full line-of-sight access to bed, overflow, gas ducts and window shield; removable smooth liners; internal corner radius ≥5 mm; no blind powder traps | Powder replacement can dominate changeover time, and automatic handling is valuable only if residual powder and soot can be verified absent. Automated powder systems and rapid-transfer concepts specifically target this downtime. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025) | Provide grounded vacuum ports, wipeable surfaces and replaceable gas-flow straighteners. Use cameras, differential pressure, gravimetry and particle-count or swab verification before releasing a different alloy. |
| 18 | Optional multi-material feeder module | Two to four independently removable reservoirs; composition changes by discrete layers initially; changeover target ≤3 min after validation | A modular system handled up to four powder types, changed accessible reservoirs in under 3 min and demonstrated alternating 316L/bronze layers. Double-hopper/mixing and ultrasonic local-deposition concepts also exist. (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000050) | Keep multi-material hardware optional because it greatly increases cross-contamination risk. Begin with whole-layer changes; add local deposition only after proving cleaning, dosing and composition uniformity. |
| 19 | Machine API, provenance and autonomous-lab interface | Timestamped API for plate ID, hopper ID, O₂, pressure, flow, temperatures, recoater current, layer images, laser job and interlocks; command acknowledgement and state confirmation for every step | Proposed autonomous AM laboratories combine multi-hopper printing, robotic transfer, heat treatment and automated characterization using equipment APIs, safety interlocks and supervisory agents. (pqac-00000028, pqac-00000030, pqac-00000031) | Use a deterministic state machine beneath any AI scheduler. Expose read-only telemetry broadly but restrict hazardous commands; require physical interlocks, signed recipes, audit trails and human-defined stopping conditions. |
| 20 | Require production-machine correlation builds | At least three matched coupons per alloy on Ø25/50 mm and production-scale plates; match layer time, scan strategy, gas-flow direction and thermal history; predefined equivalence limits for density, crack density, grain size and hardness | Small beds change plate thermal mass, lateral heat loss, inter-layer time and part spacing. AlSi10Mg preheating creates build-height/time-dependent ageing, and changing local thermal history alters microstructure; therefore small-plate results are screening evidence, not automatic production qualification. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000032) | Include thermal witness coupons and vary specimen spacing deliberately. Transfer promising parameters using melt-pool dimensions and measured thermal history, then re-optimize on the production machine rather than relying only on nominal volumetric energy density. |
