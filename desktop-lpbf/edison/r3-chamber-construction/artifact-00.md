| **1. Monolithic 6061-T6 pocket** | Best intrinsic construction: no body seam; only lid, door, ports and feedthroughs can leak. A sound FKM face seal should easily support 1 mbar evacuation and +5–20 mbar gauge operation. Positive pressure suppresses convective air ingress; residual ingress is mainly defects, permeation and opening cycles. | Approximately **6.38 L** geometric volume before internal hardware. Pumping to 1 mbar leaves about **210 ppm O₂** before backfill; one more ideal evacuation/backfill cycle gives about **0.21 ppm**, so <100 ppm should normally take **one pumpdown plus several minutes of mixing/argon flow (est.)**, provided there is no trapped air. Water—not O₂—is the slow residual species. Published clean-metal water outgassing is approximately `3×10⁻⁹/t` mbar·L·s⁻¹·cm⁻², with `t` in hours; baked Al hydrogen outgassing can be about `5.5×10⁻¹² Pa·L·s⁻¹·cm⁻²` (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000005). | Excellent before stress release; expect movement after removing ~78% of the billet. Rough, unclamp/age, then finish both sealing faces. **0.03–0.08 mm over 300 mm (est.)** is realistic for a careful shop. | **CAM 20–40 h; machine/setup 35–70 h; inspection/deburr 6–12 h (est.)**. A few hundred hours is credible only for the *whole machine*, not this body alone. | **US$1.5k–4.5k (est.)** including billet, 150 mm-reach cutters/holders, machine time and scrap risk; substantially more at commercial shop rates. | Mechanically robust and simplest to leak-test, but highest one-part scrap consequence. The 120 mm depth plus R5 corner forces a ≤10 mm finishing tool at roughly 12:1 reach/diameter, making chatter, taper, wall error and chip evacuation the dominant risks. |
| **2. Through-frame from thick plate, bolted O-ring floor** | Nearly as good as monolithic if the floor uses one continuous face O-ring and adequate bolt spacing. One additional static seal is a small practical penalty; avoid fasteners penetrating into atmosphere without sealing. A helium leak target of **≤10⁻⁶ mbar·L/s for the complete assembly (recommended)** is ample for this application and far less demanding than welded accelerator chambers reported below `3×10⁻⁹ mbar·L/s` (pqac-00000009). | Similar clean-metal behavior to option 1. The second gland adds little purge burden, but blind tapped holes, saw pockets and rough waterjet texture exposed to the chamber can retain water or powder; finish the bore and vent blind volumes. **<100 ppm in roughly 5–15 min after reaching 1 mbar and backfilling/mixing (est.)**. | Both frame faces can be face-milled in simple setups. **0.03–0.08 mm over 300 mm (est.)** is readily achievable; the separate floor can be lapped or face-milled and replaced if damaged. | Core removal: **bandsaw/drill 3–8 h**, waterjet **1–3 h**, or wire EDM **12–30 unattended h (est.)**; CNC machining **15–30 h**, CAM **8–16 h**, inspection/assembly **5–10 h (est.)**. | **US$1.0k–3.0k (est.)**; waterjet or bandsaw is usually cheaper than wire EDM. The recovered core may have useful stock value rather than becoming chips. | **Best university-shop compromise.** No weld qualification, ordinary-length tools can enter from both sides, damaged floor is replaceable, and there is only one extra seal. Preserve a continuous gland land around the front-door cutout and provide dowels so repeated assembly does not fret the O-ring face. |
| **3. TIG-welded box; 6061, 5083, or 304L/316L; post-machined** | Proper full-penetration, continuous vacuum welds can greatly exceed the requirement. A6061-T651 accelerator chambers assembled from machined plates by AC-TIG reportedly achieved leakage below **`3×10⁻⁹ mbar·L/s`**; the same work reported machining deformation below **0.1 mm** and weld deformation around **±0.3 mm** on much larger chambers (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010). Every weld must be accessible for visual and helium testing; intermittent structural welds are unsuitable. | Weld oxide, crevices, filler contamination and shop oils can delay water cleanup unless pickled/cleaned. Clean Al and stainless behave similarly for unbaked water removal, while measured baked Al hydrogen outgassing was much lower than untreated stainless in one controlled comparison (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002). **10–30 min to <100 ppm after 1 mbar evacuation/backfill (est.)**, longer after humid exposure or inadequate cleaning. | Weld first with machining allowance, stress-relieve/season if appropriate, then machine lid, floor datum, door and port pads. Raw small-box distortion **0.5–2 mm (est.)** is plausible; post-machined **0.05–0.10 mm over 300 mm (est.)**. TPS practice used symmetric welding, fixturing and tack sequences to reduce residual stress (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011). | Plate preparation/CAM **8–16 h**, fixture and TIG **10–20 h**, cleaning/leak repair **4–12 h**, post-weld machining **15–30 h (est.)**. | Al: **US$1.5k–4.0k (est.)**; stainless: **US$2.5k–6.0k (est.)**, driven by qualified welding and post-machining rather than plate price. | Commercially orthodox and very robust when made by an experienced vacuum welder. **5083** is easiest to weld and retains useful as-welded strength; **6061-T6** loses temper in the HAZ and has cracking sensitivity; **304L/316L** is cleanable and weld-friendly but heavy, slower to machine and thermally conductive less well. Primary university risk is achieving leak-tight welds without repeated repair and distortion. |
| **4. Heavy-wall rectangular Al extrusion with bolted/clamped end plates** | Seamless extrusion removes four longitudinal joints; two continuous end O-rings give predictable sealing. Extruded straight Al vacuum chambers are an established accelerator construction, with ports and end components subsequently welded on (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000015). Leak performance should resemble option 2 if end faces are machined square. | Extruded Al is suitable for vacuum service after detergent cleaning and thorough drying; avoid porous anodizing on interior surfaces. Two end seals have negligible impact relative to doors and feedthroughs. **5–15 min to <100 ppm after pump/backfill (est.)**. | Extrusions can have twist, bow and wall-thickness variation. Machine both ends in one datum scheme and use thick, stiff plates. **0.05–0.15 mm end-face flatness (est.)** after machining; gland should preferably be in the removable plates. | If suitable stock exists: CAM **5–10 h**, sawing/squaring **3–6 h**, plates and ports **10–20 h (est.)**. A custom extrusion shifts time from machining to procurement and die development. | Stock profile: **US$0.8k–2.5k (est.)**. Custom heavy-wall die plus minimum run: approximately **US$5k–15k+ (est.)**, making one-off use unattractive. | Excellent repeatability and low part count **if a close stock size exists**. The principal problem is procurement: an approximately 312 × 222 mm outside profile with 16 mm walls is unlikely to be a stocked structural tube. Thin commercial tube may buckle under external atmospheric pressure unless analyzed and stiffened. |
| **5. Bolted box of separate plates with O-rings in every joint** | Straight two-surface joints can seal, but ordinary O-rings cannot simply terminate at corners. Three-way junctions require molded gaskets, dovetailed/interlocking glands, bonded scarf joints, corner blocks, or a seal topology that makes every O-ring a continuous closed loop. More seals, bolts and handling interfaces mean more opportunities for dust damage and virtual leaks. | Large total elastomer length, corner cavities and blind fasteners increase retained air and permeation. With careful venting and clean glands, **10–30 min to <100 ppm (est.)**; poorly designed corner junctions may never stabilize below 100 ppm without continuous argon bleed. | Individual plates are easy to machine flat—**0.03–0.08 mm (est.)**—but assembled squareness and uniform compression depend on dowels, bolt pitch and plate stiffness. Each maintenance cycle can alter alignment. | CAM **8–15 h**, plate machining **15–30 h**, gasket development/assembly/leak debugging **10–30 h (est.)**. Geometrically simple, but seal-detail engineering consumes time. | **US$0.8k–2.5k initially (est.)**; custom molded gasket or repeated leak-debugging can erase the saving. | Attractive for modular experiments but the least forgiving long-term LPBF chamber: powder contaminates numerous glands, fasteners loosen under thermal cycles, and three-way corners are failure-prone. Prefer welded corner rails or one-piece frame elements so every gasket remains a planar closed loop. |
| **6. Buy commercial rough-vacuum/pressure chamber and modify** | Lowest leak risk when the supplier provides a pressure rating and helium-leak certificate. MIT’s research HPLM testbed used a custom **Parr Instrument** chamber rather than fabricating the pressure boundary in-house; its cylindrical bore was 2.5 × 6.5 in and rated to 1900 psig (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000022). Modifications must be welded and re-tested; unqualified drilled NPT or window changes can invalidate the rating. | Commercial electropolished stainless or properly cleaned Al gives predictable pumpdown. Existing unused ports and double-wall penetrations can create trapped volumes. **5–20 min to <100 ppm after pump/backfill (est.)**, assuming the geometry permits good mixing. NIST’s AMMT instead uses a larger vacuum chamber with an internal plexiglass flow enclosure, illustrating that the pressure vessel and optimized gas-flow enclosure can be separate (pqac-00000008). | Supplier flange faces are commonly the best controlled. Modified mounting datums may still need a separate precision internal tooling plate rather than machining the vessel. | Vendor integration/CAD **10–30 h**, local adapters and internal frame **15–40 h**, chamber modification **5–20 h (est.)**; least in-house pressure-boundary work. | Used standard chamber **US$2k–8k (est.)**; new custom chamber **US$8k–25k+ (est.)**, plus windows, certified modifications and freight. | Lowest fabrication uncertainty and easiest safety review, but often poorest fit to a rectangular recoater, door and cross-flow geometry. Best when schedule and safety assurance outweigh compactness; preserve the certified shell and place LPBF-specific glands, slots and mechanisms on removable internal plates or vendor-designed ports. |
