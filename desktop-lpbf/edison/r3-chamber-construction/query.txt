We are designing a small, lab-built laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) machine for aluminium alloy screening. Its process chamber is a box with internal dimensions 280 x 190 x 120 mm (16 mm walls, 20 mm floor), a 16 mm lid sealed by an FKM O-ring face gland, a drop-down front door (220 x 92 mm opening), cross-flow gas slots and KF ports. The chamber is pumped to about 1 mbar with a dry scroll pump, then back-filled with argon and held at +5 to +20 mbar gauge with O2 below 100 ppm (ideally below 50 ppm) and a 200 C heated build plate inside. The current design machines the body from one 13 x 9.5 x 6 in. 6061-T6 block (about 78 percent of the block becomes chips, a 120 mm deep pocket needing 6 in. long end mills, R5 corners).

A university machinist says the deep pocket is the part he is most worried about: expensive, hard to find tooling that long, and that the whole machine would be a few hundred hours of CAM programming and machining. He also notes the CAM plan assumed 5-axis operations, but he thinks the features could be done on a 4-axis (rotary) mill.

Please compare, with sources, the construction options for a chamber like this, for leak rate and O2 ingress at +5 to +20 mbar argon, outgassing and water vapour (time to reach less than 100 ppm O2 after a purge), distortion and flatness of sealing faces, machining time and tooling, cost, and robustness for a university shop:
1. monolithic pocket machined from a block (as now);
2. a through-frame (walls only, open top and bottom) cut from thick plate, with the core removed by drilling and band-sawing, waterjet or wire EDM rather than milling to chips, and a bolted floor plate with a second O-ring face seal;
3. a welded box from 6061, 5083 or 304L/316L plate (TIG), machined after welding, as vacuum-chamber makers build them;
4. a length of heavy-wall extruded aluminium rectangular tube with clamped or bolted end plates (the glands in the plates);
5. a bolted box of separate plates with O-rings in every joint (and the problem of three-way O-ring junctions);
6. buying a commercial rough-vacuum or glovebox-style chamber and modifying it.
Also: what do commercial small LPBF machines (for example Sharebot MetalONE, Xact Metal XM200, One Click Metal, Aconity MINI/MIDI, AO Metal, Lasefinity, Farsoon, the open-architecture research machines such as those from ORNL, NIST AMMT, or university builds) use for their build chambers; rules of thumb for deep pockets (length-to-diameter limits, corner radii, long-reach tool deflection), how much 3+2 or 4th-axis indexing actually helps for a box like this, and the honest machining time a machinist would quote for each option. Give concrete numbers where the literature or trade sources have them and say clearly where you are estimating.
