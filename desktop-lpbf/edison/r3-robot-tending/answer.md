Question: We are checking whether a UR12e collaborative arm (the renamed UR10e: 12.5 kg payload, 1300 mm reach) with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper and a wrist camera, riding on a MiR250 mobile base, can tend a small lab-built laser powder bed fusion machine that sits on a vertical lift module tray: open and close a drop-down chamber door sealed by an O-ring, exchange a 25 to 75 mm heated (200 C) build plate held by a bayonet or a drawbar, swap powder reservoirs and recoater blade cartridges, pull a cover-slide drawer, empty an overflow jar, lift out chamber liners for cleaning, and exchange a 2.5 kg build module that leaves through the chamber floor. We also want to know whether two such mobile arms could do the machine's assembly and the machine tending of a Haas VF-series CNC mill to make its parts.

Please summarise, with sources: (1) published and industrial examples of robotic or automated LPBF tending (build plate and build module exchange, depowdering, unpacking, sieving, zero-point clamping and plate interfaces, door and hatch actuation, cobots vs industrial robots, mobile manipulators) and what made them work or fail; (2) mobile manipulator accuracy: MiR docking repeatability, the need for fiducial or camera re-registration, and achievable placement accuracy for peg-in-hole and zero-point engagement; (3) end-of-arm tooling for this kind of work: tool changers, custom fingertips, vacuum cups, compliance units, and how to design machine features (handles, lead-ins, chamfers, kinematic couplings, detents) so a cobot can work them; (4) force limits: how much force a UR10e/UR12e can apply in force mode and how designs avoid needing more (pneumatic clamps, toggle latches pulled by actuators, gas struts); (5) robotic CNC machine tending with cobots: loading vises and pneumatic fixtures, payload limits, chip and coolant problems, door opening, and what fraction of a prototype job's operations are realistic; (6) collision checking and motion planning practice for these cells (MoveIt, FCL, swept volumes, safety zones). Give concrete numbers and say clearly where you are estimating.

# Feasibility assessment: two mobile cobots for LPBF and CNC tending

**Bottom line.** The proposed UR12e-class arm on a MiR250 can plausibly perform *engineered, repeatable exchanges* of the small printer’s plates, cartridges, drawers, jars and 2.5 kg module. It should not be expected to operate an as-built machine with human-style latches, exposed 200 °C parts, loose powder and tight unregistered fits. Two such robots could automate selected assembly steps and repetitive Haas VF-series loading, but the evidence does **not** support a claim that they could autonomously build the whole printer or execute an arbitrary prototype machining job. These are engineering judgments, not measured success rates for the proposed cell. (pereira2021advancesinmultiprocess pages 4-7, pereira2021advancesinmultiprocess pages 2-4, scamarcio2025reliableandrobust pages 3-6, bonifazio2024automatedcncloadingunloading pages 13-18)

## 1. What LPBF automation has actually demonstrated

| Example | What worked—and what it does not establish |
|---|---|
| **HyProCell: ADIRA LPBF, EROWA robot and Haas VF-2.** Its LPBF machine gained a robot-access door, seals and automated in-chamber vacuuming. An EROWA ERD 500 transferred pallets among the printer, SOLUKON powder-removal station, magazine and Haas VF-2; a dust box contained powder during transfers. Substrate plates were **screwed to a common handling pallet**, rather than being grasped by their printed edges. The smallest CNC chamber constrained pallet size to **400 × 500 mm**. A dedicated telescopic handler accommodated loads up to **300 kg**; the reported **2 µm** is precision *after its dedicated clamp engages*, not free-space robot accuracy. A roughly **20 m** integrated layout was impractical, so scanning remained outside the physical cell. This is the closest published printer-to-Haas analogue, but uses substantial fixed industrial handling equipment, not a mobile cobot. | (pereira2021advancesinmultiprocess pages 4-7, pereira2021advancesinmultiprocess pages 2-4) |

The described three-module printer has a **420 × 420 × 400 mm** build envelope. The account does not specify the gripper, latch geometry or a measured exchange success rate. | (bakhshifarkoush2024investigatingtheeffects pages 42-47) |
| **LPBF-to-CNC zero-point interface.** Boivie and colleagues put EROWA chucks in an LPBF machine and CNC mill. Their PowerChuck P uses a **10,000 N spring clamp**, pneumatic release and **0.002 mm manufacturer-specified** pallet repeatability. Milling trials found adequate stability, but coolant migrated beneath the pallet: the interface had to be cleaned before returning to LPBF. This is precisely why the *machine fixture*, not the robot, must establish the printing and machining datums. | (boivie2011developmentofa pages 8-10, boivie2011developmentofa pages 10-11) |
| **Alternative part interfaces.** Ferchow and colleagues validated LPBF-integrated sacrificial bolts that act as gripping/machining datums: **250 N** produced at most **12.5 µm** displacement in one test. Their separate sheet-metal carrier reduced re-clamped deformation but measured part-position deviations up to **0.23 mm**. Even excellent fixture repeatability does not remove printed-part deformation or recoater-clearance concerns. | (ferchow2021designandvalidation pages 1-2, ferchow2022designandvalidation pages 13-15, ferchow2021designandvalidation pages 14-16) |

**Powder work is a separate automation problem.** Commercial examples combine closed powder circulation, vacuum transfer, machine-side unpacking and programmed depowdering—not an ordinary gripper carrying an open jar. ADIRA’s described system used four vacuum conveying systems; HyProCell combined in-machine vacuuming with part-specific SOLUKON programs. Automated sieving and recirculation exist, but recovery must track alloy, batch, reuse, contamination and environmental exposure. A studied semi-automatic unpacker found that internal cavities and intervening part walls obstruct vacuum removal; adding vacuum points showed diminishing benefit beyond four in its simulated arrangement. Fine metal powders present exposure, contamination and, for applicable alloys, fire/explosion hazards. Qualify sealed reservoirs, contained overflow disposal and powder-specific cleaning with the actual alloy safety data; do not infer that a standard wrist camera, vacuum cup or cobot is suitable inside a contaminated chamber. ## 2. Docking, registration and achievable insertion

**Do not equate navigation, docking, arm repeatability and finished placement.** In a *MiR250-based* laboratory manipulator, reported base odometry precision was approximately **±50 mm**; typical vision/SLAM positioning was described as approximately **±10 mm**. The researchers consequently navigated to a station, localized a station marker, imaged an instrument marker with a wrist camera and touched instrument reference faces to register its frame. Their resulting plate handover precision was **±1.2 mm**; this is a *whole-system instrument-relative result*, not a MiR250 docking-repeatability specification or a result for the proposed UR12e. An independent **non-MiR** fiducial-docking study reported, over **1,500** trials, position-distribution ranges of **8.4 mm in x and 4.7 mm in y**, versus **61.9 and 8.5 mm** for navigation alone; these are ranges, not guaranteed absolute errors. A NIST mobile-manipulator calibration study still reported approximately **1.19 mm mean-square residual** and demonstrated that end-effectors could get within a few millimetres of targets after base-to-arm registration. (scamarcio2025reliableandrobust pages 3-6, bian2023fimareaderacosteffective pages 10-11, scamarcio2025reliableandrobust pages 8-9, bostelman2015performancemeasurementof pages 137-140)

**Design target, explicitly an estimate:** make the MiR park repeatably enough to see a *machine-mounted* datum; calibrate camera-to-tool and arm-to-base transforms; re-observe that datum **after every arrival and after every vertical-lift move**; and use guarded, force-limited final insertion into lead-ins. Budget approximately **1–3 mm instrument-relative approach error as an initial development target**, to be measured across stations, lift heights, temperatures and loaded arms—not as a manufacturer guarantee. Reserve **submillimetre final seating** for passive datums/zero-point clamps plus verified closure, not visual open-loop positioning. As evidence that insertion tolerance can exceed free-space pose accuracy, a UR5 study inserted a **15.97 mm** unchamfered peg into a **16.07 mm** unchamfered hole using a specialized compliant search; success depended on initial offset and tilt, and one **5 mm** offset case failed where **2 mm** succeeded. It is not evidence that this MiR/UR/gripper combination can routinely insert a 0.1 mm-clearance pin. (boivie2011developmentofa pages 8-10, scamarcio2025reliableandrobust pages 3-6, bian2023fimareaderacosteffective pages 10-11, bostelman2015performancemeasurementof pages 137-140, wang2019aroboticpeginhole pages 9-11)

## 3. Design the printer and tools around the robot

The stated **12.5 kg arm payload and 1,300 mm reach** are useful screening inputs, but the **Robotiq 2F-85 is separately rated for 5 kg**: published specifications give **85 mm stroke**, **20–235 N programmable grip force** and about **0.9 kg gripper mass**. Include camera, changer, fingers, wiring, carried part, centre-of-gravity offset and acceleration in the *robot* payload calculation; do not interpret 235 N jaw force as a 235 N capacity for pulling a stuck door. A design study found that tool mass and a **260 mm** flange offset reduced usable payload substantially, illustrating why reach and wrist moment must be checked in each posture. For the **2.5 kg** module, nominal mass alone passes the gripper’s 5 kg rating, but an open powder container, awkward grasp, dynamic moment or hot/smooth surface can still make the grasp unsuitable. (pylarinos2018tilebot.developmentof pages 210-218, roozbahani2026amodularperceptionbased pages 5-7)

Provide a positively retained **cool-side metal handling tab or insulated carrier** for each plate; put printed parts and the plate in a tray or sealed transport cassette. A plate directly at **200 °C** requires a qualified thermal barrier, gripper-finger material and dwell-time test: neither the cited 2F-85 data nor the vacuum-gripper study establishes 200 °C operation. If using suction on *cool, clean* flat panels, provide a dedicated vacuum circuit, nonporous land, pressure confirmation and a mechanical drop catch; powder, rough surfaces and heat make suction alone a poor default. Use a changer with positively sensed tool-lock and available electrical/pneumatic pass-throughs for separate jaw, broad-plate, module-hook and cleaning tools. These are proposed design controls, not documented capabilities of the specified off-the-shelf gripper. (pylarinos2018tilebot.developmentof pages 210-218, roozbahani2026amodularperceptionbased pages 5-7, hajzargarbashi2025collaborativeroboticfinishing pages 9-10)

For the **drop-down O-ring door**, put its operating handle outside the door’s swept arc; support its weight with a hinge counterbalance/gas strut or powered actuator, and use mechanically indexed latches or pneumatic compression rather than asking the wrist to develop seal preload. Provide door-open/closed and seal/latch sensors, and prevent the door from obstructing the floor-module extraction path. For **25–75 mm plates**, standardize the *carrier* datum and robot pickup location even if plate diameters change: use an indexed bayonet driven by a dedicated keyed tool only if its seating torque and powder tolerance are tested; a pneumatically released drawbar or zero-point chuck moves the high preload into the fixture. Design each **reservoir, recoater cartridge, cover-slide drawer, overflow jar and removable liner** with a generous grasp tab, a prescribed single extraction direction, captive detents, tapered entry/lead-ins and observable seated/unseated states. Add locating cones/kinematic features where repeatability matters, keep powder off reference lands, and support drawers and liners so extraction does not bind. These are application-specific recommendations derived from demonstrated benefits of common pallets, positive fixture datums, compliant insertion and contamination control—not published validation of the proposed printer. (pereira2021advancesinmultiprocess pages 4-7, boivie2011developmentofa pages 8-10, ferchow2021designandvalidation pages 1-2, tiansoon1997acompliantendeffector pages 2-4, wang2019aroboticpeginhole pages 9-11)

**Force budget:** a published specification for the *UR10e* gives force-mode control up to **100 N**, nominal **5.5 N** force accuracy. In actual surface-finishing experiments, **100 N** was commanded, but mean absolute tracking errors were about **10.6–12.5 N** in direct-contact tests; rapidly changing contact could briefly lose contact or overshoot. Treat **100 N as documented UR10e force-mode capability, not a guaranteed UR12e force at every posture, door-opening rating, safe human-contact limit or justification to pry a jammed mechanism**. Set substantially lower task-specific search limits and verify them experimentally. A pneumatic robotic CNC vise illustrates the alternative: at **6 bar**, its lever-assisted actuator produced about **5.1 kN** clamping force with **0.35 s** clamp time, while the robot only loaded the part. A sprung latch, counterbalanced door, powered clamp and instrumented release similarly separate manipulation force from process force. Safety contact limits require their own risk assessment: constrained pinch contacts are particularly hazardous even with a cobot. (gadringer2022assessmentofforce pages 6-8, gadringer2022assessmentofforce pages 2-4, farcasUnknownyearmodularpneumaticvise pages 3-4, herbster2023modelingthecontact pages 1-2)

## 4. Haas tending and two-robot assembly

**Realistic CNC scope:** automate loading a known blank or standardized pallet, actuating an *interlocked* powered door and pneumatic vise, checking part and clamp presence, calling an already proved CNC program, unloading a part, and performing a defined cleaning/probing sequence. A robotic vise demonstrator used CNC-door/robot/clamp interlocks and reported approximately **±0.01 mm** repeatability of its fixture resets; a Haas-containing LPBF hybrid cell used a VF-2 and dedicated EROWA pallet transfer. Neither demonstrates that a MiR-mounted UR12e with a 2F-85 can operate an unmodified Haas door or hand-tighten a milling vise. A separate Haas-cell example moved **6–8 kg** castings with a **10 kg** KUKA robot and purpose-designed pneumatic three-jaw tool: these loads exceed the cited 2F-85’s **5 kg** rating. (farcasUnknownyearmodularpneumaticvise pages 3-4, arunachalam2025optimizationofthe pages 5-10, pereira2021advancesinmultiprocess pages 4-7)

**Expect chips, coolant and setup to dominate reliability.** In one CNC robotic-loading implementation, chips caused imperfect part seating, washing-emulsion viscosity changed cleaning effectiveness, and slight vise-height differences upset alignment. Wrong part placement and incorrect robot recipes caused collisions and manual resets; its approximately **12-minute** unload/clean/load cycle was reported, while **140 operating hours per week was an objective, not an immediately achieved result**. For a *prototype* printer, engineers must still generate and prove CAM, set tooling and work offsets, design fixture jaws for changing parts, inspect first articles and correct unexpected burrs or chips. **Estimated scope—not a published percentage:** a deliberately palletized, repetitive family might automate *most repeated load/unload cycles*, but an arbitrary one-off prototype may have only **roughly 10–30% of its distinct setup/assembly operations** readily robotizable without further fixtures and engineering. Do not present that illustrative planning range as observed throughput, machine utilization or fraction of machining time. (bonifazio2024automatedcncloadingunloading pages 13-18, bonifazio2024automatedcncloadingunloading pages 18-26)

For building the printer itself, two mobile arms may stage machined panels, hold a bracket while a second robot drives an accessible fastener, place standardized modules and perform prescribed functional checks. Neither a two-robot label nor simultaneous reach solves fine cable routing, optical/laser alignment, sealed gas connections, O-ring inspection, torque verification, precision recoater calibration or process qualification. Sequence a **single arm first** on one printer fixture and one CNC fixture; use the second only where the task demonstrably needs a supporting hand or parallel servicing. Two robots introduce shared-space collision, coordination and recovery problems. A published dual-UR10e system explicitly coordinated their shared workspace with joint planning, scene updates and replanning; its application was battery disassembly, not proof of fully automatic printer assembly. (horstkotte2021developmentofhandling pages 3-4, horstkotte2021generationandevaluation pages 3-4, shaarawy2026hybridtaskand pages 5-6)

## 5. Collision planning and safety practice

Create dimensioned CAD/URDF collision geometry for **both complete mobile manipulators**, including actual tools, camera, cable dress and each grasped plate/module as an attached object. In MoveIt’s planning scene, represent the printer enclosure, vertical-lift tray at *each commanded elevation*, door and drawer at *each commanded state*, Haas enclosure, fixture and tool rack. Check robot self-collision, robot–robot collision and all intermediate poses; check or conservatively approximate **swept volumes** of the door, drawer, trays, carried parts and arms, since checking only pickup and drop-off poses misses intervening collisions. Restrict arm movements to a verified parked-base pose and block navigation while an arm or machine aperture protrudes into a travel corridor. MoveIt/FCL has been demonstrated for two UR10e arms, with updated environmental depth data represented at **30 mm voxel** resolution; that is useful for broad obstacle avoidance but far too coarse to validate a plate edge or tight latch. A published UR/2F-85 MoveIt setup likewise explicitly models the gripper and worktable; custom dimensions and dynamic aperture states must be added for this cell. (shaarawy2026hybridtaskand pages 5-6, behrens2022integratedtaskand pages 29-32, roozbahani2026amodularperceptionbased pages 13-15)

**Planning is not safeguarding.** Apply a cell-level risk assessment and independent machine/safety-controller interlocks for laser enable, inert-gas/powder conditions, temperature, printer and CNC doors, spindle stopped, fixture clamped, robot/base stationary, vertical-lift position and human access. Use appropriate guarding or safety-rated zones/stops around hot surfaces, falling modules, door pinch points, moving cutters and combustible or inhalable powder; a cobot’s power-and-force-limiting mode does not make these process hazards collaborative. Test failure and recovery states—including loss of power/air, dropped plate, incomplete clamp, open powder jar and an obstructed door—before unattended runs. (gomes2023analysisanddevelopmenta pages 44-48, farcasUnknownyearmodularpneumaticvise pages 3-4, herbster2023modelingthecontact pages 1-2, hajzargarbashi2025collaborativeroboticfinishing pages 9-10, othman2026investigationofnovel pages 46-52)

**Decision gate:** demonstrate repeated station-to-fixture registration, force-limited insertion, retained handling of a thermally representative plate, powder-contained jar/module exchange, and verified recovery from intentionally introduced mis-seating *before* committing to unattended or dual-robot operation. The literature supplies useful analogues and component specifications, but no verified MiR250 docking guarantee, UR12e-specific maximum force-mode rating, or measured end-to-end success rate for the exact proposed configuration was established here. (scamarcio2025reliableandrobust pages 3-6, gadringer2022assessmentofforce pages 2-4, bian2023fimareaderacosteffective pages 10-11, bonifazio2024automatedcncloadingunloading pages 13-18)
