## Lessons Learned from Building, Commissioning and Operating Custom and Small-Format Laser Powder Bed Fusion Machines

### 1. Overview

A growing number of research groups have built or extensively modified laser powder bed fusion (LPBF / SLM) machines to gain access to process parameters, sensor data and diagnostics that proprietary commercial systems do not expose. The literature reveals a consistent set of technical challenges across custom-built open-architecture machines, miniature synchrotron replicators and small commercial research platforms. While explicit total build costs and construction timelines are rarely published, groups have reported specific component failures, design choices that required iteration, and practical operational findings. ### 2. Recoating and Powder Handling

Recoating is universally identified as one of the most troublesome subsystems. Heriot-Watt's open-architecture PBF system initially tested tungsten carbide spreaders in both knife-edge and cylindrical forms; in both cases the spreader motion was jammed by the build-up of particles between the rigid spreader and the build plate, so the group switched to a compliant 3 mm silicone cord (bidare2017anopenarchitecturemetal pages 3-5). The recoater speed (~10 mm/s) dominated the total build time of ~1.5 h for 5 mm cubes (bidare2017anopenarchitecturemetal pages 5-7). The TU Berlin synchrotron LPBF system used a gravity funnel dispenser with an elastomeric lip for levelling powder, but found that fine or cohesive powders could bridge and clog the funnel orifice; small vibration motors were added to restore flow (uhlmann2020alaserpowder pages 6-8). The LLNL testbed at SSRL initially relied on manual powder application, which produced uneven layers and spills on the glassy carbon windows, motivating development of an automated spreader (calta2018aninstrumentfor pages 4-5, calta2018aninstrumentfor pages 5-7). The Aconity Mini uses a manually loaded supplier volume with a rising piston and an interchangeable silicone wiper rake; this was described as simpler and more reliable than larger machine architectures (maddison2022insitumonitoringand pages 66-74, maddison2022insitumonitoringand pages 61-66). On the One Click Metal MPrint+, recoater collisions with spatter elevations, warped overhangs or powder buildup from poor heat dissipation can damage blades, delaminate parts or terminate builds prematurely (friedo2026developmentofintelligent pages 1-3).

### 3. Gas Purity and Atmosphere Control

Oxygen control thresholds varied across systems. NIST AMMT operated in an argon environment with oxygen below 500 ppm and laminar flow over the build plane at 300 L/min through a 100 mm × 20 mm nozzle cross-section, corresponding to a calculated maximum velocity of 2.5 m/s (lane2020processmonitoringdataset pages 1-4). The Heriot-Watt system used a 5 mm thick Perspex chamber sealed with a silicone gasket; filling was from the bottom for heavier-than-air shielding gas, and the oxygen concentration was reduced to below 0.5% using a Grove O₂ sensor, after which gas was switched to a cross-flow inlet (bidare2017anopenarchitecturemetal pages 2-3). The ESRF miniature LPBF system required three vacuum/argon purge cycles to reach below 100 ppm O₂, with continuous argon flow during experiments (lhuissier2022miniaturelaserpowder pages 3-4). The DTU systems used PID-controlled gas flow, measured by a thin-film anemometer, with a variable frequency drive coupled to a vortex flow pump (andersen2022aunifiedsystem pages 3-7). The DTU group also demonstrated experimentally that low gas flow velocities correlated with increased porosity; regions with insufficient cross-flow showed lower part quality, highlighting the importance of optimizing gas flow inlet geometry (kjer2023experimentalanalysisand pages 1-2, kjer2023experimentalanalysisand pages 2-4). At the LLNL testbed, the vacuum chamber was evacuated by a diaphragm/turbo molecular pump system and backfilled with argon through a flow control valve; a gas-species-independent pressure transducer was used for monitoring (calta2018aninstrumentfor pages 3-4). Exhaust gas at Heriot-Watt was run through a water bath to remove potentially harmful nanoparticles produced by the process (bidare2017anopenarchitecturemetal pages 2-3).

### 4. Laser Safety

Laser safety was handled differently depending on the deployment context. The LLNL testbed was designed as a light-tight, portable, Class 1 enclosed laser system, permitting convenient transportation to various beamlines (calta2018aninstrumentfor pages 3-4). The ESRF miniature LPBF system interlocked the laser source to the experimental hutch door so that no laser emission was possible when the door was open (lhuissier2022miniaturelaserpowder pages 7-9). At Heriot-Watt, the laser beam passed through an anti-reflection coated UV-grade fused silica window in the top of the Perspex shielding gas chamber, and an operator was stationed in a separate control room (bidare2017anopenarchitecturemetal pages 2-3). The Heriot-Watt system also used a folding mirror that blocked direct line of sight between the laser and the powder surface, though this design was motivated more by X-ray access than laser safety per se. All groups that published instrument papers implicitly or explicitly described their enclosures as meeting relevant Class 1 or Class 4 requirements.

### 5. Alignment, Calibration and Focus

Galvanometer scanner calibration is a recurring commissioning-intensive task. NIST developed and documented a quasi-static position calibration procedure for the AMMT, using a "mark and measure" approach: a 7 × 7 laser dot-grid pattern was created on a 100 mm × 100 mm black-anodized aluminum plate at ~15 W for 100 ms per point, and encoder signals were sampled at 100 kHz. The plate was scanned with a calibrated flatbed scanner at 4800 dpi (5.3 µm/pixel), and dot centers of mass were determined in ImageJ. The target was relative positioning accuracy better than 10⁻³ mm/mm, i.e. under 100 µm across the 100 mm field (lane2020quasistaticpositioncalibration pages 8-11, lane2020quasistaticpositioncalibration pages 5-8, lane2020quasistaticpositioncalibration pages 1-5). The AMMT laser beam profile was measured at the nominal galvo position with an Ophir-type profiling camera, yielding D4σ spot diameters of 60 µm (X) and 48 µm (Y) (lane2020processmonitoringdataset pages 4-6). At Heriot-Watt, the laser was focused to a 50 µm Gaussian spot measured with an Ophir Spiricon SP928 profiling camera, and the front spreader height was set with feeler gauges (0.5 µm per degree rotation of fine-pitch screws) between the spreader and build plate (bidare2017anopenarchitecturemetal pages 3-5). For the ESRF replicator, the focal distance was adjusted by vertically moving the scanning head and was checked by inspecting static laser shots on the build substrate when positioned at the lasing plane (lhuissier2022miniaturelaserpowder pages 3-4). The DTU group noted that their coaxial sensor baseline exhibited a non-uniform, ring-shaped intensity pattern attributed to spherical aberration in the optical path, which required achromatic corrections in the second-generation design (andersen2022aunifiedsystem pages 7-12).

### 6. Optical Component Failures

A notable failure occurred during NIST AMMT commissioning: after seven minutes at 60% laser power, the fused-silica folding mirror reached a measured 220.2 °C, with an estimated actual temperature closer to 275 °C when corrected for emissivity. Its low thermal conductivity caused localized heating and failure of the protected silver coating. The SiC scanner mirrors, in contrast, remained at 82.7 °C and 58.7 °C respectively, owing to their high thermal conductivity. NIST proposed a silicon substrate for the folding mirror, forced-air cooling for the scanner tower and improved baffling to manage scatter and back-reflections from the focusing lens (grantham2016opticaldesignand pages 8-9).

### 7. Software and Controls

Control software is frequently identified as a bottleneck. The DTU Open Additive Manufacturing Initiative developed a unified systems controller, implemented on an ARM Cortex-M4 microcontroller (Teensy 3.6), that coordinates laser power (10-bit DAC, 0.24 W resolution per increment for a 250 W laser), scanner position via the XY2-100 digital protocol at 2 MHz, gas flow, atmosphere monitoring and motor control. The controller uses direct memory access (DMA) for the 20 MHz XY2-100 output and a circular buffer for NC commands, so that the scanner is never halted while awaiting new commands (andersen2022aunifiedsystem pages 3-7, andersen2022aunifiedsystem pages 7-12). The Heriot-Watt system used a combination of LabVIEW (for z-stage, spreader and sequencing) and SCAPS SAMLight (for laser and galvo control), with process data such as number of layers and layer thickness taken as input (bidare2017anopenarchitecturemetal pages 3-5). The LLNL testbed used a delay generator (DG645) to trigger the scan head controller and camera, with the trigger to the laser empirically optimized to account for finite mirror acceleration (calta2018aninstrumentfor pages 5-7). The NIST AMMT used FPGA-based acquisition and processing for real-time monitoring and control of laser power, scan speed and spot size, with a custom SAM (Simple AM) utility software and an interpreter layer for voxel-level control (grantham2016opticaldesignand pages 5-7). On the One Click Metal MPrint+, the free software version refreshed monitoring only every few seconds on the PC, hindering timely operator response during builds—a limitation that the FASER project is addressing through PTP-synchronized camera networks and remote control (friedo2026developmentofintelligent pages 1-3).

### 8. What Failed and What Groups Would Do Differently

**Controls obsolescence:** A Realizer 250 at the Danish Technological Institute was rendered completely inoperable when its DOS-based control workstation crashed. With no service agreement in place and proprietary black-boxed electronics, the machine held "no value" and was scheduled for decommissioning despite having a mechanically serviceable laser, scanner and motors. The DTU group concluded that the proprietary control framework was the main hindrance for legacy systems, and that retrofitting with their open-source unified controller would be more economical than purchasing a new machine (average price ~€410,000 in 2022) (aagaard2023legacylaserpowder pages 1-2, aagaard2023legacylaserpowder pages 2-3).

**Recoater design iteration:** As noted above, Heriot-Watt abandoned rigid WC knife-edge and cylindrical spreaders after jamming and adopted compliant silicone cord (bidare2017anopenarchitecturemetal pages 3-5). TU Berlin added vibration motors to their funnel dispensers after cohesive powders clogged the orifice (uhlmann2020alaserpowder pages 6-8).

**Optical thermal management:** NIST's folding mirror coating failure led to recommendations for higher-conductivity substrates and active cooling (grantham2016opticaldesignand pages 8-9).

**DTU second generation:** DTU explicitly developed their second-generation open LPBF machine "with the knowledge gained from the first iteration, incorporating a strong focus on systems engineering and modularization" (aagaard2023legacylaserpowder pages 2-3). Key improvements included replacing analog scanner interfaces (susceptible to electrostatic noise) with digital XY2-100 protocols, adding achromatic doublets for the coaxial sensor path, and moving to fully digital interfaces for all subsystems (andersen2022aunifiedsystem pages 7-12).

**Synchrotron scheduling:** Heriot-Watt's system was designed for X-ray imaging at the STFC Vulcan laser facility, but experiments scheduled in the summers of 2015 and 2016 could not proceed because of issues with the external X-ray source, not the LPBF system itself—illustrating that the limited availability of synchrotron beamtime makes obtaining results "somewhat difficult" and motivating portable designs that can serve multiple facilities (bidare2017anopenarchitecturemetal pages 7-9).

### 9. Experience with Commercial Small-Format Machines

**Aconity Mini:** Described as "particularly suitable for research" due to its modular optics, Python-accessible software and simple architecture with fewer moving parts and fewer operational issues than Renishaw or EOS systems. Cleaning the Mini for alloy changeover takes approximately one hour, versus roughly five or more hours for larger Renishaw systems (maddison2022insitumonitoringand pages 66-74, maddison2022insitumonitoringand pages 61-66). Its continuous 1064 nm Nd:YAG laser is rated to only 190 W, with a 140 mm diameter cylindrical build volume up to 200 mm high. Powder is manually loaded into a supplier volume, and excess powder is collected in an overflow trap for sieving and reuse (maddison2022insitumonitoringand pages 61-66). A known limitation is that the pyrometer acquisition runs continuously, including during laser-off travel and inter-part delays, producing anomalously low readings and dark lines in thermal maps that must be masked or corrected (maddison2022insitumonitoringand pages 66-74).

**Aconity MIDI+:** Used for processing novel nickel-free stainless steel; key processing problems were parameter-dependent delamination and cracking at insufficient energy density and significant over-melting at excessive energy density (kantonen2023processingofnovel pages 1-5).

**One Click Metal MPrint+:** Investigated in the FASER project as a representative of the emerging low-cost desktop SLM market. The machine's harsh in-chamber environment (metal dust, vapor and emissions) introduced significant sensor noise. The live video monitoring via the free software refreshed only every few seconds, far too slowly for timely human intervention during recoater failures. Recoater malfunctions—collisions with spatter elevations, warped overhangs or buildups from poor heat dissipation—were identified as a primary cause of build loss (friedo2026developmentofintelligent pages 1-3).

### 10. Cross-Cutting Lessons

Several themes emerge consistently across the literature:

1. **Recoating is the hardest subsystem to get right.** Rigid blades jam; compliant or flexible recoaters are more forgiving. Gravity-fed dispensers need vibration assist for cohesive powders. Recoater speed often dominates build time in small research machines.

2. **Gas flow design is not trivial.** Insufficient cross-flow velocity leads directly to increased porosity. The DTU group measured gas velocity throughout the build area with an NC sensor gantry and correlated low-velocity regions with higher porosity, recommending systematic characterization of the flow field (kjer2023experimentalanalysisand pages 1-2, kjer2023experimentalanalysisand pages 2-4).

3. **Optics require thermal management from day one.** The NIST experience with folding-mirror coating failure at ~275 °C underscores that even broadband-coated mirrors in the beam path can fail if stray power and back-reflections are not managed.

4. **Control software is the first thing to become obsolete** and is the reason legacy machines are decommissioned while their hardware is still functional. Open interfaces, documented protocols and source-code access are essential for long-term utility.

5. **Galvo calibration and beam profiling are time-consuming but essential.** The NIST "mark and measure" procedure requires removing and scanning a plate, performing image analysis, and potentially iterating. Focus verification typically requires dedicated beam profiling hardware.

6. **Small machines are easier to change over between alloys** (Aconity Mini ~1 h vs Renishaw ~5+ h), making them attractive for alloy screening despite limited build volume.

7. **Synchrotron replicators face unique compromises** between X-ray transparency (thin substrates, boron nitride or glassy carbon windows) and thermal representativeness. All groups validated their thermal boundary conditions computationally or experimentally.

8. **Explicit total construction costs are almost never published.** The average commercial LPBF machine price was approximately €410,000 in 2022 (aagaard2023legacylaserpowder pages 1-2). The relative affordability of custom systems is implied but not quantified; individual subsystem costs (e.g., a 500 W fiber laser, a galvo scanner, a vacuum chamber) are commercially available but rarely aggregated in publications.
