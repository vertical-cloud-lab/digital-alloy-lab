We are a university lab designing a small laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) machine for screening new aluminium alloys from small powder batches (tens of grams per build, 15-63 um gas-atomised powders such as AlSi10Mg, 6061 and 7075 modified with grain refiners). Our detailed design (a machined, vacuum-purgeable ~6 L chamber, a 500 W single-mode CW fibre laser with a galvo scanner and f-theta lens, heated build modules of 25/50/75 mm diameter on a sealed piston, a dose-from-above hopper, a recirculating argon cross-flow, robot-friendly features) needs about 10-16 weeks before first build, because of a 10-12 week laser lead time, about 150-290 shop hours of custom machining, an outsourced honed bore and long-lead safety parts. We want a simpler design that gives a working machine, producing multi-layer test coupons in at least one Al alloy, within 3-6 weeks from the decision to build, and that can later grow into the full design.

Please review the literature (journal papers, theses, conference papers such as the Solid Freeform Fabrication Symposium, HardwareX and open-hardware projects) and answer each question with sources and numbers:

1. Minimal lab-built and open-architecture LPBF systems (for example Heriot-Watt's open-architecture PBF of Bidare et al. 2017, DTU's OpenAM and its Teensy-based controller, the MIT Hart group testbed, NIST AMMT, Sheffield, UT Austin, Penn State, KU Leuven, Aachen, synchrotron process replicators and hobbyist builds): what powder delivery did each use (supply piston, hopper, fixed bed with a rising recoater or rising optics, manual spreading), what build area and height, what chamber (vacuum chamber, purged Perspex or metal box, glovebox), what O2 level was reached and how, and how long each took to design and build, if reported.

2. Fixed powder bed designs, in which the substrate does not move and the recoater and the focus (or the whole scan head) rise by one layer each layer: which systems use this, and what are the reported problems (blade parallelism, focus tracking, powder edge slumping at the walls, overflow handling, build height limits) compared with a build piston? Rectangular piston wells sealed with felt or PTFE wipers, as in many SLS machines: any metal LPBF precedent, and how well they hold fine Al powder at a 200 C build plate?

3. Flow purging only (no vacuum) in small chambers (1-30 L) for aluminium LPBF: what O2 and moisture levels are reported, how many volume changes or how much time does it take to reach below 1000 ppm and below 100 ppm O2, and does purging alone give densities and properties comparable to vacuum-purged or large commercial machines for AlSi10Mg and crack-prone Al alloys? How much does a hot plate driving water off the powder matter?

4. Single-track, single-layer and few-layer experiments as an alloy screening method for crack-prone aluminium alloys (hot cracking, porosity, process windows): how well do they predict the behaviour of bulk LPBF coupons, and what is the minimum coupon height or number of layers that reproduces cracking and porosity seen in full builds?

5. Using second-hand industrial lasers and scanners (for example IPG YLR-200/400-SM single-mode lasers taken from decommissioned EOS M 270/280/290 machines, used SCANLAB hurrySCAN or intelliSCAN heads, RTC4/RTC5 cards) in research LPBF rigs: any reported pitfalls (machine-specific interfaces or firmware, missing back-reflection protection, fibre or connector damage, calibration, warranty and service), and how researchers checked them on arrival.

6. Scan control for a fast build: open-source XY2-100 controllers (DTU Teensy controller, OPAL, LinuxCNC xy2mod) against a SCANLAB RTC5 with its DLL: what laser-scanner timing, delays and skywriting each supports, and which one gets a research rig scanning correctly with the least development time?

7. Laser and powder safety for a quickly built research LPBF rig in a university: what enclosure, interlock and powder-handling features did published lab-built rigs use to run a Class 4 laser safely (Class 1 enclosure, interlocked lid, beam shutter, viewing window), and what dust-hazard practices for aluminium powder were applied at this small scale?

Please end with a ranked list of the design simplifications that would most shorten the time to a working Al LPBF screening machine, each with its main technical risk.
