|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Thermal focal shift | Absorption in fused silica, AR coatings, dirt and condensate creates radial temperature gradients, changing focus and adding aberration; contamination can dominate clean-bulk absorption. | A fingerprint-contaminated fused-silica lens shifted about −0.32 mm at 2.5 kW and −0.47 mm at 4.5 kW. Measured intensity-specific thermal refractive power was approximately 0.55 × 10⁻⁶ mm/W for new, wavelength-specific-coated fused silica versus 1.16–1.27 × 10⁻⁶ mm/W for used uncoated lenses. These results do not justify one universal 500 W shift because geometry and absorption matter. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002) | Establish cold and thermally soaked caustics with a focus monitor or Z-stack of low-duty-cycle marks; trend focus and transmitted power before every build and after window replacement. Keep optics clean and temperature stabilized. Consider crystalline quartz for highly loaded windows or lenses; its thermo-optical ratio can be about 0.0053 of fused silica in the favorable orientation. (pqac-00000039, pqac-00000041) |
| 2. F-theta lens suitability | A generic marking lens may have an adequate pulsed LIDT yet lack a guaranteed 500 W CW absorption, thermal-focus, polarization and contamination specification. CW suitability is governed chiefly by coating/bulk absorption and heat removal—not a nanosecond LIDT number. | High-quality wavelength-specific coatings produced roughly half the thermal refractive power measured for used uncoated fused-silica lenses; broadband coatings can add substantial thermal power. Fused-silica f-theta optics have nevertheless been used in LPBF with 500 W lasers and approximately 35 µm spots. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000038) | Do not qualify a low-cost lens from catalog LIDT alone. Require written approval for 500 W CW at 1070 nm, coating and total absorption data, clear aperture at every scan angle, thermal-focus data, and a warranty for the intended duty cycle. Prefer an industrial LPBF/welding-grade fused-silica assembly from a vendor that will thermally qualify the complete lens; verify it experimentally at staged power. |
| 3. Aluminium back-reflection | High total reflectance does not equal power recoupled into the fibre, but an occasional specular return aligned with the receive numerical aperture can destabilize or damage the delivery fibre, polymer components, coatings or laser source. | Aluminium reflects approximately 90% around 1070 nm; smooth surfaces can exceed 75%, whereas copper can approach 95–98%. The power returning to the source is normally only a fraction of output because the melt pool is rough, displaced from the waist and imperfectly normal to the beam; no defensible fixed wattage can be assigned without measurement. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000014) | Obtain IPG’s written back-reflection limit and protection description for the exact serial-number configuration. Use source-side reflected-power monitoring and fast shutdown; add a properly rated free-space isolator only if IPG and the scanner integrator approve its thermal load. Avoid the exact field center for stationary exposures, prohibit firing on smooth bare plate at normal incidence, and use a small intentional optical/window wedge or tilt whose returned ray is traced into a beam dump—not merely displaced onto another optic. (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000013) |
| 4. Window and cover-slide contamination | Mg/Zn-rich condensate and spatter absorb and scatter the beam, reduce delivered power and create local hot spots that can crack the sacrificial slide or damage its coating. Published deposition-rate and replacement-interval data are sparse and strongly machine/alloy dependent. | In one LPBF study, reducing nominal cross-flow from 4 to 2 m/s reduced melt-pool cross-sectional area by 30–50%; 2.5–4.0 m/s cleared plume and bent spatter downstream, while porosity rose rapidly at ≤2.0 m/s. A modified two-row nozzle increased overall spatter removal from 69% to 93%. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000032, pqac-00000021) | The proposed 2.5–3 m/s is plausible only if measured uniformly over the complete bed. Add a separate clean-argon gas knife immediately below the sacrificial slide and a broad, laminar bed cross-flow into a close-coupled exhaust. A porous flow straightener can suppress turbulence; a top inlet flowing away from the window prevents rising condensate from reaching it. Inspect and measure transmission before each build; replace by a power-loss/hot-spot criterion rather than an assumed hour interval. (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000044, pqac-00000045) |
| 5. Galvo aperture and edge spot | A nominal 20 mm entrance aperture does not guarantee adequate clear aperture on both moving mirrors at all scan angles. Clipping of a Gaussian tail heats an edge and produces asymmetric diffraction; off-axis incidence also elongates the work-plane spot. | For a 75 mm-diameter field, the paraxial chief-ray angles are arctan(37.5/160) ≈ 13.2° for f = 160 mm and arctan(37.5/254) ≈ 8.4° for f = 254 mm. Simple projection gives a tangential geometric stretch of only 1/cosθ ≈ 1.027 and 1.011, respectively, but scanner/lens aberrations can be larger. Edge elongation is associated with lower melt-pool depth in some scan orientations and increased roughness or lack-of-fusion. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026) | Treat 7.5 mm as the safer starting beam for a 20 mm basiCube; 5 mm increases the diffraction-limited waist by about 50%. Require the scanner/lens vendor’s footprint and clear-aperture trace; as a conservative alignment target, keep the worst-case clear semi-aperture ≥1.5 times the Gaussian 1/e² radius. Measure caustic, power, ellipticity and centroid at center, axes and corners after thermal soak. |
| 6. Commissioning and monitoring | Nominal laser power, catalog spot size and visible pilot alignment do not establish delivered power, focus, M², field geometry or thermal stability. | NIST’s open LPBF testbed uses a 500 W fibre laser and beamsplitter-based coaxial monitoring. LPBF beam-characterization tools reported in the literature include Focus Beam Profiler, BeamWatch AM, FocusMonitor+ and 3-D Beam Mapper. Coaxial monitoring passes the approximately 1070 nm process beam through a dichroic while routing returning melt-pool emission to photodiodes or cameras. (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000029, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016) | Minimum kit: calibrated ≥600 W thermopile or water-cooled calorimeter; sampling optic and dump; beam profiler/focus monitor capable of approximately 75 µm caustics; low-power 1070 nm card/viewer; removable visible alignment source; microscope; thermal camera for optics; and engraved/anodized calibration plates plus a metrology microscope for affine and higher-order field correction. For monitoring, a fast coaxial Si or InGaAs photodiode is the simplest fit; add an off-axis global camera first, then two-band pyrometry if calibrated temperature—not merely intensity—is required. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018) |
| 7. Laser safety | A Class 1 label depends on the complete interlocked product, including foreseeable faults and guard penetration; optical density alone does not make a viewing port safe against a focused 500 W beam. A thin aluminium skin can become a secondary specular reflector before it fails. | OD 7 attenuates 500 W to 50 nW, but the necessary OD must be calculated from the applicable accessible-emission limit, viewing geometry, wavelength band and exposure duration. No credible universal burn-through time exists for 1.6 mm aluminium: it depends strongly on spot size, focus, alloy, incidence, cooling and surface condition; aluminium reflectivity at approximately 1 µm can exceed 0.9 initially. (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000042) | Use a certified 1030–1100 nm laser-safety window with wavelength-specific rating, typically OD ≥7 as a conservative starting point, mechanically backed by a labyrinth or shutter so no direct processing beam can strike it. Prefer steel, double-wall guards with an air gap, beam traps and direct-beam exclusion over 1.6 mm aluminium. Interlock doors, removable panels, window shutters, loss of coolant/flow and scanner faults; commission the enclosure through a laser-safety officer and IEC 60825-1/IEC 60825-4 or ANSI Z136 hazard analysis and guard test rather than relying on a calculated burn-through time. |
