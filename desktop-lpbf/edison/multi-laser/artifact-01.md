| 1 | Auxiliary defocused heating laser — DLAH/DPM style | **Very high for materials research.** Retain the 1070 nm galvo beam as the melting source initially; later add an independently positioned 915 nm heating beam. A future 450 nm point-melting source could then supplement or replace the IR melting beam. | Decouples melting from local preheating/post-heating; directly useful for crack-prone experimental Al alloys, cooling-rate studies and in-situ heat treatment. The demonstrated arrangement used a 43.5–44 W, 450 nm melting spot of about 100 × 150 µm and a 140 W, 915 nm top-hat adjustable from about 6.35 × 6.35 to 12.8 × 12.8 mm; it achieved 99.99% density and altered Ti6Al4V lath morphology (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000068, pqac-00000069). | Requires accurate spatial and temporal offset between heating and melting beams; broad-beam incidence may be oblique and position-dependent. A 915 nm beam couples less efficiently to Al than blue light, and heating powder can increase oxidation, plume generation and powder disturbance. | **Optics:** reserve one large replaceable lid-window aperture, preferably about Ø100–120 mm clear, or one Ø70–90 mm scanner window plus a separate Ø40–60 mm auxiliary port; coatings must cover 915 and 1070 nm, with a separate blue-rated window if 450 nm is anticipated. **Mounting:** add a stiff, dowelled auxiliary-head rail with tip/tilt and XYZ adjustment. **Control:** one XY2-100 channel for the existing galvo plus isolated analogue/TTL laser control and encoder-trigger outputs for the heating head; provide a shared hardware clock and emergency blanking line. **Cooling:** reserve roughly 1–2 kW additional chiller rejection and independent flow/temperature monitoring. **Gas:** orient the heating footprint and trailing beam so neither lies downwind in the melting plume. **Safety:** series-interlock every source, with wavelength-appropriate shutters, beam dumps, emission indicators and monitored safe-torque-off/laser-enable contactors. |
| 2 | Fibre-coupled blue-diode array on a gantry — DAM style | **High for alloy screening and thermal-history experiments; moderate for conventional high-speed 3-D LPBF.** Particularly attractive where many independently driven low-cost beams matter more than galvo speed. | Short wavelength improves Al energy coupling: AlSi12 powder absorptivity was reported as about 48% at 450 nm, 26% at 808 nm and 19% near 1060 nm (pqac-00000035, pqac-00000038). Demonstrated heads used six 450 nm, approximately 3.5 W beams for 21 W total and about 70 µm individual spots, or nine approximately 4 W beams spanning about 659 µm; 100-channel 808 nm heads have also been constructed (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000065). Independently selected emitters can melt, preheat, post-heat or remelt in one pass. | Present low-power blue diodes have poorer beam quality and much less power per channel than single-mode fibre lasers. A moving optical head adds mass, cable/fibre management and contamination exposure. DAM cooling rates and microstructures differ markedly from conventional LPBF, so it is not a drop-in productivity upgrade (pqac-00000044, pqac-00000047). | **Optics:** provide a removable, independently sealed blue-rated port of at least Ø50–70 mm if the head remains outside the chamber; an internal head instead needs a sealed fibre/electrical feedthrough and sacrificial cover glass. **Mechanics:** reserve orthogonal gantry rails, datum pads and about 100–150 mm vertical head clearance without obstructing the recoater. **Control:** allocate 8–16 independent constant-current/TTL channels initially, expandable to at least 100, plus motion-controller triggers synchronized to gantry position. **Cooling:** reserve about 0.5–1.5 kW for a 6–9-emitter research head and manifold space for future scaling. **Gas:** move the array across the bed so its active row is transverse to cross-flow and plume from an upwind spot cannot pass through downstream beams. **Safety:** individually monitored diode enables plus a master shutter/contactor; interlock fibre disconnects and head over-temperature. |
| 3 | Dual independently galvo-scanned 1070 nm fibre lasers | **High for conventional LPBF productivity and direct comparability with industrial machines; only moderate for a Ø50 mm plate because one scanner already covers the full bed.** | Fastest and most mature route to independently programmable melt pools, arbitrary leading/trailing-beam offsets and commercial-style field stitching. Both scanners can cover the entire Ø50 mm plate, avoiding a permanent stitch boundary. Existing 300–500 W process development transfers directly to the second channel. | Highest optical, control and safety complexity; scanner-to-scanner affine calibration, dynamic timing and focal-plane matching are essential. Simultaneous nearby melt pools can collide, generate large-droplet spatter and increase gaseous pores or lack of fusion; one AlSi10Mg overlap study found 17.4% lower elongation than an isolated single-laser specimen (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000051). Two 1070 nm beams retain aluminium’s relatively poor IR coupling. | **Optics:** either one shared, replaceable Ø120–150 mm clear window accommodating both scan cones, or two symmetrically placed Ø70–90 mm windows; reserve water-cooled window frames, gas knife/sacrificial panes and adequate spacing for two f-theta assemblies. **Mounting:** two identical dowelled scanner datums with independent tip/tilt/XYZ adjustment; reserve room for f = 160–254 mm lenses and prevent scan-cone clipping over the full Ø50 mm plate. **Control:** two hardware-synchronised XY2-100/SL2-100 channels, independent power modulation, shared layer clock and collision-aware scheduler. **Cooling:** allow approximately 2–3 kW additional rejection for a second 300–500 W fibre source, scanner and electronics, subject to vendor efficiency. **Gas:** make flow uniform across the whole overlap field and schedule close parallel tracks from upwind toward downwind. **Safety:** separate keyed enables and shutters but one fail-safe safety PLC chain that removes permission from both lasers; include back-reflection protection for each source. |
| 4 | Hybrid 1070 nm galvo fibre laser plus 450 nm diode-array head | **Medium-high for research breadth, but mechanically more complex than either architecture alone.** Retains production-like IR LPBF while adding wavelength and thermal-field experiments. | Combines a fast 200–500 W fibre-laser melt source with independently selectable blue spots having superior aluminium absorption. Reported hybrid hardware paired a 200 W, approximately 100 µm IR beam with six 3 W or nine 4–4.5 W blue diodes having approximately 72–88 µm spots (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000004). It supports local preheat, post-heat, remelting and wavelength-comparison experiments without surrendering the original process. | Must register a galvo coordinate system to a mechanically translated array and synchronize systems with very different speeds and spot profiles. One common window needs broadband blue/IR performance; separate ports simplify coatings but increase alignment and lid complexity. The array can obstruct the scanner cone or recoater if packaging is not planned early. | **Optics:** reserve the existing IR scan window plus a separate Ø50–70 mm blue port, preferred over one broadband window; provide replaceable sacrificial panes and purge paths for both. **Mechanics:** maintain a clear IR scan cone and add a dowelled gantry/head datum outside it. **Control:** retain XY2-100 for the galvo; reserve 8–16 diode-current/TTL channels, a gantry motion bus, shared encoder/timebase and deterministic trigger I/O. **Cooling:** budget roughly 0.5–1.5 kW beyond the existing laser/chiller and provide separate monitored loops if blue diodes require tighter temperature stability. **Gas:** arrange the diode row transverse to cross-flow and prohibit the IR melt pool from operating in the downwind plume of active diode spots. **Safety:** broadband enclosure classification and eyewear/diagnostics for both 450 and 1070 nm; one master interlock must close both source-specific shutters. |
| 5 | Fixed diode array over a moving XY bed — simplified area-printing concept | **Low to medium.** Potentially compact and scalable, but it conflicts with a stable powder bed, recoater and gas-flow architecture and is the least mature option for research-grade metallic LPBF. | Eliminates multiple galvos and scan-field stitching; electronic emitter selection can expose many pixels in parallel. Fixed optics can be factory-calibrated, and short-wavelength emitters improve aluminium absorption. It resembles DAM but moves the specimen rather than the optical head. | Accelerating a powder-filled build module risks surface waves, powder segregation and recoater misregistration. Moving hoses, Z-stage and gas nozzles complicates vacuum sealing and thermal isolation. Low fill factor, diode power variation and pixel-to-pixel calibration limit resolution; true Seurat-style projection requires substantially more complex imaging and high-power modulation than a simple diode array. | **Optics:** reserve a large telecentric aperture, approximately Ø100–150 mm clear, or a rectangular window sized to the full projected array; provide active window cooling and a uniform purge. **Mechanics:** design the build piston/recoater module from the outset as a stiff, low-acceleration XY stage with vacuum-compatible bellows or place the whole sealed process module on crossed stages; ordinary static desktop chamber construction will not retrofit cleanly. **Control:** multi-axis deterministic motion plus tens to hundreds of synchronized emitter channels and per-pixel power calibration. **Cooling:** reserve at least 1–3 kW initially, with substantial growth margin for array scaling. **Gas:** the inlet and extraction geometry must move with the bed or maintain uniform cross-flow throughout stage travel. **Safety:** interlock stage position, array enable, window temperature and every access panel; provide a full-array shutter or fail-safe master current contactor in addition to channel-level blanking. |
