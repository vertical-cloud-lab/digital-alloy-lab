We are designing a small, research-grade (desktop) laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) machine for screening new aluminium alloys made in-house in small powder batches. The base design has a Ø25-50 mm build plate, a ~6 L vacuum-purgeable argon chamber with a recirculating cross-flow, and one 300-500 W, 1070 nm single-mode fibre laser delivered by a fixed galvanometer scanner and an f = 254 mm f-theta lens through a window in the chamber lid. Much later we want to upgrade it to multi-laser printing with several independently drivable lasers, as in the work of Adam T. Clare (University of Nottingham, and University of Nottingham Ningbo China) and his collaborators.

Please review the peer-reviewed literature, with citations and quantitative values, on:

1. Adam T. Clare's and his group's work on multi-laser, multi-beam or laser-array powder bed fusion and related laser processing with independently controlled beams: which systems they built or used (number of lasers or emitters, wavelengths, powers, spot sizes; beam delivery such as fibre-coupled diode arrays, multiple galvanometer scanners, fixed optics over a moving bed, diffractive or spatial-light-modulator beam shaping), how each beam is addressed and synchronised, and what they demonstrated (thermal-field and cooling-rate control, preheating or in-situ heat treatment with a leading or trailing beam, microstructure and texture tailoring, crack suppression in crack-prone alloys, residual stress, in-situ alloying, build rate). Give DOIs.
2. Other multi-laser and multi-beam LPBF research platforms and architectures relevant to a small machine, for example University of Sheffield diode area melting (DAM), the UT Austin open-architecture multi-laser research platform, multi-spot beam splitting with diffractive optical elements, Seurat-style area printing, VulcanForms-style many-laser arrays, dual-laser research machines (Aconity, Xact Metal XM200G), and commercial multi-laser machines. How do they handle overlapping scan fields, stitching and laser-to-laser calibration, interactions between plumes and spatter from simultaneous melt pools and the gas cross-flow, and synchronisation of several lasers and scanners?
3. Process effects of two or more beams acting near each other on the same layer: beam spacing and time delay, plume and spatter interaction, porosity and properties at stitch or overlap zones, effects on melt-pool dynamics and solidification. Give quantitative thresholds where available.
4. Design implications. For a desktop machine that has one laser now, what should the base design reserve so that independently driven lasers can be added later: lid and window area (one large window or several), window size and cooling, scanner mounting and alignment fixtures, field overlap with f = 160-254 mm f-theta lenses over a Ø50 mm plate, controller architecture (several XY2-100 channels or a multi-head controller), power, cooling and chiller budget, gas-flow direction relative to the beam layout, laser-safety interlocks for several sources, and calibration targets? Would fibre-coupled diode arrays (for example 450 nm blue or 9xx nm) be a better route to multiple independent beams for aluminium than several fibre lasers?

Please end with a short ranked list of the multi-laser architectures most suitable for a later upgrade of a desktop alloy-screening LPBF machine, and for each one what the base design has to provide now.
