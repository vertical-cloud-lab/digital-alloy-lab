We are designing a small research LPBF machine for screening new alloys from small powder batches (mainly aluminium alloys: AlSi10Mg-type, Al-Mg-Sc/Zr, and crack-prone 2xxx, 6xxx and 7xxx alloys with or without grain refiners; possibly steels, Ni and Ti alloys later). Each Ø25-75 mm build plate sits on a resistively heated block inside a honed 316L stainless build cylinder in a ~6 L argon chamber, with a dose-from-above hopper and a blade recoater. Tier A heats the plate to 200 C (6061 aluminium heater block). We have reserved a Tier B at 500 C, which would need a steel or ceramic heater block, a water-cooled piston head and larger plate-to-bore clearances. Please review the peer-reviewed literature (and machine-builder documentation where it is the only source), with citations and quantitative values, on:

1. When 400-600 C build-plate preheat is actually needed in LPBF, and when 200 C or less is enough. For each alloy class (Al 6xxx such as 6061 and 6182, 7xxx such as 7075, 2xxx such as 2024, Al-Cu-Mg with grain refiners, Al-Mg-Sc/Zr, Al-Fe and other high-temperature Al alloys, AlSi10Mg, tool steels such as H13 and M2/M50, maraging and martensitic steels, Ti-6Al-4V, TiAl and other intermetallics, NiTi, non-weldable Ni superalloys such as IN738LC, CM247LC and Rene 80, Cu alloys, W and Mo): the preheat temperature that removed cracking, reduced residual stress or distortion, or controlled phase formation, what lower temperatures failed to do, and whether alternatives (grain-refining additions such as Zr, Sc, TiB2 or Ti-C, scan strategy, laser re-scanning or a second heating beam) reach the same result without a hot plate. Which applications need it (crack-free high-strength wrought-equivalent Al, in-situ heat treatment, residual-stress-sensitive thin walls, tool and die steels). Which commercial machines offer 400 C or more (e.g. SLM 280 high-temperature option, Aconity MINI/MIDI, TRUMPF TruPrint, EOS, Farsoon) and how they do it.
2. Complications at 400-600 C: powder flowability and spreadability of hot powder (cohesion, interparticle sintering, caking, agglomeration of fine Al powder near the plate, effect on layer density and recoater forces); oxidation and nitridation of Al, Mg-bearing, Ti and steel powders held at temperature in argon with 100-1000 ppm O2, and the O2 or dew-point levels the literature says are needed; evaporation of Mg, Zn and Li from hot powder and from the melt pool, condensate and soot; powder degradation and reuse after long hot exposure; outgassing and water release from chamber walls and seals; diffusion bonding of parts to the plate and part removal; thermal distortion of the plate; heating of the lid window, O2 sensor and chamber gas; and how long builds stay at temperature.
3. Build-plate materials and fits for a heated plate in a cooler cylinder. With a 6061 plate at 200 C in a 316L bore at ~55 C, a Ø75 mm plate's radial clearance closes by ~0.14 mm. Which lower-expansion plate materials are usable for printing aluminium alloys (4032, A390 or other Al-Si alloys, spray-formed hypereutectic Al-Si controlled-expansion alloys, Al-SiC composites), and what happens when Al alloys are printed on steel, Ti, Cu or Invar substrates (intermetallics, wetting, adhesion, delamination). How commercial and research heated platforms seal the gap between a hot plate and a cooler build cylinder (felt, graphite, ceramic-fibre or labyrinth seals; clearances used), and what plate materials they recommend for each alloy.

Please end with (a) a table of alloy class against the minimum preheat temperature the literature supports for crack-free or low-stress parts, with references, and (b) a ranked list of design recommendations for the 500 C tier and for the plate material and plate-to-bore fit.
