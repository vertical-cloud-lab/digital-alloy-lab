| Rank | Recommendation | Rationale / evidence |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Do not make 500 °C the default for aluminium screening.** Use Tier A (≤200 °C) for AlSi10Mg and Al-Mg-Sc/Zr, and reserve Tier B for unmodified 6xxx experiments, in-situ heat treatment, or exceptionally stress-sensitive geometries. | AlSi10Mg normally prints without high preheat; Scalmalloy uses about 200 °C for in-situ clustering rather than crack prevention. Unmodified 6061 has been made crack-free at 500 °C, but inoculated alloys can achieve comparable crack control at much lower temperature (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000031). |
| 2 | **Prefer grain-refining chemistry over a 500 °C plate for 2xxx, 6xxx, and 7xxx development.** Screen Zr/Sc, Ti/TiB₂, TiC, CaB₆, and eutectic-forming additions first. | Zr nanoparticles produced crack-free, fully dense 6061/7075; 1 wt% Zr with 100 °C reduced 6061 cracking; TiAl₃ prevented 6061 cracking; 1.5 wt% Ti prevented cracking in Al-Cu-Mg. By contrast, 200 °C failed for 7075 and 200–400 °C failed to eliminate 2xxx cracking (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000032). |
| 3 | **Replace the 6061 heater block with a mechanically retained steel, precipitation-resistant stainless steel, Ni alloy, or ceramic heater carrier.** Do not structurally load 6061 at 500 °C. | High-temperature platforms require all supporting and moving components to tolerate sustained heat. A ceramic resistive heater can reach the required temperature, but conductive losses through surrounding steel hardware can prevent the plate from reaching its target (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000056). |
| 4 | **Thermally isolate the heater assembly from the piston and cylinder.** Use thick low-conductivity ceramic board/fibre insulation, minimized metallic contact area, slender thermal breaks, and radiation shields. | In the Aeroswift study, multiple metal contacts and only 5 mm insulation limited a nominal 600 °C system to much lower plate temperatures; the insulation was only 44% effective. Improved isolation was predicted to permit approximately 650 °C without more heater power (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000056). |
| 5 | **Use a separately water-cooled piston head, seal carrier, actuator interface, and cylinder rim—but interlock cooling flow with heater power.** | Cooling protects bearings, seals, encoder/actuator components, and the honed bore while allowing the removable plate to remain hot. The strong resulting gradient makes clearance and plate bowing critical; commercial high-temperature systems likewise incorporate chamber cooling (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000042). |
| 6 | **Size the cold diametral clearance from the worst differential expansion, not the Tier-A value.** For a Ø75 mm 6061 plate at 500 °C in a 316L bore near 55 °C, differential diametral growth is approximately 0.36–0.41 mm; specify at least about 0.50–0.65 mm cold diametral clearance before adding tolerances, cylindricity, bow, deposits, and seizure margin. | Using representative mean CTEs of approximately 24–25 µm·m⁻¹·K⁻¹ for 6061 and 16–17 µm·m⁻¹·K⁻¹ for 316L gives about 0.18–0.21 mm **radial** closure—substantially more than the stated 0.14 mm Tier-A closure. The final fit must be validated thermally because a cooled bore and hot plate are non-isothermal. |
| 7 | **Do not use an elastomer as the hot-edge powder seal.** Use a replaceable compliant graphite felt/foil ring or aluminosilicate/other ceramic-fibre wiper outside the precision guiding land; add a stepped labyrinth and powder-catching annulus. | Elastomers are unsuitable around 500 °C, while fibrous high-temperature insulation and graphite tolerate the thermal environment. A labyrinth limits direct powder migration without requiring a zero-clearance sliding fit. Published commercial clearances and proprietary seal geometries are generally unavailable, so qualification testing is required (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000045, pqac-00000056). |
| 8 | **Decouple guidance from sealing.** Guide the piston on a cool, long lower land or separate bearing; let the upper hot plate float radially within its calculated clearance. | A hot plate tightly guided by the cooler honed bore risks seizure, galling, bore scoring, and plate buckling. The compliant upper seal should exclude powder, not carry lateral load. |
| 9 | **For aluminium builds, retain an aluminium-family sacrificial build plate whenever possible.** Start with 6061/5083 at ≤200 °C; evaluate 4032 or another qualified high-Si wrought/cast plate for Tier B. | Like-on-like aluminium gives predictable remelting, wetting, and removal. Higher-Si aluminium offers lower CTE and better elevated-temperature dimensional stability than 6061, but its LPBF interface, brittleness, flatness, and machinability must be tested. Evidence for specific 4032/A390 LPBF substrate qualification is sparse, so treat this as an engineering trial rather than a literature-proven substitution. |
| 10 | **Use hypereutectic Al-Si or Al-SiC only after coupon-level interface qualification; do not make ceramic-rich Al-SiC the first Tier-B plate.** | Controlled-expansion Al-Si and Al-SiC can reduce clearance closure, but reduced ductility, heterogeneous remelting, ceramic exposure, thermal fatigue, machining difficulty, and uncertain first-layer bonding can outweigh the CTE benefit. Compositionally compatible metallic Al-Si is the lower-risk first trial. |
| 11 | **Avoid direct aluminium deposition on steel or Invar unless a deliberately engineered interlayer/interface is acceptable.** Use a removable aluminium insert clamped to a steel heater carrier instead. | Dissimilar LPBF interfaces can form brittle intermetallics, crack, or delaminate because of incompatible melting points, conductivities, and CTEs. Mo on stainless steel, for example, delaminated after roughly ten layers where a brittle diluted phase formed, demonstrating that substrate compatibility can dominate the build (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021). |
| 12 | **Use alloy-matched substrates by material family:** aluminium plate for Al, steel plate for steels, Ni-alloy plate for Ni superalloys, Ti plate for Ti alloys, and compatible Cu/Cu-alloy plate for copper. | Matched plates minimize brittle dilution zones and differential contraction. Where matching is impractical, use a replaceable sheet/interlayer: a retained Mo sheet or Cu-OFE substrate solved an otherwise unsuccessful Mo/stainless interface in published work (pqac-00000020). |
| 13 | **Control oxygen to below 200 ppm at minimum and preferably below 100 ppm before heating; target still lower values for Ti, Mg-rich Al, and long hot soaks. Measure moisture/dew point independently.** | Reported high-temperature systems operate below 200 ppm; Aconity work reports below 100 ppm. Aluminium powder exposed to laser heating gained about 120 ppm oxygen over five cycles versus 10 ppm without heat exposure, and hot spatter is especially oxidation-prone (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000037, pqac-00000044, pqac-00000057). |
| 14 | **Bake/purge the empty chamber and hot-zone hardware before admitting production powder, and use only high-temperature-compatible seals, wiring, insulation, and lubricants.** | Heating releases adsorbed water from chamber walls, oxide/hydroxide-bearing powder, insulation, and seals. Moisture thickens aluminium oxide/hydroxide films, impairs spreading and bonding, and supplies hydrogen that can create porosity (pqac-00000035, pqac-00000049, pqac-00000051). |
| 15 | **Provide uniform, controllable cross-flow plus an accessible condensate trap upstream of the filter and optics.** Characterize flow at hot operating conditions. | Mg and Zn boil at approximately 1091 and 907 °C and are selectively lost from Al melt pools; condensed metal/oxide particles can be approximately 10–150 nm, scatter the laser, contaminate optics, and accumulate if gas flow is inadequate. Excessive or turbulent flow can disturb powder, so simply increasing flow is not sufficient (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000038, pqac-00000039). |
| 16 | **Thermally shield and, where needed, actively cool the laser window, O₂ sensor sampling line, recoater drive, hopper throat, camera, and chamber lid.** Locate the O₂ sensor in a conditioned bypass rather than directly above the hot bed. | A 500 °C plate radiates strongly into a 6 L chamber; commercial high-temperature systems incorporate chamber cooling and active monitoring. Fume and condensate additionally threaten the window and sensor, while temperature shifts can bias sensors and expand recoater hardware (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000038, pqac-00000042). |
| 17 | **Use a thermally robust recoater and qualify hot spreading with the actual powder.** Prefer a replaceable metal/ceramic blade, force monitoring, and powder with tightly controlled fines; minimize particles below roughly 15 µm for Tier-B trials. | Fines, moisture, oxide coatings, agglomerates, and partial sintering increase cohesion and produce streaks or low-density layers. Agglomerates can obstruct delivery, be dragged by the blade, and create lack-of-fusion defects; heat-affected aluminium condensate further reduces flowability (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000049, pqac-00000050, pqac-00000051). |
| 18 | **Limit and log each powder lot’s cumulative hot exposure.** Initially restrict 500 °C trials to short, shallow builds; segregate hot-zone powder, sieve it, and test oxygen, PSD, flow/spread density, and Mg/Zn composition before reuse. | Thermal exposure produces necking, satellites, agglomeration and oxide/hydroxide condensate. Heat-affected aluminium powder showed much greater oxygen pickup, while agglomerates shift PSD, reduce reusable yield and impair recoating (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000037, pqac-00000049). |
| 19 | **Design the first layers and removal process for stronger plate bonding at 400–500 °C.** Include sacrificial interface stock, reduced first-layer energy where validated, EDM/bandsaw access, and a plate resurfacing allowance. | Reduced thermal gradients improve bonding and reduce delamination, but also reduce contraction-assisted release and can enlarge the metallurgically mixed interface. Dissimilar interfaces may instead form brittle phases, so mechanical keys or interlayers should be deliberate, not accidental (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021). |
| 20 | **Instrument temperature at the plate top, heater, piston head, bore, gas outlet, lid/window and sensor bay; independently trip on overtemperature, lost cooling, low flow, or rising O₂. Consider VCSEL/laser-diode top-layer heating only as a later uniformity upgrade.** | A heater thermocouple can substantially overstate plate temperature: one ceramic-heater design predicted 395 °C at the heater but only 374 °C at the substrate. EOS-type VCSEL heating plus IR feedback can homogenize each recoated layer rapidly, while laser-diode heating has demonstrated in-situ annealing; these methods add optical and control complexity but address the decay of baseplate heating with build height (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000042, pqac-00000046). |
