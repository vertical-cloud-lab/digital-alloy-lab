We are designing a small, research-grade (desktop) laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) machine for screening new aluminium alloys made in-house in small batches (a few hundred grams of ultrasonically atomized powder per alloy, 15-63 um). Please review the peer-reviewed literature (and theses / HardwareX / open-hardware papers where available) on the DESIGN and CUSTOMIZATION of LPBF systems, and give quantitative design values with citations for each of the following:

1. Open-source and open-architecture LPBF machines and custom research testbeds (e.g. NIST AMMT, university-built LPBF testbeds, low-cost or open-hardware LPBF builds, Aconity-type open platforms, miniaturized or reduced-build-volume LPBF for alloy screening). What laser, scanner, chamber, recoater and gas system did each use, what did it cost, and what lessons did the builders report?
2. Powder spreading: blade vs. roller (rotating, counter-rotating) vs. compliant/brush recoaters; recoater speed, gap and layer thickness; spreadability metrics and their dependence on particle size distribution and morphology; designs that minimise powder consumption (small build plate, build-plate inserts, dose-on-demand or reduced-volume feed, powder supply from above vs. piston feed).
3. Inert-gas environment: achievable and required O2 levels (ppm) for Al alloys (AlSi10Mg, Al-Mg-Sc/Zr), purge strategies (vacuum-purge-backfill vs. flow-through), cross-flow velocity across the plate for plume/spatter removal, nozzle and outlet design, filtration, gas consumption, and O2/H2O sensing.
4. Beam delivery: galvanometer scanner + f-theta lens vs. XY-gantry-moved optics (or hybrid gantry + galvo) for LPBF; achievable scan speeds and accelerations, effects of gantry acceleration/jerk on track quality, spot size (50-100 um) and focal-shift considerations, fiber laser power (200-500 W at 1070 nm) and its suitability for aluminium (reflectivity), protective window contamination.
5. Controls and electronics: scanner protocols (XY2-100, SL2-100), laser modulation and synchronisation with motion, open-source control software, in-situ monitoring (coaxial photodiode/pyrometer, layer cameras) used in custom LPBF machines.
6. Safety: laser safety for Class 4 enclosed systems (ANSI Z136.1, IEC 60825-1/-4, interlocks, laser-safe viewing windows), combustible/reactive metal powder handling for aluminium (NFPA 484, wet separators, filter passivation, PPE), and incidents or hazards reported in research LPBF labs.

Please end with a ranked list of the design choices most supported by the literature for a sub-$60k custom desktop LPBF machine optimised for good spreadability, safety, ease of automation, smooth motion, minimal powder (hopper) volume and high-purity inert gas.
