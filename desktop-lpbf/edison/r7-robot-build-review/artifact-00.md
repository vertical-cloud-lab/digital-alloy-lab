| Step | Cobot Feasibility | Key Challenges | Recommended Assignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unpacking deliveries | Low | Unknown box geometry, straps and film, clutter, hidden or fragile contents, variable grasp points, and pose estimation; robots remain sensitive to object and pose variation (pqac-00000045, pqac-00000047) | **Shared — change plan.** Person opens packages and resolves exceptions; robots move standardized totes and known parts to inspection or kitting stations. |
| Sawing bar stock | Medium | Requires a guarded automatic saw, stock feeder, positive stops, chip control, and safe machine interface; loose long stock is awkward for a mobile cobot. | **Shared — change plan.** Person stages long/heavy stock and sets the first cut; robot can load short standardized blanks and unload cut pieces after validation. |
| CNC mill loading/unloading, ordinary parts | High | Chips or coolant on jaws and datums, part orientation, door/chuck interlocks, tool clearance, and verification that the workpiece is fully seated; mobile tending remains largely prototype-level when the environment is not tightly structured (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000038) | **Robot with human setup — retain plan.** Automate repeat loads only after CAM, first article, fixture setup, and a proven cleaning/seating routine. |
| CNC mill loading/unloading, 34.7 kg plate | Not Feasible | Plate exceeds the two arms’ combined nominal 25 kg payload before grippers, vacuum tooling, load sharing uncertainty, dynamics, and safety margin; coordinated mobile bases are not a certified lifting device. | **Human with rated vacuum lifter — retain plan.** Do not attempt dual-arm lifting; use a hoist, lifter, or higher-payload industrial robot. |
| CNC lathe loading/unloading | High | Chuck cleanliness, jaw engagement, bar protrusion, part ejection, slippery coolant-covered parts, and confirmation of clamp state. | **Robot with human setup — retain plan.** Use chuck confirmation, force-monitored insertion, part-present sensing, and periodic human inspection. |
| Deburring | Medium | Burr location and size vary; compliant tooling can miss edges or alter critical sealing surfaces, threads, and precision bores. Force-based processes add tooling and commissioning complexity (pqac-00000051) | **Shared — change plan.** Robot only repeatable external edges with compliant tools; person handles one-off internal features, sealing lands, threads, and final tactile inspection. |
| Ultrasonic washing | High | Basket loading, liquid carryover, drying, contamination control, and chemical exposure; process must be enclosed and interlocked. | **Robot — retain plan.** Use standardized perforated baskets, drip dwell, rinse/dry stations, and presence checks. |
| Measuring and inspection with machine-readable instruments | Medium | Reliable datum seating, probe access, calibration, temperature, part identification, and interpreting unexpected results; perception and sensor robustness remain deployment barriers (pqac-00000045, pqac-00000047) | **Shared — change plan.** Robot can operate digital calipers, height probes, vision, or a gauging fixture; person owns the inspection plan, calibration, ambiguous readings, and first-article disposition. |
| Kitting parts onto the long table | High | Long-table reach requires multiple docks; kit verification, small-part singulation, and preventing wrong-part placement. Fiducial or station calibration can reduce mobile-position error substantially, but task-level local sensing remains necessary (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000035) | **Robot — retain plan.** Use compartmented trays, barcodes or vision, AprilTag registration, and a weight or vision completeness check. |
| FKM O-rings in open face glands | Medium | Elastomer variability, lubrication, stretch control, twist, incomplete seating, and weak visual contrast. | **Robot after development — retain only conditionally.** Dedicated expanding mandrel or ring-loader plus 360° post-install inspection is credible; keep a person for exceptions and leak-critical acceptance. |
| FKM O-rings in half-dovetail grooves | Low | The ring must be stretched, rolled under the retaining lip, and seated without shaving or twist; force alone does not prove correct circumferential seating. | **Shared — change plan.** Treat as a development task, not assumed production capability; robot may install with a custom roller, but require human inspection or validated full-circumference machine vision. |
| FKM O-rings in internal rod-gland grooves | Low | Occluded groove, restricted tool access, risk of cutting, twist or partial seating, and difficult inspection. | **Human initially — change plan.** Automate only after a dedicated insertion cartridge or mandrel and borescope inspection demonstrate high reliability on representative glands. |
| M3–M6 stainless screws with straight nutrunner | High | Screw presentation, bit engagement, hole localization, stainless galling, cross-threading, torque scatter, and dropped screws. Torque–angle windows and torque-rate monitoring detect abnormal tightening, but require teaching and validation; reported general fault-recognition results are about 90–97%, not zero-defect assurance (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040) | **Robot — retain plan.** Use captive or vacuum-fed screws, low-speed thread start, axial compliance, reverse-to-find-thread, torque–angle trace storage, lubrication or approved anti-seize, and human review of rejects. |
| M3–M6 stainless screws with angle-head nutrunner | Medium | Restricted access, reaction torque, tool-body collision, visibility, bit alignment, and reduced ability to recover from a bad start. | **Shared — change plan.** Automate only accessible, well-fixtured locations; redesign for straight access where possible and leave obstructed fasteners to a person. |
| Dowel pins | High | Tight alignment, chamfer condition, incorrect pin length, excessive insertion force, and false seating. Force/torque search and compliant peg-in-hole methods are established for structured assembly (pqac-00000025) | **Robot — retain plan.** Use force-limited spiral search and verify final depth plus force–displacement signature. Use a press for true interference fits. |
| Helical inserts | Medium | Tang pickup, insert compression, pitch following, cross-threading, depth control, and tang break/removal verification. | **Shared — change plan.** Robot can install repetitive inserts with a dedicated driver; person verifies threads, depth, tang removal, and repairs failures. |
| Bearings | Medium | Must press on the correct race, maintain squareness, control force, protect cleanliness, and distinguish seating from jamming. | **Shared — change plan.** Robot transfers and starts bearings; a guided instrumented press performs insertion and records force–displacement. Person handles high-value or ambiguous fits. |
| Ball screw and linear rail | Medium | Long flexible components, contamination, alignment, rail straightness, staged tightening, preload, and risk of damaging ball circuits or wipers. | **Shared — change plan.** Robots can place rails and drive accessible screws in sequence; person aligns, indicates, checks smoothness/preload, and handles the ball screw installation. |
| Cartridge heaters and thermocouples | Medium | Fragile leads, bore fit, thermal compound, bend-radius control, retention, polarity/type identification, and subsequent cable routing. | **Shared — change plan.** Robot may insert rigid cartridges and probes into clean, chamfered bores; person routes and terminates leads and verifies continuity and insulation. |
| Optics: galvo scan head placement | Medium | Cleanliness, mass properties, connector protection, precision datums, optical alignment, and calibration; scanner performance depends on machine-specific assembly and optical errors (pqac-00000045) | **Shared — change plan.** Robot may transfer and bolt a capped scan head onto kinematic datums; person performs cleanliness inspection, optical alignment, calibration, and acceptance. |
| Optics: f-theta lens on M85 thread | Low | Expensive fragile optic, contamination, fine-thread start, large threaded diameter, uncertain starting phase, cross-threading, galling or binding, and torque without damaging the lens. | **Human — change plan.** Install in a clean area with purpose-made support and torque method; robotic installation is unjustified for one unit unless the lens cartridge is redesigned with lead-ins and error-proofing. |
| Cable, hose, and fibre routing | Low | Deformable objects have high configuration uncertainty, occlusion, snagging, connector handling, bend-radius and tension constraints. Robots can route known cables through known clips with tactile sensing, but general 3-D routing remains an open problem (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021) | **Human — retain plan.** Robots may preform, present, or clip simple harness segments, but a person should route the one-off hoses, fibres, and cables. |
| TIG welding thin-wall 316L tube to flanges | Low | Joint fit-up, purge quality, distortion, arc-length and heat-input control, seam tracking, shielding, fume extraction, welding qualification, and fire hazards. In HMLV practice, programming burden can make even technically suitable cobot welding uneconomic (pqac-00000032) | **Skilled human welder — retain plan.** A one-off qualified weld is not a sensible first cobot-welding application. |
| Reading bore gauges and height gauges | Medium | Correct rocking or sweeping technique, consistent contact force, datum cleanliness, calibration, and interpretation of unstable readings. | **Human for present build — retain plan.** Automation is possible with digital gauges and dedicated fixtures, but commissioning it for one machine is unlikely to repay the effort. |
| Threading small fittings with tubes attached | Low | Flexible tube reaction, uncertain starting phase, cross-threading, wrench access, sealant control, tube twist, and inability to distinguish galling from normal tightening reliably. | **Human — retain plan.** Robot may hold or present components, but a person should start threads, control tube orientation, and make the final connection. |
| Felt-and-PTFE bore seal | Low | Soft, easily creased or damaged layers, hidden seating, friction, overlapping or split-joint control, and poor force signature. | **Human — retain plan.** Purpose-built tooling could automate a repeated design, but one-off commissioning and inspection are disproportionate. |
| Final integration: 84 kg process module onto tray | Not Feasible | Far beyond combined cobot payload; suspended-load, crush, pinch, alignment, and mobile-base stability hazards. | **Person directing a rated hoist — retain plan.** Robots may observe fiducials or guide alignment only if they never support or enter beneath the suspended load. |
