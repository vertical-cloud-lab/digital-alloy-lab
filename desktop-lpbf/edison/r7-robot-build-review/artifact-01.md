| Failure Mode | Severity (High/Medium/Low) | Detection Method | Mitigation Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| AMR docking or station-positioning error | High | Verify the AprilTag pose, reprojection residual, tag count and station transform before every manipulation; probe three or more fixed station datums with the wrist tool; reject transforms outside a validated covariance envelope. SLAM-only errors of 50–100 mm have been reported, versus ≤10 mm with ARTags; calibrated station referencing has achieved approximately ±0.12 mm in a structured example (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000035). | Use positive mechanical docking or wheel brakes, guarded fiducials, redundant tags, local datum probing and a station-specific transform—not AMR pose alone. Permit only slow free-space motion until registration passes; abort and redock on disagreement between vision and touch-off results. |
| Chips or coolant contaminating fixtures or parts | High | Dedicated close-up vision of jaws and datums; air-pressure/flow monitoring; force–displacement signature during seating; fixture gap, vacuum or part-present sensors; inspect the first part after every intervention. | Add chip-resistant jaws and datum geometry, directed air blast or wash/dry cycles, coolant-compatible grippers and protected optics. Require a clean–inspect–load–seat–verify sequence; route exceptions to a person rather than repeating a forceful insertion. |
| Part pose error in the gripper or fixture | High | Wrist-camera pose estimate immediately after pickup and again before insertion; gripper-width or jaw-position check; mass/vacuum confirmation; force-controlled guarded approach. Robotic deployment remains sensitive to object and pose variation, and force/torque data should be interpreted relative to part geometry (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000045). | Use keyed nests, asymmetrical grasp features, hard stops, poka-yoke fixtures and constrained part presentation. Regrasp or reject if independent vision and gripper-state estimates disagree; never compensate for a large pose error by forcing the part into place. |
| Cross-threading of stainless screws | High | Record torque versus angle from initial engagement onward; flag early torque rise, insufficient free-run angle, abnormal torque rate or a trace outside the taught envelope. General screwdriving-fault classifiers report roughly 90–97% recognition, but this is not zero-defect assurance (pqac-00000039, pqac-00000040). | Use chamfered holes, axial compliance, low-speed thread start and a reverse rotation to locate the thread lead. Stop at a low snug-torque threshold, back out and retry only once; quarantine repeated failures for manual thread inspection. |
| Galling of stainless-steel fasteners | High | Detect a rapid or irregular torque increase before expected seating angle, stick–slip in the torque trace, abnormal tool current or heat, and failure to back out at low reverse torque. Torque-only inference is uncertain because friction can cause errors approaching 50% (pqac-00000008). | Use compatible fastener grades/coatings, approved anti-seize or pre-applied lubricant, clean threads and slow driving speed. Avoid prevailing-torque hardware unless validated; define conservative torque–angle windows and discard—not reuse—any suspect screw or threaded component. |
| Dropped part or screw | High | Gripper-position, vacuum and pressure switches; load or wrist-force check after pickup; downward-looking vision over the transfer path; tray weight or receiving-nest part-present sensor. Threaded-part insertion experiments show that a poorly aligned part can hang insecurely and fall on release (pqac-00000025). | Use fail-safe normally closed gripping where practical, dual vacuum channels with check valves, retained or captive screws, minimum safe grip margins and low transfer heights. Provide catch trays and exclusion zones; stop the cell and reconcile inventory after every detected drop. |
| O-ring twist, cut or incomplete seating | High | Full-circumference machine vision or borescope inspection for continuity, protrusion and surface damage; compare installation force and tool travel with validated envelopes; perform leak or pressure-decay testing after closure. Force alone cannot prove untwisted seating in an occluded groove. | Control lubrication, ring orientation, stretch and insertion speed; use dedicated expanding mandrels, rollers or installation cartridges with rounded edges. Require validated automated inspection for open glands and human borescope inspection for half-dovetail or internal rod glands until process capability is demonstrated. |
| Fixture or vise seating failure | High | Verify clamp-state signal and commanded pressure; measure part-to-datum gap with displacement, proximity or vision sensing; compare insertion force–travel and work-offset probing with a golden cycle; monitor the first machining cut for anomalous load. | Clean and inspect datums before every load; use chip-shedding fixture geometry, positive locating pins, controlled clamping and redundant part-present sensing. Do not start the CNC from the clamp signal alone; require independent seating confirmation. |
| Tool breakage or severe wear during machining | High | CNC spindle-load and axis-current limits, acoustic-emission or vibration monitoring, tool-length probing after critical operations, in-process probing and dimensional checks; monitor coolant flow and machine alarms. | Retain human CAM, tooling and first-article approval; use conservative parameters, sister tools, tool-life counters and mandatory probing after high-risk cuts. Interlock unloading until the machining cycle and tool-integrity checks both pass. |
| Robot collision with CNC, fixture or workpiece | High | Safety-rated joint torque or force limits, independent safe-position or envelope monitoring, CNC door/chuck/spindle interlocks and guarded low-speed approach; validate stopping performance throughout the workspace. System-level integration and combined mobile-platform/manipulator hazards must be assessed, rather than treating the arm and base separately (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016). | Define station-specific safe corridors and forbidden zones; mechanically stabilize the base, verify registration before entering the machine and use reduced speed near hard structures. Apply safety-rated monitored stop or speed-and-separation monitoring where people can enter; perform recovery only under controlled manual mode. |
| Optics contamination or handling damage | High | Inspect capped components and mounting surfaces under controlled illumination; particle, fluorescence or dark-field inspection where justified; monitor gripper force and verify protective caps remain present until final installation. | Keep optics in sealed trays and assemble in a clean enclosure using non-shedding tooling, gloves and filtered air. Have a person perform final cleanliness acceptance, M85 lens threading, optical alignment and galvo calibration because scanner performance is sensitive to assembly-specific optical and mechanical errors. |
| Wrong part picked or assembled | High | Barcode, Data Matrix or RFID identity check at source and destination; vision confirmation of geometry and orientation; kit-tray weight/completeness check; recipe-to-part-number interlock and fastener-count reconciliation. Reliable object recognition and coordination remain manufacturing-robot deployment barriers (pqac-00000045, pqac-00000047). | Use one-part-per-pocket kits, uniquely keyed nests, segregated fastener feeders and electronic work instructions tied to the serial-number build record. Require two independent identifiers for look-alike or safety-critical parts and stop for human disposition on any mismatch. |


*Table: Key failure modes for the proposed mobile-manipulator machining and assembly cell, with practical detection and mitigation controls. Severity reflects potential damage to personnel, machines, high-value components, or latent product quality.*
