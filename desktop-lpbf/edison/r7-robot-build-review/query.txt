We are checking, in simulation, whether two collaborative robot arms (Universal Robots UR12e, the renamed UR10e: 12.5 kg payload including tooling, 1300 mm reach, built-in force/torque sensing), each on a MiR250 mobile base (docking to markers within about +-3 mm, then re-registering each station from an AprilTag with a wrist camera), could make and assemble a one-off desktop laser powder bed fusion machine in a university shop: unpacking deliveries, sawing bar, loading and unloading a Haas VF-4 vertical mill (with an HRT210 4th axis) and a Haas ST-10-class CNC lathe, deburring, ultrasonic washing, measuring, kitting parts onto a long table, then assembling about 90 steps: FKM O-rings in face glands, half-dovetail grooves and internal rod-gland grooves (with ring loaders, rollers and mandrels as end-of-arm tools), about 130 M3-M6 stainless screws driven with a nutrunner and an angle-head nutrunner, dowel pins, helical inserts, bearings, a ball screw and linear rail, cartridge heaters and thermocouples, optics (a galvo scan head, an f-theta lens on an M85 thread), and a final integration where a person with a hoist moves the 84 kg process module onto a storage-system tray. Our current plan keeps a person for: loading a 34.7 kg aluminium plate blank onto the mill with a vacuum tube lifter, CAM and first-article runs, tool setting, TIG welding a thin-wall 316L tube to flanges, reading bore and height gauges, routing hoses, fibres and cables, threading small fittings with tubes attached, installing a felt-and-PTFE bore seal, and lifting anything over the arms' combined payload.

Please review this plan critically, with sources: (1) published examples and industrial practice of mobile manipulators (AMR plus cobot) tending CNC machines and doing assembly in high-mix, low-volume or one-off settings: achieved positioning accuracy after docking and re-registration, uptime and failure rates, cycle-time penalties, commissioning effort per new task, and what still needed people; (2) which of the steps above are realistic for cobots today and which are not, and whether our person-only list is too long or too short (for example: can cobots route cables and hoses, install felt seals, TIG weld one-off parts, read bore gauges, thread fittings with tubing attached, handle 15-35 kg plates as a pair); (3) the hardest failure modes to expect (chips and coolant, fixture seating, part pose errors, cross-threading and galling of stainless screws, dropping parts, O-ring twist), and how they are detected; (4) safety for a shop where people and mobile manipulators work together: ISO 10218-1/-2 (2025), ISO/TS 15066, ANSI/A3 R15.08 for industrial mobile robots, and what a risk assessment for this cell would need; (5) a realistic estimate of the person-hours and calendar time to commission such a cell for one machine compared with simply building it by hand. Give concrete numbers where they exist and say clearly where you are estimating.
