A university research lab is designing a small laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) machine: a 500 W continuous-wave single-mode 1070 nm fiber laser (IPG YLR-500 class, Class 4) with a galvo scanner and f-theta lens over a 25 to 75 mm heated (200 C) build plate, in a sealed aluminium chamber that is pumped to about 1 mbar then held under argon at +5 to +20 mbar with O2 below 100 ppm, processing aluminium alloy powders (AlSi10Mg, Al-Mg-Sc, 6xxx/7xxx with grain refiners; Mg-bearing powders), with a sliding interlocked laser guard (OD 7+ window) and a recirculating argon loop with a cyclone and HEPA filter. The machine is meant to sit on a tray of a vertical lift module (Kardex Shuttle) and be tended by a UR12e cobot on a MiR250 mobile base (plate exchange, door, drawer, reservoir swaps).

Please give a sourced, practical safety requirements review a laser safety officer and EHS office would expect, covering: (1) laser: ANSI Z136.1 and Z136.9 (manufacturing), IEC 60825-1 and IEC 60825-4 (guards: passive guard materials and the exposure limits for 500 W at 1070 nm, how long aluminium or steel sheet survives), ISO 11553-1 (laser processing machines), making the machine Class 1 in operation and what changes during service, interlocks (two-channel, guard locking, shutter, safe torque off), viewing windows, beam dumps, back-reflection from aluminium, NHZ, eyewear OD, SOPs and training; (2) inert gas: argon asphyxiation in a small room and inside an enclosed vertical lift module, O2 monitoring and alarm set points (19.5 percent), ventilation, cylinder and regulator safety, over-pressure relief; vacuum and viewport implosion; (3) combustible metal powder: NFPA 484 and NFPA 652 (dust hazard analysis), Kst and MIE of AlSi10Mg and other Al powders, wet versus dry filters, condensate and soot from LPBF filters (pyrophoric filter cake), passivation, vacuums rated for combustible dust, grounding and bonding, PPE and respirators, storage; (4) the heated plate and hot surfaces; (5) the robot and mobile robot: ISO 10218-1/-2:2025, ISO/TS 15066, ANSI/A3 R15.08-1/-2, risk assessment (ISO 12100) for a cobot that reaches into a laser machine; (6) incidents and lessons from LPBF and laser labs (fires, filter fires, powder explosions, laser burns). Where possible, cite university EHS laser safety manuals and lab guidance, and note anything specific to Brigham Young University's published laser safety, compressed gas or combustible dust programs. Give concrete numbers and say clearly where you are estimating.
