| Parameter | Recommended values / design implications | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rod diameter | **0.8–3.0 mm** is the published practical range for informative AE transmission in rod/wire guides. A **1.59 mm** diameter was experimentally/model validated; increasing diameter raised the A0 amplitude, which approached saturation near **4.77 mm**, while 1.59 mm matched the sensor tip and preserved S0 response. | Zakharov et al. (2010); Zelenyak et al. (2015) (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009) |
| Material | Prefer **stainless/carbon steel** for strength and weldability or **alumina** for electrical/thermal isolation. In an aluminium-plate model, steel and alumina had approximately **3.5 dB relative reduction**, brass about **5 dB**, and aluminium about **10 dB**; impedance matching and temperature-dependent properties must be considered. | Zelenyak et al. (2015); Kazys and Vaskeliene (2021) (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) |
| Length | Use the shortest length that keeps the sensor cool and separates early reflections: approximately **100–300 mm** for a 200 °C plate; up to **700 mm** for much hotter sources. Published examples include **145 mm**, **276–306 mm**, **600 mm**, and **700 mm**. Each additional **76.5 mm** produced about **15 µs** arrival delay in one model, but little change in the relative FFT spectrum. | Zelenyak et al. (2015); Kazys and Vaskeliene (2021) (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) |
| Thermal reduction capability | A **145 mm** tapered stainless-steel or titanium rod reduced a **200 °C tip to about 50 °C** at the transducer. A **700 mm SS304** wire/strip guide reduced a **600 °C tip to about 26 °C**. Thermal performance depends strongly on diameter, convection, clamping and heat sinking. | Kazys and Vaskeliene (2021) (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) |
| Signal loss versus direct mounting | A **1.59 mm × 306 mm aluminium** waveguide produced approximately **13 dB amplitude loss** relative to direct mounting. Material comparisons showed aluminium around **10 dB** reduction versus about **3.5 dB** for steel/alumina; these values are geometry- and mode-dependent rather than universal per-unit-length attenuation constants. | Zelenyak et al. (2015) (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000009) |
| Hot-end tip/contact | Use a flat, polished tip normal to the plate for repeatable spring contact, or weld/braze the guide where permanence is acceptable. Published terminating concentrators include **stepwise, conical and exponential** profiles; the **exponential concentrator** was reported to minimize reflection and distortion when matching a thin rod to a larger sensor face. Hemispherical and inclined tips are specialized alternatives for focusing or mode conversion. | Zakharov et al. (2010); Kazys and Vaskeliene (2021) (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010) |
| Dispersion and modes | Cylindrical rods support multiple **Pochhammer–Chree longitudinal, flexural and torsional modes**; group velocity varies with frequency–diameter product, broadening an impulsive AE waveform and biasing arrival-time location. Thin rods limit the number of modes but can reduce amplitude. Zelenyak’s plate/rod system was dominated by **A0**, with S0 also identifiable; rectangular strips can support a less-dispersive SH mode. Calibrate using the actual guide, band and temperature. | Zakharov et al. (2010); Zelenyak et al. (2015); Kazys and Vaskeliene (2021) (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011) |
| Spring/contact force | A published spring-loaded acoustic coupler used **2–5 lbf (9–22 N)** at the rod tip. Treat this as a starting range: use a Belleville or coil spring to maintain constant load through thermal cycling, then optimize force by pencil-lead-break transfer measurements while checking that the guide does not indent or constrain the plate. | Jolly (1994) (pqac-00000037, pqac-00000038) |
| Practical starting design for a 200 °C LPBF plate | Start with a **1.5–2.0 mm diameter, 150–250 mm long 304/316 stainless-steel rod**, a polished flat hot tip, **10–20 N** spring preload, and a larger cool-end shoe/concentrator matched to the sensor. Expect roughly **5–15 dB** insertion loss and determine the actual 100–500 kHz transfer function after installation. | Engineering synthesis of the cited waveguide results (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000037) |
