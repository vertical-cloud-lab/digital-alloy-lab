We are designing a small research laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) machine for aluminium alloys with interchangeable heated build modules: Ø25, Ø50 and Ø75 mm plates on a 6061 heater block with 24 V cartridge heaters, a 200 °C set point, a Macor standoff thermal break, a 316L build cylinder (40 mm stroke) sealed with FKM O-rings under a 6061 chamber floor, and an NdFeB pot-magnet Z coupling at the rod end. Our axisymmetric finite-element model says each module loses only 30-97 W at 200 °C with the laser off, while a dense Al layout at 350 W (absorptance about 0.45, beam on 50-70 % of each layer cycle) deposits a time-averaged 80-110 W. The heater then switches off and the plate drifts to about 290 °C by the end of the stroke unless the module is cooled. Separately, the 500 W single-mode fibre laser we were quoted is forced-air cooled (1.7 kW drawn, 10-35 °C ambient) and would sit in an enclosed vertical-lift storage module.

Please review the peer-reviewed literature (and machine-builder documentation where it is the only source), with citations and numbers:
1. Measured build-plate, substrate or part temperature rise from laser heat input during LPBF, with and without platform heating: how far above the set point, over how many layers, and how it scales with scanned area fraction, interlayer time and plate size (small plates especially).
2. How commercial and research LPBF machines with platform heating (EOS, SLM/Nikon, Aconity, Trumpf, Renishaw, open-architecture rigs) take heat out of the build cylinder or platform: water-cooled cylinders or piston heads, interlayer dwell, closed-loop platform temperature control; reported overshoot or control performance.
3. What uncontrolled platform temperature drift (for example from 200 to 250-300 °C) does to Al alloys (AlSi10Mg, Al-Mg-Sc-Zr, 6xxx and 7xxx): in-situ ageing or overageing, Mg and Zn evaporation, cracking, density, residual stress and dimensional accuracy.
4. Temperature limits and design practice for parts near heated build platforms: FKM seals, NdFeB versus SmCo magnets, piston seal friction at temperature.
5. Air-cooled fibre lasers of a few hundred watts in enclosed cabinets or automated cells: reported derating, failures or ambient-temperature problems, and the trade-off against water-cooled units.
