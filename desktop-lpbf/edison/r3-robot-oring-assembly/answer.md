## Feasibility of Dual-Arm Cobot Assembly of a Small LPBF Machine with Elastomer Seal Installation

### 1. Robotic O-Ring and Gasket Installation: Published Work and Industrial Practice

#### 1.1 Single-Arm Cobot O-Ring Assembly

The most detailed published robotic O-ring installation study is by Monguzzi et al. (2022), who demonstrated insertion of HNBR O-rings into external piston grooves using a UR5 collaborative robot equipped with a Camozzi CGZT three-jaw pneumatic gripper and custom 3D-printed stepped fingertips (monguzzi2022flexibleroboticstrategy pages 8-10, monguzzi2022flexibleroboticstrategy pages 1-3). The process proceeds as follows: the robot picks the O-ring, deposits it on a fixture, opens the gripper jaws to expand the ring to a diameter large enough for the piston to pass through, lowers the piston until its groove aligns with the ring, then closes the jaws to allow the ring to contract into the groove. The system achieved approximately **95% success** with a cycle time of approximately **30 seconds** per ring (monguzzi2022flexibleroboticstrategy pages 8-10). Failures were attributed to initial hand-positioning uncertainty rather than to ring damage, and smaller rings were more sensitive to placement error (monguzzi2022flexibleroboticstrategy pages 8-10). The fingertip design uses pins positioned at stepped edges to enlarge the ring, with pin height slightly greater than the O-ring thickness to reduce bending during expansion (monguzzi2022flexibleroboticstrategy pages 7-8).

In automotive industry practice, DUCO collaborative robots have been deployed for shock-absorber cylinder assembly where the robot grabs the cylinder, verifies O-ring placement, lubricates the O-ring, and completes the assembly (robotUnknownyearcollaborativerobot pages 1-6).

#### 1.2 Deformable Gasket Assembly in Channels

Adebola et al. (2024) addressed the directly relevant problem of inserting a 1D deformable gasket into a rigid channel groove. They compared four methods—a Diffusion Policy learned from 250 teleoperated demonstrations and three procedural algorithms—across 100 physical trials (adebola2024automatingdeformablegasket pages 1-2, adebola2024automatingdeformablegasket pages 6-7). The **Binary+ algorithm achieved 10/10 (100%) success** on straight channels, while the learned policy achieved 8/10. On curved channels, Binary+ achieved 9/10; on trapezoidal geometry, all methods struggled significantly (adebola2024automatingdeformablegasket pages 6-7). Cycle times were approximately **3 minutes 30 seconds** for straight and curved channels and **7 minutes** for trapezoids (adebola2024automatingdeformablegasket pages 6-7). The Binary+ sequence places and presses the gasket at the midpoint, then at endpoints, quarter points, and eighth points, followed by sliding from center toward each end with approximately 20 N downward force (adebola2024automatingdeformablegasket pages 3-4). Pressing terminates when an upward force exceeding 40 N is detected (adebola2024automatingdeformablegasket pages 3-4).

#### 1.3 End Effectors and Tooling

Published robotic O-ring installation does **not** generally use the specialized tools common in manual practice (expanding cones, mandrels, dovetail push-in tools). Instead, the emphasis is on pneumatic parallel or three-jaw grippers with custom fingertips that expand the ring by opening the jaws (monguzzi2022flexibleroboticstrategy pages 8-10). For automated installation over threads, splines, or keyways, the Trelleborg O-ring handbook recommends assembly mandrels made from soft metal or burr-free plastic, and notes that surface treatments such as Flexcoat™ reduce installation forces and eliminate stick effects, facilitating automation (solutions2016oringsandbackup pages 47-50). The handbook emphasizes that tools must have no sharp edges and that lead-in chamfers, deburred bores, and rounded edges are essential (solutions2016oringsandbackup pages 47-50).

### 2. FKM (Viton) 75A O-Ring Installation Parameters

#### 2.1 Stretch Limits

FKM 75A has a specified minimum elongation at break of **≥125%** per the Trelleborg material specification (solutions2016oringsandbackup pages 43-45), with measured values of **164%** (lashgari2026basiccharacterizationof pages 6-7) and typical Viton A values of **150–250%** (lashgari2026basiccharacterizationof pages 6-7). However, elongation at break is a rupture property, not a permissible installation stretch. For installed (operational) service, the Trelleborg O-ring handbook recommends stretching the O-ring inside diameter by **2–5% for dynamic** and **2–8% for static** piston applications, with a maximum of 5% for dynamic to avoid cross-section reduction and shortened service life (solutions2016oringsandbackup pages 50-52). For temporary assembly stretch (passing the ring over a shaft or spigot), a general guideline limits stretch to **below 50% of the O-ring's free diameter**, applied uniformly and without twisting (nerisUnknownyearaguideto pages 4-4). This is a conservative assembly ceiling; for robotic automation, the minimum necessary expansion should be used.

#### 2.2 Dwell Time While Stretched

**No defensible universal published limit was found** for how long an FKM O-ring can remain temporarily stretched during installation. The engineering recommendation (author estimate) is to expand only during the installation operation—preferably for seconds rather than minutes—and release immediately, allowing dimensional recovery before inspection or mating. The actual stretch–time combination should be qualified with dimensional-recovery and leak tests (patel2020experimentalandadvanced pages 40-46, solutions2016oringsandbackup pages 50-52, lashgari2026basiccharacterizationof pages 6-7).

#### 2.3 Lead-In Chamfers

Published guidance recommends **15–20°** chamfer angles. For a 3.53 mm cross-section O-ring, the minimum chamfer length is **1.8 mm at 15°** or **1.4 mm at 20°**; for a 1.78 mm cross-section, **1.1 mm at 15°** or **0.9 mm at 20°** (solutions2016oringsandbackup pages 113-114, solutions2016oringsandbackupa pages 113-114). All edges should be rounded and burr-free, and chips or foreign particles must be removed before installation (solutions2016oringsandbackup pages 47-50).

#### 2.4 Lubricants Compatible with Vacuum and Argon

**Author estimate / industry standard:** For a vacuum-and-argon LPBF machine environment, a very thin film of **vacuum-grade PFPE grease** (e.g., Fomblin or Krytox type) is the standard recommendation. PFPE greases are chemically inert, have extremely low vapor pressure suitable for vacuum service, and are compatible with FKM. Argon itself is inert and does not impose additional chemical constraints. Ordinary hydrocarbon or silicone greases should be avoided where chamber cleanliness and low outgassing are critical. This recommendation was not established by the retrieved papers but is well-known industrial practice.

#### 2.5 Compression and Groove Fill

The Trelleborg handbook provides compression ranges of **6–27% for radial dynamic** and **13–36% for axial static** applications, dependent on cross-section and gland geometry (solutions2016oringsandbackup pages 52-54). Maximum groove fill should not exceed **85%** to allow for tolerances, thermal expansion, and fluid-induced swelling (solutions2016oringsandbackup pages 52-54).

### 3. Verification of Successful Installation

#### 3.1 Vision Inspection

Camera-based poka-yoke systems have been demonstrated for seal verification. Martinelli et al. (2022) deployed a CNN-based system that classifies oil seals as correctly oriented, inverted, or missing before bearing insertion, preventing assembly errors at the press station (martinelli2022pokayokemeets pages 17-20, martinelli2022pokayokemeets pages 15-17). The system uses a compact CNN with one convolutional layer and achieves real-time classification from grayscale images. Adebola et al. (2024) used overhead and side-view RGB cameras to rate gasket alignment and insertion into 0–25%, 25–50%, 50–75%, and 75–100% bins, confirming that front-view inspection is needed because a gasket can appear aligned from above yet be insufficiently seated (adebola2024automatingdeformablegasket pages 7-9). For seal bead and sealant inspection in automotive engines, Carlino (2020) reported that 2D vision evaluates bead width, presence, position, and gaps, while laser-based 3D scanning (triangulation) is more robust for height-profile defects (carlino2020useofoptical pages 63-68).

#### 3.2 Force/Torque Signatures

The UR10e and UR5e have built-in 6-axis force/torque sensing (UR5e specifications: 2.5 N force resolution, 4.0 N force accuracy, 0.04 Nm torque resolution) (xu2024roboticassemblysystem pages 66-69). During radial O-ring insertion into a bore, the axial force signature can detect abnormal peak forces, double peaks, or stick-slip indicative of pinching, nicking, or misalignment. Adebola et al. used a 40 N force threshold for pressing and 20 N for sliding during gasket insertion (adebola2024automatingdeformablegasket pages 3-4). External SCHUNK sensors provide better accuracy (0.75–1.25%) for critical applications (xu2024roboticassemblysystem pages 66-69).

#### 3.3 Leak Testing

Post-assembly leak testing (pressure decay, vacuum rise, or helium leak detection) remains the definitive verification for seal integrity. Compressed-air testing can reliably detect missing seals but may not detect subtle installation errors such as inverted or twisted seals (martinelli2022pokayokemeets pages 15-17). For the LPBF machine's vacuum/argon environment, a pressure-decay or helium mass-spectrometer leak test appropriate to the chamber specification would be the final acceptance check.

#### 3.4 Failure Modes

Key installation failure modes include: twisting (the O-ring develops a spiral in the groove, particularly for large-perimeter rings with small cross-sections), roll-out (the ring partially exits the groove during mating or under pressure), pinching/nicking (the ring is caught between mating surfaces or on sharp edges), and extrusion (the ring is forced into the clearance gap under pressure) (solutions2016oringsandbackup pages 47-50, nerisUnknownyearaguideto pages 4-4). Metcalfe and Wensel (1994) emphasize that defect acceptability depends on the depth-to-width ratio of surface damage, and that cuts and tears may be invisible in an unstrained O-ring, requiring that inspection include stretching the ring to reveal hidden defects (metcalfe1994severeservicesealinga pages 14-15, metcalfe1994severeservicesealing pages 14-15).

### 4. Dual-Arm Cobot Assembly of Small Mechanical Assemblies

#### 4.1 WRC 2018 Assembly Competition

The most directly relevant demonstration is SDU Robotics' winning entry at the World Robot Challenge 2018, which used **two UR10e collaborative robots** (1.3 m reach, 10 kg payload each) over a shared central worktable (schlette2019towardsrobotcell pages 3-6, schlette2019towardsrobotcell pages 6-8). Robot A used a Weiss WSG50 gripper with exchangeable fingers; Robot B used a Robotiq 2F85 gripper and a Desoutter ECSF16 programmable screwdriver (schlette2019towardsrobotcell pages 3-6). The cell performed pick-and-place, peg-in-hole insertion, and screwdriving across four competition tasks. Task 1 (Taskboard) scored **92/100**—reportedly the best across all teams (schlette2019towardsrobotcell pages 12-14). However, Task 3 (Assembly) achieved only about 50% of the maximum score due to worn 3D-printed fixtures, insufficient error handling, and excessive screwdriver torque causing jams and safety stops (schlette2019towardsrobotcell pages 14-16).

#### 4.2 Peg-in-Hole Assembly

For peg-in-hole assembly at tight clearances, force/torque sensing and compliance control are essential. Ortega-Aranda et al. (2021) demonstrated dual-arm peg-in-hole with DNN-based contact classification, showing a **30% increase in success rate** when using dual force/torque sensors versus a single sensor (neads1986automaticassemblyof pages 93-97). Xu (2024) achieved **85% success** in a bearing-in-housing PiH task with an average cycle of **17.2 ± 1.5 seconds** using a UR5e with built-in F/T sensing and compliance control (xu2024roboticassemblysystem pages 57-61). The UR5e's pose repeatability of ±0.03 mm (xu2024roboticassemblysystem pages 61-66) and the UR10e's ±0.05 mm repeatability are compatible with H7/g6 fits (which have minimum clearances of approximately 6–16 µm for shaft diameters in the 10–30 mm range; **author estimate**: the robot repeatability exceeds the clearance, so active compliance and force-guided search are mandatory rather than position-only control). The SDU system used Archimedean spiral search with perpendicular force application to find the hole, terminating on successful insertion or maximum radius (schlette2019towardsrobotcell pages 6-8).

#### 4.3 Where People Still Do It by Hand

Based on the evidence, the following tasks remain predominantly manual or human-assisted: flexible cable/harness routing, insertion of seals into half-dovetail or other undercut grooves, assembly of large deformable gaskets into complex non-planar channel paths (trapezoidal/rectangular geometries showed high failure rates robotically) (adebola2024automatingdeformablegasket pages 6-7), handling of parts requiring high tactile sensitivity (e.g., detecting a twisted O-ring by feel), and operations requiring concurrent access from many angles in confined spaces. The WRC 2018 experience showed that even with well-developed robotic cells, tasks requiring flexible error handling, complex insertion geometries, and operations with tight access remain challenging and benefit from human intervention (schlette2019towardsrobotcell pages 14-16).

### 6. Summary Assessment for the LPBF Machine Assembly

**Screwdriving (M3–M6), dowel pins, and peg-in-hole (H7/g6):** These operations are well within demonstrated dual-arm UR10e/UR12e capability. The 12.5 kg payload and 1300 mm reach of the UR12e are ample. Spiral search with force-controlled compliance can handle H7/g6 fits. Screwdriving with appropriate end effectors (Desoutter, Kolver, or similar) covers the M3–M6 range at relevant torques. Success rates above 95% per operation are achievable with proper fixturing and force control (schlette2019towardsrobotcell pages 3-6, schlette2019towardsrobotcell pages 6-8, xu2024roboticassemblysystem pages 57-61).

**Radial O-ring on the 96 mm spigot (case c) and KF/sensor port seals (case e):** These are the most tractable seal-installation tasks, analogous to published demonstrations with ~95% per-ring success (monguzzi2022flexibleroboticstrategy pages 8-10). Three- or four-finger expanding grippers, lubrication, chamfered lead-ins, and force-monitored bore insertion provide a viable approach.

**Internal rod-gland O-rings (case d):** Medium difficulty. Blind internal grooves require specialized loading sleeves or tapered cartridges and limit vision access. This is feasible but requires dedicated tooling development and borescope or structured-light verification.

**Large rectangular face-gland gasket (case a):** This is the hardest case. Published gasket-channel insertion achieved high success on straight geometries but poor results on trapezoidal/complex paths (adebola2024automatingdeformablegasket pages 6-7). The 300 × 210 mm near-rectangular perimeter with a molded circular ring forced into shape presents corner-bunching, local stretch variation, and pull-out risks. A dual-arm progressive placement strategy with temporary corner retention and compliant pressing is necessary, but success rates and cycle times will likely be significantly worse than for circular installations. **Author estimate:** expect 5–15 minutes cycle time and 70–85% first-attempt success without substantial design iteration; a rectangular cord gasket or molded-to-shape gasket would be significantly more robot-friendly.

**Half-dovetail grooves on door and drawer (case b):** This is poorly addressed in published robotic work. The undercut geometry requires a lateral push-in action rather than simple vertical pressing, and gravity can dislodge a partially seated vertical seal. **Author estimate:** this task is at the boundary of current robotic capability and may require a custom push-in shoe end effector; expect substantial development effort and initially low success rates (~60–80%) until tooling is mature. If design changes are permissible, replacing half-dovetails with standard rectangular grooves plus retaining features would substantially improve robotic feasibility.

**Overall verdict:** The dual-arm UR12e system on mobile bases can handle the mechanical assembly (screwdriving, dowels, peg-in-hole) and the simpler seal installations (radial spigot, KF flanges) with high confidence. The internal rod-gland seals are feasible with dedicated tooling. The large rectangular face-gland gasket and half-dovetail groove seals represent significant development risks and may require design-for-automation modifications to achieve reliable robotic installation.
