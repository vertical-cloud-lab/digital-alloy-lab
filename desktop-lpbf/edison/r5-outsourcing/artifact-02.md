| 3-axis CNC milling | **$35–75/h** | **$75–150/h** | **$100–225/h equivalent** | Planning estimates. At qty. 1–3, setup, CAM, inspection, and platform margin make an hourly conversion imprecise. A documented university case cost about **$108 internally** versus **$305–490 through Xometry, $318–326 through Fictiv, and $572–624 through Protolabs** for an aluminum upright (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016). |
| 4/5-axis CNC milling | **$60–120/h** | **$125–250/h** | **$175–350/h equivalent** | Planning estimates. Complex fixtures, probing, collision risk, and scarce capacity raise rates; unusual tool access and fixtures can materially increase price and compromise manufacturability (pqac-00000003). The lab's indexed HRT210 work is normally closer to upper-end 3-axis than simultaneous 5-axis pricing. |
| CNC turning | **$35–70/h** | **$70–140/h** | **$90–200/h equivalent** | Planning estimates. Simple rings and adapters favor in-house turning; live tooling, tight runout, deep bores, certificates, and inspection increase outsourced cost. Online quotations vary because suppliers assign setup and cycle costs differently (pqac-00000034). |
| Waterjet cutting | **$100–175/h machine time**, often **$50–150 minimum** | **$125–250/h**, often **$100–300 minimum** | Usually priced by geometry: **$75–300 minimum/order** | Planning estimates. Best used to remove the chamber-frame core or rough blanks while leaving machining allowance. Pierce count, abrasive consumption, thickness, taper, programming, and material handling dominate—not merely cutting minutes. |
| Laser cutting, sheet metal | **$75–150/h**, if campus equipment exists | **$100–225/h**, often **$75–250 minimum** | Geometry-based; commonly **$30–200 flat** or **$75–500 formed part** | Planning estimates. Online sheet specialists amortize nesting efficiently; suitable for guards, hoppers, and plenum panels. Laser-edge and bend tolerances do not establish gasket-face flatness, vacuum cleanliness, or leak tightness. |
| TIG welding, steel/aluminum | **$40–90/h** | **$90–175/h** | Rarely transparent; approximately **$125–250/h equivalent** in fabricated-part quotes | Planning estimates. Stainless back-purging, aluminum cleaning, fixturing, distortion control, weld documentation, and leak testing add cost. Finish-machine critical faces after welding. LSU's custom LPBF build used a flange welded and machined by its university shop (pqac-00000030). |
| Wire EDM | **$50–100/h** | **$100–225/h** | **$150–300/h equivalent**, often manual quote | Planning estimates. Start holes, skim passes, wire consumption, unattended burn time, inspection, and minimum charges matter. Reserve for geometries or hardened materials that cannot be milled economically—not routine brackets. |
| Honing, ID/OD | **$60–125/h** where available | **$100–225/h**, commonly **$250–750 minimum/job** | Generally manual quote; **$150–300/h equivalent** | Planning estimates. Tooling/mandrel size, stock removal, bore length, cylindricity, and measurement drive cost. Because this lab lacks honing, H7 build cylinders should go to a specialist; final honing should ordinarily follow flange welding to remove weld distortion. |
| Anodizing, Type II | Usually outside service; **$75–200 lot charge** | **$100–300 minimum lot**, then roughly **$20–100/part** | **$75–250 minimum/order** when platform-offered | Planning estimates. Masking, color matching, sealing, rack marks, dimensional growth, stripping/rework, and certification add cost. Keep O-ring faces and precision fits masked if coating thickness is not included in the tolerance. |
| Anodizing, Type III hardcoat | Usually outside service; **$150–350 lot charge** | **$200–500 minimum lot**, then roughly **$40–175/part** | **$150–400 minimum/order** when available | Planning estimates. Greater dimensional change and process variability than Type II; specify thickness, masking, sealing, and post-coating dimensions. Avoid coating vacuum-sealing faces unless explicitly designed and qualified. |
| Passivation, 316L | Usually outside service; **$100–250 lot charge** | **$150–400 minimum lot**, then roughly **$20–100/part** | **$100–350/order** when available | Planning estimates. Specify **ASTM A967/A967M** or **AMS 2700**, method, certificate, and final rinse/dry/packaging. Passivation is not electropolishing or vacuum cleaning; welded parts normally need heat-tint removal/pickling first. |
| Blanchard grinding | **$60–110/h** where available | **$100–200/h**, often **$200–500 minimum** | Usually manual quote; **$125–250/h equivalent** | Planning estimates. Useful for rapid correction of plate parallelism/flatness, but its rotary lay is not automatically suitable for an O-ring track; finish-machine or lap the actual sealing band as required. Static O-ring guidance permits approximately **Ra 1.6 µm** but rejects scratches, pits, concentric marks, and spiral scores (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014). |
| Interpretation of ranges | Academic shops often charge only direct labor/material or subsidized internal rates | Commercial rates include labor, machine ownership, overhead, tooling, QA, and profit | Platform prices bundle setup, programming, machine time, network/platform margin, QA, and sometimes freight | Except for the explicitly cited part quotations, these are **2024–2026 budgetary planning estimates**, not published tariff schedules or live quotes. Academic literature rarely reports explicit hourly rates; current released CAD, drawings, quantities, documentation, and a common inspection package should be quoted by at least three suppliers. Time-driven costing literature confirms that machine/activity duration and capacity rate are core drivers, while actual quotations also depend on batching, tooling already installed, remnants, and supplier allocation of setup versus cycle cost (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000038). |
