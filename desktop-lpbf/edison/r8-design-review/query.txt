Context: a lab-built desktop laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) machine for screening aluminium alloys (AlSi10Mg, 6061, 7075, grain-refined variants). 500 W single-mode CW fibre laser at 1070 nm into a SCANLAB basiCube 20 galvo (up to ~1.1 m/s) and a Thorlabs FTH160-1064 f-theta (f = 160 mm), ~96 um 1/e2 spot; 200-350 W; 30 um layers; plates 25-75 mm across heated to 200 C; argon at ~1 atm with a 2.5-3 m/s cross-flow (200 + 40 L/min) over the plate in a ~280 x 190 x 120 mm chamber. We modelled an in-situ monitoring design and want a critical review against the published literature and practice.

The design and what our models found:
1. Coaxial branch behind a short-pass dichroic (Thorlabs DMSP1000) that reflects 1070 nm into the galvo: a shared short-pass 1070 nm blocker; a second dichroic sends 955-990 nm to a monochrome IMX296 global-shutter camera at 7.4 um per pixel (0.94 x 0.71 mm field, ~500 fps cropped); a third splits a two-colour pair, 680-720 nm and 880-920 nm, onto two Si photodiodes (Thorlabs PDA36A2) behind 500 um pinholes (a 1.6 mm field on the plate); an optional InGaAs channel at 1625-1725 nm, or a coaxial SWIR area camera, to see the pool edge. Bands chosen to avoid the 750-875 nm stop band reported for standard 1064 nm galvo mirror coatings and the Al, Mg, K and Na lines.
2. Melt pool (3D quasi-steady conduction model with a powder layer and enhanced liquid conductivity, absorptivity 0.35 calibrated to measured AlSi10Mg track widths): at 96 um, 320 W, 1.1 m/s the pool is ~324 x 208 um on a 25 C plate and ~405 x 260 um on a 200 C plate; the surface reaches boiling from ~200 W, so a shallow vapour depression forms (transition regime), while measured tracks stay conduction-shaped to ~350 W; the 700/900 nm ratio reads ~2,570 K (the depression wall) almost independent of speed and preheat; a 12-bit silicon camera at 850-970 nm sees only surface above ~1,170 K, so it cannot see the mushy zone (750-925 K), while InGaAs at 1.55 um reaches ~800 K; the mushy zone lasts 22-72 us (AlSi10Mg, 6061) and 67-156 us (7075) at 1.1 m/s, 2-3x longer at 200 C; without skywriting the photodiode signal rises 19-31 % at track ends.
3. Plume, condensate and spatter (OpenFOAM k-omega SST, 285k cells, Lagrangian particles): ~0.47 g of condensate per hour of lasing for AlSi10Mg at 320 W; the cross-flow removes nearly all of it through the outlet; 1.5-5 % of 100-200 um Al spatter droplets reach the laser cover slide 126 mm above the plate still molten; a side window at 13 deg elevation gets at most 0.1 % of spatter; a 1-3 L/min argon purge across that window cuts condensate deposition 13-45x; a window 10 K colder than the gas collects 20-120x more condensate (thermophoresis); the plume attenuates the beam by ~0.05-0.2 %.
4. Side high-speed camera (Kron Chronos 2.1-HD) through a 31 mm port in the side wall at 13 deg elevation, 10-20 us exposures, pulsed 850 nm LED light; a steep second camera through the lid at ~60 deg.
5. Acoustic emission for cracking: a broadband sensor coupled to the plate's underside through a waveguide (or the plate's drawbar) down the hollow piston rod, preamp kept at or below 65 C, digitised at >= 2 MS/s.
6. Timing: the RTC6 scan card as the master clock; photodiodes, AE and the laser gate on one 4-channel digitizer (PicoScope 4444) whose clock is fitted every layer to the recorded laser-gate edges; a 0.25 ms galvo tracking-delay correction; camera triggers from a microcontroller gated by an RTC6 digital output.

Questions:
(a) Which of these findings conflict with published measurements or practice? In particular: the Al melt-pool regime and size at this operating point; whether a 700/900 nm ratio pyrometer on Al reads mainly the vapour depression; whether silicon cameras are effectively blind to Al solidification; how much spatter reaches cover slides; thermophoretic deposition on cooler windows; the plume attenuation.
(b) What failure modes have others hit with similar coaxial, off-axis and acoustic monitoring that this design does not address? For example scan-angle-dependent signal (vignetting) and its correction, ghost reflections and back-reflection into the coaxial path, thermal drift of dichroics and filters, emissivity changes with oxide, EMI from galvo and servo drives, AE noise from the recoater and gas loop, calibration drift, data volume.
(c) Rank the five biggest risks to getting useful data from this design and suggest the cheapest experiment to retire each.
Give quantitative evidence and sources.
