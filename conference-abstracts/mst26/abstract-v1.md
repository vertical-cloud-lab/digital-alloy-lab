## Body (~280 words)

Self-driving laboratories (SDLs) for additive manufacturing (AM) face a
structural challenge that single-step closed loops do not: the
composition-to-part pipeline spans CALPHAD screening, powder atomization,
laser powder bed fusion (LPBF), and mechanical testing, with per-experiment
costs that vary by more than two orders of magnitude (∼\$300 ultrasonic
atomization vs. costlier gas atomization vs. ∼\$1–5k per LPBF build vs. ∼\$2k
per ASTM E8 tensile bar). Treating this pipeline as a single black-box
acquisition wastes budget on uninformative expensive nodes and leaves
abundant cheap-node information on the table.

We present FLAIME (Fast Learning AI for Mineral Efficiency), a Level-3 SDL
that models the entire powder-to-part pipeline as a cost-aware function
network and selects experiments using the partial Knowledge Gradient for
Function Networks (pKGFN) acquisition function over Bayesian Optimization
of Function Networks (BOFN). Each pipeline stage—CALPHAD (TC-Python /
TCAl10 / AM Module), atomization (rePOWDER / Valimet), LPBF, and the
microstructure–hardness–tensile ladder—is a node with its own GP surrogate
that forwards posterior beliefs as structured inputs downstream. Cost-aware
acquisition densely queries cheap nodes and sparingly queries the expensive
ones, jointly optimizing strength, printability, and an embedded
USGS-derived mineral-burden objective for Al–Mn–Cr–Zr and Al–Zr families.

We will report (i) closed-loop benchmarking on AM-like synthetic networks
on BYU HPC showing ≥3× sample efficiency over conventional Bayesian
optimization, (ii) preliminary CALPHAD + AlSi10Mg LPBF + tensile data
calibrating the network surrogates, and (iii) a digital-thread schema
(FAIR, hosted on the American Science Cloud) that exposes per-node GP
posteriors to enable cross-laboratory replay of the autonomous campaign.
