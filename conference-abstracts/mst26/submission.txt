TITLE (sentence case)
Cost-aware Bayesian optimization across a CALPHAD–atomization–LPBF function network for critical-mineral-lean aluminum alloy discovery

AUTHORS
Sterling Baird, Xavier Zaitzeff, Gage Erickson, Ronnie Guymon, Carl Robison, Timothy Commins
Department of Mechanical Engineering, Brigham Young University, 701 E University Parkway, 350 EB, Provo, UT 84602

ABSTRACT BODY (150 words; paste as one paragraph — no hard returns; special characters use the portal's \####; codes per organizer guidance)

Autonomous alloy discovery for additive manufacturing is a multi-stage decision problem with per-query costs spanning more than an order of magnitude: \8776;$1500 per ultrasonic atomization run, \8776;$1–5k per LPBF build, \8776;$200 per ASTM E8 tensile bar, and substantially more per industrial gas atomization batch. Tiered screening (e.g., experimentally validating top-performing simulations) wastes budget on uninformative expensive queries and forfeits the algorithm's ability to strategically sample and learn cross-measurement biases early. We present FLAIME (Fast Learning AI for Mineral Efficiency), which applies the partial Knowledge Gradient for Function Networks (pKGFN) to a directed acyclic graph of CALPHAD (TC-Python/TCAl10/AM Module), atomization, LPBF, and microstructure–hardness–tensile nodes; each is a Gaussian process whose posterior feeds its children. Ultimate tensile strength, yield strength, elongation, and a printability score are jointly maximized via expected hypervolume improvement while penalizing a USGS-derived critical-mineral burden. We present synthetic-network benchmarks showing \8805;3\215; sample efficiency and an initial campaign spanning 10+ atomization/print cycles.
