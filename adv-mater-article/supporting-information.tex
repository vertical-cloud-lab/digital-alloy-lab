\section{The function-network model and the optimizer's policies}
\label{sec:Smodel}\checksec{12}
\tbd{The live campaign's model: node definitions, kernels and priors, cost model
(Table~1 of the main text), acquisition settings, batching, handling of failed
and abandoned runs (\#274), seeding (\#275). Its acquisition function is decision
M7, fixed in the pre-registration (\cref{sec:Sprereg}).} \ifbofnfull The rest of this
section describes the optimizer and its baselines as they were tested on the
synthetic workflow of \cref{sec:Stwin}.\else The optimizer and its baselines, as
tested on the synthetic workflow of \cref{sec:Stwin}, are described in the
companion article.\bofncite\fi
\ifbofnfull

\paragraph{The model.} Every node is an exact Gaussian process on its own inputs
(the design variables it uses plus its parents' outputs), with an RBF kernel
whose hyperparameters are a maximum a posteriori fit in
BoTorch.\cite{balandat2020} The network posterior at a design is carried as 64
Monte Carlo samples, pushed through the nodes in order.\cite{astudillo2021}

\paragraph{The policies} (\cref{tabS:policies}). The partial-evaluation
knowledge gradient (pKGFN) scores, for each step, the candidates whose inputs
already exist (compositions to simulate, simulated alloys to atomize, lots to
print, builds to test) by the expected increase in the best network posterior
mean from that one result, per dollar,\cite{buathong2024} and buys the best.
When every knowledge gradient is zero it falls back to network expected
improvement per dollar. Its full-chain counterpart (KGFN) values running the
whole chain at the same candidate designs. Network expected improvement per
dollar, with or without reprinting existing lots, completes a $2\times2$ of
acquisition function $\times$ evaluation mode (the rows also differ in their
candidate pools). Standard BO and screen-then-test use one GP from the seven
design variables to the objective. Screen-then-test simulates 256 compositions,
keeps the four with the largest strength minus 0.3 times the burden among those
with $f\le0.25$, and optimizes the atomizer and laser settings of those four.

\subsection*{The synthetic benchmark of the optimizer}

\paragraph{Why a synthetic benchmark.} Whether choosing the step pays depends on
the workflow: the step costs, how much a cheap step reveals about the expensive
ones, and how wrong the screen is. A physical campaign can be run once, so the
optimizer and its baselines were first compared on a synthetic version of the
workflow whose optimum is known and in which the screen's error can be set.
\ifbofnfull\else The workflow, the protocol and the full results are in the
companion article;\bofncite{} \cref{tab:contrasts} summarizes the comparisons
this article relies on.\fi
\ifbofnfull

\paragraph{The synthetic workflow.} A design has four composition variables
(Zn+Mg, Cu, Zr and Sc levels) and three process variables (atomizer setting,
laser power, scan speed), all on $[0,1]$. One CALPHAD call (\$10) returns a
strengthening-phase fraction $s$, a freezing range $f$ and a nucleant fraction
$g$; atomization (\$1,500) returns a powder quality; printing (\$2,500) returns
the dense, crack-free fraction of the build; a tensile test (\$200) returns a
strength $U$. The objective is $U$ minus 0.3 times a known critical-mineral
burden that weighs Sc heaviest.\cite{nassar2020} A powder lot must exist before
it is printed and a build before it is tested, and a lot can be printed any
number of times. The node functions are closed forms written to mirror the
qualitative metallurgy (listed in the code); nothing is fitted to data. The costs
are assumptions; Table~1 of the main text gives the measured ones. Cracking
depends on an effective freezing range
$f_\mathrm{eff}=f\,[1-0.85\,\kappa\,g/(g+0.12)]$: at $\kappa=1$, Zr and Sc
nucleants suppress cracking as in Zr-modified printed
alloys,\cite{martin2017,croteau2018} which a screen on $f$
alone\cite{kou2015} cannot see; at $\kappa=0$ they do not, and the screen is
right. At $\kappa=1$ the optimum is a high-solute, Zr-inoculated, Sc-free alloy
that the screen ($f\le0.25$) rejects, and the best design that passes the screen
scores 65\% of the optimum. The policies are those of \cref{sec:Smodel}
(\cref{tabS:policies}).

\paragraph{Protocol.} Each seed fixes the shared start: CALPHAD on 32
compositions and five random designs carried through every step, a sunk cost
that is the same for every policy. Each run then has \$100k of new budget
(about 23 full make--print--test cycles; a run stops when its cheapest next
purchase no longer fits). The score is the true objective of the best alloy that
has been printed and tensile-tested, as a percentage of the optimum. Seeds per
policy: \stat{k1-n} at $\kappa=1$, \stat{k05-n} at $\kappa=0.5$ and
\stat{k0-n} at $\kappa=0$, the same seeds for every policy. Differences are
means over matched seeds, with 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals (10,000
draws) that resample seeds jointly for both policies.\cite{efron1993} The
speed-up over a baseline is the spend at which the baseline's mean curve reaches
its own \$100k mean, divided by the spend at which the policy's mean curve
reaches it.

\paragraph{Results.} \Cref{figS:curves} and \cref{tab:contrasts} summarize the
runs. When the screen missed the nucleant effect ($\kappa=1$), screen-then-test
plateaued at \stat{k1-tiered-v100-mean}\% of the optimum, below random designs
(\stat{k1-random-v100-mean}\%), and every policy that searched the whole design
space with a model finished \stat{k1-pkgfn-tiered-v100-abs} to
\stat{k1-eifn_reuse-tiered-v100-abs} points above it. pKGFN reached screening's \$100k result at
\stat{k1-su-pkgfn-tiered}$\times$ less spend (95\% CI
\stat{k1-su-pkgfn-tiered-ci95}), BOFN with powder reuse at
\stat{k1-su-eifn_reuse-tiered}$\times$ (\stat{k1-su-eifn_reuse-tiered-ci95}),
and the full-chain policies at \stat{k1-su-eifn-tiered}$\times$ (BOFN) and
\stat{k1-su-kgfn-tiered}$\times$ (KGFN) (\cref{figS:speedup}). Choosing the
step bought an early lead, \stat{k1-c-0-v25-abs} points over full-chain evaluation
at \$25k under the knowledge gradient (difference \stat{k1-c-0-v25-ci}), which was
gone by \$100k (\stat{k1-c-0-v100}, \stat{k1-c-0-v100-ci}); in four reruns
with four times the Monte Carlo samples, pKGFN's \$25k result fell from 69\% to
46\% of the optimum, so this lead may depend on its Monte Carlo settings. The knowledge
gradient was not better than expected improvement per dollar: with partial
evaluation it finished \stat{k1-c-2-v100-abs} points behind (difference
\stat{k1-c-2-v100-ci}), mostly
because it bought prints of a few powders (\stat{k1-pkgfn-nA} powders and
\stat{k1-pkgfn-nL} prints per run, against \stat{k1-eifn_reuse-nA} and
\stat{k1-eifn_reuse-nL} for BOFN with reuse). When the screen was right
($\kappa=0$), screen-then-test was the most reliable policy
(\stat{k0-tiered-v100-mean}\% of the optimum at \$100k, 95\% CI
\stat{k0-tiered-v100-lo}--\stat{k0-tiered-v100-hi}) and no other policy's mean
reached that level within the budget. The network policies with expected
improvement averaged \stat{k0-eifn_reuse-v100-mean}\% (with powder reuse) and
\stat{k0-eifn-v100-mean}\%, and the knowledge-gradient policies had the
lowest means of the model-based policies (pKGFN \stat{k0-pkgfn-v100-mean}\%, KGFN
\stat{k0-kgfn-v100-mean}\%):
pKGFN atomizes only alloys it has simulated, and the optimum at $\kappa=0$ is a
lean, nucleant-free alloy that the seed compositions do not reach.
With the screen half right ($\kappa=0.5$), BOFN with powder reuse finished ahead
of screen-then-test (\stat{k05-eifn_reuse-v100-mean}\% against
\stat{k05-tiered-v100-mean}\%, difference \stat{k05-eifn_reuse-tiered-v100-ci}
points), full-chain BOFN was level with it, and pKGFN trailed
(\stat{k05-pkgfn-v100-mean}\%).

\paragraph{Validation.} Reruns of the stored runs with the pinned environment
matched them action for action (the same step at the same spend at every
decision, and the same scores to within $10^{-9}$): 140 of 140 at $\kappa=1$,
56 of 56 at $\kappa=0.5$ and 56 of 56 at $\kappa=0$, on three CPU models. The full-chain knowledge gradient agrees with a brute-force
recomputation to within $10^{-13}$, and none of the 62,350 Gaussian-process fits
failed. The
code, every run record and the statistics are openly available
(\code{benchmarks/bofn-paper} in the article's repository).
