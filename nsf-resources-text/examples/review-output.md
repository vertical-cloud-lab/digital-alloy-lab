## Summary judgment

**Return without review (high risk).** The Project Summary lacks the three
explicitly-named subsections that PAPPG II.D.2.b requires every NSF
proposal to contain ("an overview, a statement on the intellectual merit
of the proposed activity, and a statement on the broader impacts of the
proposed activity"). It also reads as an abstract of the full proposal —
a usage that PAPPG II.D.2.b expressly forbids ("It should not be an
abstract of the proposal"). Two additional formatting issues (an embedded
URL, a self-numbered page footer) compound the risk.

## PAPPG Chapter II compliance findings

### Finding 1 — Major — `pappg-24-1#II.D.2.b`

**Section path:** `Chapter II › D. Proposal Contents › 2. Sections of the Proposal › b. Project Summary`
**PDF page:** 49
**Requirement strength:** required (matched phrases: `must`, `should`)

The draft does not contain a paragraph or heading explicitly labelled
"Overview", "Intellectual Merit", or "Broader Impacts". PAPPG II.D.2.b
states:

> "The Project Summary consists of an overview, a statement on the
> intellectual merit of the proposed activity, and a statement on the
> broader impacts of the proposed activity."

NSF FastLane / Research.gov enforces this as **three separate text
boxes**, so a single un-subdivided paragraph fails on submission. The
draft *implicitly* discusses each topic (the second paragraph touches
"intellectual merit"-flavored content, the third touches "broader
impacts"-flavored content) but never labels them.

**Suggested fix.** Insert three explicit headings — `Overview`,
`Intellectual Merit`, `Broader Impacts` — and rewrite the prose into
those three boxes. Move "we will train two graduate students … run a
one-week summer workshop" into the Broader Impacts box. Move "a
surrogate model that the broader AI-for-materials community can fine-tune"
into Intellectual Merit (advancement of knowledge) *or* Broader Impacts
(community benefit) — pick one and don't double-count.

### Finding 2 — Major — `pappg-24-1#II.D.2.b`

**Section path:** same as Finding 1
**Requirement strength:** required

The closing paragraph reads as an abstract of the full proposal,
referring forward to "Section 2", "Section 3", "Section 4". PAPPG
II.D.2.b explicitly states:

> "It should not be an abstract of the proposal."

**Suggested fix.** Remove the "Section 2 … Section 3 … Section 4"
roadmap. The Project Summary should stand on its own and be informative
to peers in the field; pointers into the Project Description belong in
the Project Description's own opening paragraph.

### Finding 3 — Minor — `pappg-24-1#II.D.2.d.(ii)`

**Section path:** `Chapter II › D. Proposal Contents › 2. Sections of the Proposal › d. Project Description › (ii). Page Limitations and Inclusion of Uniform Resource Locators (URLs) within …`
**PDF page:** 50
**Requirement strength:** prohibited (matched phrases: `may not`, `must not`)

The draft includes an inline URL: `https://example.edu/doe-prior-work`.
The cited snippet is *literally* about the Project Description, not the
Project Summary, so the strict prohibition does not apply here — but the
underlying rationale ("the information could circumvent page
limitations; the reviewers are under no obligation to view the sites;
the sites could be altered or deleted between the time of submission
and the time of review") applies just as well in the Summary.

**Suggested fix.** Remove the URL. Cite the prior-work paper in the
References Cited section instead, and refer to it by author + year here.

### Finding 4 — Minor — `pappg-24-1#II.C.1`

**Section path:** `Chapter II › C. Format of the Proposal › 1. Proposal Pagination Instructions`
**PDF page:** 42
**Requirement strength:** recommended (matched phrase: `should`)

The draft ends with `----- end Project Summary, page 1 of 1 -----`.
PAPPG II.C.1:

> "Each section of the proposal that is uploaded as a file should leave
> out page numbering unless otherwise directed within Research.gov."

Research.gov auto-paginates on submission; a hand-rolled footer can
collide with the auto-paginator and produce duplicate page numbers in
the assembled PDF.

**Suggested fix.** Delete the trailing footer line.

### Finding 5 — Major — `pappg-24-1#II.D.2.b`  (length / layout — *flagged but not verifiable from prose alone*)

**Requirement strength:** required ("not more than one page in length")

The draft is ~530 words. At 11pt single-spaced with 1-inch margins
(PAPPG II.C.2 baseline), 530 words typically renders in just under one
page; with the project title, PI line, and section heading the cushion
is small. **Cannot be verified from the prose** — needs to be checked
against rendered PDF page count. Marked as a *risk*, not a confirmed
finding.

**Suggested fix.** Trim to ~450 words to leave margin headroom; verify
by rendering the section as a standalone PDF before submission.

## Intellectual Merit (NSF Merit Review Criterion 1)

*Not applicable to the Project Summary in isolation — IM is reviewed
against the Project Description.* The Summary's IM-relevant sentences
("a >10x reduction in time-to-discover", "a trained surrogate model that
the broader AI-for-materials community can fine-tune") are evaluable but
need to be lifted into an explicit IM box first (see Finding 1).

## Broader Impacts (NSF Merit Review Criterion 2)

Same caveat as IM. The BI-relevant sentences ("two graduate students and
four undergraduates", "one-week summer workshop for high-school
teachers") are good raw material but need an explicit BI heading
(Finding 1).

## Top-3 revisions, ordered by ROI

1. **Add the three required headings** (Overview / Intellectual Merit /
   Broader Impacts) and migrate existing sentences into them. Without
   this the proposal is at risk of return-without-review.
2. **Strip the abstract-style forward references** ("Section 2 …
   Section 3 …"). Replace the closing paragraph with a 1-sentence
   statement of the project's significance to the field.
3. **Remove the inline URL and the hand-rolled page footer.** Both are
   trivially fixable formatting hygiene; both will trigger reviewer
   irritation if left in.

## Caveats / failure modes the dogfood test surfaced

- **D5 (URL).** The framework's strength labeller correctly tagged
  II.D.2.d.(ii) as `prohibited` but the labeller has no notion of
  *scope* — the prohibition is on URLs in the Project *Description*, not
  the Summary. The reviewer-style output had to add a hedge ("strictly
  not the same section, but the rationale carries"). This is the
  "scope-limited modal" failure mode flagged in
  [`../snippets/README.md`](../snippets/README.md) §"v2 ideas" and in
  the Edison feedback prompt (`/tmp/edison-fb/00-PROMPT.md` §3).
- **D7 (page-length).** Layout-dependent requirements ("not more than
  one page in length") cannot be checked against prose alone; this needs
  a sibling tool that takes a rendered PDF and counts pages.
- **No discovery cost.** The framework relies on the reviewer / driver
  knowing *which* snippet ids govern a Project Summary review. For a
  v2 we'd want either (a) an MCP server that does retrieval over the
  snippet store, or (b) a lookup table from "proposal section name" →
  "snippet ids". Today the lookup is implicit in the playbook's
  "required-context file list" but is not section-keyed.
