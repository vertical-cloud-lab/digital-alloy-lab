# Composition Space Scaling: NChooseK Element Palette

*Response to: "Why 526 compositions? What if I were to expand the number of unique elements being considered?"*

## Framing: Unified 15-Element Representation with NChooseK Constraint in the Acquisition Inner Loop

Per the FLAIME search-space design (PR #52), the scope is **not** a fixed small alloy family but an **NChooseK palette**: Al as the matrix plus *k* additional elements (with **k ∈ {2, …, 8}**) selected out of a **fixed palette of 15 non-Al elements** (Mn, Cr, Zr, Mg, Si, Cu, Ti, Fe, Ni, Ce, Sc, Li, Er, Zn, Sn). Al-Mn-Cr-Zr and Al-Zr are initial-focus sub-systems used for early validation, not the limit of the design space.

**Representation (what the surrogate sees).** A single, non-degenerate 16-dimensional composition vector `x = (x_Al, x_Mn, x_Cr, x_Zr, …, x_Sn)` on the simplex (Σ xᵢ = 1), where each coordinate is always *the same element*. There is **no** sub-system one-hot, no hierarchical "pick-then-compose" split, and no re-embedding of the same element into different slots in different sub-problems. The GP / SVDKL / RF surrogate therefore learns **one** consistent mapping from elemental fractions (plus temperature and any LPBF process features) to properties — the representation is not obfuscated by an outer categorical choice the model then has to invert.

**Constraint (what the acquisition optimizer enforces).** The NChooseK structure is imposed **only during the inner-loop optimization of the acquisition function**, and **only at the root CALPHAD node** of the BOFN DAG. A candidate `x` is feasible iff `‖x_{non-Al}‖_0 ∈ {2, …, 8}` (number of non-zero non-Al entries) and `x_Al ≥ 0.85` (Al-rich). Standard gradient-based `optimize_acqf` is not well suited to the 0-norm / combinatorial part of this constraint, so the inner loop uses a **genetic algorithm** (or mixed-integer / evolutionary optimizer) over the 15 non-Al slots: chromosomes are 15-element composition vectors, a mask gene selects which ≤8 entries are non-zero, real-valued genes give the fractions (re-normalized against `x_Al`), and the fitness is the acquisition value (qLogEI / p-KGFN / TSFN composite). Mutation/crossover operators that respect the sparsity pattern (e.g., swap a non-zero slot with a zero slot; perturb within the active support) preserve feasibility cheaply. **Library options** (per Edison R2, `composition-scaling-edison-r2-unified.md`): for strict GA, **PyMoo** has BoTorch bridges for mixed-integer/constrained acquisition optimization; alternatively, the BoTorch-native **SEBO** (Sparsity Exploration Bayesian Optimization, *Liu et al., AISTATS 2023, arXiv:2203.01900*) directly handles `L_0` constraints via a differentiable homotopy relaxation and integrates with `botorch.optim.optimize_mixed`. See also Xian et al., *Digital Discovery* 2025 on feature-gradient acquisition with linear scaling in component count.

**DAG depth matters.** When p-KGFN evaluates **intermediate downstream nodes** (Powder, LPBF, Characterization), the input space is *not* the 15-element compositional simplex — candidates are generated by sampling from the posteriors of the parent GPs (e.g., phase fractions → powder PSD), and the `L_0` / Al-rich constraints **do not apply**. Standard continuous acquisition optimization over process parameters is appropriate at those nodes. The GA + NChooseK machinery is therefore confined to the root-node acquisition optimization.

**Numerical stability for sparse high-dim inputs.** A 15D input where most points have 7–13 exact zeros is pathological for a stock `SingleTaskGP` with ARD lengthscales: lengthscales of inactive dimensions get pushed to ∞, but because different points have different active dimensions, the kernel becomes ill-conditioned and a single Cr-active observation can inflate the noise term to "explain away" Cr-Zr interactions ("GP collapse"). The recommended remedies (per Edison R2) are **shrinkage priors** — `SaasFullyBayesianSingleTaskGP` (BoTorch) uses horseshoe priors specifically designed for high-dimensional sparse inputs — or compositional kernels for radially-transformed data with many zeros (Park et al., *ICML* 2022).

**Why this replaces the earlier hierarchical framing.** An earlier draft of this doc described a Level 1 (pick `k` elements from 15 → choose a sub-system) / Level 2 (enumerate compositions within the sub-system) split. That framing creates two problems: (1) the model sees a *different* input schema per sub-system (unless you add a 15-slot one-hot, which then requires the model to infer the equivalence between the same element appearing in different sub-systems — information the unified representation gives for free); (2) it implies "screen → filter → refine" / "three-stage funnel" semantics that are unnecessary. With the unified 15-element representation and the NChooseK constraint in the acquisition inner loop, there is a **single async, cost-aware BO loop** that jointly proposes (support pattern, composition) and — via partial evaluations — selects which DAG node to evaluate next based on per-node cost and expected information gain. The combinatorics tables below are still useful for capacity planning (how much of the space can be held as a dense lookup, what surrogate class is feasible at a given effective `k`), but they are **diagnostics of the space size, not stages of the algorithm**.

## The 526 Number — Correction and Explanation

The original estimate of 526 compositions for Al-Mn-Cr-Zr (Al ≥ 85 at.%, 1 at.% resolution) was **incorrect**. The correct count using the stars-and-bars combinatorial formula is:

**C(15 + 3, 3) = C(18, 3) = 816 compositions**

This counts all non-negative integer solutions to Mn + Cr + Zr ≤ 15 at 1 at.% steps (since Al = 100 − Mn − Cr − Zr ≥ 85). The 816 × 10 temperatures = 8,160 TC sims — still well within exact GP capacity.

## How Composition Count Scales with More Elements (effective support size)

The combinatorics below are **diagnostics of the feasible-region size at a given effective support size `k`**, not algorithmic stages. In the unified 15-element representation, `k` is simply the 0-norm of the non-Al part of the composition vector; the surrogate always sees all 15 element coordinates.

Adding elements causes **polynomial explosion** of the composition space *at a given support size*. Two counts matter, and they are **different** (Edison R2 caught a double-counting error in an earlier draft of this section):

- **Including-zeros count** — number of integer solutions to `x_1 + … + x_k ≤ 15` (1 at.% steps, Al ≥ 85 at.%) with `x_i ≥ 0`. Stars-and-bars gives **N_loose(k) = C(15 + k, k)**. This count includes degenerate compositions where some of the `k` selected slots are actually zero (e.g. at k=3 it includes the binary "Al-Mn" with Cr=Zr=0), so it overcounts the strictly-supported feasible region.
- **Strict-support count** — number of integer solutions with `x_i ≥ 1` for every selected element (i.e. `‖x_{non-Al}‖_0 = k` exactly). Via stars-and-bars with the substitution `z_i = x_i − 1` and slack variable `y_0 = 15 − Σx_i` (so `y_0 + z_1 + … + z_k = 15 − k`, all `≥ 0`), this gives **N_strict(k) = C(15, k)**. *This is the count enforced by the GA inner loop* (which strictly requires every selected non-Al element to have ≥1 at.%), and it is the correct denominator for capacity planning of the unified surrogate.

**All of the closed-form counts in this document are verified computationally** in [`composition-scaling-verify.py`](composition-scaling-verify.py):

- Brute-force enumeration matches `C(15,k)` and `C(15+k,k)` for every `k ∈ {1,…,8}` (per-pattern).
- Brute-force enumeration of `(support, composition)` pairs matches `C(15,k)²` for `k ∈ {1,…,4}` (tractable range).
- The binomial decomposition `N_loose(k) = Σ_{s=0..k} C(k,s)·N_strict(s)` holds for every `k ∈ {1,…,8}` — this confirms that the old "loose" table overcounted by exactly the amount expected if degenerate lower-support compositions are collapsed into their strict support.
- Cross-check of the global bound via Vandermonde: `Σ_{k=0..15} C(15,k)² = C(30,15) = 155,117,520` (holds exactly). The feasible region used in planning, `Σ_{k=2..8} C(15,k)² = 118,967,759`, is the corresponding partial sum. ## NChooseK Combinatorics: Total Feasible-Region Size

If the BO loop is free to explore **every** support pattern of size *k* on the 15-element palette (Al matrix, Al ≥ 85 at.%, 1 at.% resolution), the total **strict-support** composition count at a given *k* is:

**N_total(k) = C(15, k) · C(15, k) = C(15, k)²**

(Edison R2 correction: an earlier draft used `C(15, k) · C(15+k, k)`, which double-counts because `C(15+k, k)` already mixes in lower-support compositions; at k=8 the overcount factor is ~76×.)

This is a **capacity diagnostic** — it is the size of the strictly-feasible region the GA-constrained acquisition optimizer must search over at support size `k`, *not* a set of stages the algorithm traverses. The unified surrogate is trained on whatever (x, y) pairs have been evaluated so far, regardless of which support pattern they fall under. Summing N_total(k) for k ∈ {2, …, 8} gives a global feasible region of **≈ 119 million** strictly-supported distinct compositions — large but finite, and tractable for a constrained acquisition optimizer to navigate.

Two consequences follow directly:

1. **Full enumeration is impossible beyond k=2–3.** Even at *k*=2, exhausting all 105 support patterns at strict support costs 11,025 TC sims — still HPC-tractable, but for *k*=3 already approaches 207K and for *k*=4 exceeds 1.8M. Dense CALPHAD lookup as a supplement to the live surrogate only survives in the *k*=2 slab (or a hand-picked shortlist of support patterns at *k*=3).
2. **Support pattern selection is learned, not pre-filtered.** With 6,435 candidate patterns at *k*=7 or 8, the GA-constrained acquisition optimizer prioritizes which patterns (and which compositions within them) to propose, guided by the unified surrogate's posterior over the full 15-element space. Cost-aware BO with partial evaluations then decides which DAG node to query, so cheap CALPHAD screens rule out unpromising patterns before the full pipeline budget is spent on them — but all of this happens inside a single outer loop against a single surrogate.

## Key Implications for Strategy

### Per-Support-Pattern Capacity Regimes

Inside the single async cost-aware BO loop on the unified 15-element surrogate, the only thing that varies with *k* is how much of a given support pattern's slice can be pre-computed as a dense lookup versus left to GA-driven adaptive sampling:

1. **k ≤ 4** (≤ 5 non-zero components, Al ≥ 85%): per-pattern CALPHAD grid has ≤ ~4K compositions. Exhaustive enumeration of the initial-focus patterns (Al-Mn-Cr-Zr, Al-Zr, plus a small shortlist) is HPC-tractable in days and feeds the unified surrogate. The BO loop then spends its budget on downstream (powder / LPBF / characterization) evaluations and on letting the GA explore *other* support patterns.

2. **k = 5** (6 non-zero components, ~15K compositions): per-pattern exhaustive enumeration is borderline (≈1–2 weeks per pattern on BYU HPC at typical rate limits). The unified CALPHAD-node surrogate switches to SVDKL so that information flows across patterns through shared elemental features rather than enumerating each pattern in isolation.

3. **k ≥ 6** (7+ non-zero components, 54K–490K compositions per pattern): pure enumeration is impractical even for a single pattern. The async cost-aware BO drives all CALPHAD sampling adaptively via the GA-constrained acquisition, SVDKL or RF+conformal as the CALPHAD-node surrogate, with partial evaluations ensuring the expensive downstream nodes are only triggered on promising candidates.

### The "Free Compute" Advantage Shifts

At small *k*, free compute means "enumerate every composition in the anchor sub-systems." At large *k*, free compute means "run as many adaptively-chosen TC sims as the rate limit allows per day" — the bottleneck shifts from budget to **wall-clock time** and **surrogate quality**.

Concrete HPC capacity estimate (BYU fairshare):
- Assume ~100–500 TC sims/day rate limit (depending on queue load and per-sim runtime)
- 4-component sub-system (k=3, Al-rich): 816 compositions → **2–8 days** to exhaustively cover (feasible)
- 7-component sub-system (k=6, Al-rich): 54,264 compositions → **108–543 days** to exhaustively cover per sub-system (impractical → must sample adaptively)

### Dimensionality Also Hurts GP Prediction Quality

Beyond just the number of training points, higher dimensions degrade GP prediction:
- **Curse of dimensionality**: Data points become sparse relative to the volume. With 6+ free composition variables + temperature, regions of the space will be poorly covered even with 100K data points.
- **Lengthscale estimation**: ARD kernels need enough data per dimension to estimate lengthscales. With 7+ dimensions, need O(thousands) of observations before the GP can make meaningful predictions in all directions.
- **Phase boundary complexity**: More components = more possible phase equilibria = sharper, more numerous phase boundaries. The non-stationarity problem that motivates SVDKL over plain GP becomes more severe.

### Initial-Focus Support Patterns and the Palette

Al-Mn-Cr-Zr (k=3) and Al-Zr (k=1) are **initial-focus support patterns** — the first few the BO loop densely samples — not the final scope. The full search spans all C(15, k) patterns for k ∈ {2, …, 8}, with representative examples from the palette including: Al-Mn-Cr-Zr-Ti (k=4), Al-Mn-Cr-Zr-Si (k=4), Al-Mn-Cr-Zr-Ti-Si (k=5), Al-Mn-Cr-Zr-Ti-Si-Cu-Mg (k=7). Which patterns are actually probed — and how deeply — is an *output* of the GA-constrained cost-aware BO on the unified 15-element surrogate, not a hand-picked list.

## Integration with Async BO on BYU HPC

For ≥6 components, the async BO architecture from the scaling strategy becomes critical:

1. **Space-filling initialization**: Sobol sequence on the (d−1)-simplex, ~1000–5000 points
2. **Parallel TC evaluation**: Submit batch to SLURM, each with fairshare priority
3. **Streaming surrogate updates**: As each job completes, retrain SVDKL incrementally (warm-start from previous parameters)
4. **Acquisition-driven infill**: Use p-KGFN to select next TC compositions, accounting for pending jobs as fantasies
5. **Rate-limit awareness**: Cap batch size to stay within fairshare allocation; use turbo-like trust region to avoid over-exploring early

## Simplex Handling for d > 5 Components

With many components, the composition simplex constraint (Σ xᵢ = 1) requires careful handling:

1. **Drop-one-variable** (current approach): Works for 3–4 free vars. Actually **preferable** for the Al-rich corner (Al ≥ 85%) because ILR transform performs poorly near zero-composition boundaries where log(0) is undefined.
2. **ILR (Isometric Log-Ratio) transform** (Aitchison 1986, Egozcue et al. 2003): Maps the (d−1)-simplex to ℝ^(d−2). Best for the simplex *interior*, not the Al-rich corner.
3. **Ax/BoTorch linear constraints**: Native `optimize_acqf` with `inequality_constraints`. Most practical for 5–6 components.
4. **Feature gradient approach** (Xian et al., Digital Discovery 2025): Scales linearly with component count for acquisition optimization — worth considering for 7+ components.

**Simplex-aware sampling** (important for initialization): Standard Sobol/LHS does NOT naturally respect the simplex constraint. Projecting Sobol points onto the simplex via normalization destroys the space-filling property. Use instead:
- **Dirichlet sampling** with appropriate concentration parameters
- **Simplex LHS** (Petelet et al. 2010) — specifically designed for compositional spaces
- **Sobol + softmax transformation** — preserves quasi-random properties while mapping to the simplex

## Dimension Screening for 7+ Components

For high-dimensional systems (7+ components), a preliminary **sensitivity analysis** step can dramatically reduce effective dimensionality:

1. **Morris method** on an initial TC surrogate (~100–300 screening sims) to rank element importance
2. **Sobol sensitivity indices** computed on a ~1000-point space-filling design
3. If 2–4 elements dominate the property variation, fix the others at nominal values and reduce the problem back to an exhaustive-enumeration regime at low effective *k*

This is especially effective for alloy design where many minor additions have small effects below their solubility limits.

## Per-Element Resolution Requirements (from Edison LITERATURE_HIGH on property sensitivity)

The 1 at.% / 0.5 at.% grids assumed above are **uniform**, but published quantitative sensitivity data (see `property-sensitivity-vs-composition.md`, Edison `LITERATURE_HIGH` task `278f1234`) show that LPBF-relevant properties are not equally sensitive to every element. Highlights tying directly into **AutoTrickler dispensing accuracy** (byu-vcl PR #94, issue #4), **induction-melting stoichiometry drift** (byu-vcl issue #16 comment 4261062561), and **TC/TCAL10 input grid resolution**:

- **Cracking susceptibility (CSI) cliffs**: For Al-Si, CSI peaks at ~0.5 wt% Si (5,875 °C) and drops ~60× by 10 wt%; experimentally a change of just 2→4 wt% Si transitions cracked → crack-free in LPBF (Hyer 2021). Al-Mg CSI peaks at 2.5 wt% (no back-diffusion) shifting to 5.5 wt% with realistic cooling (Zhang 2021). 7075 + 3.74 wt% Si reduces CSI by ~39%, transitioning crack-prone → crack-free.  
  → **TC/CALPHAD grid spacing of ≤0.1 wt% is required near CSI peaks (dilute hypo-eutectic regimes); 0.5–1 wt% suffices near eutectic compositions.**
- **Solid-solution strengthening — strong elements (Cr, Mn)**: Leyson DFT coefficient K(Cr) = 871 MPa/(at.frac)^{2/3}, K(Cu) = 430, K(Mg) = 422, K(Si) = 170 (Leyson 2010). Mn gives ~90 MPa at 0.6 at.%. → **The CALPHAD-node surrogate needs short ARD lengthscale priors on the Cr and Mn axes**; 0.1 wt% changes produce measurable (5–15 MPa) strength differences.
- **Solid-solution strengthening — weak elements (Zn, Li)**: Per Michi 2022, both have very low strengthening per atom in Al. → **Coarser resolution (≥1 wt%) suffices on these axes.**
- **Precipitation strengthening (Sc, Zr, Er, Ce)**: Scalmalloy aging response is exquisitely sensitive — 0.1 wt% Sc shifts the peak-aged YS by tens of MPa around the 0.5–0.8 wt% Sc range. → **Fine TC grids (≤0.1 wt%) are essential near peritectic / nanoprecipitate compositions for Sc/Zr/Er.**
- **AutoTrickler / induction-melting implications**: Dispensing accuracy of ~0.01 g on 10 g charges (~0.1 wt% resolution) is adequate for most axes except Cr/Mn near solubility limits, and Sc/Zr/Er across their entire useful range. Induction-melting stoichiometry drift (preferential evaporation of Mg, Zn) is a first-order signal that must be captured by **post-melt ICP-MS** before the composition is logged into the surrogate — using the *target* composition would inject ~0.5–2 wt% noise on volatile elements and overwhelm the sensitivity-driven gains from a tight TC grid.

These per-element resolution requirements feed back into the BO loop as **input-noise / input-uncertainty terms** on the composition coordinates the surrogate sees (rather than as a finer global grid), and into the GA's mutation step sizes per slot.

## Summary: One Unified Surrogate, NChooseK-Constrained GA Acquisition (at the Root), Partial Evaluations

The architecture is **one** asynchronous, cost-aware BO loop over a **unified 15-element composition representation** (plus temperature + LPBF process features), running against the 4-node DAG (CALPHAD → powder → LPBF → characterization). Three coupled pieces:

1. **Surrogate**: a single GP / SVDKL / RF model in the 15-element space. The same coordinate slot always means the same element, so the model shares information across support patterns for free — no sub-system one-hot, no hierarchical split.
2. **Acquisition inner loop**: at the **root CALPHAD node**, a **genetic algorithm** (or BoTorch SEBO via `optimize_mixed`) optimizes the acquisition function (qLogEI / p-KGFN / TSFN composite) subject to the NChooseK constraint (0-norm of non-Al part ∈ {2, …, 8}) and Al ≥ 85 at.%. Mask + real-valued genes, sparsity-preserving mutation/crossover. At **intermediate DAG nodes** (Powder, LPBF, Characterization), inputs are sampled from parent-GP posteriors, the L_0 / Al-rich constraints do not apply, and standard continuous acquisition optimization over process parameters is used.
3. **Partial evaluations**: p-KGFN / TSFN selects which DAG node to evaluate on the winning candidate given per-node cost and expected information gain; async execution treats pending jobs as fantasies.

What changes with the effective support size *k* is **not** the algorithm but the **surrogate class** for the CALPHAD node and the **fraction of the feasible region that can be held in a dense lookup**:

| k | Per-pattern strict comps | All-pattern strict comps | CALPHAD node surrogate | Notes |
|---:|---:|---:|:---|:---|
| 2 | 105 | 11,025 | Exact GP; full lookup optional | Exhaustive CALPHAD enumeration is HPC-tractable across all 105 support patterns |
| 3 | 455 | 207,025 | Exact GP; partial lookup | Initial-focus patterns (Al-Mn-Cr-Zr, Al-Zr) densely sampled; others sampled adaptively by the GA-driven acquisition |
| 4–5 | 1,365 – 3,003 | 1.9M – 9.0M | Exact GP near upper limit; SVDKL or `SaasFullyBayesianSingleTaskGP` for cross-pattern sharing | A single global SVDKL/SAAS over (composition, temperature, process) becomes preferable |
| 6–8 | 5,005 – 6,435 | 25M – 41M | SVDKL (non-stationary) or RF + conformal | Pure enumeration is infeasible; GA-constrained cost-aware BO with partial evaluations is essential |

The loop structure is the same throughout: (1) the GA proposes a feasible 15-element composition (mask + fractions) by optimizing the acquisition against the unified surrogate's posterior, (2) cost-aware partial-evaluation selects which DAG node to query, (3) per-node surrogates and the unified CALPHAD surrogate are updated incrementally as BYU HPC / experimental jobs complete, (4) pending jobs are treated as fantasies for the next proposal. The only knobs that change with *k* are the **CALPHAD-node surrogate class** and the **initial design density**.
