Question: For a laser powder bed fusion single-track campaign whose goal is to calibrate and validate a melt-pool model (the Thermo-Calc AM Module, steady state) against measured cross-sections, how should the power-speed (and possibly spot-size or layer) settings be chosen: a quasi-random space-filling design such as scrambled Sobol or Latin hypercube, or batch Bayesian optimization / active learning, and with what batch sizes and replicates? Context: we are planning single-track laser powder bed fusion (PBF-LB/M, L-PBF) experiments in 316L stainless steel on a Concept Laser M2 cusing (continuous-wave Yb fibre laser at about 1070 nm, 200 W or 400 W depending on configuration, focused spot about 50-90 um 1/e^2 diameter, galvanometer scanner, nitrogen or argon shielding gas flowing across the plate). Commercial gas-atomized 316L powder (about 15-45 um) is spread as ONE powder layer (nominally 30-50 um) on 316L substrate panels (roughly 80 mm square, bolted flush into a 250 mm build plate), and each track is a single scan vector with its own laser power P and scan speed v (P about 50-400 W, v about 0.1-3 m/s). Each build holds tens to about a hundred tracks. After the build we cut the panels with a bandsaw and a precision abrasive saw, grind to a section plane perpendicular to the scan direction (a transverse cross-section through all tracks in a row at once), mount, polish, etch and image in an SEM, and measure melt-pool width, depth below the substrate surface, height above it and area, and classify the regime (lack of fusion/discontinuous, balling, conduction, transition, keyhole). The measured cross-sections will be compared with STEADY-STATE single-track predictions of the Thermo-Calc Additive Manufacturing (AM) Module (CALPHAD-derived properties, Gaussian or keyhole heat source with absorptivity to calibrate), and with the analytical Eagar-Tsai model (travelling Gaussian surface source, constant properties). We want to calibrate the model's heat-source parameters on part of the tracks and validate on the rest. We expect roughly 50-100 tracks per build, about one build per week, 4-7 builds in total (about 250-600 tracks), with sectioning and SEM as the bottleneck.

The consolidated recommendation for the 316L Concept Laser M2 single-track campaign is a hybrid three-phase design: (1) initial space-filling with a 64-point Owen-scrambled Sobol sequence in (P, log v) space plus control tracks (Builds 1-2, strongly supported by DOE literature); (2) batch Bayesian active learning with composite acquisition combining integrated variance reduction and boundary entropy, with batch size equal to one build of approximately 50-60 unique settings (Builds 3-4, moderately supported); (3) locked-model validation on a pre-declared held-out set of 20-25 percent of unique settings (Builds 5-7, moderately supported).
