## Minimum Track Length and Cross-Section Location for Steady-State Melt-Pool Characterisation in Single-Track LPBF of 316L

### 1. Start Transient

In conduction mode, the melt pool reaches quasi-steady-state dimensions within approximately 1–3 melt-pool lengths (0.1–0.5 mm at 0.5–1.0 m/s, establishment time ~0.1–1 ms). In keyhole mode, synchrotron X-ray imaging (Cunningham et al., Science 363:849, 2019; Zhao et al., Sci. Rep. 7:3602, 2017) and CFD simulations (Khairallah et al., Acta Mater. 108:36, 2016; Science 368:660, 2020) show the keyhole forms in 50–200 µs but quasi-steady depth requires 0.3–1 ms (~0.15–0.5 mm at 0.5 m/s). The start bead is typically 1.5–2× wider than steady-state over the first 100–300 µm. Scaling: d_start ≈ (1–3) × L_pool.

### 2. End-of-Track Phenomena

Keyhole collapse occurs within 50–200 µs of laser-off, creating end-of-track pores (Cunningham et al. 2019). Martin et al. (Nat. Commun. 10:1987, 2019) showed pores at vector ends and demonstrated power ramp-down mitigation over 100–300 µm. End crater extends ~0.5–1 pool lengths. Conservative end exclusion: 2–3 mm (5 mm for keyhole).

### 3. Scanner Dynamics

Galvanometer acceleration at 1 m/s takes ~0.1–1 ms (~0.05–0.5 mm). Skywriting eliminates this if properly configured. Without skywriting on a Concept Laser M2, mark delays of 50–500 µs at 1 m/s affect 50–500 µm. At 2 m/s: 0.5–3 mm total affected zone.

### 4. Published Practices

Track lengths of 5–20 mm are standard (10 mm most common). Sections are taken from the "central" or "interior" region. Explicit discard distances are rarely quantified. CV of width/depth: 5–15% (stable tracks), 20–30% (near instability). Key references: Yadroitsev et al. 2010 (JMPT 210:1624), Scipioni Bertoli et al. 2017 (Mater. Des. 113:331).

### 5. Thermo-Calc AM Module

The steady-state calculation corresponds to the central portion of a long track. Transient mode can estimate distance to steady state.
