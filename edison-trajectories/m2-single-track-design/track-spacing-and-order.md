# Isolating Single Tracks in Laser Powder Bed Fusion: Spacing, Exposure Order, and Practical Design

## 1. Powder Denudation: Width, Mechanism, and Scaling

**Mechanism.** At atmospheric pressure (1 atm Ar or N₂), the dominant denudation mechanism is vapour-jet-driven ambient-gas entrainment. The metal vapour plume rising from the melt pool entrains surrounding shielding gas, creating a low-pressure zone near the substrate that draws gas and powder particles inward toward the melt track. Particles are either incorporated into the melt pool or ejected upward as spatter. This was demonstrated by Matthews et al. (2016, *Acta Materialia* 114, 33–42, DOI: 10.1016/j.actamat.2016.05.017) using high-speed imaging in 316L SS and Ti-6Al-4V. At reduced pressures (<1–10 Torr), the mechanism reverses: expanding vapour directly ejects particles outward, creating an even wider denuded zone. Ly et al. (2017, *Scientific Reports* 7, 4085, DOI: 10.1038/s41598-017-04237-z) confirmed the entrainment mechanism and identified melt-pool-wetting capture as a secondary mechanism. Bidare et al. (2018, *Acta Materialia* 142, 107–120, DOI: 10.1016/j.actamat.2017.09.051) systematically varied pressure (0.01–1000 mbar Ar) and confirmed the mechanism transition.

**Quantitative widths.** For 316L at P≈150–200 W, v≈0.5–1.5 m/s, ~50 µm spot, 30–50 µm layer, 1 atm Ar: the total denuded zone (track plus cleared regions on both sides) is typically 2–5× the solidified track width. For track widths ~100–200 µm, denudation extends ~100–400 µm beyond the track edge on each side (Matthews et al. 2016; Ly et al. 2017). At higher power or lower speed (keyhole regime, P≈300–400 W, v<0.8 m/s), total denuded widths reach 0.5–1.0 mm or more. In Ti-6Al-4V, denudation is similar or somewhat larger. In AlSi10Mg, denudation can be particularly wide due to the high vapour pressure of Mg (Yin et al. 2020, *Additive Manufacturing* 31, 100958, DOI: 10.1016/j.addma.2019.100958). The ratio of total denuded-zone width to track width is 2–5× at atmospheric pressure and increases with linear energy density (P/v).

## 2. How a Previously Fused Neighbour Changes the Next Track

When a second track is placed within the first track's denuded zone, several effects alter the second track: (a) reduced or absent powder on the near side creates asymmetric bead geometry with reduced height; (b) the first track's raised solidified bead (10–40 µm above substrate) alters local geometry and gas flow; (c) particles previously captured by the first track's melt pool are unavailable; (d) modest residual heat if exposed soon after. Yadroitsev et al. (2010, *J. Materials Processing Technology* 210, 1624–1631, DOI: 10.1016/j.jmatprotec.2010.05.010) showed that hatch spacing below ~60–70% of single-track width degrades second-track geometry and promotes balling. To ensure isolation, centre-to-centre spacing must exceed the total denuded-zone width, practically ≥3× the expected melt-pool width, or ≥1.5–2.5 mm for typical 316L conditions.

## 3. Spatter and Ejecta

Spatter particles travel 1–20 mm from the scan track, with most landing within 2–5 mm. Smaller particles carried by cross-flow shielding gas can travel >10 mm downstream. In commercial L-PBF machines like the Concept Laser M2, shielding gas flows at approximately 1–3 m/s across the build plate (Ladewig et al. 2016, *Additive Manufacturing* 12, 307–319, DOI: 10.1016/j.addma.2016.06.012). The optimal exposure strategy is to expose tracks progressively from the gas-outlet (downstream) side toward the gas-inlet (upstream) side, so that spatter and condensate are carried onto already-exposed regions rather than contaminating remaining powder. For scan direction, scanning into (against) the gas flow is preferred as the plume is blown away from the advancing melt pool, minimizing beam attenuation by condensed nanoparticles in the plume.

## 4. Thermal Effects from Neighbouring Tracks

For 316L (thermal diffusivity α ≈ 4–5 mm²/s), the Rosenthal solution gives a quasi-steady-state temperature rise at 1.5–2 mm lateral distance of order 1–10°C for P = 200 W, v = 1 m/s. At ≥2.5 mm, the rise is <1–2°C. Within 0.1–1 s after the laser passes, temperatures at >1 mm are within a few degrees of ambient. For single-layer isolated tracks separated by ≥1.5–2 mm on a thick substrate with galvo delays (~0.01–1 s), thermal effects are negligible compared to melt-pool temperatures (~1400–1700°C). Exposure order matters for denudation and spatter reasons, not thermal reasons.

## 5. Published Single-Track Study Practices

Key practices: Yadroitsev et al. (2010): 10–20 mm tracks, 1–3 mm spacing, Phenix PM100, 50–70 W, 70 µm spot. Kamath et al. (2014, *IJAMT* 74, 65–78, DOI: 10.1007/s00170-014-5954-9): Concept Laser M2, 316L, 150–400 W, 30 µm layer, ~2 mm spacing, 10 mm tracks, dozens per 80 mm substrate. NIST AM Bench (2018, 2022): IN625 and 316L, tracks 1–12 mm, spacing 1.25–4 mm (Montgomery et al. 2019, DOI: 10.1007/s40192-019-00155-y). Sectioning: precision saw kerf ~0.3–0.8 mm; 25–30 mm mounts fit 12–15 tracks at 2 mm spacing.

## 6. The Effective Powder Layer

Packing fraction of spread 15–45 µm 316L powder is ~50–65%, giving effective solid thickness t_eff ≈ 15–30 µm for 30–50 µm nominal layers. Substrate height offsets add directly. The Thermo-Calc AM Module models powder as a continuum with reduced conductivity (~1–5% of bulk) and reduced density; packing fraction and thickness are inputs, absorptivity is calibrated. The Eagar–Tsai model does not explicitly model the powder layer.
