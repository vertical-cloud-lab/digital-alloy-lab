# Critical Literature-Grounded Review of the "Digital Thread Completeness" KPI for FLAIME

## 1. Is ≥90% Digital Thread Completeness Realistic and Meaningful for a 9-Month Phase I?

The ≥90% target is ambitious but achievable in principle, provided that "completeness" is operationally defined against a fixed schema and that the closed-loop automation architecture is prioritized from the outset. However, no existing SDL platform has published an explicit, quantified "digital thread completeness" metric comparable to what FLAIME proposes.

**BIRDSHOT**, the most comparable large-scale accelerated alloy discovery program, established core data infrastructure including a Google Drive–based repository (~18,000 files, 59 GB), standardized sample naming conventions encoding alloy-design code, iteration, composition, fabrication method and batch, JSON-formatted folder schemas, and sample travelers for metadata capture (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 9-13, mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 13-15). Yet even after substantial investment (~1.5 months of infrastructure setup in year one), BIRDSHOT reported that metadata creation remained partly manual, raw-data uploads were not fully automated across all groups, and sample tracking initially relied on a designated email account rather than automated systems (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 23-26, mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 15-17). The knowledge graph and electronic lab notebook migrations were still in progress at the time of publication (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 23-26). No formal completeness percentage was disclosed.

**DataScribe** (Singh et al., 2026) represents a more mature architecture that assigns persistent UUIDs to every dataset, workflow execution, and model update, with comprehensive lineage metadata captured at ingestion via structured traveler forms and schema templates with ontological tags (singh2026datascribeanainative pages 17-19, singh2026datascribeanainative pages 12-14, singh2026datascribeanainative pages 1-2). The platform enforces schema-on-write validation and FAIR-aligned metadata practices. However, DataScribe is a preprint-stage platform and does not report empirical completeness metrics from production campaigns.

**MEAD/MekG** (Soedarmadji et al., 2019; Statt et al., 2023) demonstrates that large-scale lineage tracking is feasible—managing millions of experiments with linked plate identifiers, experiment/analysis file references, and function-version tracking (soedarmadji2019trackingmaterialsscience pages 2-3). The neo4j graph database approach (MekG, 52M+ nodes, 111M+ edges) enables fast provenance queries that are "significantly faster and far easier to write than equivalent SQL queries" (statt2023thematerialsexperiment pages 2-3).

**Assessment**: Relative to the baseline of typical materials science practice—where curated nanomaterial datasets average a completeness score of only 0.43 (public) to 0.68 (industry-provided) against standardized checklists (comandella2020qualityofphysicochemical pages 1-2, comandella2020qualityofphysicochemical pages 7-8)—a 90% target is highly ambitious. In archival additive manufacturing literature, metadata incompleteness is pervasive, with many required fields (SOPs, instrument settings, sample preparation) commonly unreported (wietechareiman2025buildingarobust pages 31-36). The 90% target is therefore meaningful and differentiated, but should be accompanied by a ramped trajectory (e.g., 75% by month 4, 85% by month 6, 90% by month 9) to account for infrastructure maturation during the build phase.

## 2. What Constitutes "Full Provenance"? The Need for a Formal Schema

The KPI as currently stated does not define what metadata fields constitute "full provenance," which is a critical gap. The materials data management literature consistently emphasizes that reproducibility and reuse depend on explicit, machine-readable metadata schemas with defined required fields (scheffler2022fairdataenabling pages 1-4, ghiringhelli2023sharedmetadatafor pages 13-14). BIRDSHOT's experience highlights the importance of defining data management requirements early, including traceability, consistency across operators and instruments, and hierarchical metadata linking measurements to composition samples (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 3-5).

For FLAIME's CALPHAD → atomization → LPBF → tensile testing → BOFN pipeline, a minimum provenance schema should include:

- **Composition layer**: Target composition (wt% or at%), measured composition (ICP-OES or equivalent), powder supplier/lot, atomization parameters (gas pressure, melt temperature)
- **CALPHAD layer**: Software version, database version, predicted phases, solidification path
- **Build layer**: LPBF machine ID, build plate ID, build parameters (power, speed, hatch, layer thickness), atmosphere, build orientation, build date/time
- **Testing layer**: Specimen geometry per ASTM E8, test machine ID, strain rate, temperature, raw stress-strain data, extracted properties (YS, UTS, elongation)
- **Optimization layer**: BOFN iteration number, acquisition function used, predicted vs. actual values, exploration/exploitation balance, recommendation trace

The NIST AM digital thread framework organizes this as a four-tier information map spanning design, material, process, and qualification data packages (kim2017towardadigital pages 1-3, kim2017towardadigital pages 3-4). DataScribe's schema templates approach—encoding types, units, nullability, and ontological tags per field—provides a practical model (singh2026datascribeanainative pages 17-19).

**Recommendation**: Publish the provenance schema as a deliverable and make field-level completeness auditable. Ghiringhelli et al. (2023) emphasize that experimental materials data require hierarchical metadata organization and that many environmental and instrument parameters are frequently incomplete because they are not tracked continuously (ghiringhelli2023sharedmetadatafor pages 13-14).

## 3. UUID-Based Provenance Linking: Standard Practice and Alternatives

UUID-based linking is emerging as a best practice in materials SDL platforms but is not yet universally adopted. DataScribe assigns persistent UUIDs to datasets, workflows, and experiments, combined with OAuth-governed API access (singh2026datascribeanainative pages 12-14, singh2026datascribeanainative pages 1-2). The MEAD system uses unique `plate_id` identifiers as primary lineage keys, and the `rcp`/`exp`/`ana` file structure maintains provenance through explicit file references rather than UUIDs (soedarmadji2019trackingmaterialsscience pages 2-3). BIRDSHOT uses human-readable sample naming conventions encoding composition and iteration metadata, but does not employ a UUID-centric system (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 9-13).

**Alternatives to UUID-based linking include:**

- **Graph-database approaches**: The MekG knowledge graph uses neo4j to encode provenance as directed edges between Sample, Process, and Sample-Process nodes, enabling complex multi-hop provenance queries that are far more performant than flat-file or SQL approaches (statt2023thematerialsexperiment pages 2-3, statt2023thematerialsexperiment pages 1-2). This is arguably superior to UUID-only linking because graph structures naturally capture many-to-many relationships.
- **DOI-based linking**: NOMAD and FAIRmat support DOI assignment for datasets at publication time, creating citable, persistent identifiers suitable for external sharing (draxl2020bigdatadrivenmaterials pages 6-8). However, DOIs are too heavyweight for intra-campaign provenance linking.
- **Hash-based linking**: Content-addressable hashing provides tamper-evident data integrity but does not inherently capture relational provenance.
- **W3C PROV-O ontology**: The standardized provenance ontology provides a formal, interoperable vocabulary for describing entities, activities, and agents in provenance chains, and has been applied in knowledge graph platforms for provenance management (aggour2024semanticsenableddatafederation pages 1-2).

**Assessment**: For FLAIME, a hybrid approach is recommended: UUIDs for entity identification, a graph database (e.g., neo4j) for relational provenance, and PROV-O–aligned vocabulary for interoperability. UUID-only linking in flat files would be insufficient for the complex many-to-many relationships in an alloy discovery campaign.

## 4. Relationship to FAIR Data Principles and the Case for Explicit Sub-Metrics

The FAIR Guiding Principles (Wilkinson et al., 2016) specify 15 sub-principles organized into Findable (F1–F4), Accessible (A1–A2), Interoperable (I1–I3), and Reusable (R1–R1.3) categories (d.2016thefairguiding pages 3-4, d.2016thefairguiding pages 1-2). Provenance is explicitly required under R1.2 ("(meta)data are associated with detailed provenance"), and machine-actionability is framed as a continuum rather than a binary state (d.2016thefairguiding pages 3-4). FAIRness is defined as modular and incremental, with communities expected to make assessment criteria explicit and measurable (dumontier2018advancingdiscoveryscience pages 6-7, d.2016thefairguiding pages 4-5).

Digital thread completeness and FAIR compliance overlap but are not identical. A dataset may have complete internal provenance (high digital thread completeness) but fail FAIR interoperability because it uses proprietary formats or lacks persistent identifiers. Conversely, FAIR-compliant data may be findable and accessible but lack the end-to-end process linkage that a digital thread implies.

In materials science, the FAIRmat consortium operationalizes FAIR through tiered data availability (Tier 0 = not shared, through Tier 3 = published with DOI), federated data with centralized metadata, common schemas, and domain-aware portals (scheffler2022fairdataenabling pages 7-10). Scheffler et al. (2022) emphasize that comprehensive metadata—including workflow and provenance information—is the "most critical and laborious first step" for FAIR implementation (scheffler2022fairdataenabling pages 1-4).

**Recommendation**: The digital thread completeness KPI should be decomposed into explicit sub-metrics mapping to FAIR categories: (i) Findability (persistent identifiers, searchable metadata), (ii) Accessibility (API access, standard protocols), (iii) Interoperability (standard vocabularies, ontological tags), (iv) Reusability (provenance, licenses, rich descriptors), plus (v) End-to-end linkage completeness. This decomposition would make the KPI more defensible to DOE reviewers and prepare the Phase II FAIR compliance target.

## 5. Is 90% the Right Target?

The 90% target is well-calibrated relative to existing practice when viewed from the broader materials science community. Quantitative assessments of metadata completeness in curated materials datasets are sobering: Comandella et al. (2020) found that publicly available nanomaterial datasets in eNanoMapper achieved an average completeness score of only 0.43, with industry-provided datasets reaching 0.68 (comandella2020qualityofphysicochemical pages 1-2, comandella2020qualityofphysicochemical pages 7-8). Per-section analysis reveals that outcome/sample fields achieved ~0.52, methods/instrument details ~0.35, sample preparation ~0.24, and SOPs only ~0.15 (comandella2020qualityofphysicochemical pages 7-8). In archival AM literature, incomplete records are common and listwise deletion of incomplete cases dramatically reduces usable data (wietechareiman2025buildingarobust pages 31-36). Over 25% of critical metadata are omitted in genomics repositories (comandella2020qualityofphysicochemical pages 1-2).

Against this backdrop, 90% is a strong target for a purpose-built SDL platform with schema validation at ingestion. However, the risk is that in a 9-month Phase I, early iterations will inevitably have lower completeness as the infrastructure matures. The base target of ≥85% is more realistic as an overall campaign average, with 90% achievable for later iterations once the pipeline is stabilized.

**Assessment**: The 90% target is neither too easy nor unrealistically hard, provided that: (a) completeness is measured against a defined schema rather than ad hoc expectations, (b) early ramp-up iterations are expected to have lower completeness, and (c) the metric counts schema-validated records rather than just the presence of any data. A tiered scoring approach (not binary complete/incomplete) would add nuance: for instance, Tier 1 (composition + build linked) → Tier 2 (+ test results) → Tier 3 (+ BOFN trace) → Tier 4 (FAIR-ready).

## 6. Should the Digital Thread Include Supply-Risk Metadata for Topic 3B?

**Yes—strongly recommended.** Topic 3B is specifically about "reducing demand for critical minerals," and the digital thread should preserve the supply-risk rationale behind composition design decisions, not merely the performance outcomes.

Helbig et al. (2020, 2021) provide a well-established framework using 12 elemental-level indicators across four categories (supply-reduction, demand-driven, market concentration, political risks), with alloy-level aggregation via arithmetic mean, mass-share, cost-share, or maximum-element methods (helbig2020supplyriskconsiderations pages 12-14, helbig2020supplyriskconsiderations pages 7-9, helbig2021anoverviewof pages 1-2). Their work on Ni-based superalloys demonstrates that elements like Al, Ti, and Cr have relatively low supply risk, while elements like Re, Mo, and Co pose higher risks (helbig2020supplyriskconsiderations pages 12-14, helbig2020supplyriskconsiderations pages 1-3).

Mulukutla et al. (2024) have demonstrated supply-risk-aware alloy design using Helbig-style indicators integrated directly into batch Bayesian optimization for high-entropy alloys, showing that supply risk can be treated as an explicit optimization objective alongside mechanical performance (mulukutla2024supplyriskawarealloy pages 2-5). This approach is directly aligned with FLAIME's material strategy for Al-Zr and Al-Mn-Cr-Zr families and Cu-Cr-Zr complement, and mirrors the "policy-aware optimization (cost, carbon, supply-risk)" capabilities described in DataScribe (singh2026datascribeanainative pages 4-6).

**Recommendation**: Each composition in the digital thread should carry: (i) an alloy-level supply-risk index computed from Helbig-style elemental indicators, (ii) per-element criticality scores for all constituent elements, (iii) the DOE Critical Minerals List classification for each element, and (iv) a supply-risk delta showing improvement relative to incumbent alloys (e.g., Be-Cu for Cu-Cr-Zr replacements). This directly evidences FLAIME's mission alignment and provides auditable supply-risk provenance.

**Additional considerations:**

- **Automation is key**: Maffettone et al. emphasize that SDLs can generate metadata automatically and improve reproducibility, but practical data acquisition and digitization remain barriers (maffettoneUnknownyearwhatismissing pages 3-4). FLAIME's closed-loop architecture (CALPHAD → atomization → LPBF → testing → BOFN) should enforce automated metadata capture at each handoff point.

- **Schema versioning**: The provenance schema itself should be version-controlled, as BIRDSHOT found that schema modifications were necessary over the course of the campaign to improve accessibility and reduce inconsistencies (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 3-5).

- **Negative results and failed builds**: BIRDSHOT's data infrastructure intentionally accommodates negative and trial-and-error results (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 15-17). FLAIME should ensure that failed powder runs, aborted builds, and out-of-spec tensile specimens are captured in the digital thread with appropriate status flags, as these are critical for BOFN learning.

- **Defensibility for DOE review**: The KPI becomes strongest when it includes: a published provenance schema (deliverable), a quantitative scoring rubric, evidence of schema validation at ingestion, supply-risk metadata per composition, and a Phase II audit plan tied to external FAIR assessment criteria. This transforms "digital thread completeness" from a vague process metric into a concrete, measurable, and mission-aligned programmatic deliverable.

In summary, the ≥90% digital thread completeness target is well-motivated and substantially above the norms of typical materials science practice, where completeness scores of 0.43–0.68 are common even in curated datasets (comandella2020qualityofphysicochemical pages 1-2, comandella2020qualityofphysicochemical pages 7-8). The target is achievable for a purpose-built SDL platform with automated ingestion and schema validation, but requires: (a) a formally defined provenance schema, (b) graph-backed UUID provenance rather than file-level linking alone, (c) explicit FAIR sub-metrics, (d) supply-risk metadata for Topic 3B alignment, and (e) a ramped trajectory with tiered scoring to accommodate infrastructure maturation during the 9-month Phase I.
