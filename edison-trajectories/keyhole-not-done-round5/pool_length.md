## Measured and Predicted Melt-Pool Dimensions in LPBF of Al-Si Alloys at 200–400 W, 0.3–1.5 m/s

### 1. Melt Pool Length — Why It Is Scarce and What Is Available

Melt-pool **length** is the least commonly reported dimension in Al-Si LPBF studies because most investigations rely on transverse post-mortem cross-sections, which yield only width and depth. The most direct measurement of pool length in AlSi10Mg comes from the high-speed synchrotron X-ray radiography of Guo et al. (2020), who imaged the longitudinal (y-z) plane of the melt pool at 140 kHz frame rate at the APS 32-ID-B beamline (guo2020insitufullfieldmapping pages 6-9, guo2020insitufullfieldmapping pages 13-16, guo2020insitufullfieldmapping pages 16-19). For AlSi10Mg at **364 W, 0.6 m/s** (depression/keyhole-transition mode, D4σ ≈ 80 µm), the flow-speed quantification behind the depression was performed across 12 consecutive 100 µm sections (Y1–Y12), indicating the melt pool extended **at least ≈1200 µm** behind the rear depression wall before flow speed dropped to the measurement resolution of 0.025 m/s (guo2020insitufullfieldmapping pages 16-19). Including the depression zone itself and the region ahead of it, the total pool length is likely in the range of **≈1.3–1.5 mm** at these parameters, as can be estimated from the longitudinal X-ray images in Figures 3 and 6a of that work (guo2020insitufullfieldmapping media 12f43b84, guo2020insitufullfieldmapping media 600a4920). At **260 W, 0.6 m/s** (conduction mode), the pool was visibly shorter (Figures 2a–b), though no explicit length value was stated (guo2020insitufullfieldmapping pages 9-13, guo2020insitufullfieldmapping pages 13-16).

This synchrotron-derived pool length of ≈1.2–1.5 mm at 364 W/0.6 m/s is notably shorter than the 1.47 mm predicted by the Thermo-Calc AM Module for the user's Al-24Si blend at 375 W/0.4 m/s, but reasonably consistent given the different alloy, different beam size (80 vs. 90 µm), and different speed. The user's own models giving 0.35–0.6 mm appear to underpredict the pool length significantly compared to the Guo et al. synchrotron data, possibly because the rearward tail—sustained by Marangoni-driven surface flow and slow heat conduction at Péclet number Pe ≈ 0.9—extends well beyond what simple energy-balance or analytical keyhole models capture (guo2020insitufullfieldmapping pages 23-27).

**Bogdanova et al. (2024)** performed single-track experiments on AlSi10Mg at 325–375 W and 300–1200 mm/s, measuring cross-sectional width and depth on both LPBF-fabricated and cast substrates (bogdanova2024mesoscalesimulationof pages 9-10). From Figure 4, at **375 W/0.6 m/s** (LPBF substrate), the depth is approximately **400 µm** and the width approximately **350 µm**, indicating a keyhole-dominant regime with depth > width (bogdanova2024mesoscalesimulationof media 6d2c8e73). At 375 W/0.3 m/s, the depth reaches approximately **500 µm**. Cast substrates yielded values approximately **10% lower** than LPBF substrates, attributed to the Si-network thermal resistance in the cast microstructure (bogdanova2024mesoscalesimulationof pages 10-13, bogdanova2024mesoscalesimulationof pages 9-10).

**Zavala-Arredondo et al. (2019)** measured AlSi10Mg melt pools using pixel-counted cross-sections at different exposure times (zavalaarredondo2019useofpower pages 10-12). At 20 µs exposure, width = 253 ± 30 µm and depth = 232 ± 15 µm (W/D ≈ 1.09). At 30 µs, width = 332 ± 25 µm and depth = 308 ± 15 µm. At 40 µs, width = 358 ± 30 µm and depth = 472 ± 15 µm (W/D ≈ 0.76), which fell below the **critical W/D ratio of 0.8** identified for the onset of keyhole porosity (zavalaarredondo2019useofpower pages 12-14). Above a melt depth of approximately **410 µm**, keyhole porosity limited densification to below 99% (zavalaarredondo2019useofpower pages 12-14).

**Piedra et al. (2026)** combined OpenFOAM CFD (with Marangoni convection, recoil pressure, and phase change) and experiments for AlSi10Mg at 370 W with 50 and 100 µm spot diameters. At 370 W/350 mm/s/50 µm spot, the experimental track width was **349 µm** and the CFD prediction was **363 µm** (<4% error) (piedra2026experimentalandnumerical pages 7-10). At higher speeds (1750 mm/s), the width decreased to **145 µm** experimentally. Their CFD captured the transition from keyhole-mode to conduction-mode and reproduced keyhole-induced macroporosity at VED = 845 J/mm³ (Figures 7–9) (piedra2026experimentalandnumerical pages 7-10, piedra2026experimentalandnumerical pages 10-13).

**Leis, Weber & Graf (2021)** systematically mapped the AlSi10Mg process window from 10.8 to 1754 W and 0.7–1.3 m/s, identifying the key-bowl melting (KBM) regime at P_L/d_b ≈ 3000–5200 W/mm as optimal for low porosity and high process efficiency (7–8%), with an aspect ratio (depth/width) near unity (leis2021processwindowfor pages 7-8, leis2021processwindowfor pages 8-10). Deep-penetration melting (aspect ratio > 1.4) occurred above ≈6000 W/mm, producing keyhole pores exceeding 10% porosity (Table 2, Figures 5–9) (leis2021processwindowfor pages 8-10).

### 3. AlSi12 and Hypereutectic Al-Si Alloy Data

For **Al12Si**, Ghomashchi & Nafisi (2023) reported measured melt-pool depth of **51 ± 8 µm** (mean, range 50–80 µm) and a transverse diameter of **195.5 ± 11.7 µm** from etched longitudinal cross-sections, though the specific laser power and scan speed were not stated in the extracted data (ghomashchi2023solidificationofal12si pages 4-6). No published measurements of melt pool length, width, or depth were found for **hypereutectic Al-24Si** alloys at 200–400 W/0.3–1.5 m/s. This represents a significant gap in the literature: studies of LPBF-processed hypereutectic Al-Si alloys (e.g., Risse et al. 2023 for AlSi50/AlSi70, Cai et al. 2022 for Al-42Si) focus on microstructure and density rather than reporting measured melt pool geometry.

### 4. CFD-Predicted Pool Length and Rearward Surface Flow Speed

**Ni et al. (2024)** developed a 2D multiphase CFD model of AlSi10Mg at 300 W with recoil pressure, Marangoni force (dγ/dT = −3 × 10⁻⁴ N m⁻¹ K⁻¹), and surface tension for 1200–2400 mm/s scan speed. The simulated width-to-depth ratios were 2.09–2.50, consistent with predominantly conduction-mode pools at these high speeds (ni2024thermodynamicstudyof pages 3-5, ni2024thermodynamicstudyof media c76e3c63, ni2024thermodynamicstudyof media 8606fc73). The model confirmed that Marangoni force drives circulating flow, gasification recoil pressure causes keyhole indentation, and surface tension governs late-stage melt pool oscillation (ni2024thermodynamicstudyof pages 9-12).

**Gusarov et al. (2024)** noted that their CFD model of AlSi10Mg underestimated the pool depth (≈130 µm computed vs. ≈150–180 µm experimental), attributing part of the discrepancy to neglect of vapor recoil pressure which deepens the pool via surface depression (gusarov2024twodimensionalheattransfer pages 18-22).

**Piedra et al. (2026)** presented CFD results with OpenFOAM including free-surface VOF, Marangoni, and recoil pressure. At 370 W/1050 mm/s/100 µm spot, the simulation produced a deep keyhole-shaped pool, while at 2100 mm/s the pool became shallow and parabolic (Figures 10–11). Their velocity fields showed "intense recoil pressure-driven flow and Marangoni convection" with "upward and rearward jets" in keyhole mode (piedra2026experimentalandnumerical pages 10-13). No explicit pool-length value was tabulated, but the longitudinal cross-section images in Figures 10–13 show melt regions consistent with several hundred micrometers in length (piedra2026experimentalandnumerical pages 7-10, piedra2026experimentalandnumerical pages 10-13).

- **Rearward surface flow speed** (flow E, Marangoni-driven) in depression-mode AlSi10Mg at **364 W/0.6 m/s**: average projected speed ≈ **0.52 m/s** and maximum ≈ **1.25 m/s** in the first 100 µm behind the depression, decaying to ≈ **0.08 m/s** average at ~1200 µm behind the depression (guo2020insitufullfieldmapping pages 16-19).

### 6. Implications for the User's Model

The literature data suggest the following for the user's model validation at 375 W/0.4 m/s:

1. **Pool width and depth** at 375 W and 0.3–0.6 m/s in AlSi10Mg should be approximately **350–500 µm deep** and **300–400 µm wide** based on the Bogdanova et al. (2024) data (bogdanova2024mesoscalesimulationof media 6d2c8e73, bogdanova2024mesoscalesimulationof pages 9-10). These dimensions are in keyhole mode (depth > width).

2. **Pool length** of ≈1.2–1.5 mm at 364 W/0.6 m/s (from Guo et al. synchrotron imaging) is consistent with the Thermo-Calc AM Module prediction of 1.47 mm at 375 W/0.4 m/s. The user's models predicting 0.35–0.6 mm appear to significantly underestimate the rearward pool tail, which is sustained by Marangoni-driven backward surface flow. Including Marangoni flow in the model is likely critical for capturing this extended tail (guo2020insitufullfieldmapping pages 16-19, guo2020insitufullfieldmapping pages 23-27).

3. **Rearward surface flow** for Marangoni implementation should use velocities on the order of **0.5–1.3 m/s** near the keyhole/depression, decaying over the pool length. The surface-tension temperature coefficient for AlSi10Mg is approximately **−1.5 × 10⁻⁴ N m⁻¹ K⁻¹** (pelevin2025influenceofalsi10mg pages 7-10) or **−3 × 10⁻⁴ N m⁻¹ K⁻¹** (ni2024thermodynamicstudyof pages 3-5), depending on the source, and this value strongly affects the flow pattern and pool shape.

4. No measured melt pool data were located for **hypereutectic Al-24Si** at the specified parameters; the nearest comparisons are AlSi10Mg and Al12Si data above. Given the higher Si content (and thus lower thermal conductivity in the melt and different surface tension characteristics), pool dimensions in Al-24Si may differ modestly, but quantitative validation data for this composition do not currently exist in the open literature.
