## Scope and framing
DOE Genesis Mission Topic 3B (Critical Minerals, Focus Area B) calls for “automated discovery platforms” and “closed-loop systems” that integrate hypothesis→experiment→validation→manufacturing with the explicit objective of **reducing demand** for critical minerals. In practice, the most competitive proposals will therefore (i) target a high-leverage critical-minerals demand driver (batteries, magnets, grid/electrification) and (ii) demonstrate an end-to-end autonomous loop that includes *manufacturing and qualification*, not only computation or benchtop synthesis. ## (1) Main categories of DOE-funded critical minerals research (and minerals emphasized)
DOE’s Critical Materials Strategy frames investment across the full supply chain, with five pillars that map directly to common funding categories: **(1) diversify & expand supply, (2) develop alternatives (substitution), (3) materials and manufacturing efficiency, (4) circular economy (recycling/reuse), (5) enabling activities (criticality assessment, etc.)** (bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 1-9). 

Within this umbrella, DOE’s 2023 Critical Materials Assessment emphasizes energy-technology drivers and repeatedly highlights the following **materials and application clusters**:
- **Batteries / stationary storage / EVs**: lithium, cobalt, nickel, graphite (and manganese) as key constraints (bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 16-18, bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 21-23).  
- **Permanent magnets (EV motors, wind)**: rare earth elements (notably neodymium and dysprosium/terbium) as high-risk inputs (bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 16-18, bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 133-136).  
- **Hydrogen electrolyzers**: iridium and platinum demand growth (bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 16-18).  
- **Power electronics**: gallium and silicon carbide (SiC) (bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 16-18, bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 133-136).  

The Critical Materials Institute (CMI) cumulative report provides concrete examples of DOE-funded critical-minerals R&D categories: selective separations, recovery from unconventional/waste resources (coal ash, phosphogypsum), recycling and recovery from batteries/e-scrap, beneficiation, plus TEA/LCA and thermodynamic databanks/models to accelerate process design (lograsso2024criticalmaterialsinstitute pages 11-14, lograsso2024criticalmaterialsinstitute pages 14-18). CMI explicitly ties workstreams to minerals including rare earths (Nd, Dy, Tb, etc.), lithium, cobalt, nickel, manganese, graphite, gallium/indium and others (lograsso2024criticalmaterialsinstitute pages 11-14, lograsso2024criticalmaterialsinstitute pages 14-18).

## (2) Autonomous/AI-driven materials discovery platforms for critical-minerals applications
The current “competitive set” for Topic 3B can be grouped into a few recurring autonomous-platform types:

### A. Autonomous solid-state synthesis (powder discovery) platforms
A flagship example is the **A-Lab** autonomous lab (UC Berkeley + LBNL + Google DeepMind), which integrates computational screening and literature/historical knowledge with robotics for solid-state powder synthesis, ML/NLP-assisted recipe suggestion, and ML-driven XRD phase identification in a **closed-loop active-learning workflow** (szymanski2023anautonomouslaboratory pages 1-2). The workflow schematic is shown in Fig. 1 of the paper (szymanski2023anautonomouslaboratory media 152cd734). While broadly applicable (battery cathodes, magnet compounds, substitutes), it is primarily a *powder synthesis + phase validation* engine rather than a manufacturing/qualification engine (szymanski2023anautonomouslaboratory pages 1-2).

### B. Experiment–theory autonomous loops for alloy/phase-space mapping
AMASE (University of Maryland + NIST) demonstrates a self-driving loop that couples autonomous experiments to **live thermodynamic computation (CALPHAD/Thermo-Calc)** with Bayesian/GP decision-making, achieving a large reduction in experiments for mapping a phase diagram (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3). This archetype is directly relevant to substitution alloy design, but the manufacturing/qualification step is typically not central (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3).

### C. Battery materials formulation platforms (critical battery minerals adjacency)
A **PNNL + Argonne** high-throughput robotic platform combined with Bayesian active learning is demonstrated for electrolyte/solvent discovery, screening >2,000 candidates while experimentally evaluating <10% (noh2024anintegratedhighthroughput pages 1-2). This is strongly aligned with the battery critical-minerals ecosystem (Li/Co/Ni/graphite constraints are key DOE concerns), but it is primarily chemistry/formulation rather than structural-material substitution or manufacturing qualification (noh2024anintegratedhighthroughput pages 1-2, bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 16-18).

### D. AI-driven recycling/extraction optimization loops
Closed-loop ML + experimental pipelines exist for **hydrometallurgical recycling** optimization (e.g., lithium-ion battery recycling), often integrating TEA/LCA and iteratively improving models as new experiments are run (kim2025machinelearning–drivenhydrometallurgical pages 1-2). In the national-lab ecosystem, **Argonne’s MERF** is explicitly positioned as a scale-up platform integrating automation and AI/ML-enabled smart manufacturing, with case studies including *selective membranes for critical mineral recovery* and *lithium recovery from battery recycling waste via electrodialysis* (zhang2026acceleratingdiscoveryto pages 1-2). These efforts are highly competitive in “circular economy” framing but tend to be **supply-side** (recovery) rather than **demand-reduction via substitution** (kim2025machinelearning–drivenhydrometallurgical pages 1-2, zhang2026acceleratingdiscoveryto pages 1-2).

### E. Computational closed-loop substitution discovery (rare-earth-free magnets)
For direct demand reduction, computational closed-loop workflows for **rare-earth-free magnets** are a natural competitor class. One example uses CGCNN + adaptive feedback and DFT to discover candidate Fe–Co–P compounds below the convex hull, explicitly positioned as rare-earth-free magnet discovery (liao2024machinelearningaccelerateddiscovery pages 1-4). These proposals are likely to be common in Topic 3B because they directly address a high-visibility critical-material bottleneck (rare earths in magnets) highlighted by DOE (bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 16-18, liao2024machinelearningaccelerateddiscovery pages 1-4).

## (6) How an autonomous Al/Cu alloy discovery platform differentiates
An **autonomous Al/Cu alloy discovery platform** can differentiate by bridging three proposal clusters that often appear separately:

1) **From “materials discovery” to “manufacturing-qualified materials”**: Many self-driving labs focus on powder synthesis + phase ID (A-Lab) (szymanski2023anautonomouslaboratory pages 1-2), or on experiment–theory loops without part manufacturing (AMASE) (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3), or on purely computational loops (rare-earth-free magnet screening) (liao2024machinelearningaccelerateddiscovery pages 1-4). A powder→print→test system directly operationalizes the Topic 3B requirement to integrate manufacturing and qualification into the loop.

2) **Closed-loop qualification evidence (not only discovery speed)**: ORNL’s AM work shows that in situ analytics can (a) detect anomalies with ML and (b) even drive autonomous parameter adjustments, while also connecting in situ signals to ex situ observations—explicitly targeting reductions in expensive testing campaigns relevant to certification (scime2019viabilityofdata pages 1-7, scime2019viabilityofdata pages 21-25). A platform that integrates **printing + in situ monitoring + mechanical testing + model updates** is structurally aligned to this logic.

3) **Demand reduction framing via substitution + efficiency**: DOE explicitly funds “develop alternatives” (substitution) and “materials/manufacturing efficiency” as strategic pillars (bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 1-9). Al/Cu alloys can be positioned as **abundant-element structural substitutes** that (i) avoid/minimize short-term critical elements highlighted by DOE (e.g., cobalt, nickel, rare earths, gallium, iridium) and (ii) enable lightweighting/energy-efficiency benefits, while the autonomous loop reduces development and qualification time/cost.

4) **Manufacturing-aware decision-making**: A manufacturing-coupled loop can include process constraints (printability windows, defect rates, microstructure control) as first-class objectives in the optimizer. DOE manufacturing BRN documents emphasize that progress requires multimodal sensing, data fusion, surrogate models for real-time use, and embedded feedback between in situ characterization and computation—i.e., the core requirements for an autonomous print/qualification platform (jenks2020basicresearchneeds pages 38-40, jenks2020basicresearchneeds pages 147-149).

## (7) Advantages of a physical powder→print→test closed loop over purely computational approaches
A physical closed-loop system has several evidence-supported advantages in the Topic 3B context:
- **Direct manufacturability and part-quality control**: ORNL’s Peregrine demonstrates ML-based anomaly detection and examples where process parameters are “autonomously adjusted,” showing that physical loops can correct manufacturing in real time (scime2019viabilityofdata pages 1-7). Computational discovery alone cannot establish this.
- **Accelerated qualification and reduced test burden**: ORNL explicitly targets correlation of in situ data with ex situ observations and argues this integration can reduce expensive testing campaigns that dominate qualification (scime2019viabilityofdata pages 21-25). 
- **Geometry- and build-aware control strategies**: Physics-guided LPBF control can generate layer-varying processing plans unique to each part geometry, addressing a key transferability/qualification barrier in AM (riensche2025rapidautonomousand pages 2-3).
- **Integration of heterogeneous sensing + models**: DOE’s manufacturing BRN emphasizes the need for orthogonal high-speed sensing, data fusion, and fast surrogate modeling for adaptive control and online evaluation/qualification (jenks2020basicresearchneeds pages 38-40, jenks2020basicresearchneeds pages 147-149). This is naturally realized in a powder→print→test cyber-physical loop.
