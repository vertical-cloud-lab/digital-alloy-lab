# Project Naming Research

*Edison Scientific LITERATURE_HIGH query + web research*

Question: What are the names, acronyms, and branding of existing funded research projects, platforms, software frameworks, and laboratories in the fields of autonomous materials discovery, AI/ML-accelerated alloy design, closed-loop experimentation, LPBF additive manufacturing, and digital materials informatics? What best practices exist for creating memorable research project acronyms?

## 1. Best Practices for Memorable Research Acronyms

Web research on acronym best practices (sources: Proposia/Horizon Europe, The Scientist, Mind the Graph, Pixelshrink, QuillBot) identifies these key principles:

| Principle | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Pronounceability** | The acronym should form a word people can say easily | STORM, DART, SQUID |
| **3–7 letters** | Shorter acronyms balance brevity and clarity | ARES (4), FORGE (5), CADENCE (7) |
| **Thematic relevance** | Must reinforce the mission, topic, or strategy | METALS (alloys), ULTIMATE (temperature) |
| **Backronym-first** | Settle on a catchy acronym first, then build the full name around it | DARPA METALS, ARPA-E ULTIMATE |
| **Dual meaning** | Names that carry both technical and cultural/metaphorical meaning are most memorable | NOMAD (exploration), ARES (Greek god), JARVIS (Marvel AI + real acronym) |
| **Avoid conflicts** | Check for negative connotations, existing well-known uses, and overlap in other languages | ATLAS (CERN), SPARK (Apache) |
| **Test aloud** | If it "trips off the tongue," that's a good sign | — |

### 2d. Naming Convention Patterns (from Edison)

| Pattern | Examples | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Mythological / historical figure | Ada, Clio, Phoenics, Chimera, Gryffin | Culturally recognizable, makes labs feel agentic and intelligent |
| Descriptive acronym | ARES, SARA, AMASE, RAPID, ESCALATE, HT-READ, JARVIS | Pronounceable acronym whose expansion states the function |
| Government backronym | ULTIMATE, DMREF, METALS, AIM | Mission-forward, designed for impact and reuse in discourse |
| OS-suffix | AlabOS, ARES OS, ChemOS, NIMS-OS | Signals orchestration and modular extensibility |
| Simple descriptive | A-Lab, Polybot, Materials Project | Accessibility and immediate comprehension |
| Compound / portmanteau | AFLOW, CALPHAD, NOMAD | Compresses technical phrases, may carry metaphorical meaning |
| Pop-culture adjacent | JARVIS (Marvel AI + real acronym) | Extra memorability from familiar cultural references |

## 3. Name Conflict Analysis

An initial web scan (2026-04-02) did not identify prominent use of certain metallurgical terms as program names. The table below summarizes conflict risk based on both web and literature searches.

## 5. Selected Name

**FLAIME** — **F**ast **L**earning **AI** for **M**ineral **E**fficiency

Selected by PI (2026-04-02, name confirmed; 2026-04-10, backronym updated for Topic 3B) as the project name for the BYU Vertical Cloud Lab's Genesis Mission proposal targeting **Topic 3B — Critical Minerals: AI-Enabled Materials Discovery, Development, and Qualification (AMMTO)**.

- **Usage:** "The FLAIME platform at BYU's Vertical Cloud Lab," "the FLAIME pipeline," "FLAIME framework"
- **Backronym:** "Fast Learning AI for Mineral Efficiency" — clean F-L-AI-M-E mapping, strongest Topic 3B signal. "Mineral Efficiency" directly signals DOE priorities around reducing critical-mineral dependence through substitution and efficiency strategies.
- **Genesis Mission proposal title for Topic 3B:** See `edison-trajectories/proposal-title.md`, Section 9 for top 3B title candidates. Edison's top recommendation: "FLAIME: Accelerating Critical Mineral Substitution through AI-Guided Autonomous Alloy Discovery, Development, and Qualification" (10/10).

FLAIME is distinctive, pronounceable, conflict-free, and embeds "AI" directly in the name — immediately signaling the program's core methodology. It evokes the laser in LPBF and the heat of metallurgical transformation while being unique enough to be searchable and brandable.

### Name Retention for Topic 11C

**Recommendation: Keep the FLAIME name. Change the backronym, not the brand.**

The FLAIME *name* (the 6-letter word) has properties that are topic-agnostic and should be preserved regardless of whether the proposal targets Topic 12F or 11C:

1. **No naming conflicts** — confirmed via Edison LITERATURE_HIGH validation (no results in materials science, federal programs, or commercial)
2. **"AI" embedded visually** — signals AI methodology at a glance, equally valuable for 11C ("AI-Accelerated Science") and 12F ("AI-Enabled Materials Discovery")
3. **"Flame" imagery** — laser/heat association relevant to LPBF regardless of whether the framing is optimization or mechanism discovery
4. **Distinctive + searchable** — 6 letters, pronounceable, no Google/Scholar collision risk
5. **Backronym flexibility** — the "backronym-first" best practice (Section 1) explicitly anticipates that expansions can evolve while the brand stays fixed

The *backronym* "Fast Learning AI for Materials Experimentation" scores only 5/10 for 11C (see `edison-backronym-11c-emphasis.md`), but Edison provided two high-scoring replacements that preserve the FLAIME name:

| Alternative Backronym | 11C Fit | Total Score |
|---|---:|---:|
| Function-Linked AI for Mechanistic Exploration | 9/10 | 26/30 |
| Falsification-Led AI for Materials Elucidation | 10/10 | 27/30 |

Changing a backronym while keeping the project name is standard DOE/DARPA practice — the name is the *brand*, the expansion is the *framing*. The entire Edison analysis in `edison-backronym-11c-emphasis.md` was structured around preserving FLAIME while pivoting the expansion and title for 11C.

### Name Retention for Topic 3B (Selected Topic)

**Decision: Keep the FLAIME name. Backronym updated to "Fast Learning AI for Mineral Efficiency" for 3B.**

Edison LITERATURE_HIGH assessment (task `a0c015a8`, ~26K chars) confirms FLAIME scores **8/10 for Topic 3B** — the embedded "AI" directly mirrors 3B's "AI-Enabled" framing, "flame" imagery still resonates for LPBF, and no conflicts with critical minerals programs were found. A full rename scored only 5/10 (brand fragmentation risk). See `edison-name-title-3b-assessment.md` for the full analysis.

The original backronym ("Fast Learning AI for Materials Experimentation") scored **6/10 for 3B** (lacks critical minerals vocabulary). The initial Edison recommendation ("Fast-Learning AI for Mineral-Efficient Alloy Design," 9/10) was **downgraded** because "Alloy Design" creates silent letters — AD doesn't map to any letter in FLAIME.

A follow-up Edison query (task `6028f16d`, ~16K chars, 12 candidates scored) evaluated clean-mapping alternatives. PI-selected replacement:

> **"Fast Learning AI for Mineral Efficiency"** (35/40)

**F**ast **L**earning **AI** for **M**ineral **E**fficiency — every word maps cleanly to F-L-AI-M-E with no silent letters. "Mineral Efficiency" directly signals DOE priorities around reducing critical-mineral dependence through substitution and efficiency strategies. See `edison-backronym-3b-refinement.md` for the full 12-candidate evaluation.

Runner-up: "Federated Learning AI for Mineral Efficiency" (35/40) — adds multi-lab AI collaboration framing but may over-specify if the project doesn't center federated learning.

The 11C backronym alternatives ("Function-Linked AI for Mechanistic Exploration," "Falsification-Led AI for Materials Elucidation") are **wrong for 3B** — they were optimized for scientific-method framing and score poorly on critical mineral supply-risk language. This confirms that backronyms should be topic-specific.
