No. ## Finding

**I found no documented ultrasonic-atomization run identified as Cu–10Ni (UNS C70600) or Cu–30Ni (UNS C71500).** This is a statement about the accessible literature, not proof that such a run has never been performed. The closest *apparatus-level* evidence is a 2025 AMAZEMET rePOWDER paper that explicitly shows an **induction-melted “copper alloy”** pouring onto a vibrating blade. Crucially, it does **not identify that alloy as Cu–Ni** or report its melt temperature, superheat, blade composition, particle-size distribution, powder oxygen content, or process failures. Its photograph therefore cannot establish a successful C70600 or C71500 run. (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom media aa36e827)

### Closest ultrasonic precedents

| Material and method | Conditions actually reported | Powder results and limitations |
|---|---|---|
| **Unspecified copper alloy; induction-fed rePOWDER** | A stream from an induction-heated crucible strikes a **100-mm vibrating blade at 60 kHz**. The authors describe lifting a rod that seals the nozzle and using **argon overpressure** to control pouring; their photograph shows a focused spray from the copper alloy. The blade material, absolute gas pressure, vibration amplitude, temperature, and crucible/stopper compositions are **not disclosed for this copper-alloy example**. | Demonstrates that this *type of apparatus* has atomized a copper alloy, **not** which alloy or the properties of its powder. (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom media aa36e827) |
| **AZ31 magnesium; induction-fed rePOWDER** | A different, explicitly characterized induction-and-pour example used a **graphite crucible**, **950 °C**, a refractory plate at **40 kHz**, and **75% relative amplitude**—not a calibrated displacement. Reported pressure difference was **approximately 0.15 mbar**; atomization occurred under Ar overpressure with chamber oxygen **below 50 ppm**. Plate and stopper materials were not specified. | These settings belong to **AZ31, not copper or Cu–Ni**; chamber oxygen is **not powder oxygen**. In another induction-fed Mg–Ag example, the authors report needing **1223 K** for stable flow, with suspected Zn evaporation, deposits on particles, and particle bonding; they suggest a larger nozzle could permit a lower pouring temperature. (dobkowska2024microstructureandcorrosion pages 2-4, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6) |
| **Cu- and Ni-containing multicomponent alloy; arc-fed rePOWDER** | An **Al₅Cr₅Cu₁₀Fe₃₅Mn₁₀Ni₃₅ alloy (atomic %)** was arc-remelted into rods and ultrasonically atomized on a **molybdenum sonotrode at 40 kHz**, with **5 L/min Ar** and **250 A plasma current**. Chamber O₂ was **23 ppm initially, 50 ppm during operation**. This was **not** an induction-crucible pour and **not** binary cupronickel. | Approximately **90 mass % passed a 100-µm sieve**; particles were predominantly spherical, but some had satellites, oxide spots, and irregular coarse particles. **Mo appeared in particles, with 2.6% average Mo reported in the analyzed alloy**, attributed to sonotrode erosion. The cited O₂ measurements concern the **chamber**, not the oxygen concentration *in the powder*. (goncharov2026designofcobaltfree pages 1-2, goncharov2026designofcobaltfree pages 2-4, goncharov2026designofcobaltfree pages 10-13) |
| **Other arc-fed rePOWDER alloys** | An Oak Ridge/AMAZEMET account describes an arc-melting implementation at **20 kHz**, with droplets carried away in low-pressure Ar; it illustrates powder production and an Inconel 625 application. Separately, a reported **C103 Nb-alloy** run used a **30-mm TZM rod sonotrode at 40 kHz in Ar**. | These demonstrate Ni-alloy processing and TZM hardware, respectively, but provide **no Cu–Ni induction-pour recipe**. The Oak Ridge report’s **graphite crucible and 40-psi Ar** refer to its *different aerosol-jetting process*, **not** rePOWDER ultrasonic atomization. (zach2023sphericalpowderscontrol pages 4-4, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3, zach2023sphericalpowderscontrol pages 2-4) |

**For C70600 and C71500 specifically, all requested operating and powder-quality values remain unreported:** measured melt temperature and superheat above the *applicable alloy liquidus*; choice among Mo/TZM, graphite/carbon composite or ceramic sonotrode; actual frequency and displacement amplitude; Ar pressure and purity; crucible, nozzle and stopper materials (including whether BN was used); D10/D50/D90, quantitative sphericity and powder oxygen; and any nozzle blockage, freezing, reaction, erosion or contamination. ### Cu–Ni melt-contact evidence and practical cautions

* **Molybdenum is not necessarily inert to a Cu–Ni melt.** A direct immersion study held Mo sheet in Cu–Ni melts in **alumina crucibles under hydrogen** at **1200 and 1300 °C**, measuring saturation after roughly **3–6 hours**. Its nickel concentration is explicitly a **weight fraction relative to Cu + Ni**. At **9.1 wt% Ni**, measured equilibrium dissolved Mo was **0.0265 wt% at 1200 °C** and **0.0447 wt% at 1300 °C**; at approximately **32 wt% Ni**, it was **3.71 wt% at 1300 °C** (no 1200 °C measurement at that composition). These are long-contact equilibrium measurements, **not predictions of contamination during millisecond sonotrode contact**. Together with the independently observed Mo pickup from a vibrating Mo sonotrode, they warrant checking Mo in trial powders, particularly for the more Ni-rich grade. (teodorovich1980solubilityofmolybdenum pages 1-3, goncharov2026designofcobaltfree pages 10-13)
* **Boron nitride has a relevant but limited precedent.** A 1993 report describes melting a copper–nickel alloy called **Constantan** in a **yttria-coated alumina crucible**, then inserting a **BN casting die** into its melt. The chamber was backfilled with Ar and held at about **3 inches of water** overpressure. The investigators successfully melted the charge and controlled its temperature **above 1400 °C**, but **did not achieve the intended continuous casting**: Mo *heater leads* failed, and alumina crucibles cracked on thermal cycling. The report does **not** establish BN nozzle/stopper lifetime in an ultrasonic atomizer, identify Constantan as C70600/C71500, or demonstrate a BN–Cu–Ni contamination limit. Its Mo heater failure must not be confused with melt-induced Mo-sonotrode erosion. (jasinski1993castingofmetallic pages 23-29, jasinski1993castingofmetallic pages 29-33)
* **Gas atomization is a separate comparison, not evidence for the vibrating-plate route.** A published study identifies **gas-atomized Ni–Cu-based Alloy 400 powder**; Alloy 400 is Ni-rich rather than the requested Cu-rich 90/10 or 70/30 grades. I did not verify a primary-source gas-atomization process record with particle-size, oxygen and operating specifications for **C70600 or C71500**, so none can responsibly be supplied here. (sojoodi2025integrationofcircular pages 6-7)

**Bottom line:** the published induction-fed copper-alloy image is the nearest demonstration of the requested configuration, while the Cu–Ni/Mo immersion measurements and Constantan/BN casting apparatus provide the most directly relevant material-contact evidence. Neither establishes a tested melt superheat, sonotrode choice or powder specification for Cu–10Ni or Cu–30Ni. (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom media aa36e827, teodorovich1980solubilityofmolybdenum pages 1-3, jasinski1993castingofmetallic pages 23-29)
