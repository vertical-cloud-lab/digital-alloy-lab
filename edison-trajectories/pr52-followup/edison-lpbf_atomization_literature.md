## Precedent Search: Closed-Loop, ML-Driven Adaptive Experimentation for LPBF with Integrated Powder Atomization

### 1. Core Finding: No Fully Integrated Atomize–Print–Test–Decide Closed Loop Exists

After an extensive search of peer-reviewed publications, preprints, technical reports, and patents, **no published work was found that demonstrates a fully closed-loop, machine-learning- or Bayesian-optimization-driven adaptive experimentation workflow where powder atomization of a new composition and LPBF printing are both automated within the same decision loop** (i.e., atomize → print → test → ML-decide → repeat, automatically). The concept exists as an aspiration in the field and is partially addressed by several groups, but the key bottleneck—integrating custom powder atomization as an automated step inside an ML-driven experimental campaign—has not been closed in any published demonstration.

The comparison table below summarizes the closest published and patented efforts, evaluated against the three criteria requested: (a) whether atomization is automated and closed into the ML decision loop, (b) what mechanical testing is used, and (c) whether critical-mineral content is addressed.

### 2. Closest Approaches to the Target Workflow

#### 2.1 Taheri-Mousavi, Hart et al. (MIT) — Hybrid ML/CALPHAD Design → Amazemet Atomization → LPBF

This is the closest published work to the requested workflow. Taheri-Mousavi et al. used a hybrid machine-learning/CALPHAD (ICME) framework with Bayesian optimization to inverse-design a novel Al-Er-Zr-Ni alloy system for LPBF (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 9-12, taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 1-4). The computational loop converged in ~4 rounds of BO connected to ICME simulations, and a neural-network surrogate reduced sample requirements (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 9-12, taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 7-9). The optimized composition was then atomized into custom powder by AMAZEMET and printed via LPBF, achieving crack-free parts with as-built hardness ~180 HV and retained strength after aging (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 9-12). Mechanical testing included Vickers microhardness (100 g) and tensile testing per ASTM E8 (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 25-28). Critically, the work explicitly addressed critical-mineral content by designing alloys to reduce reliance on expensive/scarce Sc, incorporating cost and sustainability metrics (glaser2025computationaldesignof pages 1-3). However, **(a) the atomization step was outsourced to AMAZEMET and was not automated within the ML decision loop**—it was a one-time custom powder production after the computational design converged (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 25-28). A related provisional patent (PCT/US2023/078228) covers the methodology, compositions, and processing (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 25-28). A companion study by Glaser, Hart, and Taheri-Mousavi incorporated material cost explicitly into the BO loop and noted that "powder production is a challenging and expensive step" and that LPBF of custom powders "will be performed" for their ultimate designs, with a CMU-filed provisional patent (glaser2025computationaldesignof pages 11-13).

#### 2.2 ULTIMATE: BIRDSHOT (Texas A&M, Arróyave et al.)

The BIRDSHOT project is a $9.5M DOE-funded effort that employed closed-loop, multi-objective, multi-information-source Bayesian optimization for refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) design in the Al-Cr-Mo-Nb-Ta-Ti-V-W-Zr-Hf system (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 1-5, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 11-14). High-throughput synthesis produced ~140 unique RHEA compositions (~13 alloys/week) via arc melting, with 116 homogenized and characterized by SEM/EDX, XRD, and Vickers microhardness (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 1-5, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 9-11). Hierarchical Gaussian process models fusing hardness data with analytical solid-solution strengthening models guided Bayesian alloy selection (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 11-14). Mechanical testing included Vickers hardness, nanoindentation, and 1300°C compression testing on 31 alloys, with tensile and creep testing methods established (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 9-11, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19). The project's partner Retech has pilot-scale plasma arc melting (PAM) atomization capability (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19), and DED printability frameworks were developed separately with Mo, Nb single tracks and MoNb cubes printed (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 9-11). However, **(a) atomization was not demonstrated as an automated step within the ML-driven loop**—synthesis was by arc melting, and AM was explored as a parallel track. The work addresses critical minerals (Nb, Ta, W, Cr, Hf, Zr) as core design-space elements for high-temperature turbine blade replacement of Ni-based superalloys (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 1-5). Related computational-only work from the same group established constrained multi-objective BO with active learning of design constraints (Khatamsaz et al. 2023), identifying 21 Pareto-optimal Mo-Nb-Ti-V-W alloys in silico using 48-sample parallel batches (khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 1-2, khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 3-5).

#### 2.3 ExAM Patent (US11872634B2, Eonta 2024) — Mobile Foundry + LPBF + ML

The Expeditionary Additive Manufacturing (ExAM) patent describes a containerized system combining a mobile foundry (cold-hearth mixing + gas atomization) with an LPBF printer and an ML-enabled computing stack connected to cloud services (US11872634B2 pages 7-10, US11872634B2 pages 1-4, US11872634B2 pages 4-7). The system includes a composition-correction subsystem to tailor powder chemistry for specific parts, layer-by-layer powder-bed monitoring with a negative feedback control loop (KEYENCE sensor → MATLAB → Arduino controller), and machine-learning programs for predictive control and first-time-right production (US11872634B2 pages 1-4, US11872634B2 pages 4-7, US11872634B2 pages 13-14). The patent describes printing witness pins for chemical analysis and product verification requirements (US11872634B2 pages 7-10, US11872634B2 pages 10-13). **(a) This is the closest conceptual match to the target workflow**, but it is a patent/concept, not a demonstrated experimental closed-loop system. No peer-reviewed experimental results or mechanical test data have been published from this system.

### 3. AMAZEMET (rePOWDER) Publications

The AMAZEMET rePOWDER ultrasonic atomization system has been used in numerous publications for custom powder production for additive manufacturing, but in no case was ML-driven closed-loop experimentation integrated:

- **Ciftcli et al. (2025)** demonstrated "close-loop of materials" using rePOWDER for recycling Ti machining chips, C103 niobium alloy, and Mg-Ag alloys, achieving >85% powder yield in the <100 µm fraction suitable for LPBF (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3). Process parameters (pouring temperature, nozzle size, torch power, atomizer frequency) were identified as controllable inputs suitable for ML optimization, but no ML was applied.
- **Bajaj et al. (2026)** used an AMAZEMET rePOWDER for ultrasonic plasma atomization (UPA) of NiTi shape memory alloys, achieving ~50% energy reduction and ~95% material recovery, and noted that integration of ML and closed-loop recycling models is a future roadmap goal (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6).
- **Taheri-Mousavi et al. (2024)** used AMAZEMET for custom Al-alloy powder production as described above (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 25-28).
- **Ukabhai et al. (2025)** investigated Al-10Cu powder production using rePOWDER, confirming suitability for AM but noting that LPBF printing needed to be tested (ukabhai2025investigationofalcu pages 7-10).
- Other studies used rePOWDER for Ti-based BMGs (Schönrath et al. 2024), copper upcycling (Cabrioli et al. 2025), and nuclear target materials at ORNL (Zach et al. 2023) (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3).

### 4. ATO Lab / 3D Lab Publications

The ATO Lab (3D Lab sp. z o.o.) ultrasonic atomizer has been used in several studies, primarily for powder characterization, but none integrated ML-driven closed-loop experimentation:

- **Bashmakova et al. (2026)** explicitly investigated the ATO Lab Plus US35 as a "closed-loop route" for converting PBF-LB-manufactured SS316L parts into reusable powder, characterizing morphology, microstructure, PSD, chemistry, and gas impurities (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 1-2). However, "closed-loop" here refers to material recycling, not ML-driven adaptive experimentation.
- **Yankin et al. (2025)** used the ATO Lab Plus US35 for AlSi12 powder production, developing a modified AM suitability factor and identifying controllable variables (current, arc angle, vibration amplitude) suitable for future optimization (yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 1-2).
- **Grzelak et al. (2022)** and **Bałasz et al. (2024)** used ATO Lab systems for 316L powder production, comparing UA-produced powder with gas-atomized alternatives and finding comparable LPBF part properties (halapi2023ultrasonicpowderatomization pages 1-4).
- **Halapi & Varga (2023)** produced stainless steel powder using ATO Lab equipment and characterized it via SEM/EDS/sieve analysis (halapi2023ultrasonicpowderatomization pages 1-4).

### 5. Institutional Search Results

- **Texas A&M (Arróyave group):** Most advanced closed-loop Bayesian alloy design for MPEAs/RHEAs; experimental loop uses arc melting, not integrated atomization+LPBF (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 1-5, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 11-14).
- **Georgia Tech (Saldana/Kalidindi):** High-throughput combinatorial AM workflows with multihopper powder blending, Bayesian methods, and high-throughput mechanical testing (spherical indentation, LD curves), but uses pre-purchased powders, not in-house atomization (adapa2025rapiddevelopmentof pages 22-24, adapa2025rapiddevelopmentof pages 25-27, adapa2025rapiddevelopmentof pages 19-21). Studies included IN625/718 Ni superalloy mixtures.
- **NIST (Kusne/Takeuchi/CAMEO):** Demonstrated autonomous closed-loop materials discovery with Bayesian active learning for thin-film composition-structure-property mapping, discovering a phase-change memory material—an important methodological precedent but not applicable to metal powder AM (khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 1-2).
- **NASA MSFC (Stuckner et al.):** Published a comprehensive BO framework benchmarking study for materials discovery and autonomous laboratories, finding GP surrogates with expected improvement acquisition functions optimal for low-data campaigns (stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 6-8, stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 1-6, stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 24-26). This provides methodological guidance for configuring BO in future atomize-print-test loops but does not itself include atomization or LPBF experiments.
- **Ames Lab (D.D. Johnson):** Contributed DFT-based computational models (KKR-CPA) to Texas A&M BIRDSHOT effort (khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 3-5).
- **CMU:** Referenced in a provisional patent filed by Glaser/Taheri-Mousavi related to cost-effective AM aluminum alloy design (glaser2025computationaldesignof pages 11-13).
- **BYU:** No publications matching ML-driven LPBF+atomization workflows were identified.

### 6. ML-Driven Adaptive LPBF Experimentation (Without Atomization Constraint)

For context, ML-driven adaptive LPBF process optimization using pre-purchased powders is well established:

- **Liu et al. (2020)** used Gaussian process regression to optimize LPBF processing of AlSi10Mg, identifying a larger processing window for ≥99% density and achieving hardness 118–137 HV, UTS 297–389 MPa, and elongation 6.3–10.3% (liu2020machinelearningassistedlaser pages 1-5, liu2020machinelearningassistedlaser pages 29-34).
- **Zhang (2025)** applied GPR + Bayesian optimization with upper confidence bound acquisition to LPBF of low-alloy steel, using dimensionless process parameters and XCT porosity characterization (zhang2025machinelearningaidedoptimizationc pages 92-96, zhang2025machinelearningaidedoptimization pages 92-96).
- **Kavas et al. (2025)** used Bayesian optimization for in-situ controller autotuning in LPBF, demonstrating the first BO-tuned, in-layer-controlled printed parts with microstructural analysis.
- **Seifoddini et al. (2026)** integrated GPR with Q-learning-based reinforcement learning for LPBF Ti6Al4V melt pool depth optimization, achieving R² = 96%.
- **Adapa, Kalidindi, Saldana (2025)** reviewed AI/ML-driven high-throughput AM workflows, describing Bayesian network frameworks for hierarchical calibration of coupled simulation models and multihopper composition synthesis strategies (adapa2025rapiddevelopmentof pages 19-21).

All of these use commercially sourced or pre-atomized powders; none include atomization in the experimental decision loop.

### 7. Critical-Mineral Content

Among the surveyed works, critical-mineral content is addressed by:
- The MIT/Hart group, which explicitly designed alloys to reduce Sc dependence and incorporated cost metrics (glaser2025computationaldesignof pages 1-3).
- The BIRDSHOT project, which investigates refractory elements (Nb, Ta, W, Mo, Zr, Hf) as potential replacements for Ni-based superalloys (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 1-5).
- The ExAM patent, which envisions using indigenous/scrap feedstocks in expeditionary settings (US11872634B2 pages 1-4).

However, no work explicitly framed the ML-driven loop around minimizing critical-mineral dependence as a primary optimization objective within an integrated atomize-print-test workflow.

### 8. Conclusions

The fully integrated, ML-driven closed-loop workflow of **atomize a new composition → LPBF print → test → ML-decide → repeat** has not been published. The key barriers are: (1) custom powder atomization remains slow and expensive relative to the ML optimization cycle time; (2) laboratory-scale atomizers (AMAZEMET rePOWDER, ATO Lab Plus) are capable of small-batch production but have not been software-integrated into automated experimental orchestration systems; and (3) the field has addressed the computational closed-loop (alloy design via BO/ML) and the LPBF process optimization closed-loop separately, but combining these with an in-loop atomization step represents a next frontier. The ExAM patent (US11872634B2 pages 7-10, US11872634B2 pages 1-4) is the closest conceptual embodiment, and the Taheri-Mousavi/Hart work (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 9-12, taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 1-4) is the closest demonstrated workflow that includes all three elements (ML design → custom atomization → LPBF), albeit with atomization performed offline rather than as an automated step within the decision loop.
