**Task ID:** 3e908c1c-671d-4378-9a77-25ed5fb14474

No. ## Executive finding (precedent query)
No publication or vendor technical note retrieved in this search provides evidence of a **fully autonomous closed-loop, ML-driven or Bayesian-optimization-driven adaptive experimentation workflow for metal LPBF that also includes the powder atomization step inside the same decision loop** (i.e., “atomize a new composition → LPBF print → mechanically test → algorithm decides next composition/processing → repeat automatically”). Across the best-matching candidates, the closest precedents are **manual/experimental closed-loop powder recycling concepts** linking LPBF prints to ultrasonic atomization (powder regeneration), but **without** ML/BO-driven decision-making and **without** in-loop mechanical testing automation. (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 2-5, bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 1-2, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6)

## Closest partial precedents that include atomization + LPBF (but not ML/BO closed-loop)
### 1) LPBF → ultrasonic atomization → reuse as powder (manual “closed-loop” recycling)
Bashmakova et al. explicitly study ultrasonic atomization as a *closed-loop recycling route* for LPBF/PBF-LB 316L: print rods from powder, remelt/atomize printed rods to powder, sieve, and reuse for another PBF-LB cycle. However, the work is executed as a controlled experimental study (designed parameter matrix) and **does not** describe ML/Bayesian optimization, autonomous “decide-next” logic, or in-loop mechanical testing; evaluations focus on powder morphology/PSD/chemistry/microstructure and gas impurities. (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 2-5, bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 1-2)

A workflow schematic of this (commercial powder → PBF-LB rods → ultrasonic atomization → sieving → secondary PBF-LB) is shown in their Figure 1. (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic media b2780176)

### 2) “Close-loop of materials” via ultrasonic atomization (sustainable feedstock), but not an adaptive ML loop
Ciftcli et al. describe an integrated arc-melting + ultrasonic-atomization workflow using AMAZEMET rePOWDER to create powders from novel/recycled feedstocks, including recycling machining scrap of “strategic materials like titanium alloy.” The paper supports feasibility of producing AM-suitable powder fractions and reports powder characterization (SEM/EDS, PSD, oxygen pickup), but **does not** report an ML/BO-driven closed loop that selects new compositions based on LPBF + mechanical test feedback. (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6)

### 3) AMAZEMET rePowder powder-making papers without LPBF+test+decide automation
Other AMAZEMET-linked papers found here (e.g., alloy preparation and powder characterization using rePowder) document powder production and characterization aimed at LPBF-suitable PSD, but do not demonstrate autonomous adaptive iteration including LPBF + mechanical testing in the loop. (ukabhai2025investigationofalcu pages 1-3, zach2023sphericalpowderscontrol pages 4-4)

## Vendor-specific searches requested by user (AMAZEMET; ATO Lab / 3D Lab)
### AMAZEMET (rePOWDER)
Within retrieved AMAZEMET-linked publications, the strongest “loop” language is around **closed-loop recycling** or “close-loop of materials,” but none of the texts retrieved here document (i) atomization being automatically triggered by an ML/BO policy, (ii) automated LPBF + post-build mechanical testing as the objective function, or (iii) end-to-end autonomous repetition. (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 2-5, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6, ukabhai2025investigationofalcu pages 1-3)

### ATO Lab / 3D Lab (ATO Lab atomiser)
Bałasz et al. (Materials 2024) study ultrasonic atomization of 316L using a Taguchi DoE varying torch current and wire feed speed, characterize powders (sieving into PBF-relevant fractions, PSD, SEM/EDS), and state that suitability for PBF technologies was assessed and that future LPBF studies are planned. The work does not show ML/BO-driven adaptive looping or integrated print-test-decide automation, and it does not include mechanical testing in the reported study. (bałasz2024aninvestigationof pages 2-4, bałasz2024aninvestigationof pages 4-7, bałasz2024aninvestigationof pages 9-11)

## Parallel context: ML/BO/RL-driven adaptive LPBF experimentation (without atomization constraint)
The adaptive/autonomous workflows that *do* exist in the retrieved corpus are concentrated on **LPBF process control** (in-situ) or **sequential experimental design** (offline), generally assuming supplied powder feedstock.

### In-situ closed-loop LPBF control with Bayesian optimization or reinforcement learning
- Kavas et al. implement an **in-layer closed-loop** LPBF controller (PI acting on laser power) and use **Bayesian optimization** to autotune controller gains using pyrometer-derived metrics. Their powder is stated to be **gas-atomized 316L (15–45 µm)**, i.e., powder supply is external to the loop; they report microstructural inspection noting lack-of-fusion porosity under some control settings, but no in-loop mechanical testing is described in the cited excerpts. (kavas2025insitucontrollerautotuning pages 1-3, kavas2025insitucontrollerautotuning pages 26-29, kavas2025insitucontrollerautotuning pages 3-6)
- Park et al. demonstrate **real-time RL control** of laser power using in-situ melt-pool measurements and scan-geometry information; again, no powder atomization or alloy-feedstock regeneration is part of the closed loop. (park2025realtimemeltpool pages 1-2)

### Sequential (offline) Bayesian optimization / active learning for AM experiments
Zhang et al. describe Bayesian optimization explicitly as an iterative experiment-selection loop for AM (surrogate → propose next experiment/batch → run experiments → update → repeat), emphasizing external objective evaluation (offline), not in-situ control, and not including powder atomization. (zhang2021bayesianoptimisationfor pages 1-4, zhang2021bayesianoptimisationfor pages 14-16)

### ML/BO-guided alloy design validated by LPBF (mechanical testing present, but atomization not in the loop)
Taheri‑Mousavi et al. show a hybrid CALPHAD/ICME + ML + Bayesian optimization workflow for designing Al alloys for LPBF, followed by experimental LPBF validation. Mechanical testing is explicitly reported (Vickers hardness and room-temperature tensile testing after aging). However, the excerpts do not specify that powder atomization is an automated step coupled to the optimizer, nor that powder production is in the closed loop; it is presented as a design-and-validate pipeline. (taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 12-15, taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 1-4, taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 15-18)

A broader review of sensor-fusion monitoring/control in LPBF summarizes that BO, GP surrogates, MPC, and RL have been used for closed-loop control and tuning in LPBF, but does not describe powder production/atomization being integrated into those loops. (papatheodorou2025recentadvancesin pages 22-25, papatheodorou2025recentadvancesin pages 14-16)

## Notes on “critical minerals”
Within the retrieved atomization-linked works, the most direct critical-material signals are: (i) discussion of recycling machining scrap of *strategic* materials (titanium alloy) in the rePOWDER sustainability framing, and (ii) production of powders for *enriched stable isotope* nuclear targets (rare materials) with an AMAZEMET tool. These are not, however, presented as critical-mineral-optimized autonomous loops. (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6, zach2023sphericalpowderscontrol pages 4-4)

## Overall conclusion
Based on the evidence retrieved and inspected here, the state of the art separates into:
1) **Atomization + LPBF “closed-loop recycling” concepts** that are primarily experimental/manual and focus on powder regeneration/characterization (e.g., Bashmakova 2026), and
2) **ML/BO/RL-driven adaptive LPBF workflows** that focus on in-situ process control or sequential parameter/composition design but assume externally supplied powders.

A fully integrated, automated, ML-driven atomize→LPBF→mechanical-test→decide→repeat workflow for metal LPBF was **not found** in the retrieved literature and vendor-linked documents in this run. (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 2-5, bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 1-2, kavas2025insitucontrollerautotuning pages 1-3, park2025realtimemeltpool pages 1-2, taheri‐mousavi2026additivelymanufacturablehigh‐strength pages 12-15)
