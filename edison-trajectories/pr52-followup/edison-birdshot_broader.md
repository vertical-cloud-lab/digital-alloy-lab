# BIRDSHOT Publication Landscape (2022–2026): A Comprehensive Enumeration

## 1. Overview of the BIRDSHOT Program

BIRDSHOT (Batch-wise Improvement in Reduced Design Space using a Holistic Optimization Technique) is a multi-institutional center led by Raymundo Arroyave at Texas A&M University, funded initially through the ARPA-E ULTIMATE program and subsequently through the Army Research Laboratory HTMDEC initiative ($9.5M follow-on) (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19). The program integrates batch Bayesian optimization with high-throughput experimental workflows for accelerated discovery of refractory and FCC high-entropy alloys (HEAs). Below, all identified BIRDSHOT-related publications from 2022–2026 are enumerated with details on the five requested dimensions.

## 2. Core Experimental Campaign Papers (Closed-Loop Alloy Discovery)

### 2.1 Paramore et al. (2025) — *Acta Materialia* (doi:10.1016/j.actamat.2025.120670)
**(a) Search space:** 7-element BCC refractory system Ti–V–Nb–Mo–Hf–Ta–W at 2.5 at.% resolution, yielding ~9.37 million raw candidates. Successive filtering (entropy ≥ R ln 3, density, melting point, BCC single-phase at 800 °C) reduced this to ~97,940 feasible alloys, further clustered via k-medoids to a 1,000-alloy optimization space. Two batches of 24 alloys (48 total) were experimentally evaluated (paramore2025twoshotoptimizationof pages 3-5, paramore2025twoshotoptimizationof pages 1-3, paramore2025twoshotoptimizationof pages 5-7).
**(b) Automation:** Computational screening was automated via TC-Python on HPC; synthesis used vacuum arc melting (VAM) with a custom 9-slot crucible for batch melting and a 12-sample metallographic polisher. Humans remain in the loop for melting, homogenization (1925 °C, 24 h), mounting/polishing, and characterization execution (paramore2025twoshotoptimizationof pages 3-5, paramore2025twoshotoptimizationof pages 5-7).
**(c) Mechanical testing:** Nanoindentation (CSM mode, Berkovich tip, iMicro2) for specific hardness and specific modulus (the two optimization objectives); Vickers microhardness at 500 gf; SEM-EDS and XRD for phase verification. No tensile or compression testing was performed in this paper (paramore2025twoshotoptimizationof pages 9-11, paramore2025twoshotoptimizationof pages 7-9).
**(d) Atomization/LPBF:** Neither atomization nor LPBF are part of this campaign; synthesis is exclusively VAM (paramore2025twoshotoptimizationof pages 5-7).
**(e) BOFN/pKGFN:** Not used. The optimization framework is batch BO with a Gaussian process surrogate (squared-exponential kernel) and Expected Hypervolume Improvement (EHVI) acquisition function (paramore2025twoshotoptimizationof pages 9-11).

### 2.2 Hastings et al. (2025) — *Acta Materialia* (doi:10.1016/j.actamat.2025.121173; arXiv preprint 2024)
**(a) Search space:** 6-element FCC system Al–V–Cr–Fe–Co–Ni on a 5 at.% grid (~53,124 candidate compositions); after constraints (FCC stability >700 °C, solidification range <100 K, density <8.5 g/cm³), 2,437 feasible alloys remained. Stacking fault energy (SFE) predicted by an ML model trained on DFT data was used to partition alloys into low- and high-SFE subspaces. Five iterations of ~20 alloys each (~80 total) were synthesized, sampling only ~0.15% of the feasible space (hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 2-3, hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 3-5, hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 1-2).
**(b) Automation:** Integrated cyber-physical workflow with automated batch candidate selection via BO; VAM synthesis and characterization remain human-operated. Cloud-based data management, standardized naming, and job travelers support coordination (hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 11-12, hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 8-9).
**(c) Mechanical testing:** Extensive—tensile testing (YS, UTS, UTS/YS, total elongation), high-strain-rate nanoindentation (hardness, modulus, strain-rate sensitivity), and multi-property Pareto analysis (hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 13-15, hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 12-13).
**(d) Atomization/LPBF:** Not included; VAM only (hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 1-2).
**(e) BOFN/pKGFN:** Not used. Employs GP ensemble with randomized length scales plus EHVI, with SFE-informed partitioning (hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 11-12).

### 2.3 Mulukutla et al. (2024) — *Integrating Materials and Manufacturing Innovation* 13, 453–473
**(a) Search space:** 6-element FCC system Fe–Ni–Co–Cr–V–Al; ~50,000 candidate compositions; 3-objective Pareto discovery (hardness, strain-rate sensitivity, UTS/YS ratio) achieved over 5 iterations using ~80 tested alloys, yielding an estimated 100–2,000× acceleration (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 5-7, mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 23-26).
**(b) Automation:** Semi-automated, explicitly human-in-the-loop distributed workflow. Automated elements include batch BO candidate selection (MISBBO), ETL pipelines, traveler template generators, bespoke analysis code with metadata injection, and a recommendation engine. Humans still perform synthesis (VAM and DED), sample shipping (tracked via dedicated email), polishing, characterization, and weekly coordination meetings (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 20-23, mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 5-7, mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 9-13, mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 23-26, mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 15-17).
**(c) Mechanical testing:** The broadest suite in the BIRDSHOT portfolio—tensile testing, microhardness, nanoindentation strain-rate jump tests, HSR nanoindentation (10⁴–10⁵ s⁻¹), small punch tests (SPT for DED), SHPB compression, and LIPIT (10⁷–10⁸ s⁻¹) (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 5-7).
**(d) Atomization/LPBF:** Directed Energy Deposition (DED) is used in parallel with VAM to produce "alloy twins"; no LPBF or atomization in-loop (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 5-7).
**(e) BOFN/pKGFN:** Uses Multi-Information Source Batch Bayesian Optimization (MISBBO) / MOBBO, which selects among information sources (experiments, models) and uses a utility-based acquisition function. Not named BOFN/pKGFN (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 5-7).

### 2.4 Arroyave (2024) — ULTIMATE: BIRDSHOT Final Technical Report (doi:10.2172/2305503)
**(a) Search space:** The broadest campaign—10-element RHEA space (Al–Cr–Mo–Nb–Ta–Ti–V–W–Zr–Hf); 252 quinaries planned with ~50,000 calculations per quinary (~12.6M total). 234 alloys planned; 140 unique RHEA compositions synthesized, 116 homogenized, at a throughput of ~13 alloys/week. Top candidates: Nb₇₀V₁₀W₁₀Cr₅Zr₅, Cr₅Mo₃₀Nb₄₀Ta₅Ti₅V₁₅, and Cr₅Mo₄₅Nb₃₀Ti₁₀V₁₀ (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 5-7, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 9-11, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 11-14, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 1-5, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19).
**(b) Automation:** High-throughput but human-in-the-loop: arc melting in Ar, homogenization (≥1800 °C, ≥10 h), characterization (SEM/EDX, XRD) are human-operated; computational screening (CALPHAD, DFT, ML) and k-medoids initialization are automated. DED printability experiments also human-operated (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 9-11, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19).
**(c) Mechanical testing:** The most extensive elevated-temperature campaign—Vickers microhardness (60 as-cast, 108 homogenized), nanoindentation (39 as-cast), room-temperature compression and tension, **1300 °C compression** (31 alloys), 1300 °C tensile testing procedures developed, oxidation (24 wedge tests), stress relaxation, thermal conductivity (laser flash), and CTE (dilatometry RT–1300 °C). Creep testing and scale-up to 1300 °C tensile specimens were planned (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 9-11, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 11-14, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19).
**(d) Atomization/LPBF:** DED printability framework explored—Nb and Mo single tracks and MoNb binary cubes printed on Nb/Mo substrates. Atomization discussed for scale-up (Retech as OEM partner with 50–100 tons/year capacity). No LPBF in-loop (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 9-11, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19).
**(e) BOFN/pKGFN:** Uses multi-information source BO with hierarchical GPs incorporating physics-informed (informative) priors; Bayesian updating fuses low-fidelity analytical models (Tabor, solid-solution strengthening, ROM elastic constants) with high-fidelity experiment and DFT data. Not named BOFN/pKGFN (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 11-14, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19).

## 3. In-Silico Bayesian Optimization / Methods Papers

### 3.1 Khatamsaz et al. (2022) — *Acta Materialia* 236, 118133
Mo–Nb–Ti–V–W, 5 elements, ~10,626 compositions at 5 at.%. Purely in silico constrained multi-objective BO with active learning of design constraints for ductile refractory MPEAs. No physical testing, no LPBF, no BOFN/pKGFN (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19, khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 5-7).

### 3.2 Khatamsaz et al. (2023) — *npj Computational Materials* 9, 49
Mo–Nb–Ti–V–W, 5 elements, 10,626 compositions, 5 at.% grid. Entropy-based active learning approach for constrained BO; feasible region learned with only 420 queries (~96% reduction vs. brute force). In silico; no experimental loop, no LPBF, no BOFN/pKGFN (khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 7-8, khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 5-7).

### 3.3 Khatamsaz, Vela & Arróyave (2023) — *Materials Letters* 351, 135067
Multi-objective Bayesian alloy design using multi-task Gaussian processes. Listed as a BIRDSHOT output in the final technical report. In silico methodology; no BOFN/pKGFN (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19).

### 3.4 Khatamsaz et al. (2025) — arXiv (BO over Problem Formulation Space)
Nb–Mo–Ti–V–W turbine-blade case study; later batches expand to include Re and printability metrics (5–6 elements). Novel framework for BO over the problem formulation space itself, enabling autonomous evolution of design objectives. In silico; no physical testing, no LPBF; not BOFN/pKGFN (khatamsaz2025towardsautonomousexperimentation pages 4-5, khatamsaz2025towardsautonomousexperimentation pages 3-4).

### 3.5 Mulukutla et al. (2024) — arXiv (Supply Risk-Aware Alloy Discovery)
MoNbTiVW, 5 elements, 10,621 → 7,991 feasible compositions at 5 at.%. MOBBO with per-objective GP surrogates and hypervolume improvement acquisition, integrating supply-chain risk as an objective. In silico; no LPBF, no BOFN/pKGFN (mulukutla2024supplyriskawarealloy pages 10-12).

### 3.6 Khatamsaz & Allaire (2022) — *AIAA SciTech Forum* (conference paper)
Active subspace batch BO for materials design—methodology paper demonstrating dimensionality reduction in BO for high-dimensional alloy spaces. In silico; no BOFN/pKGFN.

## 4. Surrogate Modeling / Machine Learning Papers Using BIRDSHOT Data

### 4.1 Alvi et al. (2025) — *npj Computational Materials* 11
Al–V–Cr–Mn–Fe–Co–Ni–Cu, 8 elements, >100 synthesized compositions from the BIRDSHOT dataset (two campaigns: FCC 6-element then expanded 8-element). Benchmarks prior-guided deep Gaussian processes (DGPs) against conventional GPs, encoder–decoder networks, and XGBoost. The underlying dataset spans miniaturized tensile, quasi-static tensile, HSR nanoindentation, SPT, SHPB, and LIPIT. Multi-task/multisource surrogate modeling but not BOFN/pKGFN (alvi2025accurateanduncertaintyaware pages 2-4, alvi2025accurateanduncertaintyawarea pages 2-4, alvi2025accurateanduncertaintyawarea pages 4-7).

### 4.2 Alvi et al. (2026) — *npj Computational Materials* 12
Mo–Nb–Cr–Ta–W–Hf, 6 elements on a 5 at.% grid. In-silico benchmark of deep-GP-based cost-aware batch BO (hDGP-BO) with heterotopic querying strategy. Demonstrates cost-aware candidate selection and multi-property correlation exploitation. No physical testing, no LPBF; not BOFN/pKGFN but represents the most advanced multi-fidelity-capable BO framework in the BIRDSHOT lineage (alvi2026deepgaussianprocessbased pages 6-9, alvi2026deepgaussianprocessbased pages 1-2).

### 4.3 Attari & Arroyave (2025) — *Digital Discovery*
Deep tabular learning benchmarks (FDN-R, DNF-Net, 1D CNN, TabNet, XGBoost) on BIRDSHOT + ATLAS-RHEA datasets. Predicts YS, UTS, modulus, hardness, elongation, HDYN/HQS ratio. No experimental loop; no BOFN/pKGFN (attari2025decodingnonlinearityand pages 7-9).

## 5. Computational Support and Phase Diagram Papers

### 5.1 Vela et al. (2023) — *Acta Materialia* 248, 118784
High-throughput CALPHAD-based exploration of WMoVTaNbAl refractory MPEAs under multiple-property constraints. 6-element computational screening study; core BIRDSHOT RHEA screening work (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19). (Full text was unobtainable for detailed characterization.)

### 5.2 Vela et al. (2023) — *Acta Materialia* 261, 119351
Data-augmented Bayesian modeling for yield strength of refractory HEAs. Fuses experimental and analytical data for hierarchical yield-strength predictions (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19).

### 5.3 Singh et al. (2023) — *Acta Materialia* 257, 119104
A ductility metric for refractory-based MPEAs supporting BIRDSHOT constraint formulations (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19).

### 5.4 van de Walle et al. (2022) — *JOM* 74, 3478–3486
Interactive exploration of high-dimensional phase diagrams; supported 5-component phase-diagram predictions for (Cr, Mo, Nb, V, W) in BIRDSHOT (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19).

### 5.5 van de Walle et al. (2023) — *Calphad* 83, 102628
Revisiting SGTE lattice stability of BCC aluminum; supports CALPHAD database accuracy for BIRDSHOT (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19).

## 6. Platform, Visualization, and Framework Papers

### 6.1 Singh et al. (2026) — arXiv (DataScribe)
AI-native web platform for multi-objective materials design supporting BIRDSHOT VAM campaigns. Embeds multi-objective multi-fidelity BO with EHVI/qEHVI, cost-aware fidelity handling, ontology-backed data ingestion, and instrument/HPC connectors for closed-loop propose–measure–learn cycles (singh2026datascribeanainative pages 8-10, singh2026datascribeanainative pages 4-6).

### 6.2 Hastings et al. (2026) — *Digital Discovery* 5, 277–289
Domain-knowledge-informed initialization for BO; uses BIRDSHOT implementation on FCC Al–V–Cr–Fe–Co–Ni (~50K compositions) and BCC Ti–V–Nb–Mo–Hf–Ta–W search spaces. Shows initialization strategy affects convergence equivalent to ~100 additional evaluations (hastings2026leveragingdomainknowledge pages 2-3, hastings2026leveragingdomainknowledge pages 4-5, hastings2026leveragingdomainknowledge pages 3-4).

### 6.3 Vela et al. (2025) — *Digital Discovery* 4, 181–194
Methods for visualizing high-dimensional HEA spaces; supports BIRDSHOT BO and alloy-design visualization.

### 6.4 Hardcastle et al. (2025) — *Digital Discovery* 4 (arXiv preprint)
Physics-informed GP classification for constraint-aware alloy design using CALPHAD-derived constraints.

### 6.5 Maguire et al. (2025) — arXiv
Feasibility vs. Pareto-optimality framing for alloy design; conceptual contribution to BIRDSHOT design philosophy.

### 6.6 Arroyave et al. (2022) — *MRS Communications* 12, 1037–1049
Perspective on Bayesian methods for materials discovery; frames BIRDSHOT within broader BO taxonomy including multi-information-source, multi-objective, and batch BO.

### Key Takeaways

**(a) Search-space size and dimensionality** range from 5-element quinary systems (~10,600 compositions) to the full 10-element RHEA space (~12.6M calculations), with the experimental campaigns typically filtering down to manageable candidate pools of 1,000–2,500 alloys and synthesizing 48–140 compositions (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 5-7, paramore2025twoshotoptimizationof pages 3-5, hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 3-5).

**(b) Automation:** All experimental BIRDSHOT campaigns remain substantially **human-in-the-loop**. Arc melting, homogenization, sectioning, polishing, and most characterization steps are performed by humans. Automation is concentrated on the computational/decision side (batch BO candidate selection, CALPHAD screening on HPC, data pipelines, traveler template generators) and partial automation of sample preparation (9-slot crucible, 12-sample polisher, DED wagon-wheel holder). Sample tracking is still manual (email-based), though QR-code tracking and project-management software adoption are planned (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 20-23, mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 5-7, mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 23-26, mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 15-17).

**(c) Mechanical testing** evolves from hardness-only in the early in-silico papers through nanoindentation + Vickers in Paramore et al. 2025, to the full multi-scale suite in the HTMDEC/BIRDSHOT FCC campaign: tensile, HSR nanoindentation, SHPB, SPT, LIPIT, and Vickers. The ULTIMATE refractory campaign additionally includes **1300 °C compression** (31 alloys tested), planned 1300 °C tensile testing, oxidation, stress relaxation, thermal conductivity, and CTE measurements (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 9-11, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 11-14, alvi2025accurateanduncertaintyawarea pages 4-7).

**(d) Atomization/LPBF:** No BIRDSHOT publication to date incorporates LPBF in the closed experimental loop. DED (Directed Energy Deposition) is used in parallel with VAM in the FCC campaign to produce alloy twins (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 5-7). In the ULTIMATE RHEA campaign, DED printability is explored (Nb/Mo single tracks, MoNb binary cubes) and atomization is discussed for scale-up via Retech as an OEM partner, but these are not routine in-loop steps (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 9-11, arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 16-19).

**(e) BOFN/pKGFN:** No BIRDSHOT publication explicitly uses or names a BOFN (Bayesian Optimization with Function Networks) or pKGFN (parallel Knowledge Gradient for Function Networks) framework. The closest methodological analogs are: (i) Multi-Information Source Batch BO (MISBBO) in the FCC campaign, which selects among information sources of varying fidelity (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 5-7); (ii) hierarchical GPs with physics-informed priors and Bayesian updating of low-fidelity analytical models with high-fidelity data in the ULTIMATE RHEA campaign (arroyave2024ultimatebirdshot.final pages 11-14); (iii) deep Gaussian process-based cost-aware batch BO (hDGP-BO) with heterotopic querying in Alvi et al. 2026 (alvi2026deepgaussianprocessbased pages 6-9, alvi2026deepgaussianprocessbased pages 1-2); and (iv) multi-objective multi-fidelity BO with cost-aware fidelity selection in the DataScribe platform (singh2026datascribeanainative pages 8-10). These represent custom multi-fidelity and multi-information-source approaches rather than the specific BOFN/pKGFN formulations from the Astudillo–Frazier line of work.
