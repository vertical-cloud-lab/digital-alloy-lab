## C) What would convince a materials-science reviewer that BOFN is grounded in metallurgy?

> - **CALPHAD / solidification criteria as constraints or objectives:** Include a composition-to-printability map showing freezing range, predicted solidification path, phase fractions, eutectic fraction, and a hot-cracking susceptibility indicator derived from Kou-style criteria; use these as explicit BOFN feasibility constraints or penalty terms rather than post hoc interpretation. Stronger still: show how grain-refinement and eutectic-solidification strategies (e.g., refiners promoting CET and eutectic additions improving terminal liquid feeding) were encoded as design rules that prune unprintable alloy regions before printing (uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 2-4, uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 1-2).
> - **Experimental validation artifacts reviewers will trust:** Show at least one staged validation chain from low-cost to high-value tests: single tracks or melt lines for process-window screening, cubes/cuboids for density and crack screening, then micro-CT, SEM/metallography, EDS, and XRD on selected candidates. The key is not just that these tools exist, but that the preliminary data link predicted CALPHAD/BOFN rankings to observed porosity, crack density, grain structure, and phase constitution in real LPBF specimens (uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 2-4, uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 1-2).
> - **Defect-mechanism linkage, not just black-box prediction:** Explicitly connect model variables to known LPBF failure modes: wide freezing range / poor terminal liquid feeding → hot cracking; insufficient delivered energy or controller oversuppression → lack-of-fusion porosity; unstable melt-pool signatures / overheating → defect-prone processing. If in situ signals are used, identify which signatures are meant to proxy these mechanisms and show at least one example where an in situ anomaly matched ex situ defect evidence (kavas2025insitucontrollerautotuning pages 26-29, kavas2025insitucontrollerautotuning pages 1-3).
> - **Constrained multi-objective framing is essential:** A materials reviewer will be more convinced by a feasible-region optimization that balances printability, defect suppression, and property targets than by a single unconstrained merit score. Preliminary results should therefore show that BOFN avoids infeasible regions and explicitly guards against underexposure / lack-of-fusion while still seeking improved alloy-performance combinations, consistent with BO-controlled LPBF evidence that naive optimization can drive power outside the process window (kavas2025insitucontrollerautotuning pages 26-29, kavas2025insitucontrollerautotuning pages 1-3).
> - **Transferability and reproducibility evidence:** Include replicate builds, repeat coupons across different days or powder lots, and at least one statement about machine drift / calibration / sensor consistency so the reviewer sees the framework is learning metallurgy rather than one-off machine noise. If possible, show that a ranking, classifier, or feasible window remains stable across repeats, because literature on LPBF closed-loop control emphasizes standardization, robustness, and reproducibility as major credibility gaps (papatheodorou2025recentadvancesin pages 14-16).
> - **What separates "interesting AI" from "grounded metallurgy":** The strongest preliminary evidence is a closed computational–experimental loop in which CALPHAD-derived printability constraints, real LPBF builds, and multi-modal characterization all agree on why certain alloys/processes succeed or fail. That combination demonstrates the BOFN is not merely interpolating data, but operating inside a physically defensible digital thread for qualification acceleration (uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 2-4, chawla2025acceleratedmaterialsdiscovery pages 1-4, sabanzagil2025bestpracticesfor pages 1-3, uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 1-2)

Two critical “metallurgy credibility” points to foreground:
1) **Defect mechanisms must be explicit, not implicit.** For LPBF, show how your objectives/constraints prevent known failure modes (e.g., hot cracking via solidification criteria; lack-of-fusion via process-window constraints). LPBF closed-loop BO literature demonstrates that optimizing a controller can inadvertently induce lack-of-fusion porosity if it drives power out of the process window—this is exactly the kind of failure a materials reviewer will look for you to preempt with constrained/multi-objective design (kavas2025insitucontrollerautotuning pages 1-3, kavas2025insitucontrollerautotuning pages 26-29).
2) **Validation must include microstructure/porosity evidence, not just “model accuracy.”** CALPHAD-guided LPBF printability studies that are convincing validate with metallography/SEM/EDS/XRD and micro-CT to show reduced defects and confirm predicted solidification strategies (grain refinement, eutectic feeding) (uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 1-2, uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 2-4).

## Practical guidance: “interesting demo” → “real-world impact”

To ensure the 2–3 page Preliminary Results section reads as impact rather than capability statements:
- Lead with **one integrated figure** that ties (i) BOFN decision step, (ii) what is printed next, (iii) what is measured (low→high fidelity), (iv) how that changes the feasible region and qualification confidence.
- Ensure every preliminary algorithmic claim has a **real-world proxy**: even a small pilot loop (20–40 experiments) with staged measurements and at least one microstructure/CT panel can carry more reviewer weight than extensive synthetic benchmarking.
- Treat cost-awareness and multi-fidelity as **measured claims** (budget-normalized curves, where cost is defined), not as architecture buzzwords—consistent with best-practice calls for unified MFBO assessment criteria (sabanzagil2025bestpracticesfor pages 1-3) and demonstrated cost-aware closed-loop gains in real instruments (chawla2025acceleratedmaterialsdiscovery pages 1-4).
