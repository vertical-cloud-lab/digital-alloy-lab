# Databases, Datasets, and Data Sources for Materials Criticality Assessment and Critical Mineral Supply Risk Analysis

## 1. Introduction

Materials criticality assessment is an inherently data-intensive endeavor that draws on geological, economic, geopolitical, and environmental data sources to quantify supply risk and economic importance of raw materials. This review identifies and characterizes the principal databases, datasets, characterization factor sets, alloy-level criticality methods, and ML-relevant tools available for constructing an AI-guided alloy design platform targeting critical minerals. ## 2. Government and Institutional Data Sources

### 2.1 USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries and Critical Minerals Methodology

The USGS National Minerals Information Center maintains the most widely cited public database for mineral commodity data, publishing annual Mineral Commodity Summaries covering production, reserves, net import reliance, trade, and price context for over 80 mineral commodities (cimprich2019rawmaterialcriticality pages 3-5, cimprich2019rawmaterialcriticality pages 5-6, cimprich2019rawmaterialcriticality pages 11-11). These data serve as the primary production statistics input for virtually all criticality assessment methods, including GeoPolRisk, ESP, ESSENZ, and the Yale/Graedel methodology (cimprich2019rawmaterialcriticality pages 3-5, bach2019analyzingchangesin pages 3-5). The USGS 2025 critical minerals methodology introduced a probabilistic economic-effects assessment covering 84 commodities across more than 1,200 disruption scenarios, using price elasticities and input-output tables to quantify GDP impacts (nassar2025methodologyandtechnical pages 9-10). USGS production and reserves data are updated annually and recommended as a core data source for supply risk indicator construction (helbig2021anoverviewof pages 18-20).

### 2.2 DOE 2023 Critical Materials Strategy

The DOE 2023 Critical Materials Strategy (CMS) provides short-term (2020–2025) and medium-term (2025–2035) criticality matrices that plot material criticality along two axes: importance to energy and supply risk (bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 133-136, bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 16-18). The 2023 assessment screened 38 materials across eight major energy technologies, deeply evaluating 23 materials and classifying them as critical, near-critical, or noncritical. Seven materials were identified as short-term critical (cobalt, dysprosium, gallium, natural graphite, iridium, neodymium, terbium), with nine classified as near-critical including lithium, nickel, and electrical steel (bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 133-136). The CMS introduced a formal scoring rubric and expanded the definition of "materials" to include engineered materials such as silicon carbide and electrical steel (bauer20232023criticalmaterials pages 16-18).

### 2.3 EU JRC Raw Materials Information System (RMIS)

The European Commission's JRC Raw Materials Information System (RMIS v3.0, released March 2023) is a comprehensive knowledge platform covering approximately 100 raw material profiles with dashboards containing 20+ indicators on reserves, production, EU trade, recycling, use, and sustainability (manfredi2024rawmaterialsinformation pages 1-2, manfredi2024rawmaterialsinformation pages 2-3). RMIS provides material-specific, country-level, and sector/technology profiles and supports the EU's triennial CRM assessment (delia2022buildingafricaeuraw pages 9-11). The EU criticality assessment methodology itself plots materials on an Economic Importance (EI) vs. Supply Risk (SR) space, with materials exceeding thresholds (EI ≥ 1 and SR ≥ 2.8) deemed critical (eynard2023criticalrawmaterials pages 32-36). The 2023 EU CRM list expanded to 34 critical raw materials and introduced a "strategic raw materials" category for materials essential to green/digital transitions (troster2024insearchof pages 11-13).

### 2.4 British Geological Survey (BGS) Supply Risk Index

The BGS Supply Risk Index uses seven scored criteria—production concentration, reserve distribution, recycling rate, substitutability, governance of top producer/reserve holder, and companion metal fraction—aggregated and normalized to a 1–10 index (bachmann2022d1.4critical pages 121-124).

## 3. Elemental Criticality Characterization Factor Methods

### 3.1 GeoPolRisk

The Geopolitical Supply Risk (GeoPolRisk) method uses World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators (specifically "Political Stability and Absence of Violence/Terrorism") weighted by import shares from the UN Comtrade database to produce region-specific characterization factors (cimprich2019rawmaterialcriticality pages 3-5, cimprich2019rawmaterialcriticality pages 2-3). The method uniquely accounts for domestic production as geopolitically "risk-free" and has been extended to an endpoint indicator (GeoPolEndpoint) that models socio-economic damage through price elasticity considerations (cimprich2019rawmaterialcriticality pages 8-9).

### 3.2 ESSENZ

The ESSENZ method (Integrated Method to Assess Resource Efficiency) produces characterization factors for approximately 40 abiotic resources across 11 supply-risk categories, including political stability, demand growth, mining capacity, trade barriers, concentration of production and reserves, company concentration, co-production, price volatility, and feasibility of exploration (bach2019analyzingchangesin pages 1-3, bachmann2022d1.4critical pages 34-36). ESSENZ uses a distance-to-target approach normalized by global production amounts (bach2019analyzingchangesin pages 3-5). Data sources include USGS, World Bank WGI, World Economic Forum data, and the SNL metals and mining database for company concentration (bach2019analyzingchangesin pages 3-5). Updated ESSENZ characterization factors are published at the TU Berlin website: https://www.see.tu-berlin.de/menue/forschung/ergebnisse/essenz/parameter/en/ (bach2019analyzingchangesin pages 1-3).

### 3.3 Economic Scarcity Potential (ESP)

ESP aggregates three WGI dimensions (voice and accountability, political stability, government effectiveness) and incorporates the Human Development Index alongside supply-disruption factors including mining capacity, recycling, price volatility, and demand growth (cimprich2019rawmaterialcriticality pages 5-6, cimprich2019rawmaterialcriticality pages 2-3). ESSENZ supersedes ESP with broader indicator coverage and more current data (cimprich2019rawmaterialcriticality pages 8-9).

### 3.4 Yale/Graedel Criticality Methodology

The foundational Yale methodology developed by Graedel et al. locates individual metals in a 3D "criticality space" with axes for supply risk, vulnerability to supply restriction, and environmental implications (graedel2012methodologyofmetal pages 23-26). This framework uses indicators including HHI for market concentration, WGI-PV for political stability, PPI for policy perception, HDI for regulation proxy, Net Import Reliance, Global Innovation Index, and companion metal fraction (graedel2012methodologyofmetal pages 23-26, graedel2012methodologyofmetal pages 48-53, graedel2012methodologyofmetal pages 53-57). Uncertainty is handled through Monte Carlo simulation with lognormal distributions calibrated from ecoinvent LCI data and WGI confidence intervals (graedel2012methodologyofmetal pages 53-57).

### 3.5 Comprehensive Meta-Review of Supply Risk Indicators

Helbig et al. (2021) systematically reviewed 88 criticality assessment methods published through 2020, documenting 618 individual applications of supply risk criteria with 98 unique criteria belonging to 10 indicator categories (helbig2021anoverviewof pages 2-4, helbig2021anoverviewof pages 1-2). The most frequently used categories are concentration, scarcity, and political instability, with the most common individual criteria being country concentration of production, depletion time of reserves, and geopolitical risk (helbig2021anoverviewof pages 1-2). The supplementary spreadsheets provide a structured meta-dataset that is valuable for ontology building and automated indicator selection (helbig2021anoverviewof pages 18-20).

### 4.1 Ferro & Bonollo Alloy Criticality Index

Ferro and Bonollo (2019) define an Alloy Criticality Index (CIA) that quantifies supply risk per unit mass of alloy by aggregating element-level CICRM values weighted by composition (ferro2020alloysubstitutionin pages 7-9, ferro2019materialsselectionin pages 3-4). Their CICRM metric incorporates modified HHI indices combining governance (HHIWGI) and environmental performance (HHIEPI), supply risk (SR) computed from production concentration, substitutability, and recycling rate, economic importance (EI), and end-of-life recycling input rate (ferro2019materialsselectionin pages 3-4, ferro2023lightweightdesignversus pages 7-8, ferro2019materialsselectionin pages 9-9). The method uses EU CRM factsheet data as inputs and embeds CIA into Ashby-style material selection charts for multi-objective design (ferro2019materialsselectionin pages 1-2, ferro2023lightweightdesignversus pages 1-2).

### 4.2 Helbig et al. Alloy-Level Supply Risk

Helbig et al. (2020) assess supply risk for 12 alloying elements in nickel-based superalloys using 12 indicators across four risk categories, then aggregate to alloy level via four methods: arithmetic mean, mass-share weighting, cost-share weighting, and maximum-element approach (helbig2020supplyriskconsiderations pages 9-12, helbig2020supplyriskconsiderations pages 5-7, helbig2020supplyriskconsiderations pages 7-9). The cost-share approach reveals the largest spread in alloy-level scores, primarily driven by rhenium introduction in second-generation single-crystal alloys (helbig2020supplyriskconsiderations pages 1-3). Supplementary data files include alloy compositions, element prices, and computed supply risk values (helbig2020supplyriskconsiderations pages 14-15).

## 5. ML-Ready Datasets and Supply-Risk-Aware Alloy Optimization

### 5.1 Mulukutla et al. SupplyRiskAwareDesign

Mulukutla et al. (2024) present the most directly relevant ML-ready resource for supply-risk-aware alloy design. Their open-source GitHub repository (https://github.com/mrinalinimulukutla/SupplyRiskAwareDesign) contains tabulated elemental supply risk data including Table S1 (Elemental Info), Table S2 (Calculation Detail), Table S3 (HHIL Calculation), and Table S4 (Element Price History) (mulukutla2024supplyriskawarealloy pages 16-18). The approach uses an LLM-based pipeline to automatically extract supply risk statistics from USGS Mineral Commodity Surveys, periodicals, and research papers via document retrieval, keyword-based data reduction, LLM classification and extraction, and validation steps (mulukutla2024supplyriskawarealloy pages 18-20, mulukutla2024supplyriskawarealloy pages 6-8, mulukutla2024supplyriskawarealloy pages 20-22). These indicators are then integrated into a multi-objective Batch Bayesian Optimization framework to identify Pareto-optimal high-entropy alloys balancing performance with minimized supply risk, demonstrated on the MoNbTiVW system (mulukutla2024supplyriskawarealloy pages 18-20).

### 5.2 Matminer and Matbench

Matminer is an open-source Python toolkit for materials data mining with 47 featurization classes and data retrieval interfaces to the Materials Project, Materials Data Facility, and other databases (ward2018matmineranopen pages 2-3, ward2018matmineranopen pages 1-2). It provides standardized pandas DataFrame outputs compatible with scikit-learn pipelines (ward2018matmineranopen pages 2-3). Matbench provides 13 benchmark ML tasks spanning 312 to 132,752 samples covering optical, thermal, electronic, thermodynamic, tensile, and elastic properties (dunn2020benchmarkingmaterialsproperty pages 2-3, dunn2020benchmarkingmaterialsproperty pages 1-2). While not criticality-specific, these tools provide the essential materials informatics infrastructure for building composition-to-property surrogate models that can be combined with criticality objectives in multi-objective optimization.

### 5.3 Taylor Sparks and Collaborators

Baird and Sparks developed multiple tools relevant to materials informatics including matbench-genmetrics for benchmarking crystal structure generative models, DiSCoVeR for materials discovery screening based on chemical uniqueness, and CrabNet for attention-based materials property prediction. Notably, Mohanty, Sayeed, and Sparks published a 2025 study in PRX Energy on comprehensive insights into global mineral commodities using analysis, visualization, and intelligent assistance applied to USGS mineral commodity data—a directly relevant tool for mineral criticality data exploration, though full details were not retrievable in this search.

## 6. Underlying Data Sources Common Across Methods

Virtually all criticality assessment methods draw from a common set of underlying data sources (cimprich2019rawmaterialcriticality pages 3-5, cimprich2019rawmaterialcriticality pages 5-6, bakkerUnknownyearastudyon pages 44-45, bach2019analyzingchangesin pages 3-5):

- **Production data**: USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries (annually updated, 80+ commodities)
- **Trade data**: UN Comtrade (HS-coded bilateral trade flows)
- **Governance indicators**: World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators (six dimensions, 200+ countries, annual)
- **Human development**: UNDP Human Development Index
- **Mining policy**: Fraser Institute Policy Perception Index
- **Environmental performance**: Yale Environmental Performance Index
- **Market/price data**: Bloomberg, Fastmarkets Metal Bulletin, S&P Global SNL (proprietary)
- **Recycling/substitution**: EU CRM factsheets, expert assessments

## 7. Programmatic Access Considerations

For integration with Bayesian optimization platforms, the most directly programmatically accessible resources are: (1) the Mulukutla et al. GitHub repository with its LLM-based extraction pipeline and structured JSON indicator definitions (mulukutla2024supplyriskawarealloy pages 20-22); (2) Matminer's API-based data retrieval modules (ward2018matmineranopen pages 2-3); (3) USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries (downloadable annually but requiring ETL from PDF/tables) (helbig2021anoverviewof pages 18-20); (4) UN Comtrade API for trade data; (5) World Bank WGI data portal; and (6) ESSENZ characterization factors available at the TU Berlin website (bach2019analyzingchangesin pages 1-3). The EU JRC RMIS platform provides rich dashboard data but is more oriented toward interactive exploration than bulk API access (manfredi2024rawmaterialsinformation pages 3-5).

## 8. Identified Gaps

No single, centralized, ML-ready criticality dataset exists that combines all relevant indicators across all elements in a standardized, versioned, and API-accessible format. The field remains fragmented across government reports (often PDF-first), academic supplementary materials, and proprietary commercial databases. The Mulukutla et al. (2024) LLM-extraction approach and the Helbig et al. (2021) meta-review of 88 methods with 618 indicators represent the most promising foundations for constructing such a unified resource (mulukutla2024supplyriskawarealloy pages 18-20, helbig2021anoverviewof pages 2-4). The Andrieu et al. (2025) global database of critical mineral lists across 206 jurisdictions, built with LLM-assisted discovery, represents an emerging resource for cross-country policy comparison but is at the preprint stage.
