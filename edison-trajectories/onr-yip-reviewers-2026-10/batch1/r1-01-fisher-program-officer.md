His co-authorship with Michele V. Manuel and John J. Mecholsky Jr. at the University of Florida Department of Materials Science & Engineering indicates graduate work at UF, likely a PhD in materials science completed ca. 2015–2017 (fisher2019areactiveelement pages 1-2, fisher2019areactiveelement pages 6-7).

**Main research topics.** Fisher's published record spans three interlocking areas:

- **Temperature-dependent material property databases for ICME.** A multi-year series in *Integrating Materials and Manufacturing Innovation* (IMMI), establishing pedigreed, publicly available thermophysical, mechanical, and phase-transformation data from room temperature to near-melting for naval steels (DH36, HSLA-65, HSLA-80, HSLA-100, HY-80, HY-100), cast and wrought NAB (C63200), wire-arc additive NAB, and Cu–Ni alloys (70-30 and 90-10). These data feed finite-element welding/ICME codes such as SYSWELD and Simufact for residual stress and distortion prediction (semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 1-2, semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 2-4, semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 26-27).

- **Additive manufacturing and welding of copper alloys (NAB and Cu–Ni).** Orzolek, Semple, and Fisher (2022) studied the influence of processing on NAB microstructure in additive manufacturing, finding that within a moderate parameter range the as-fabricated structure was dominated by α and martensitic β phases, with detrimental κ precipitates not resolved at the examined magnification (heniken2025influenceofmicrostructurea pages 54-55, doyle2022additivemanufacturingof pages 53-58). Hatala, Wang, Reutzel, Fisher, and Semple (2021) developed thermo-mechanical simulations for laser hot wire (LHW) DED of NAB, demonstrating that substituting constant room-temperature properties for temperature-dependent ones can cause the model to predict displacement in the wrong direction (semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 1-2, semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 2-4).

- **Self-healing metallic alloys.** Fisher et al. (2018, 2019) used thermodynamic alloy design to select reactive element additions (Zn in Sb-based model alloys) that reduce passivating oxides on crack surfaces, improving wetting, bond area, and interfacial fracture behavior. This work was linked explicitly to powder AM oxygen contamination as a future application (fisher2019areactiveelement pages 1-2, fisher2019areactiveelement pages 2-4, fisher2019areactiveelement pages 6-7).

- **Computational welding mechanics.** Nahshon and Fisher (2015) coupled SYSWELD and Abaqus to simulate weld residual stress and distortion in naval structures.

**Recurring co-authors.** Jennifer K. Semple (NSWCCD), Justin E. Norkett (NSWCCD), Daniel H. Bechetti (NSWCCD), Sean M. Orzolek (NSWCCD), Wei Zhang (Ohio State University), Michele V. Manuel (University of Florida), Glenn W. Hatala and Edward W. Reutzel (Penn State/ARL) (semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 1-2, fisher2019areactiveelement pages 1-2).

**Scope note.** No peer-reviewed publications by Fisher on titanium alloys or on formal qualification of printed parts (e.g., MIL-PRF-32804, -32815, -32816) were identified. The "Charles G. Fisher" who publishes extensively in spine surgery is a different person. ## 3. Open Problems and Next Steps Stated in Fisher's Papers and Related Literature

Fisher's papers and the closely related literature they cite identify several unresolved issues:

- **κ-phase control remains the central open problem for AM NAB.** Orzolek, Semple, and Fisher (2022) found that within a limited processing window, detrimental κ precipitates were not observed at the examined magnification in as-fabricated NAB, but explicitly called for broader energy-input studies and transmission electron microscopy to resolve whether fine precipitates are present (doyle2022additivemanufacturingof pages 53-58). Independent work confirms that as-built WAAM NAB can outperform heat-treated NAB in chloride corrosion precisely because rapid solidification suppresses κ precipitation (shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 12-14, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 1-2, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 10-10). However, the standard C95800 anneal (675 °C, 6 h) promotes κII, κIII, κIV, and κV phases that act as cathodic sites, increasing microgalvanic attack of the α matrix (shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 12-14, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 10-12).

- **Process-specific material property databases are needed for each DED modality.** The 2026 WAAM NAB database (Norkett, Canaday, Orzolek, Fisher) extends Fisher's pedigreed-property methodology from plate/cast copper alloys to wire-arc additive components, but no blown-powder laser DED–specific database yet exists. The HSLA-80 paper explicitly invites other researchers to contact Fisher for un-used plate material to expand databases (semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 2-4).

- **As-deposited versus heat-treated use** is positioned as an open question rather than a settled answer. The Doyle (2022) thesis working with Fisher's NAB data found that as-printed LDED NAB exceeded cast C95800 minimum YS (410.5 vs. 241 MPa), UTS (757.8 vs. 586 MPa), and elongation (29% vs. 15%), suggesting post-build heat treatment may be unnecessary—but noted that extensive corrosion testing is still required (doyle2022additivemanufacturingof pages 53-58, doyle2022additivemanufacturingof pages 58-62).

- **Compositional modification for AM** is explored in recent literature (e.g., Ti microalloying of WAAM NAB to reduce porosity and improve properties), but Fisher's own papers do not propose new alloy compositions; they characterize existing specification alloys.

## 4. Implications for the Proposal

The following six implications synthesize Fisher's published priorities into guidance for a proposal that designs new pre-alloyed copper alloy powders for blown-powder laser DED:

> 1. **Pedigreed properties, not generic inputs.** Fisher’s database series treats traceable, temperature-dependent thermophysical, mechanical, and transformation data as prerequisites for credible ICME. He would likely value CALPHAD-guided screening but probe how predictions for each finalist will be experimentally anchored to relevant NAB and Cu–Ni baselines. Cite and build on his database methodology; “CALPHAD replaces property measurement” would land poorly, whereas “adaptive screening culminates in a pedigreed database for the selected composition” would land well. (semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 1-2, semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 2-4, semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 26-27)
>
> 2. **Make the thermal-history model process- and composition-specific.** Hatala et al. showed that room-temperature constants can drastically alter NAB DED distortion predictions, even reversing predicted substrate displacement. Fisher would therefore probe whether the heat-flow/CALPHAD chain incorporates temperature-dependent conductivity, heat capacity, expansion, modulus, flow stress, phase evolution, and measured thermal-cycle validation for blown-powder laser DED—not borrowed wire-arc or cast values without uncertainty bounds. (semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 1-2, semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 2-4)
>
> 3. **The as-deposited objective is plausible, but κ control must be demonstrated rather than assumed.** Fisher’s NAB processing work found broadly similar α/β microstructures across a limited processing window and did not resolve detrimental κ precipitates at the examined scale; it explicitly leaves broader heat-input studies and higher-resolution characterization open. Independent WAAM evidence strengthens the premise: as-built NAB with fewer, finer κ phases outperformed annealed conditions in chloride corrosion, while κ precipitation promoted microgalvanic attack. He would likely welcome eliminating the costly anneal but demand TEM/quantitative phase analysis, thermal-history coverage, long-duration seawater exposure, and corrosion comparisons against wrought C71500—not hardness and one-hour electrochemistry alone. (heniken2025influenceofmicrostructurea pages 54-55, doyle2022additivemanufacturingof pages 53-58, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 12-14, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 1-2, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 10-10)
>
> 4. **Treat novelty and Navy adoption as separate success criteria.** A new composition may optimize deposition and corrosion yet remain unusable without allowable chemistry, reproducible powder and feedstock, repair/substrate compatibility, property allowables, inspection criteria, and a qualification route. Fisher’s published record establishes specification-relevant, pedigreed materials work but does not itself document qualification under MIL-PRF-32804, MIL-PRF-32815, or MIL-PRF-32816; those requirements should therefore be verified and mapped explicitly rather than attributed to him. “Discover a replacement alloy for Navy use” would overreach; “identify composition windows and generate the evidence needed to judge whether specification revision or a controlled repair consumable is warranted” is more credible. (semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 1-2, semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 2-4, semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 26-27)
>
> 5. **Connect CALPHAD alloy design to powder oxidation and interfacial integrity.** Fisher’s reactive-element research used oxide-formation thermodynamics to select additions that reduce passivating films, improve wetting and bonded area, and shift fracture away from an interface; it also identified powder AM oxygen contamination as a potential application. He would likely value explicit treatment of ultrasonic-atomized powder oxygen, oxide stability, volatilization, dilution into NAB and Cu–Ni substrates, and crack susceptibility. Framing minor additions solely as equilibrium phase modifiers would miss this natural connection to his work. (fisher2019areactiveelement pages 1-2, fisher2019areactiveelement pages 2-4, fisher2019areactiveelement pages 6-7)
>
> 6. **Present Bayesian optimization as disciplined allocation of experiments, not a substitute for qualification-quality evidence.** Fisher’s programs use broad, standardized test matrices and openly pedigreed datasets, so he may probe whether cost-aware BO can handle censored failures, batch and facility effects, uncertain corrosion screens, and transfer from pre-placed tracks to kilogram-scale blown-powder builds. The strongest framing is a two-stage workflow: BO economizes exploration and identifies robust composition–process regions; finalists then receive replicated builds, orientation and substrate tests, complete temperature-dependent characterization, and long-duration corrosion validation. (semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 1-2, semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 2-4, semple2022temperaturedependentmaterialproperty pages 26-27)

## 5. Caveats

Several limitations should be noted. (a) Many of Fisher's 2025–2026 publications (Cu–Ni 70-30, Cu–Ni 90-10, NAB WAAM databases) were identifiable through citation metadata and DOIs but could not be retrieved as full text; their findings are inferred from titles and abstracts only. (b) Fisher's ONR Code 332 program officer role is reported by the user and appears on public program pages but is not stated in any peer-reviewed publication retrieved here. (c) No publication by Fisher addresses MIL-PRF-32804, -32815, or -32816 directly, nor titanium alloys, nor formal qualification protocols for AM parts; claims about his views on those topics are therefore inferences from his broader emphasis on specification-relevant, pedigreed data. (d) The name "Charles R. Fisher" at NSWCCD appears unambiguous—there is no evidence of a second materials-science researcher at Carderock with this name, but the spine surgeon Charles G. Fisher (University of British Columbia) has a much larger publication record and can cause confusion in automated searches.
