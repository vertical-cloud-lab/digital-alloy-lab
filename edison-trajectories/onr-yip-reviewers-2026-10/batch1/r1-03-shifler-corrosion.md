## Implications for the proposal

1. **Make the one-hour measurements a down-selection assay, not a service-life claim.** Specify specimen finish and exposed area, open-circuit stabilization, solution temperature/pH, dissolved oxygen, aeration, reference electrode, replicate count, and measurement order; avoid destructive polarization before subsequent immersion on the *same* specimen. Measure screen-to-immersion **rank agreement and false-negative rate**, with confidence intervals, using the proposed random audit of rejected candidates. An additional set deliberately spanning the acceptance threshold would test where misranking is most consequential. State explicitly that neither LPR-derived instantaneous rates nor 30-day mass loss prove years of C71500-equivalent service: Cu–Ni films and corrosion rates evolve over much longer periods. This is a proposal recommendation supported by copper-alloy literature, **not an attributed Shifler quotation**. (lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 14-17, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 17-19)

2. **Expose the actual repair system, not just free-standing alloy coupons.** Include as-deposited DED-on-DED and DED-on-*each* substrate—cast NAB and wrought Cu–Ni—with exposed fusion boundary, dilution/heat-affected region, and specified deposit-to-substrate area ratios; test uncoupled constituents and coupled joints. Map local attack across the interface and document surface condition and accessible defects. Report galvanic current/potential or spatial electrochemical evidence where practicable, alongside the planned mass loss and maximum attack depth. Shifler’s galvanic-modeling paper is an appropriate motivation; Lewis and LaQue’s finding of general copper-alloy compatibility **does not guarantee** compatibility of a compositionally graded repair with unfavorable area ratios. (lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 19-21)

3. **Test the failure mode the average current can miss.** Use cross-sections and phase/element maps before and after exposure to quantify deepest selective attack, its location at α/κ or other phase boundaries, and any pore, crack, or interface-associated initiation. Pair selected exposures with dissolved-metal measurements and surface-film characterization, especially where the optimizer varies Fe, Mn, Cr, or Al. Corrosion can be incongruent, so a favorable bulk current or average mass loss need not exclude preferential elemental or phase dissolution. The 2024 review establishing this point **acknowledges Shifler as an ONR program officer; it is not his publication**. [Single-source acknowledgment.] (ghorbani2024currentprogressina pages 2-3, ghorbani2024currentprogressina pages 15-16, basumatary2015thesynergisticeffects pages 9-11, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 15-16)

4. **Bridge synthetic static immersion to Navy-relevant exposure explicitly.** Retain ASTM D1141 as a reproducible *screen*, then budget at least a small matched validation matrix for natural versus synthetic seawater and controlled flow or impingement, preferably with a named Navy-lab handoff, sample count, and decision criterion. Track film evolution with time and identify whether representative locations are stagnant, flowing, creviced, biofouled, or subject to cavitation; do not promise that a static 30-day bath resolves all of them. Literature reports that Fe content beneficial under impingement can have different sheltered-area consequences, that Cr-modified Cu–Ni can trade flow resistance against quiet-water localization, and that cavitation changes NAB’s mode of damage. These are **Lewis/LaQue and Basumatary results**, not verified findings of Shifler’s seawater-testing conference papers. (lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 17-19, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 14-17, basumatary2015thesynergisticeffects pages 9-11, basumatary2015thesynergisticeffects pages 1-2)

5. **Frame prediction as calibrated, environment-specific inference.** Carry thermal-history/CALPHAD phase predictions through measured as-deposited microstructure to distinct endpoints—screen response, time-dependent mass loss, maximum penetration, and interface attack—with uncertainty and an explicit validation set. Include C71500 tested **side by side in the same condition** as the primary benchmark, plus the relevant cast NAB and wrought Cu–Ni substrates; make reduced Ni a secondary Pareto objective only after deposit integrity and corrosion criteria are met. Wording likely to land well is *“mechanism-checked, cost-aware screening with quantified escape risk and a staged path to flowing-seawater validation.”* Wording likely to invite challenge is *“one-hour polarization predicts 30-year service”* or *“ASTM D1141 certifies seawater equivalence.”* The latter are proposed-reviewer inferences, **not documented personal statements by Shifler**. (ghorbani2024currentprogressina pages 14-15, ghorbani2024currentprogressina pages 15-16, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 15-16, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 14-17)
