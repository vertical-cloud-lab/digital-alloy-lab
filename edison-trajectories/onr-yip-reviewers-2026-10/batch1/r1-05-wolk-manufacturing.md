## Implications for the YIP proposal

1. **Frame this as alloy science that generates qualification-relevant evidence, not a certification program.** A defensible promise is to discover pre-alloyed powders and establish reproducible composition–thermal-history–microstructure–property–corrosion relationships for specified blown-powder DED builds. Do **not** promise a “Navy-qualified alloy,” certified repair, approved casting replacement, or a universal rapid-qualification framework from coupons and a limited set of purchased build sessions. The workshop explicitly distinguishes proposed rapid-qualification methods from the unresolved problems of build consistency, geometry-dependent properties, inspection, defects and property databases. **[Inference from a workshop report, not a verified Wolk preference.]** (babu2016reportonadditive pages 23-25)

2. **Make the digital thread physically auditable.** Preserve powder-lot chemistry and size/flow measurements, atomization history, DED machine and parameter records, thermal-history estimates, sampled locations and orientations, defects, test conditions, and model uncertainties. Explain how CALPHAD evaluated along modeled thermal histories and cheap tracks/screens will be calibrated against full blown-powder builds—not treated as substitutes for them. This builds on the workshop’s call to curate CAD-to-part and process records and link computational modeling with characterization, while addressing published cautions about model calibration and validation. **[Workshop-level inference plus peer-reviewed review.]** (babu2016reportonadditive pages 23-25, kouraytem2021modelingprocess–structure–propertyrelationships pages 1-3)

3. **Treat self-build and repair as separate evidentiary cases.** Pre-specify observations at deposit interiors, build boundaries and the cast-NAB or wrought-Cu-Ni interface, including dilution/compositional gradients, cracking, porosity and location-dependent hardness or other measured properties. The Nimer–Wolk–Zupan paper is an apt *methodological* citation for location- and orientation-resolved characterization of a dissimilar processing zone; it is **not** precedent for qualifying DED repair or predicting copper fatigue. **[Application of an indirectly accessed paper.]** (rego2018fatiguetestingmachine pages 63-72, babu2016reportonadditive pages 23-25)

4. **Define the marine benchmark narrowly and test it directly.** Compare *as-deposited* specimens and interfaces with wrought C71500 under matched, reported seawater-exposure and surface-condition protocols; use electrochemical screens to prioritize candidates and longer immersion to test whether their rankings persist. Density, lack of visible cracks, one-hour corrosion response and hardness cannot alone demonstrate marine-service equivalence, fatigue resistance or suitability to replace a casting. State lower nickel content as a conditional optimization subject to the corrosion and integrity objectives. **[Proposal recommendation; no Wolk-specific copper or seawater result was verified.]** (babu2016reportonadditive pages 23-25, babu2016reportonadditive pages 25-32)

5. **Use precise claims that survive qualification scrutiny.** “A traceable, cost-aware discovery workflow and qualification-*relevant* property/defect data for bounded alloy–process–substrate conditions” fits the record better than “rapidly qualify naval replacement castings.” Identify what later component-level, statistically replicated, fatigue, inspection and authority-specific acceptance work would remain. Do not attribute the ORNL workshop’s ICME or casting language to Wolk, and do not claim her personal endorsement of copper DED without an attributable source. **[Workshop-level inference; personal priorities unverified.]** (babu2016reportonadditive pages 32-37, babu2016reportonadditive pages 23-25, babu2016reportonadditive pages 25-32)
