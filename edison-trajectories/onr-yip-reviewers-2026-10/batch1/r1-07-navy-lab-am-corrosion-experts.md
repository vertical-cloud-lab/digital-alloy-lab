**Most relevant publication.** Hangarter, Carlos M., Andrew B. Geltmacher, Nicole R. Tailleart, Patrick G. Callahan, Dillon S. Watring, Scott M. Olig, and William Kinee. “X-ray Computed Microtomography Evaluation of Electrochemically Accelerated Stainless Steel Crevice Corrosion.” *ECS Advances* **4**, no. 1 (2025): 011501. DOI: **10.1149/2754-2734/adb16a**. In a controlled crevice cell, XCMT resolved potential-dependent propagation and produced volume-loss estimates consistent with electrochemical measurements; the authors present the method as a proof of concept, subject to tomography limitations. # Implications for the proposal

1. **Corrosion endpoint and benchmark—Hangarter and NRL coauthors.** Cite their XCMT/electrochemistry study when explaining why a one-hour screen is a *ranking assay*, not proof of long-term service performance. Specify identical as-deposited preparation and exposure conditions for candidate deposits and wrought **C71500**, replicate measurements, localized-damage endpoints, and how short tests will be checked against 30-day immersion. Examine deposit, remelted interface, and adjacent substrate separately. A claim that a lower short-term corrosion current alone establishes “seawater resistance at least as good as C71500” would overreach their finding. This is a proposal-specific inference, not a reported opinion of any author. (hangarter2025xraycomputedmicrotomography pages 1-2, hangarter2025xraycomputedmicrotomography pages 6-8)

2. **Microstructure-to-corrosion link—Brewick.** Cite his AM-informed versus homogeneous-pit comparison to justify retaining spatial descriptors alongside composition: phase distribution, segregation, grain structure, and the actual deposited/repair interface. State how the thermal-history–CALPHAD link and corrosion model will be checked against *observed* microstructure and damage; do not imply equilibrium phase predictions alone forecast pit initiation or depth. His own stated model limitations also argue for measured validation rather than presenting a coupled model as a completed digital twin. (brewick2022simulatingpittingcorrosion pages 1-4, brewick2022simulatingpittingcorrosion pages 28-30)

3. **Value of a cheap experiment—Jamieson.** Cite Huang–Jamieson–Basak for uncertainty-aware use of scarce DED data. Explain which bead and dilution measurements from pre-placed-powder tracks genuinely inform **single-stream blown-powder** deposition, and which require recalibration at the purchased facility. Report uncertainty or explicit failure criteria when moving between powder lots, Cu–Ni and NAB, substrates, and machines; reserve the expensive build to test those transfers rather than treating a good track as a dense, crack-free repair. Frame the proposal as *validated, cost-aware transfer across fidelities*, not as universal process-map portability. (huang2026uncertaintyawarereconstructionof pages 1-2)

4. **Repair versus material coupon—Phan.** Cite the crack-growth review to acknowledge that hardness, density, and corrosion do not by themselves qualify a structural repair. Distinguish the proposed alloy-discovery deliverable from future component certification, but measure or plan discriminating evidence on bond integrity, interface defects, distortion/residual stress, and property variability for both **cast NAB** and **wrought Cu–Ni** substrates. Avoid wording that equates “crack-free by visual inspection” with damage tolerance. This is an extrapolation from Phan’s published qualification focus, not a claim that his paper studied marine copper alloys. (jones2026areviewof pages 1-2)

5. **Powder/process precision and evidentiary boundaries—Ansell and the candidate list.** Document what ultrasonic atomization actually controls—composition across lots, particle-size distribution, morphology, oxygen/oxide content, and delivered-powder behavior—and which tests are specific to *laser powder DED*, rather than borrowing assurances from other AM modalities. State that lowering nickel is subordinate to verified **as-deposited** marine performance. Do not assign such affiliations or specialize these researchers beyond their publications. No cited source identifies who will actually review this proposal.
