## Implications for the proposal

1. **Name the computational advance precisely.** Frame it as a *thermal-history-conditioned, uncertainty-aware, multi-fidelity decision system* linking pre-alloyed powder composition, measured powder state, DED thermal cycles, predicted phases, defects and **as-deposited** properties. Evaluating CALPHAD along a heat-flow history is an input to that advance; the testable novelty is how linked predictions and their uncertainties select the **next experiment per unit cost**. This aligns with ONR’s published emphasis on connecting AM thermal cycling and local metallurgical phenomena to experimentally checked ICME models. **[Proposal recommendation based on a program solicitation, not an attributed Varanasi preference.]** (timeUnknownyearfiscalyear(fy) pages 53-55, richter2026thermodynamicmodelingof pages 1-6, krajewski2024extensiblestructureinformedprediction pages 20-24)

2. **Expect scrutiny of uncertainty at every link.** Separate uncertainty in powder chemistry and DED thermal-history estimates; thermodynamic-database extrapolation; phase/defect and corrosion surrogates; and measurement variation. Show how each propagates into the probability of meeting *all* requirements, rather than reporting a single optimum composition. Compare acquisition policies against random, composition-only and equal-cost search baselines; ONR-supported work has benchmarked active-learning policies on experimental Fe–Co–Ni landscapes and found that performance depends on response-landscape complexity. **[Inference about likely reviewer questions, not a documented statement by Varanasi.]** (richter2026thermodynamicmodelingof pages 1-6, wang2022benchmarkingactivelearning pages 9-11, wang2022benchmarkingactivelearning pages 5-9)

3. **Make experimental validation difficult to dismiss.** Predefine held-out compositions and builds; independently measure deposited rather than nominal composition, dilution at **both** repair interfaces, density/cracking, phases and hardness. Treat the one-hour electrochemical screen as a **surrogate requiring calibration** against longer immersion, and compare as-deposited corrosion with wrought C71500 under matched test conditions. Budget and access to purchased DED sessions should be explicit: a promising cheap-track result is not proof of a successful blown-powder repair. ONR’s published AM topic specifically flags evaporation, segregation, corrosion variation and computationally guided experiments. **[Program solicitation; proposed validation design.]** (timeUnknownyearfiscalyear(fy) pages 53-55)

4. **Demonstrate transfer rather than advertise it.** Test whether a model trained on Cu–Ni–Fe–Mn–Cr can rank aluminum-bronze/NAB candidates—or whether a new model, database assessment or small calibration set is required. Report out-of-family prediction error, uncertainty calibration and the cost of adaptation; test deposits on themselves **and** on cast NAB and wrought Cu–Ni. SIPFENN’s explicit out-of-dataset and few-point transfer tests are a more defensible precedent to cite than a blanket assertion that ‘AI generalizes.’ **[Inference grounded in an ONR-supported publication, not Varanasi’s own result.]** (krajewski2024extensiblestructureinformedprediction pages 20-24)

5. **Keep attribution and rhetoric disciplined.** Cite Quinton *et al.* only for Varanasi’s documented experimental processing work. Describe SIPFENN, CALPHAD uncertainty quantification and the AM topic as **ONR-supported research or ONR program context**, not as ‘Varanasi’s results’ or confirmed CAMD awards.
