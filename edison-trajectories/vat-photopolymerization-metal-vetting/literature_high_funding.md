# Funding of Metal Vat Photopolymerization Research and Development

## Overview

This survey identifies the public and private funding sources that support research and development of metal vat photopolymerization (VP)—including sinter-based metal DLP/stereolithography with powder-loaded photoresins, lithography-based metal manufacturing (LMM), metal-ion/metal-salt photoresins, hydrogel infusion additive manufacturing (HIAM), and two-photon lithography (TPL) routes that yield metal or metal-oxide parts after calcination, reduction, debinding, or sintering. The analysis is based on acknowledgment sections, grant identifiers, and patent records retrieved from published literature.

## 1. U.S. Department of Energy (DOE): The Dominant Funder

The DOE Office of Science, Basic Energy Sciences (BES), is the single most prominent funder of metal VP research identified in this survey. The flagship grant is **DE-SC0016945**, awarded to **Julia R. Greer** (PI) at the California Institute of Technology, with the project title "New Deformation Mechanisms in Nanocrystalline Nano-porous Small Scale Metals as Defined by Kinetically-driven Microstructures" (greer2025newdeformationmechanisms pages 1-4). This BES award has supported the development of hydrogel infusion additive manufacturing (HIAM) for Cu, Ni, Ag, Cu-Ni, and multicomponent alloys, as well as two-photon-lithography/HIAM fabrication of Ni nanopillars (saccone2022additivemanufacturingof pages 7-8, greer2025newdeformationmechanisms pages 4-8). The same grant number appears in the acknowledgments of the seminal Saccone et al. 2022 *Nature* paper ("This work was supported by the U.S. Department of Energy, Office of Science, Basic Energy Sciences, under Award No. DE-SC0016945") (saccone2022microscalefabricationof pages 9-12), the corresponding preprint (saccone2022microscalefabricationof pages 9-12), the Greer 2025 DOE final report (greer2025newdeformationmechanisms pages 1-4), and the Serles et al. 2026 *Nature Reviews Materials* review (serles2026nanoscaleadditivemanufacturing pages 20-21).

At **Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL)**, the work of Yaw et al. (2025) on precursor design for HIAM of ceramics (including CuO and ZrO₂) was "performed under the auspices of the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) by the Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) under Contract No. **DE-AC52-07NA27344**" (yaw2025precursordesignfor pages 16-17). A graduate fellowship from the DOE **University Nuclear Leadership Program** (grant **DE_NE0009089**) also supported this LLNL work (yaw2025precursordesignfor pages 16-17). While the Yaw et al. study focused on ceramic endpoints, the authors explicitly developed HIAM process knowledge directly applicable to metal fabrication.

## 2. U.S. Department of Defense (DoD)

Multiple DoD agencies appear in the acknowledgments of metal VP publications:

- **Office of Naval Research (ONR)**: Grant **N00014-17-1-2977** supported the Oran et al. 2018 *Science* paper on Implosion Fabrication (ImpFab) at MIT (PI: Edward S. Boyden), which demonstrated volumetric silver deposition in photopatterned hydrogel scaffolds followed by dehydration-induced shrinkage to nanoscale features (oran20183dnanofabricationby pages 3-5).

- **U.S. Army Research Laboratory and Army Research Office (ARL/ARO)**: Contract **W911NF1510548** co-funded the same Oran/Boyden ImpFab project (oran20183dnanofabricationby pages 3-5). Separately, ARO contract **W911NF-19-D-0001** supported the Lee et al. 2024 *Nature Communications* work at Caltech on stereolithography-printed metallo-polyelectrolyte gels via the Institute for Collaborative Biotechnologies (lee2024molecularcontrolvia pages 11-11).

- **DoD Vannevar Bush Faculty Fellowship**: Acknowledged in the Yee et al. 2019 *Advanced Materials* paper (Caltech, PI: Julia R. Greer) on two-photon lithography of zinc-ion photoresins to produce 3D-architected ZnO nanostructures, though no specific fellowship number was stated (yee2019additivemanufacturingof pages 7-8).

## 3. National Institutes of Health (NIH)

NIH grants appear in multiple metal VP projects:

- **T32GM112592** (NIH Biotechnology Leadership Training Program) also supported the same Yee et al. 2019 study (yee2019additivemanufacturingof pages 7-8).
- Multiple NIH grants supported the Boyden/MIT ImpFab silver work: **1R01EB024261**, **1U01MH106011**, **1DP1NS087724** (NIH Director's Pioneer Award), **1R01MH103910**, **2R01DA029639**, **1RM1HG008525**, and **1R24MH106075** (oran20183dnanofabricationby pages 3-5).

## 4. National Science Foundation (NSF)

The NSF **Graduate Research Fellowship Program** (award **1122374**) supported Samuel G. Rodriques on the Oran et al. 2018 ImpFab project at MIT (oran20183dnanofabricationby pages 3-5, oran20183dnanofabricationby pages 5-5). No direct NSF research grants (e.g., NSF CMMI or NSF SBIR/STTR) were identified in the retrieved metal VP literature, though this may reflect incomplete coverage of all published work in this field.

## 5. European and Non-US Public Funders

- **European Regional Development Fund (ERDF/EFRE)**: The Scheibler et al. 2024 study on LMM of copper at the Fraunhofer Institute for Ceramic Technologies and Systems (Fraunhofer IKTS, Germany) was funded by the ERDF through the **AddiMet** project (funding reference **100405446**), with co-financing from tax revenues of the Free State of Saxony (scheibler2024lithographybasedmetalmanufacturing pages 15-17).

- **TU Wien / Incus GmbH (Austria)**: The Mastny 2026 thesis at TU Wien, which used an Incus LMM printer for copper alloy (Ag and Cu-Sn bronze) feedstock development, did not identify any external government grants or Austrian FFG funding in its acknowledgments. The thesis was supervised by Christian Gierl-Mayer and Atul Anand at TU Wien's Institute for Chemical Technologies and Analytics, Powder Metallurgy division (mastny2026highstrengthcopperalloys pages 1-5). This suggests the work may have been internally funded or supported by industry collaboration with Incus GmbH, whose commercial LMM platform (the Hammer series) was used.

- **Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC)** and the **Swiss ETH Domain Strategic Focus Area—Additive Manufacturing Program** are acknowledged in the Serles et al. 2026 *Nature Reviews Materials* review, along with the Schmidt Science Fellowship and Rhodes Trust (serles2026nanoscaleadditivemanufacturing pages 20-21).

- **Gwangju Institute of Science and Technology (GIST)**, South Korea (grant **CG2014**), co-funded the Yee et al. 2019 Caltech two-photon lithography work (yee2019additivemanufacturingof pages 7-8).

## 6. Company-Associated Work

- **Holo Inc.** (Oakland, CA): Multiple patents were identified (US10245785B2, US10464259B2, US11351735B2, US11945166B2) assigned to Holo Inc. and invented by Brian Adzima, Pierre Lin, Richard M. ## 7. Which Agencies and Programs Fund the Most

Based on the evidence retrieved, the **DOE Office of Science, Basic Energy Sciences** program is the most prolific single funder of metal VP research, primarily through grant DE-SC0016945 to the Greer group at Caltech, which has generated the majority of high-impact publications in this space (the 2022 *Nature* paper with 258 citations, the 2026 *Nature Reviews Materials* review, and multiple theses and DOE reports). DOE also funds the LLNL HIAM program via the laboratory operating contract.

The **DoD** complex (ONR, ARL/ARO, and the Vannevar Bush Fellowship) collectively constitutes the second largest U.S. public funder, supporting both the MIT ImpFab work and Caltech metallo-polyelectrolyte and two-photon lithography projects. **NIH** provides significant co-funding, particularly for the MIT and Caltech projects, often through training grants and neuroscience/biomedical mechanism awards.

In Europe, the **EU ERDF** (via German state co-financing) supports LMM copper development at Fraunhofer IKTS. Austrian public funding for Incus or TU Wien LMM metal research was not identified in the documents retrieved, suggesting that much of the Austrian LMM development may be commercially funded by Incus GmbH and its predecessor Lithoz.

**NSF** appears modestly in the form of a graduate fellowship supporting the MIT ImpFab work. No NSF CMMI, NSF SBIR/STTR, DARPA, ARPA-E, NASA, AFOSR, or NIST awards were specifically identified as funding metal VP research in the retrieved literature, though this absence may reflect limitations in the search rather than a true absence of such awards. In particular, the Nguyen and Chung group at Michigan State University has published on photopolymerization of stainless steel 420 metal suspensions (cited in the Mastny thesis as reference 18), but their funding acknowledgments were not retrievable in this search.

## Caveats

This survey is based on papers, patents, and reports retrievable through academic databases and patent search systems. It may not capture all relevant SBIR/STTR awards (which are often disclosed in federal award databases like SBIR.gov rather than in patent texts), classified or ITAR-restricted programs, or industry-funded work where acknowledgments are omitted. The question of Austrian FFG funding for Incus/TU Wien LMM work remains open; such grants may exist in Austrian funding databases but were not found in the English-language academic literature retrieved here.
