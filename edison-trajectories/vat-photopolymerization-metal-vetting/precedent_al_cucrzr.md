Question: Has anyone made aluminum-alloy or copper-chromium-zirconium (Cu-Cr-Zr, C18150 or C18200) parts by
vat photopolymerization of a metal-powder slurry (DLP, stereolithography or lithography-based metal
manufacturing) followed by debinding and sintering? If so, report density, oxygen and carbon
content, strength, electrical conductivity and the sintering atmosphere. Also report any vat
photopolymerization of aluminum powder at all (including cure-depth studies of Al-filled resins),
and any Cu-Cr, Cu-Zr or oxide-dispersion-strengthened copper made by photopolymerization of a metal
or metal-oxide feedstock.


Yes. ## Finding

**Yes for an aluminum alloy, but I could not verify a corresponding demonstration for Cu–Cr–Zr (C18150 or C18200).** Zhang and colleagues’ 2021 paper, *Digital light processing 3D printing of AlSi10Mg powder modified by surface coating* (DOI: **10.1016/j.addma.2021.101897**), is identified in a contemporary review as a powder-slurry DLP → debinding → sintering study. Its polystyrene-coated AlSi10Mg feedstock contained **45 vol% powder**; the review reports **450 °C debinding** and **600 °C sintering under N₂**, with a well-formed honeycomb structure and bonded layers. A later paper independently describes the 45-vol% DLP-printed green parts. The original Zhang paper was not obtainable in this search, so I cannot verify a *final metallic density, oxygen or carbon concentration, strength, or electrical conductivity* for its parts; the cited review reports none of those values. In particular, 45 vol% is **slurry loading, not final density**. (yan2023reviewofthe pages 3-7, yan2023reviewofthe pages 7-9, yan2023reviewofthe pages 11-12, zhao2025digitallightprocessing pages 3-4)

For **Cu–Cr–Zr/C18150/C18200**, I found **no verifiable report** establishing the specified powder-slurry vat-printing, debinding, and sintering sequence. Accordingly, there is **no substantiated final density, O or C assay, strength, electrical conductivity, or sintering atmosphere to report for that exact process and alloy**. This is a limit of the located literature, not proof that an unpublished part does not exist. Do not substitute values from laser powder-bed fusion, directed-energy deposition, conventional powder metallurgy, or pure-copper DLP. (mastny2026highstrengthcopperalloys pages 5-9, mastny2026highstrengthcopperalloys pages 9-11)

## Related copper systems: distinguish alloy, precursor, and process

A 2026 thesis by **Mastny** investigated DLP/lithography-based metal manufacturing with **GRCop-42, Cu–Cr–Nb**, mixed with silver or Cu–Sn bronze, and explored hydrogen- and argon-containing sintering environments. It reports problematic densification and oxidation of the Nb-bearing phase, including measured oxygen in tested sintered specimens, but **GRCop-42 is not Cu–Cr–Zr, Cu–Cr, or Cu–Zr**; its oxygen measurements must not be assigned to C18150/C18200. The available evidence does not establish a qualified strength-and-conductivity dataset for these mixtures. (mastny2026highstrengthcopperalloys pages 87-90, mastny2026highstrengthcopperalloys pages 5-9, mastny2026highstrengthcopperalloys pages 9-11)

I likewise could not verify a **photopolymerized metal- or oxide-feedstock Cu–Cr or Cu–Zr metallic part**, or a **photopolymerized oxide-dispersion-strengthened copper metal part**, with confirmed composition and post-processing. An oxide-containing photopolymer, a DLP-printed *CuO–ZnO–ZrO₂ catalyst*, and oxide-powder patents are **not evidence of a reduced Cu–Zr alloy or oxide-dispersion-strengthened copper conductor**. Ordinary copper-powder micro-stereolithography *has* produced sintered conductive copper microstructures; the reviewed 2006 example used vacuum sintering at **950 °C for 3 h** and reported resistivity about **2 × 10⁻⁷ Ω·m**, but it supplies **no chromium or zirconium**. (yan2023reviewofthe pages 3-7, yan2023reviewofthe pages 7-9, yan2023reviewofthe pages 11-12)

**Evidence limitation:** The AlSi10Mg original article and the Gan article could not be examined directly. Therefore, absence of their requested composition and performance numbers above means **not verified from accessible evidence**, not that those articles necessarily did not measure them.
