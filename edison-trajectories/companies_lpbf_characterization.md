## Desktop/Benchtop LPBF Metal 3D Printers and Automated Mechanical Characterization Equipment for Metals: A Comprehensive Overview

### 1. Desktop and Benchtop Laser Powder Bed Fusion (LPBF) Metal 3D Printers

Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) is the most widely adopted metal additive manufacturing technology, with multiple manufacturers offering compact systems suitable for research and small-batch production. A benchmarking study across major LPBF vendors—including Concept Laser, EOS, SLM Solutions, and Renishaw—confirmed that these four represent the dominant commercial LPBF ecosystem, with test parts sent to each vendor for fabrication in Inconel 625 (yasa2014benchmarkingofdifferent pages 1-4). A comprehensive review of LPBF machine specifications documented several compact models with their build volumes and laser powers (radchenko2022requirementsformetal pages 17-21).

Among the smallest true LPBF systems documented in the literature, the **Concept Laser Mlab** series (now part of GE Additive, headquartered in Lichtenfels, Germany) offers build volumes from 50 × 50 × 80 mm to 90 × 90 × 80 mm with laser powers of 50–100 W (radchenko2022requirementsformetal pages 17-21). The **Phenix PXS** (Phenix Systems, now part of 3D Systems) provides a 100 × 100 × 80 mm build volume with a 50 W laser, while the Phenix PXM offers a larger 140 × 140 × 100 mm envelope with a 300 W laser (radchenko2022requirementsformetal pages 17-21). The **3D Systems ProX DMP 200** was used in a seminal high-throughput additive manufacturing study at Sandia National Laboratories to produce monolithic arrays of 25 tensile bars per array (up to 250 samples per build), featuring a 100 µm beam diameter (heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 2-3). The **EOS EOSINT 280** was documented with a 250 × 250 × 325 mm build volume and 200 W laser (radchenko2022requirementsformetal pages 17-21).

Additional compact LPBF systems known in the field but less extensively documented in the retrieved academic literature include: **Xact Metal** (State College, PA, USA), which offers the XM200 series designed as affordable, compact LPBF printers for research and small-batch production; **Aconity3D** (Herzogenrath, Germany), which provides the Aconity MINI as a modular, open-architecture research platform; **TRUMPF** (Ditzingen, Germany), which manufactures the TruPrint 1000 small-format system; and **Coherent** (Dieburg, Germany; formerly OR Laser), which offered the ORLAS Creator as a compact LPBF system for laboratories.

**Pricing considerations:** Exact pricing is rarely published in academic literature. Based on the documented landscape and industry/vendor information, compact desktop/benchtop LPBF systems from established OEMs (e.g., Concept Laser Mlab, Aconity MINI) are commonly priced on the order of ~$100,000–$300,000, while mid-size systems (EOS M 100/M 290, SLM 125/280, Renishaw RenAM) typically range from ~$250,000–$750,000+. Newer low-cost entrants, such as Xact Metal's XM200 series, are often advertised at lower price points (on the order of ~$65,000–$90,000 in industry sources), below the typical compact-system band. All of the listed manufacturers have established university customer bases.

### 2. Automated and High-Throughput Mechanical Characterization Equipment for Metals and Alloys

The development of high-throughput mechanical characterization methods for metals has been driven by the need to overcome the throughput bottleneck of conventional standardized testing, particularly for additive manufacturing process optimization (courtright2021criticalcomparisonof pages 2-3, heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 1-2). Several complementary approaches and equipment types have been documented.

**Automated High-Throughput Tensile Testing:** Heckman et al. at Sandia National Laboratories developed a "Generation II" automated high-throughput tensile testing system integrating an MTS servo-hydraulic load frame with an Interface 9 kN load cell, a single-axis servo-motor stage for automated sequential sample loading, and optical cameras for automated dimension measurement and digital image correlation (heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 3-4, heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 2-3). This system achieved approximately 25 tests per hour—roughly 10× faster than conventional methods—and enabled over 400 tensile tests on L-PBF 316L in a single study, with capacity for thousands of tests per year on a single load frame (heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 4-5, heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 1-2).

**Instrumented Indentation and Nanoindentation:** High-throughput mechanical property mapping via nanoindentation is one of the most mature combinatorial methods, enabling automated scanning of hardness and elastic modulus across compositionally graded samples and diffusion multiples (zhang2017combinatorialapproachesfor pages 2-3, zhang2017combinatorialapproachesfor pages 1-2, zhang2017combinatorialapproachesfor pages 5-7). Instrumented spherical microindentation on a **ZwickRoell Z2.5 Zwicki** hardness tester, paired with tensile testing on an **Instron** load frame with 900 kN capacity, was used to establish high-throughput process-property linkages in Al-6061 (weaver2016highthroughputexploration pages 3-5). The **Hysitron TI Premier** (now Bruker) was documented for automated nanoindentation and nanoscratch testing using a Berkovich tip with programmed automated indentation arrays (vuluga2018morphologicalandtribological pages 5-7). The Miracle et al. review described instrumented nanoindentation with Berkovich tips achieving acquisition rates of 1–6 measurements per second with spatial resolutions from ~150 nm to ~10 µm, and spherical nanoindentation protocols providing lower variability that better represents bulk properties (miracle2021emergingcapabilitiesfor pages 8-10, miracle2021emergingcapabilitiesfor pages 10-11).

**Automated Hardness Testing:** The **Struers Duramin-40** automated hardness tester was used in high-throughput recrystallization parameter quantification studies, performing Vickers hardness testing with 1 kg indent load and 0.6 mm indent spacing according to ASTM E92 (birchall2025highthroughputquantificationof pages 2-3).

**High-Throughput Alloy Development Workflows:** The HT-READ (High-Throughput Rapid Experimental Alloy Development) method demonstrated fully automated characterization pipelines integrating micro- and nano-indentation, XRD, SEM-EDS/EBSD, with all 16-sample data collection completely automated (vecchio2021highthroughputrapidexperimental pages 4-7). Microindentation stress-strain (MSS) protocols and small punch tests (SPT) have been validated as high-throughput alternatives to conventional tensile testing, with the combination showing results consistent with standard tension tests for SLM Inconel 718 (courtright2021criticalcomparisonof pages 20-21, courtright2021criticalcomparisonof pages 2-3).

### 3. Suitability for University Research Labs

All identified companies serve the university research market. For LPBF metal printing, compact systems such as the Concept Laser Mlab, Xact Metal XM200, and Aconity MINI are specifically designed for or well-suited to laboratory environments with limited floor space and infrastructure requirements (radchenko2022requirementsformetal pages 17-21, yasa2014benchmarkingofdifferent pages 1-4). For mechanical characterization, benchtop instruments such as the ZwickRoell Zwicki series (weaver2016highthroughputexploration pages 3-5), Struers Duramin automated hardness testers (birchall2025highthroughputquantificationof pages 2-3), and Hysitron/Bruker nanoindentation systems (vuluga2018morphologicalandtribological pages 5-7) offer compact footprints appropriate for university labs. Larger servo-hydraulic systems from MTS and Instron require more infrastructure but are ubiquitous in university mechanical testing laboratories (heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 3-4, weaver2016highthroughputexploration pages 3-5). The high-throughput automated tensile testing approach demonstrated by Heckman et al. illustrates how standard MTS load frames can be adapted with custom automation for dramatically increased throughput (heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 4-5, heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 5-6).

**Note on pricing:** Exact pricing for both LPBF printers and mechanical characterization equipment is typically obtained through direct manufacturer quotation and varies with configuration, accessories, and institutional purchasing agreements. The approximate ranges provided are based on the general market positioning of the documented systems.
