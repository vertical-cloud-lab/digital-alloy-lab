### Detailed Verification Results

**1. Amazemet (Warsaw, Poland)** — The listed CEO is confirmed as the key figure affiliated with Amazemet Sp. z o.o., R&D Department, at Al. Jana Pawła II 27, 00-867 Warsaw, Poland. A published corresponding-author email exists (zrodowski2021ultrashortsinteringand pages 1-2) [email removed]. He also holds a dual affiliation with the Faculty of Materials Science and Engineering, Warsaw University of Technology (zach2023sphericalpowderscontrol pages 1-2). However, the title "CEO" was not confirmed in any retrieved publication; he appears consistently as a research author and corresponding author. Amazemet's rePOWDER ultrasonic atomization system is extensively documented for producing AM-grade powders across diverse alloy systems (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3, yadegari2025aluminumlaseradditive pages 9-11).

**2. Elementum 3D (Erie, CO, USA)** — Dr. Jacob Nuechterlein is confirmed as the Project Director/Principal Investigator (PD/PI) on DOE-funded projects and co-inventor on the foundational RAM (Reactive Additive Manufacturing) patent, US Patent 10,507,638 B2, filed March 15, 2016, and issued December 12, 2019, with co-inventor Jeremy Iten (kleindienst2025theeffectsof pages 10-11). The company is formally registered as Sinter Print, Inc. d/b/a Elementum 3D, Inc., at 400 Young Ct. Unit 1, Erie, CO 80516 (nuechterlein2021additivemanufacturingfeedstock pages 3-9). The "CEO" title was not explicitly confirmed in the retrieved literature, though Nuechterlein is clearly the principal leader. RAM technology enables in-situ synthesis of ceramic grain refiners (TiB₂, TiC, TiAl₃) during LPBF, producing commercially available products including A6061-RAM, A2024-RAM, and A5083-RAM7 (rosito2023processabilityofa6061 pages 5-8, nuechterlein2021additivemanufacturingfeedstock pages 55-59, nuechterlein2021additivemanufacturingfeedstocka pages 11-13).

### Additional Companies in Scandium-Free / Rare-Earth-Free Aluminum Alloys for AM

Several companies are developing high-strength aluminum alloys for LPBF that avoid or minimize scandium and rare earth elements:

- **Aeromet International (UK):** Developer and patentee of the A20X alloy (Al-Cu-Mg-Ag + TiB₂ nanoparticles), a scandium-free, high-strength aluminum alloy for LPBF and casting (yadegari2025aluminumlaseradditive pages 14-15).

- **Constellium:** Aheadd® CP1 (Al-Fe-Zr) and Aheadd® HT1 (Al-Mn-Ni-Cu-Zr) are entirely scandium-free and rare-earth-free, using Zr-based L1₂ precipitation for strengthening (yadegari2025aluminumlaseradditive pages 14-15).

- **Elementum 3D:** RAM-modified alloys (A6061-RAM, A2024-RAM, A5083-RAM7) use in-situ reactive synthesis of ceramic grain refiners rather than scandium, enabling crack-free LPBF of wrought-class aluminum alloys (nuechterlein2021additivemanufacturingfeedstock pages 55-59, nuechterlein2021additivemanufacturingfeedstocka pages 11-13).

- **Fortius Metals (Lafayette, CO):** Identified in inoculated aluminum alloy / AM-related welding literature, adjacent to critical-mineral-substitution alloy development (kleindienst2025theeffectsof pages 10-11).

### DOE-Funded / CMI Hub-Connected Companies

The DOE's Critical Materials Innovation Hub (formerly Critical Materials Institute, CMI) has been instrumental in aluminum-cerium (Al-Ce) alloy development as a rare-earth substitution strategy:

- **Eck Industries (Wisconsin, USA):** A named CMI Hub industry partner that licensed Al-Ce alloys in Year 4 of the CMI program, with continuous production orders including atomized powders for automotive, military, and aerospace applications (lograsso2024criticalmaterialsinstitute pages 27-29). Eck Industries is a co-assignee with UT-Battelle on CMI Hub-funded AM patents (thomas2024anevaluationof pages 29-31, thomas2024anevaluationof pages 33-36).

- **Intelligent Composites (USA):** Assigned patents on aluminum alloys containing cerium and graphite, with leadership ties to Eck Industries (CEO of Intelligent Composites is also VP at Eck Industries) (thomas2024anevaluationof pages 29-31).

- **Arconic, Hamilton Sundstrand (Raytheon), and University of Texas:** Identified as assignees citing CMI Hub-funded cerium-modified aluminum alloy patents, indicating downstream development of critical-mineral-substitution alloys for AM applications (thomas2024anevaluationof pages 29-31).

### Beryllium-Free Copper Alloy Replacements

The literature on beryllium-free copper alloys identifies Corson-type (Cu-Ni-Si) and Hyper-Corson (e.g., CuNi3.9Co0.9Si1.2Mg0.1, with yield strengths exceeding 900 MPa) alloy systems as leading candidates for beryllium substitution (altenberger2015materialpropertiesof pages 13-17). However, no specific companies developing beryllium-free copper alloys specifically for additive manufacturing were identified in the retrieved literature. Known industry participants in copper AM powders (e.g., 6K Additive for CuCrZr via UniMelt, EOS for CuCrZr, and NASA for GRCop-42/GRCop-84) have been reported in trade literature but were not confirmed in the peer-reviewed publications retrieved for this analysis.
