## A. Fit Assessment

### Where the overlap exists

The symposium's Topic 1 explicitly calls for "advanced characterization of trace elements," and the abstract directly addresses this by demonstrating that standardless eZAF EDS over-reports Mg in AlSi10Mg by a factor of 3–4× due to the Mg Kα/Al Kα spectral overlap and soft-X-ray background modeling errors. This is a real and well-documented problem in SEM-EDS metrology: Newbury and Ritchie (2015; DOI: 10.1007/s10853-014-8685-2) showed that standardless analysis requires ±30% relative error to capture 95% of results, that normalization to 100% masks geometric artifacts, and that misidentification rates increase substantially for minor and trace constituents (newbury2015performingelementalmicroanalysis pages 7-9, newbury2015performingelementalmicroanalysis pages 4-5). The abstract's approach of anchoring EDS results against ICP-MS/MS mirrors the standards-based validation philosophy that the EDS community increasingly advocates (newbury2015performingelementalmicroanalysis pages 23-25, giunto2026accuratesem‑edsquantification pages 9-11).

Topic 3 ("Impacts on additive manufacturing") provides a secondary connection via the LPBF AlSi10Mg and powder-characterization focus. Contamination in recycled aluminum powder for LPBF is an emerging concern: iron contamination exceeding 0.5 wt% forms brittle β-Al₅FeSi intermetallics that severely compromise ductility (yadegari2025aluminumlaseradditive pages 9-11), and no universal standards exist for determining viable reuse cycles for AM powder (yadegari2025aluminumlaseradditive pages 11-12).

### Where the mismatch is severe

The abstract does not mention scrap, recycling, secondary aluminum, tramp elements, residual elements, circular economy, sustainability, or impurity tolerance anywhere. It studies nominally primary-route AlSi10Mg and "lean, supply-chain-resilient" alloys without connecting these to scrap-derived feedstock streams. The symposium title is "Characterizing Tramp Elements in Scrap Metals for Sustainable Manufacturing"—every word of which is absent from the abstract's framing.

The committee is strongly steel/ferrous-weighted (organizers Ma, Cho, and arguably Chen operate primarily in ferrous metallurgy), and the symposium's call explicitly names Cu, Sn, Ni, and Pb as exemplar tramp elements. The abstract's motivating example—Mg quantification error in AlSi10Mg—concerns a *major alloying element* in a *primary alloy*, not a tramp/residual element in a scrap-derived system. Furthermore, the abstract's emphasis on AI/agentic decision-support systems and RAG-based parameter optimization is a methods/tooling contribution, whereas the symposium audience expects metallurgical mechanisms: how do residual elements affect phase stability, defect formation, and mechanical performance? Raabe (2023; DOI: 10.1021/acs.chemrev.2c00799) frames this as the "science of dirty alloys," emphasizing the need for understanding how tramp elements alter phase equilibria, transformation kinetics, and fracture behavior—none of which the abstract addresses (raabe2023thematerialsscience pages 147-148).

## D. Tailoring Playbook

If the team decides to proceed, the following concrete reframing steps are recommended:

### D1. Lead with the scrap/recycling problem, not the AI/method

Rewrite the title and opening to center the symposium's core concern: *"Quantification Fidelity of Tramp Elements in Recycled Aluminum Feedstocks for Additive Manufacturing: A Multi-Modal, AI-Assisted Characterization Approach."* The first sentence should state that as aluminum recycling rates increase toward the 50%+ secondary-aluminum targets projected for 2030 (pereira2025aluminumscraprecycling pages 10-12), reliable quantification of Fe, Cu, Zn, Sn, Ni, and Pb in scrap-derived feedstock is a critical metrology bottleneck. Cite Raabe (2023; DOI: 10.1021/acs.chemrev.2c00799), who explicitly identifies the need for advanced characterization and ML-assisted approaches to understand processing-structure-property relationships in impurity-containing alloys (raabe2023thematerialsscience pages 147-148).

### D2. Replace the Mg example with tramp-element examples

The Mg Kα/Al Kα overlap is a legitimate EDS artifact, but Mg is an intentional alloying element in AlSi10Mg, not a tramp element. Instead, demonstrate the parameter-optimization framework on elements the symposium cares about:
- **Fe quantification** in recycled Al-Si: Fe above 0.5 wt% triggers β-Al₅FeSi formation (pereira2025aluminumscraprecycling pages 27-31, yadegari2025aluminumlaseradditive pages 9-11), so accurate Fe measurement is essential for accept/reject decisions on secondary feedstock.
- **Cu quantification**: Cu accumulates in recycled 6xxx alloys beyond specification limits (caro2023effectofrecycling pages 8-12, caro2023effectofrecycling pages 2-4) and accelerates galvanic corrosion (pereira2025aluminumscraprecycling pages 24-27). The Cu Lα line at 0.93 keV can suffer interference issues in Al matrices.
- **Sn, Pb, Zn**: These form low-melting segregated phases promoting hot cracking (pereira2025aluminumscraprecycling pages 27-31, raabe2023thematerialsscience pages 148-149). Quantifying them at the sub-0.1 wt% level by EDS is at or near the detection limit, making the parameter-optimization problem directly consequential.

Newbury and Ritchie (2015; DOI: 10.1007/s10853-014-8685-2) showed that trace-level EDS quantification with detection limits below 1000 ppm is possible within 500 s using SDD-EDS with proper standards, but requires careful protocol adherence (newbury2015performingelementalmicroanalysis pages 1-2). Demonstrating that the agentic system can automatically select conditions that achieve these detection limits for tramp elements would be a strong fit.

### D3. Use scrap-derived or recycled powder as the sample set

If the lab has access to secondary-aluminum atomized powder or can obtain it (e.g., from partners working on aluminum scrap upcycling; see Shankar et al. 2025; DOI: 10.1007/s40962-024-01482-7), running the parameter-optimization framework on a feedstock with real impurity variability would transform the abstract's relevance. The "DNA of Twitch" study found that mixed automotive scrap averages ~91% Al with ~0.6% Fe, ~1.5% Cu, ~0.8% Zn, plus minor Ni, Sn, Pb, Cr, and Ti (shankar2025upcyclingoflow pages 7-9, shankar2025upcyclingoflow pages 9-10, shankar2025upcyclingoflow pages 4-6). Characterizing atomized powder derived from such scrap and comparing EDS-optimized compositions against ICP-MS/MS would directly address the symposium's scope.

### D4. Frame the deliverable as "de-risking residual-element measurement for circular AM"

The abstract's provenance-tracked dataset and transferable parameter recipes should be positioned as infrastructure for the circular-economy transition in aluminum AM. The literature notes that foundries typically restrict post-consumer scrap content to <20% in critical applications precisely because of compositional uncertainty (pereira2025aluminumscraprecycling pages 10-12). If AI-assisted characterization optimization can reduce that uncertainty, it directly enables higher recycled content—a sustainability outcome the symposium values.

### D5. Acknowledge the committee's steel bias and bridge to it

Note that the EDS parameter-optimization framework is alloy-agnostic. A brief mention that the same approach could address Cu/Sn/Ni quantification in scrap-based steels—where Cu becomes problematic at 0.1 wt% and Sn at 0.04 wt% (raabe2023thematerialsscience pages 89-91)—would signal to the ferrous-oriented organizers that the method has broader applicability.

### D6. Add one microstructure–property connection

Even a single result showing that optimized EBSD mapping reveals Fe-rich intermetallic phase fractions more accurately, or that improved EDS quantification changes the predicted solidification path in a Scheil simulation, would satisfy the committee's desire for metallurgical substance beyond pure metrology.
