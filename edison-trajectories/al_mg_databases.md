# Databases for Aluminum and Magnesium Alloy Properties and Compositions

## Overview

These resources can be broadly categorized into: (1) curated engineering handbooks and commercial databases, (2) CALPHAD-based computational thermodynamic databases, (3) general-purpose materials informatics platforms, and (4) open research datasets published alongside journal articles. ## 1. MMPDS / MDHB (Metallic Materials Properties Development and Standardization)

MMPDS (formerly MIL-HDBK-5) is the authoritative source for aerospace metallic material design allowables, managed by Battelle Memorial Institute. It covers wrought and cast aluminum alloys across the 1xxx–8xxx series and selected magnesium alloys. Properties include room-temperature and elevated-temperature tensile data (yield strength, UTS, elongation), compression, shear, bearing strength, fracture toughness, fatigue (S-N), fatigue crack growth (da/dN-ΔK), creep and creep-rupture, stress–strain curves, and elastic moduli (Young's modulus, shear modulus, Poisson's ratio) (gavert2018criticalsurveysof pages 16-18, jackson2007definitionofdesign pages 5-13). Data are exclusively experimental, derived from producer and government reports subjected to rigorous statistical analysis for A-basis and B-basis design allowables (jackson2007definitionofdesign pages 5-13). Access is proprietary via paid subscription and registration. The data format is printed/PDF looseleaf pages organized in tables, graphs, and diagrams searchable by alloy class and key chemistry; no programmatic API is available (gavert2018criticalsurveysof pages 16-18). Due to the non-machine-readable format and restrictive licensing, MMPDS has low direct suitability for training ML models, though it remains the gold standard for validated engineering properties.

## 2. ASM Alloy Center Database

The ASM Alloy Center Database, maintained by ASM International, provides comprehensive coverage of aluminum alloys (1xxx–8xxx wrought and cast series) and magnesium alloy families (AZ, AM, ZK, WE, and others). Available properties include chemical compositions, physical properties, phase diagrams, microstructures, and corrosion data (wang2022data‐drivenmaterialsinnovation pages 49-51, wang2024datadriveninnovationsin pages 64-66). ASM International also maintains the ASM Phase Diagrams database, which provides thermodynamic equilibrium data critical for alloy design (wang2022data‐drivenmaterialsinnovation pages 49-51). The data are experimental and literature-curated. Access is proprietary via institutional or individual subscription with a web-based interface that supports some data export functionality (wang2022data‐drivenmaterialsinnovation pages 49-51). ML suitability is moderate; while the breadth of data is excellent, bulk programmatic extraction is limited by licensing restrictions.

## 3. Total Materia (Key to Metals)

Total Materia is one of the largest metallic materials databases, encompassing aluminum and magnesium alloys among a broad collection of metals and alloys (wang2024datadriveninnovationsin pages 66-68). It provides mechanical properties, physical properties, chemical compositions, and cross-references to international standards. The data are experimental and sourced from standards organizations and literature. Access is proprietary via subscription, with a web portal that supports some data export. ML suitability is moderate, constrained by licensing and the lack of a bulk download or API interface.

## 4. Knovel (Elsevier)

Knovel serves as an engineering reference platform that hosts digitized versions of handbooks and technical references, including those covering aluminum and magnesium alloys. Properties available depend on the source documents but commonly include mechanical, thermal, and corrosion data (wang2022data‐drivenmaterialsinnovation pages 49-51, wang2024datadriveninnovationsin pages 64-66). Data are experimental. Access is proprietary via institutional subscription. The platform provides interactive tables and Excel export for individual datasets, but systematic bulk access is not straightforward (wang2022data‐drivenmaterialsinnovation pages 49-51). ML suitability is low to moderate.

## 5. MatNavi / NIMS (National Institute for Materials Science, Japan)

MatNavi is the database platform operated by NIMS covering metals (including aluminum and magnesium alloys), polymers, ceramics, and inorganic materials (wang2024datadriveninnovationsin pages 64-66). It provides mechanical, physical, and thermal properties for metallic materials, primarily experimental and literature-derived. Access requires free registration, making it one of the more accessible institutional databases (ramakrishna2019materialsinformatics pages 4-5, wang2020machinelearningapproaches pages 10-11). The web interface supports data browsing, and MatNavi has been cited as a general materials data resource in ML review literature (wang2020machinelearningapproaches pages 10-11). ML suitability is moderate.

## 6. Aluminum Association Data (Teal Sheets / Standards and Data)

The Aluminum Association publishes the definitive chemical composition limits for all registered wrought aluminum alloys (1xxx–8xxx series) and cast aluminum alloys, commonly known as the "Teal Sheets" (gavert2018criticalsurveysof pages 16-18). These provide official composition ranges and typical mechanical properties that serve as the basis for alloy specification worldwide. The data are standards-based and experimental in origin. Access requires purchase of the published standards. The format is printed/PDF with no programmatic access, resulting in low ML suitability without manual digitization.

## 7. Granta / ANSYS CES Selector and CES EduPack

The Granta CES Selector (now ANSYS Granta) and its educational variant CES EduPack provide a comprehensive materials property database encompassing aluminum and magnesium alloys with mechanical, thermal, electrical, fatigue, and corrosion resistance data (mangos2105aluminiumalloydesign pages 19-22). Data are experimental. Access is proprietary (commercial license) or educational (university site licenses). The software is desktop-based with limited export capability. CES EduPack has been used as a source for ML training datasets, for instance in the Mangos & Birbilis aluminum alloy ML study, which compiled 1,154 mechanical property entries partly from CES EduPack (mangos2105aluminiumalloydesign pages 3-7, mangos2105aluminiumalloydesign pages 19-22). ML suitability is moderate, limited by export restrictions.

## 8. MatWeb

MatWeb is a freely accessible online materials property database that includes datasheets for many aluminum and magnesium alloys, providing mechanical, thermal, and physical property data (mangos2105aluminiumalloydesign pages 19-22). Data are experimental, sourced from manufacturers and literature. Access is free via web, though systematic bulk download is restricted. ML suitability is moderate.

## 9. CALPHAD Thermodynamic Databases

CALPHAD-based computational thermodynamic databases represent a critical resource for alloy design, particularly for properties not readily measured experimentally such as solidification range, hot cracking susceptibility indicators, and phase stability.

**Thermo-Calc with TCAL database**: The TCAL3 aluminum thermodynamic database supports multicomponent phase equilibrium calculations, Scheil solidification simulations (providing solidification range and fraction solid vs. temperature), and kinetic modeling via TC-PRISMA for nucleation, growth, and coarsening of precipitates (cote2020ontheelemental pages 8-10, cote2020ontheelemental pages 4-6). Thermo-Calc supports programmatic access via TC-Python, enabling high-throughput computational data generation ideal for ML training.

**JMatPro**: JMatPro links CALPHAD thermodynamic calculations with physical and mechanical property predictions, calculating yield and ultimate tensile strengths, electrical and thermal conductivities, elastic moduli (Young's, shear, bulk), Poisson's ratio, density, thermal expansion, TTT and CCT diagrams, and Scheil solidification curves (chinella2011computationalthermodynamicscharacterization pages 16-19, cote2020ontheelemental pages 8-10). JMatPro includes both aluminum and magnesium alloy databases (cote2020ontheelemental pages 8-10).

**Pandat**: Pandat provides similar CALPHAD thermodynamic calculation capabilities with its PanAl and PanMg databases, supporting batch calculations (cote2020ontheelemental pages 4-6, cote2020ontheelemental pages 8-10).

All commercial CALPHAD tools require proprietary software licenses but offer batch/API modes that make them highly suitable for generating computational training data for ML models (chinella2011computationalthermodynamicscharacterization pages 16-19, cote2020ontheelemental pages 4-6).

## 10. Multi-Component Mg CALPHAD Database (Xu et al. 2016)

Xu et al. developed a 23-component CALPHAD thermodynamic database for magnesium alloys covering the system Mg–Ag–Al–Ca–Ce–Cu–Fe–Gd–K–La–Li–Mn–Na–Nd–Ni–Pr–Si–Sn–Sr–Th–Y–Zn–Zr, encompassing 145 binary and 48 ternary sub-systems (xu2016thermodynamicdatabaseof pages 13-15). The database supports equilibrium and non-equilibrium (Scheil) solidification calculations and was validated against experimental observations for commercial Mg alloy families including AZ91/AZ91D, AM50A, AM60B, AE42, AE44, AS21, and AS31 (xu2016thermodynamicdatabaseof pages 11-12). This covers the major commercial Mg alloy families (AZ, AM, AE, AS series) and, through its elemental coverage, can model ZK, WE, and Elektron-type rare-earth-containing alloys. The parameters are published in the literature and usable with CALPHAD software tools (xu2016thermodynamicdatabaseof pages 13-15).

## 11. Citrine Informatics (Citrination Platform)

Citrine Informatics operates a data platform that aggregates experimental and computational materials data, including alloy compositions and properties (ramakrishna2019materialsinformatics pages 4-5, wang2022data‐drivenmaterialsinnovation pages 49-51). The platform integrates machine-learning-based recommendation engines and has been used for materials discovery workflows (ramakrishna2019materialsinformatics pages 4-5). Citrine offers both free and commercial tiers with API access, REST interfaces, and built-in ML tooling, making it highly suitable for data-driven alloy design (ramakrishna2019materialsinformatics pages 4-5, wang2020machinelearningapproaches pages 10-11).

## 12. First-Principles Computational Databases (Materials Project, AFLOWLIB, OQMD)

The Materials Project, AFLOWLIB (approximately 1.6 million entries), and the Open Quantum Materials Database (OQMD) provide DFT-calculated properties including formation energies, thermodynamic stability, elastic constants, and electronic structure for inorganic compounds including metallic systems (ramakrishna2019materialsinformatics pages 4-5, wang2020machinelearningapproaches pages 10-11). These are open-access with REST APIs and bulk download capabilities, making them highly suitable for ML (wang2020machinelearningapproaches pages 10-11). While these databases focus on crystalline compounds rather than engineering alloy compositions with specific tempers and processing, they provide foundational thermodynamic and elastic property data that can inform alloy design workflows.

## 13. Open Research Datasets Published with Journal Articles

Several important open datasets have been published alongside peer-reviewed articles, specifically designed for ML applications:

**FatigueData-CMA2022 (Zhang et al. 2023)**: A comprehensive fatigue database for complex metallic alloys containing 272 fatigue datasets (S-N, ε-N, da/dN-ΔK) compiled from 1,249 articles. The database includes material compositions, processing conditions, testing parameters, and mechanical properties (YS, UTS, elongation, toughness) with hierarchical metadata. It is available in MAT, JSON, and Excel formats on Figshare under CC BY 4.0 license, with companion scripts on GitHub under MIT license (zhang2023fatiguedatabaseof pages 1-2, zhang2023fatiguedatabaseof pages 5-6, zhang2023fatiguedatabaseof pages 5-5, zhang2023fatiguedatabaseof pages 11-11, zhang2023fatiguedatabaseof pages 12-13). Highly suitable for ML with programmatic access via provided APIs and scripts.

**LPBF Relative Density Dataset (Barrionuevo et al. 2025)**: A curated dataset of 1,579 samples from 85 peer-reviewed articles covering laser powder bed fusion of six commercial alloys (316L, IN718, Ti6Al4V, CuCrZr, AlSi10Mg, 18Ni-300). Features include ten processing parameters (laser power, scan speed, hatch distance, layer thickness, spot size, D50, atmosphere, scanning strategy, geometry, geometric factor) with relative density as the target variable. Available as Excel on Harvard Dataverse with analysis code on GitHub (barrionuevo2025laserpowderbed pages 3-4, barrionuevo2025laserpowderbed pages 1-2, barrionuevo2025laserpowderbed pages 2-3, barrionuevo2025laserpowderbed pages 6-8). Highly suitable for ML benchmarking.

**AlSi10Mg Process-Structure-Property Dataset (Luo et al. 2024)**: A dataset covering 60 processing parameter combinations for LPBF AlSi10Mg, with eight processing features, nine microstructural features (porosity, pore morphology, grain size, cell size from XCT/EBSD/SEM), and five mechanical properties (Vickers hardness, YS, UTS, elongation, elastic modulus). Hosted on Zenodo in Excel format with raw data files and images (luo2024datasetofprocessstructureproperty pages 1-3, luo2024datasetofprocessstructureproperty pages 3-4, luo2024datasetofprocessstructureproperty pages 13-15). Highly suitable for process-structure-property ML models.

**AlloyML / DoS Dataset (Mangos & Birbilis)**: Two datasets for Al-Mg (5xxx series) alloys: a degree-of-sensitization (DoS) dataset with 516 entries and a mechanical properties dataset with 1,154 entries covering YS, UTS, and elongation. Sources include CES EduPack, MatWeb, Matminer, and open literature. Available as Excel files via Dropbox and a GitHub repository (AlloyML) (mangos2105aluminiumalloydesign pages 3-7, mangos2105aluminiumalloydesign pages 19-22). Highly suitable for ML with composition and processing features as inputs.

## 14. CINDAS

The Center for Information and Numerical Data Analysis and Synthesis (CINDAS) maintains a high-performance alloys physical properties database covering thermal, electrical, and mechanical properties at multiple temperatures (henry2015areviewof pages 6-8, wang2022data‐drivenmaterialsinnovation pages 49-51). Data are experimental and evaluated. Access is proprietary via institutional subscription. ML suitability is moderate.

## Summary Assessment for Machine Learning

For training ML models for alloy design, the most immediately useful resources are the open research datasets (FatigueData-CMA2022, Barrionuevo LPBF dataset, Luo AlSi10Mg PSP dataset, Mangos & Birbilis AlloyML datasets), which provide clean, structured, machine-readable data with explicit features and targets (zhang2023fatiguedatabaseof pages 1-2, barrionuevo2025laserpowderbed pages 3-4, luo2024datasetofprocessstructureproperty pages 1-3, mangos2105aluminiumalloydesign pages 3-7). CALPHAD tools (Thermo-Calc, JMatPro, Pandat) are invaluable for generating large computational training datasets covering solidification behavior, phase stability, and thermophysical properties that are difficult to measure experimentally at scale (chinella2011computationalthermodynamicscharacterization pages 16-19, cote2020ontheelemental pages 4-6, cote2020ontheelemental pages 8-10). The commercial databases (MMPDS, ASM, Total Materia, Granta) contain the most comprehensive experimentally validated property data but are constrained by licensing and format limitations that impede direct ML use (gavert2018criticalsurveysof pages 16-18, wang2022data‐drivenmaterialsinnovation pages 49-51). Review articles emphasize that data scarcity, heterogeneous reporting, and the need for standardized benchmarking datasets remain key challenges in ML-driven alloy design (rahman2025reviewmachinelearning pages 1-3, rahman2025reviewmachinelearning pages 10-12).
