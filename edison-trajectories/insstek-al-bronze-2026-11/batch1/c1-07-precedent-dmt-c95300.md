No. ## Finding

**No published match was verified.** Published work establishes that nickel-free Cu–Al–Fe powder can be deposited by blown-powder laser DED—including on an InssTek DMT machine—but I found no publication reporting approximately **628 MPa in XY and 598 MPa in Z** for *monolithic* UNS C95300, or another nickel-free Cu–(9–11)Al–(0.8–1.5)Fe deposit. The two values supplied in the question are an **InssTek material-database summary, not a traceable published tensile dataset**. Its machine, power, condition, yield strength, elongation, and corrosion performance therefore remain unverified. The distinctions below matter: published strengths near or above 600 MPa sometimes belong to **bronze–steel composites**, not aluminum bronze alone. ### Top 5 things the PI should know

1. **Treat 628 MPa XY/598 MPa Z as an unpublished vendor lead**, not a citable published C95300 result; yield, ductility, processing history and corrosion remain unknown. (makarenko2020analyticalevaluationof pages 3-4, makarenko2021physicalandmechanical pages 25-29)
2. **The nearest InssTek publication already uses nickel-free approximately Cu–10Al–1Fe powder**, but measures a bronze/316L gradient—not an isolated alloy in two tensile directions. Its 500-W *average* bronze setting is especially relevant to assessing machine feasibility; its **750-W maximum** cannot be assumed available on a 500-W MX-Lab. (makarenko2020analyticalevaluationof pages 4-8, makarenko2020analyticalevaluationof pages 3-4, makarenko2021physicalandmechanical pages 10-15)
3. **The most directly relevant nickel-free build precedents are Freiße’s CuAl10Fe1 tooling and Linga’s Cu–Al–Fe/H13 experiments**, neither of which reports isolated-bronze XY/Z tensile properties or marine corrosion. A near-600-MPa *steel/bronze sandwich* value is particularly easy to miscite. (freisse2016investigationsondry pages 5-7, linga2022insituxraymicrotomography pages 1-3, linga2025directedenergydeposition pages 9-11)
4. **The best ultrasonic-atomized powder-DED mechanical benchmark is nickel-bearing and β′-rich:** Müller’s >99.8%-dense material achieved 911 MPa UTS but only approximately 9% XY elongation; it supplies **no seawater assurance** for C95300 or NAB repair.
