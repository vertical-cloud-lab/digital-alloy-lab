## Blown-Powder Laser DED and Related Additive Manufacturing of Nickel-Free Aluminum Bronzes: Literature Review

### Overview

A systematic search of the published literature reveals a critical finding: **no peer-reviewed publication reports blown-powder laser DED (or InssTek DMT) of a standalone, monolithic nickel-free aluminum bronze (C95300-type: Cu–9-11Al–0.5-1.5Fe, no Ni) with tensile property data.** The InssTek material database entry for "Al-Bronze / UNS C95300" with UTS of 628 MPa (XY) and 598 MPa (Z) appears to be unpublished internal data. The closest published comparisons come from: (a) laser cladding of CuAl10Fe1 (tribology/hardness only), (b) wire-laser DED and DED-Arc of nickel-containing NAB (C95800), (c) WAAM of ERCuAl-A2/CuAl9 (nickel-free, with tensile data), and (d) LPBF of Cu-Al-Ni-Fe alloys (compressive data only).

The following sections address each question systematically, with full citations.

---

### (1) As-Deposited Microstructure

**Laser cladding of CuAl10Fe1 (nickel-free):** Freiße et al. (2016) laser-cladded gas-atomized CuAl10Fe1 powder (45–125 µm) onto cold-work tool steel 1.2379 at 3 kW laser power, 0.5–2 m/min speed, and 20 g/min feed rate. The cladding showed good bonding, no cracks, and pores ≤50 µm. On steel substrates, liquid-phase separation produced spherical Fe-rich (austenitic) particles within the Cu-rich matrix, which was depleted in Al near the interface. On aluminum-bronze substrates, the HAZ reached 352 HV0.5 while the clad was approximately 50% harder. No crystallographic phase identification (α, β′, γ₂) was performed (freisse2016investigationsondry pages 5-7, freisse2016investigationsondry pages 2-3, freisse2016investigationsondry pages 9-10, freisse2016investigationsondry pages 7-9).

**InssTek MX-1000 DED of Cu-10Al-1Fe (in FGM with AISI 316L):** Makarenko, Dubinin & Shishkovsky (2020, DOI: 10.3390/ma13245665) deposited alternating layers of aluminum bronze (Cu-10Al-1Fe) and stainless steel on an InssTek MX-1000 using a 1 kW Yb fiber laser at 500 W average power, 0.85 m/min scan speed, 3.5 g/min powder feed. The aluminum-bronze system was described as containing an α copper-rich solid solution; above 9% Al, an eutectoid α + γ′ (Cu₃₂Al₉) structure forms. Iron additions refined grains and improved mechanical/antifriction properties. Sharp-transition FGMs cracked at the steel/bronze boundary, while alternating multilayers avoided cracking by redistributing stresses (makarenko2020analyticalevaluationof pages 3-4, makarenko2020directenergydeposition pages 7-9, makarenko2020analyticalevaluationof pages 4-8).

**Wire-laser DED of C95800 NAB (nickel-containing, closest blown-laser analog):** Doyle (2022 thesis) used a MELTIO M450 system (six 200 W diode lasers, 976 nm, 0.9 mm spot) with C95800 NAB wire (Cu-8.64Al-4.21Ni-3.19Fe-1.32Mn). The as-printed microstructure consisted of copper-rich α phase with martensitic β regions; κ precipitates were detected by XRD but not resolved metallographically at the magnifications used. After annealing at 675 °C for 6 h, the β′ phase intensity increased and κ precipitation became more evident. EDS showed that β regions were enriched in Fe and Ni relative to α (doyle2022additivemanufacturingof pages 47-53, doyleUnknownyearadditivemanufacturingof pages 47-53, doyleUnknownyearadditivemanufacturingof pages 53-58).

**DED-Arc NAB:** The Kuprienko thesis (2025) provides a comprehensive framework: at moderate cooling rates (100–1000 °C/s, typical of DED-Arc), the microstructure is fine Widmanstätten α with intergranular κII (globular Fe₃Al, 5–10 µm), κIII (lamellar NiAl), and nanoscale κIV (<10 nm Fe-rich) within α grains. κI formation (rosette-like, 20–50 µm) is suppressed. At fast cooling rates (>1000 °C/s, typical of laser DED/cladding), the microstructure is predominantly martensitic β′ with some Widmanstätten α; very fine κ precipitates persist even under extremely fast laser cooling. NAB alloys with Al >11 wt% may form deleterious γ₂ (Cu₉Al₄), but modern compositions suppress it (kuprienko2025analysisofnickel pages 55-61, kuprienko2025analysisofnickel pages 18-23).

**How microstructure changes with layer reheating and height:** Kuprienko reports that in DED-Arc NAB, strength remained fairly uniform through the build height (<3.6% variation in YS and UTS), but elongation ranged from ~34% near the base plate to ~26% at higher elevations (kuprienko2025analysisofnickel pages 45-50). Dharmendra et al. (2019) found no retained β or martensite in WAAM-NAB, with reheating during multilayer deposition not causing detrimental microstructural changes (dharmendra2019microstructuralevolutionand pages 15-16).

**Laser surface melting of near-nickel-free Al bronze:** Guo et al. (2026, DOI: 10.3390/ma19183995) studied an alloy of Cu-11.44Al-2.37Fe-0.49Ni-0.48Mn. The as-cast microstructure comprised blocky α, lath β′ martensite, and granular/petal-like κ. Laser surface melting produced a remelted layer dominated by lath β′ and granular κ, because rapid cooling retained β without full eutectoid decomposition to α + κ (guo2026enhancingcorrosionand pages 1-2, guo2026enhancingcorrosionand pages 4-6).

### (2) Tensile Properties As-Deposited and After Heat Treatment

**Wire-laser DED of C95800 NAB (Doyle, 2022):**
- As-printed: UTS 757.8 ± 5.4 MPa, YS 410.5 ± 6.3 MPa, EL 29.0 ± 1.0%, hardness 218.8 ± 4.4 HV
- Heat-treated (675 °C/6 h/FC): UTS 714.5 ± 8.3 MPa, YS 372.2 ± 6.7 MPa, EL 31.2 ± 0.25%, hardness 204.1 ± 3.1 HV
- As-cast C95800 reference: UTS 585.9 MPa, YS 290.5 MPa, EL 18.6%
- Density: 99.58–99.89% relative (doyleUnknownyearadditivemanufacturingof pages 53-58, doyle2022additivemanufacturingof pages 53-58, doyleUnknownyearadditivemanufacturingof pages 43-47)

**DED-Arc NAB (Kuprienko, 2025):** Typical ranges for GMA-P DED-Arc NAB are UTS 620–710 MPa, YS 300–390 MPa, elongation 22–37%. Hardness: 187 HV0.3 (wall) to 204 HV0.3 (block). All specimens (except one with a lack-of-fusion defect) met the MIL-E-23765-3A minimum UTS of 586 MPa (kuprienko2025analysisofnickel pages 76-81, kuprienko2025analysisofnickel pages 81-87).

**Hardness of laser-cladded CuAl10Fe1:** 186.6 ± 9.8 HV0.5 for the additively manufactured punch (freisse2016investigationsondry pages 7-9). Ali (2023) reported ~250 HV0.3 for aluminum bronze laser-cladded on steel pipe (ali2023lasercladdingand pages 89-94).

**Is InssTek's 628 MPa (XY) / 598 MPa (Z) for DED C95300 consistent with published data?**

The InssTek values of 628/598 MPa are **plausible but notably higher than what would be expected for a nickel-free Cu-10Al-1Fe alloy.** Here is the reasoning:

- WAAM of standalone nickel-free CuAl9 (ERCuAl-A2: Cu-8.9Al-0.8Fe) produced only UTS 501.5 ± 4.2 MPa, YS 207.2 ± 1.1 MPa, and EL 60.7 ± 1.8% (chang2024heterogeneousinterfacesof pages 7-9). This is substantially below 628 MPa.
- Wire-laser DED of C95800 NAB (with 4.2% Ni) achieved 757.8 MPa—well above 628 MPa—indicating that the nickel-containing alloy is considerably stronger.
- DED-Arc NAB (with ~4.5% Ni) typically reaches 620–710 MPa (kuprienko2025analysisofnickel pages 76-81).
- Cast C95300 (ASTM B148) has minimums of only 450 MPa (as-cast) and 515 MPa (TQ50 heat-treated).

The 628 MPa value would require significant retained β′ martensite (which is hard and strong but brittle) in the nickel-free alloy. At ~10% Al without Ni, rapid laser cooling could produce substantial β′, which would elevate UTS but potentially at the expense of elongation and corrosion resistance. **The phase responsible for 628 MPa in a nickel-free Cu-10Al-1Fe DED deposit would almost certainly be retained β′ martensite**, as the α + κ equilibrium microstructure of C95300 cannot reach this strength level. However, **no published data exist to validate this specific combination of composition, process, and properties**, making it impossible to verify the InssTek claim independently. The associated yield strength would likely be in the range of 250–350 MPa, and elongation could be 10–25%, but these are estimates by analogy, not published values.

### (3) Comparison with Cast C95300 and Cast NAB C95800

**Cast C95300 (ASTM B148):**
- As-cast minimums: UTS 450 MPa, YS 170 MPa, EL 20%
- Heat-treated TQ50 (temper-quench at 885 °C, temper at 620 °C): UTS 515 MPa, YS 205 MPa, EL 20%
- Typical as-cast values are somewhat above minimums but well below 628 MPa

**Cast C95800 NAB (ASTM B148):**
- As-cast minimums: UTS 585 MPa, YS 240 MPa, EL 15%
- Measured cast values (Dharmendra et al. 2019): UTS 670 ± 8 MPa, YS 300 ± 15 MPa, EL 16 ± 2% (dharmendra2019microstructuralevolutionand pages 13-14)
- Measured cast values (Doyle 2022): UTS 585.9 MPa, YS 290.5 MPa, EL 18.6% (doyleUnknownyearadditivemanufacturingof pages 43-47)
- Measured cast values (Fayanas et al. 2025): UTS 773 ± 4 MPa, YS 439 ± 5 MPa, EL 16.7%, hardness 200 HV10 (fayanas2025nickelaluminiumbronze pages 2-5)

The InssTek DED C95300 value of 628 MPa (XY) exceeds the cast C95300 TQ50 minimum (515 MPa) by ~22% and approaches or exceeds the cast C95800 ASTM minimum (585 MPa). This is consistent with the general observation that AM processes produce finer microstructures and higher strength than casting, but the magnitude of improvement for a nickel-free alloy is unusually large relative to its cast baseline, reinforcing the suspicion that significant β′ martensite is present.

### Top 5 Things the PI Should Know

**1. The InssTek C95300 DED database entry (628/598 MPa) is unpublished and cannot be independently verified.** No peer-reviewed paper reports blown-powder DED of standalone nickel-free aluminum bronze with tensile data. The claimed UTS far exceeds cast C95300 (450–515 MPa) and the only published WAAM nickel-free CuAl9 value (501.5 MPa), strongly suggesting the deposit contains substantial retained β′ martensite. This phase is hard but may be detrimental to ductility and seawater corrosion resistance.

**2. The only standalone tensile data for WAAM nickel-free aluminum bronze (CuAl9/ERCuAl-A2) comes from Chang et al. (2024): UTS ~502 MPa, YS ~207 MPa, EL ~61%.** These are far below the InssTek values and below cast NAB C95800 minimums (585 MPa). The high elongation suggests a predominantly α microstructure with minimal β′, consistent with the lower (~9%) Al content and moderate WAAM cooling rates.

**3. Laser-rate cooling (>1000 °C/s) of Cu-9-11Al alloys reliably produces retained β′ martensite** (kuprienko2025analysisofnickel pages 50-55, kuprienko2025analysisofnickel pages 55-61, guo2026enhancingcorrosionand pages 4-6). For nickel-free alloys at ~10% Al, this β′ could explain 628 MPa UTS, but the elongation and corrosion resistance of such a microstructure are unknown in the DED context. The PI's goal of seawater corrosion resistance "as deposited" may conflict with a heavily martensitic microstructure, since β′ in aluminum bronzes is known to be susceptible to selective corrosion.

**4. The Makarenko group's InssTek MX-1000 work (Cu-10Al-1Fe + SS316L FGM) is the only published DED study using an InssTek machine with aluminum bronze** (makarenko2020analyticalevaluationof pages 3-4, makarenko2020directenergydeposition pages 7-9). It provides DED parameters (500 W, 0.85 m/min, 3.5 g/min) relevant to the PI's planned InssTek MX-Lab work, but reports no standalone bronze mechanical or corrosion data—only FGM hardness and cracking behavior.

**5. There is a major gap: no published study reports tensile properties, corrosion behavior, or phase quantification for blown-powder laser DED of any nickel-free aluminum bronze (C95300, C95200, C95400, C61400, CuAl10Fe1, or CuAl10Fe3).** All published laser DED tensile data for Cu-Al bronzes are for nickel-containing NAB (C95800). The PI's proposed work would be the first to fill this gap and would have high novelty. The PI should plan to characterize phase fractions (especially β′ vs. α) as a function of laser parameters and layer reheating, since these will determine whether 628 MPa-class strength can coexist with adequate ductility and seawater corrosion resistance.
