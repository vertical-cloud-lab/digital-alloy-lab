# Published copper-alloy work: InssTek DMT and relevant comparators

**Scope and evidentiary limit.** I found published copper-bearing builds on an **InssTek MX-1000** and an **MX-Lab L5**, plus a published account of copper-containing rocket hardware made by InssTek. None of those sources demonstrates the proposed combination of a *single pre-alloyed copper powder*, self-supporting and cast-NAB/Cu–Ni repair deposits, and **as-deposited** corrosion performance at least equal to wrought C71500. Where a paper did not report a requested quantity—or its full text could not be obtained—I do not infer it from another machine or alloy. (makarenko2020directenergydeposition pages 3-7, makarenko2021physicalandmechanical pages 10-15, rubioejchel2026laserpowderdirected pages 11-12, lim2025casestudyof pages 5-5, song2024microstructuresandcorrosion pages 1-2)

DOI: 10.1088/1757-899X/969/1/012104.** **[CITE] [NUMBERS] [INSSTEK]** Process: blown-powder laser DED/InssTek DMT, **MX-1000**, of approximately Cu–10Al aluminum bronze alternating with AISI 316L; **not** nickel aluminum bronze or a C95300 tensile test. A sharp bronze/steel transition cracked; an alternating multilayer transition did not show cracks optically, although SEM identified small pores. ## Requested attribution and qualification decisions

- **UNS C95300 database figures:** I found **no independently published source** verifying the supplied InssTek database’s **~83.8-µm mean powder size or 628 MPa XY / 598 MPa Z UTS**. Treat them as **unpublished vendor-reported values**. Neither a C95300 build machine/power nor yield, elongation, density, heat-treatment state, or corrosion performance follows from those three numbers. The published MX-1000 **Cu–Al–Fe/316L** results are different materials and different tensile specimens. **[GAP]** (makarenko2020directenergydeposition pages 3-7, makarenko2021physicalandmechanical pages 10-15, makarenko2021physicalandmechanical pages 25-29)
- **Shim/Yao equipment:** **Unknown on the accessible primary evidence.** The three specified DOIs were identified, but their methods were not available to verify whether they used any named InssTek machine or what laser power was used. Do not attribute the 500-W MX-Lab or 1-kW MX-1000 to that group without its methods sections. **[GAP]** (makarenko2020analyticalevaluationof pages 3-4, rubioejchel2026laserpowderdirected pages 11-12)
- **Marine acceptance:** I found **no published demonstration** that an InssTek **copper-alloy DED process or propeller/valve** has Korean Register, DNV, ABS, Lloyd’s Register, or Bureau Veritas **process/part approval**, or documented adoption by the named Korean shipbuilders, navy, or KRISO. A vendor material listing and an application label are **not certification**. The published InssTek industrial copper-hardware case located here concerns **rocket propulsion**, while the used-propeller coupon experiment employed a **Trumpf** laser. This is a finding about the **located public evidence**, not proof that private qualifications or deployments do not exist. **[GAP]** (lim2025casestudyof pages 5-5, lim2025casestudyof media e43775c4, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 2-4, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 4-7)

## Top 5 things the PI should know

1. **The nearest demonstrated InssTek power envelope is not the proposed result:** MX-1000 bronze/steel used **500 W average, 750 W maximum for bronze**, whereas published MX-Lab GRCop used **250 W through a 0.4-mm spot**. Neither demonstrates as-deposited seawater-resistant, self-supporting NAB-family powder on the **500-W-maximum MX-Lab**. **[INSSTEK] [GAP]** (makarenko2020directenergydeposition pages 3-7, rubioejchel2026laserpowderdirected pages 11-12)
