# Marine bronze repair: documented evidence and qualification gaps

**Scope.** The strongest directly relevant laser experiment used **Cu–38Ni powder on a coupon cut from a service-worn NAB propeller**. Its short electrochemical test favored the coating, but measured the *machined coating surface*, not corrosion or fatigue across the fusion line. Other studies demonstrate processes or quantify the hazards of welded interfaces; they do not establish fleet-qualified, as-deposited repairs. All compositions below are wt%; none of the cited research is authored by Sterling G. Baird. (iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 2-4, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 13-14, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 1-6)

## Itemized findings

**1. European propeller-coupon reconditioning with Cu–Ni. [CITE] [NUMBERS] [GAP]** Iatan, G.; Cuculea, D.; Ardelean, G.; Stanciu, E.; Pascu, A. “Optimization of Pulsed Laser Cladding for Reconditioning of Ni–Al–Bronze (NAB) Marine Propeller.” *Materials* **18** (2025), 4301. DOI: **10.3390/ma18184301**. This was coaxial blown-powder *pulsed* Nd:YAG laser cladding, **not InssTek DMT**, onto ClassNK KAℓBC3 propeller material (Cu 80.80, Ni 6.00, Al 5.49, Fe 4.15, Mn 3.55); Metco 57NS powder was Cu 62/Ni 38, **45–75 µm**. For the four multipass samples, the reported peak-pulse-power settings were **3,200–3,800 W**, pulse energies **9.6–11.2 J**, duration **3 ms**, frequency **42 Hz**, preheat **165 °C**, powder feed **3.75 g/min**, traverse approximately **16–18 cm/min**, and overlap **40%**. These peak-power settings must not be equated with continuous-wave power or assumed transferable to a 500-W machine. As-clad cross-sections showed no visible cracks; sample 1 contained three isolated small pores. Approximately **48% dilution** was reported for the highest-energy sample. No post-clad heat treatment was reported. (iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 2-4, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 4-7, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 9-13, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 7-9)

On a polished top surface after removing approximately one-third of the coating, three replicate linear-polarization measurements in **3.5% NaCl** used **1 cm²** exposure and **1 mV/s** scan. The substrate and samples 1–4 respectively gave *E*<sub>corr</sub> **−0.380, −0.235, −0.211, −0.222, −0.213 V versus SCE**; measured *I*<sub>corr</sub> **18.141, 9.442, 7.423, 5.117, 3.383 µA per 1 cm²**; and extrapolated corrosion rates **0.58 ± 0.03, 0.30 ± 0.04, 0.24 ± 0.03, 0.16 ± 0.03, 0.10 ± 0.04 mm/year**. Sample 3 reached **304 HV0.2**; sample 4, **263 HV0.2**. The fusion region contained Al/Fe-rich precipitates and a κ-bearing HAZ, but **no fusion-line-selective corrosion depth, natural-seawater exposure, fatigue result, or repaired full propeller** was reported. These instantaneous electrochemical rate estimates are not service-life measurements. (iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 13-14, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed media aa4b10e9, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed media 4f7924af, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 14-16)

**2. German industrial propeller-swarf route: matching, nickel-bearing NAB powder. [CITE] [NUMBERS] [GAP]** Müller, V.; Fasselt, J. M.; Kruse, T.; Klötzer, C.; Kleba-Ehrhardt, R.; Choma, T.; Biegler, M.; Rethmeier, M. “Using ultrasonic atomization to recycle aluminium bronze chips for additive laser directed energy deposition.” *IOP Conference Series: Materials Science and Engineering* **1296** (2023), 012036. DOI: **10.1088/1757-899X/1296/1/012036**. Fraunhofer IPK and propeller manufacturer Mecklenburger Metallguss used **CuAl10Ni5Fe5** grinding swarf from cast propellers. An **AMAZEMET rePOWDER**, **1,300 °C**, **40 kHz**, argon atomization yielded **77%** powder: *d*10/*d*50/*d*90 **45/96/179 µm**, oxygen **0.0125 wt%**, Hausner ratio **1.05**; carbon-sonotrode fibres were a possible contamination source. As-deposited blown-powder laser DED on **1.4404 steel**, not a repaired NAB propeller, used **1,300 W**, **1-mm spot**, **750 mm/min**, **11 g/min**, and produced **>99.8% density**, **266 HV**, in-plane yield strength **646 MPa**, UTS **911 MPa**, and elongation approximately **9%**. The authors associated high hardness/low ductility with a β′-like needle structure and judged the as-deposited tensile combination unsuitable for end-use propellers. There was **no post-build treatment, seawater test, or NAB fusion-line corrosion measurement**. This is unusually pertinent powder-production precedent, but neither a nickel-free filler nor proof of a 500-W repair process. (muller2023usingultrasonicatomization pages 1-3, muller2023usingultrasonicatomization pages 3-6, muller2023usingultrasonicatomization pages 6-8, muller2023usingultrasonicatomization pages 8-12)

**3. U.S. Navy-supported wire-laser NAB build—not powder repair. [CITE] [NUMBERS] [NAVY] [GAP]** Doyle, R. P. *Additive Manufacturing of FeCrCoCuNi High Entropy Alloy via Laser Powder Bed Fusion and Nickel Aluminum Bronze via Laser Directed Energy Deposition* (2022 thesis; **no DOI established**). A wire-fed laser DED head with six **200-W**, **976-nm** diodes and **0.9-mm** spot deposited **0.89-mm C95800 wire** (Cu 82.0, Al 8.64, Ni 4.21, Fe 3.19, Mn 1.32) on steel under argon. Four build settings (**800 W/300**, **1,000 W/600**, **1,200 W/600**, **1,200 W/900 mm/min**) yielded **99.84%, 99.58%, 99.89%, 99.61%** relative densities. As printed: yield **410.5 ± 6.3 MPa**, UTS **757.8 ± 5.4 MPa**, elongation **29.0 ± 1.0%**, hardness **218.8 ± 4.4 HV**. After **675 °C/6 h, furnace cooling**: **372.2 ± 6.7 MPa**, **714.5 ± 8.3 MPa**, **31.2 ± 0.25%**, **204.1 ± 3.1 HV**, respectively. The thesis acknowledges a Navy grant and calls for corrosion testing; it establishes neither a NAVSEA-approved as-deposited repair nor fusion-line seawater performance. (doyle2022additivemanufacturingofa pages 47-53, doyle2022additivemanufacturingofa pages 53-58)

**4. WAAM matching-NAB corrosion versus post-build annealing. [CITE] [NUMBERS] [GAP]** Shahriari, A.; Chalasani, D.; Amirkhiz, B. S.; Mohammadi, M. “Corrosion performance of wire arc additively manufactured NAB alloy.” *npj Materials Degradation* **7** (2023). DOI: **10.1038/s41529-023-00405-x**. GMAW/WAAM used **AWS A5.7 ERCuNiAl** wire on **316L steel** and compared a cast NAB saltwater impeller; exact wire wt% was not recoverable from the accessible text. In naturally aerated **3.5% NaCl, 23 ± 2 °C**, the as-built WAAM specimen showed the best overall electrochemical resistance and a broad approximately **−0.233 to +1.1 V versus Ag/AgCl** pseudopassive interval. Treatments **350 °C/2 h**, **550 °C/4 h**, and **675 °C/6 h** gave reported approximate *i*<sub>corr</sub> of **4.4, 6.6, 8.0 µA/cm²**, respectively; the higher-temperature treatments were worse than as-built. Attack concentrated in interdendritic α near cathodic κ phases, with approximately **3-µm** maximum measured surface depression. **As-built/cast immersion was 96 h; treated specimens were immersed 24 h**—an important comparison caveat. This was not a corrosion test across an NAB-on-NAB repair fusion line. (shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 12-14, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 10-12, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 10-10, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 2-2, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 14-14)

**5. Chinese Ti-modified NAB WAAM: as-deposited design comparator. [CITE] [NUMBERS] [GAP]** Zhang, L.-M.; Cai, X.-Y.; Song, B.-L.; Dong, B.-L.; Lin, S.-W.; Khoshnaw, F. “Effect of low-level Ti microalloying on the microstructure, mechanical properties, and corrosion behaviour of WAAM-processed nickel-aluminium bronze.” *Advanced Materials Joining* **1** (2026), article 17. DOI: **10.1007/s44500-026-00016-x**. CMT wire-arc DED onto NAB plate used NAB wire reported as Al **8.73**, Ni **4.28**, Fe **3.22**, Mn **1.44**, Cu balance; the text anomalously prints “Mg: Bal.” rather than Cu balance. Ti nanoparticles were introduced between layers at **0, 0.03, 0.05, 0.1%**—**not single-stream pre-alloyed powder**. Initial/subsequent arc settings were **137 A/11.6 V/5 m/min wire** and **126 A/11.5 V/4.6 m/min wire**, at **6 mm/s** travel. In the as-deposited state, longitudinal UTS/yield/elongation changed from **664.2/334.9 MPa/28.6%** without Ti to **718.2/372.5 MPa/28.0%** at 0.1% Ti. Static **3.5% NaCl, 7–21 days** gave 0.1%-Ti mass-loss rate **0.167 g m⁻² day⁻¹**; polarization *i*<sub>corr</sub> changed from **10.06 to 8.49 µA/cm²** and *E*<sub>corr</sub> from **−192.91 to −188.57 mV versus SCE**. Neither natural seawater nor an excavated repair fusion line was tested. (zhang2026effectoflowlevel pages 2-4, zhang2026effectoflowlevel pages 8-11, zhang2026effectoflowlevel pages 11-13, zhang2026effectoflowlevel pages 13-16)

**6. British marine-weld fusion-zone and fatigue warning. [CITE] [NUMBERS] [GAP]** Dobson, T. H. E.; Yunnie, A.; Kaloudis, D.; Larrosa, N. O.; Coules, H. E. “Biofouling and corrosion rate of welded nickel aluminium bronze in natural and simulated seawater.” *Biofouling* **40** (2024), 193–208. DOI: **10.1080/08927014.2024.2326067**. Dobson, T. H. E.; Larrosa, N. O.; Reid, M.; Rajamudili, K.; Ganguly, S.; Coules, H. E. “Corrosion mechanisms of plasma welded nickel aluminium bronze immersed in seawater.” *Corrosion Science* (2024). DOI: **10.1016/j.corsci.2024.112004**. Dobson, T. H. E.; Wilson, P.; Larrosa, N. O.; Williams, M.; Coules, H. E. “Natural seawater impact on crack propagation and fatigue behavior of welded nickel aluminum bronze.” *Fatigue & Fracture of Engineering Materials & Structures* **47** (2024), 4678–4695. DOI: **10.1111/ffe.14456**. Detailed methods and results of this related research are reproduced in Dobson’s University of Bristol **2024 PhD thesis**, *Corrosion Mechanisms and Fatigue Behaviour of Marine Biofouled Plasma Welded Nickel Aluminium Bronze* (**thesis DOI unavailable**). These are **plasma weld overlays, not laser DED**. Cast NAB was approximately Cu balance, Al **9.14**, Fe **4.38**, Ni **4.84**, Mn **0.09**; filler was matching **SIFMIG 44/Cu6328/ERCuNiAl**, preheated **250 °C**. A separate thesis specimen reports Cu **81.66**, Al **9.89**, Fe **3.75**, Ni **4.71**; do not conflate the two reported parent analyses. No post-weld heat treatment was reported for the immersion coupons. The HAZ measured **0.96 ± 0.09 mm**, with retained β′. (dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 92-95, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 130-135, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 81-86, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 1-6)

Coupons hung **1 m deep** in Plymouth natural seawater for **12 or 18 months**, with simulated-seawater and air controls. Mean general corrosion estimates were **1.27 mm/year** over the initial two months and **0.11 mm/year** over twelve months in natural water, versus **0.06 mm/year** in simulated water; these are whole-coupon estimates, **not measured fusion-line penetration rates**. Weld-toe corrosion pits included a measured **0.394-mm** deep pit after twelve months; selective attack involved **β′ in the HAZ** and **κIII immediately outside it**. Subsequently tested **in air** at stress ratio **0.1** and **10–20 Hz**, natural-seawater-exposed welds had a reported fatigue-life knock-down factor **11**; pit depth **≥0.2 mm** distinguished deeper-damage specimens. An interrupted test at **220 MPa, 20 Hz** followed κIII-linked corrosion cracks. Thus this is compelling *weld-toe/HAZ* evidence, but not an in-situ flowing-seawater fatigue test of a laser-DED fusion line. (dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 107-114, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 119-122, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 130-135, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 194-203, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 186-190, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 203-208)

## Heat-treatment rules and actual deployment

- **Do not merge casting specifications, Navy mechanical requirements, and weld-repair approval. [CITE] [NAVY]** Doyle quotes **ASTM B505** as requiring *C95800 castings* to be annealed at **675 ± 10 °C for at least 6 h**; the experiment then furnace-cooled. Its separate NAVSEA citation supports minimum **cast-coupon** yield approximately **241 MPa**, UTS **586–587 MPa**, and elongation **15%**—it does **not** demonstrate that NAVSEA itself prescribed that anneal for every NAB weld or exempted as-deposited DED repairs. An applicable NAVSEA welding procedure and its approved local-versus-whole-propeller tempering requirements were not retrieved; the proposed “NAVSEA 675 °C stress relief/temper anneal” must therefore be checked against the **specific current NAVSEA material, weld-repair and drawing requirements**, not represented here as a verified universal rule. (doyle2022additivemanufacturingofa pages 53-58)
- **UK naval casting/revalidation is a different rule set. [CITE] [NAVY]** Dobson cites **Defence Standard 02-747 Part 2** for NAB casting treatment **675 ± 15 °C, at least 6 h, followed by cooling in still air**, and **Def Stan 02-872 Parts 2 and 3** for validation/revalidation of seawater-wetted naval castings and weld repairs. Its account says critical remaining wall thickness must exceed **90% of finished drawing thickness** for straightforward revalidation. This is not evidence that a new laser repair may omit a prescribed post-weld temper anneal. These standards have **no DOI identified** and are cited here through the thesis, not represented as independently inspected original rules. (dobson2024corrosionmechanismsanda pages 35-39, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 119-122)
- **Classification societies and national navies. [GAP]** The propeller-coupon study identifies **ClassNK Part K, Chapter 7** and material grade KAℓBC3; it does not supply a ClassNK-approved laser repair procedure or its zone-specific allowable excavation, filler, inspection or heat-treatment clauses. Original ABS/DNV/LR/ClassNK propeller-repair approvals and welding procedure qualifications were not obtained. Neither these experiments nor a vendor material listing establishes production approval or fleet use in the **Korean or Japanese navies, Chinese navy, European navies, or U.S. Navy/NAVSEA**. The German swarf study documents real propeller-*manufacturing* waste and laboratory DED, not an in-service propeller repair. DOI: **not applicable/verified for the class and Navy rules**. (iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 2-4, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 16-16, muller2023usingultrasonicatomization pages 1-3)
- **InssTek's C95300-like entry. [INSSTEK] [GAP]** The user-provided database summary describes an InssTek DMT-qualified **nickel-free Cu–9–11Al–0.8–1.5Fe powder/material** and XY/Z UTS **628/598 MPa**; it gives **no DOI or published test conditions** for repair, corrosion, heat treatment, density or fusion-line fatigue. These vendor figures are **not** an independently verified study of depositing C95300 on C95800. Likewise, the cited Korean-affiliated CuNi2SiCr-on-NAB and in-situ Cu–Al/NAB publications could not be examined in full here; their titles alone cannot establish exact InssTek machine use, single-stream powder chemistry, as-deposited corrosion performance or fusion-line safety. **No accessible primary result demonstrates C95300-like or ERCuAl-A2 nickel-free filler repairing an NAB propeller with measured seawater corrosion at its fusion line.** The consequences therefore remain **unknown**, not proven beneficial or harmful. (chang2024heterogeneousinterfacesof pages 3-5, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 13-14)

## Top 5 things the PI should know

1. **Prioritize fusion-line and weld-toe qualification.** The actual Cu–38Ni/NAB laser-clad coupon improved short-term polished-*surface* electrochemistry, but did not test the interface; matching-NAB plasma welds developed β′/κIII-selective attack, **0.394-mm** weld-toe pits and an **11-fold** fatigue-life knock-down after natural seawater. (iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 13-14, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 130-135, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 194-203, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 1-6)
2. **The closest powder-production precedent is already strikingly relevant, but exposes the phase problem:** 40-kHz rePOWDER NAB swarf made **>99.8%-dense** laser DED material, yet **~9% elongation** and β′-like microstructure without demonstrated seawater performance. The reported process used **1,300 W**, not 500 W. (muller2023usingultrasonicatomization pages 3-6, muller2023usingultrasonicatomization pages 8-12)
3. **Do not claim nickel-free NAB repair is an established precedent.** ERCuAl-A2 CuAl9 WAAM and nickel-free CuAl10(+Cr) laser cladding were demonstrated on **steel/dissimilar builds**, not seawater-tested NAB propeller repairs. Even nickel-free powder can yield a **nickel-containing diluted fusion zone** when deposited on NAB. (chang2024heterogeneousinterfacesof pages 3-5, yang2020effectofcr pages 2-3, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 13-14)
4. **Separate the as-deposited research objective from certification.** ASTM B505’s quoted **675 ± 10 °C/≥6 h** is for C95800 castings; UK naval casting treatment differs slightly, and no original NAVSEA/class repair waiver was established. WAAM's **550–675 °C** treatments worsened measured short-term chloride response in one study, which is a research rationale for testing as deposited—not a waiver of applicable repair rules. (doyle2022additivemanufacturingofa pages 53-58, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsanda pages 35-39, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 10-12)
5. **Use two environmental tiers and an honest benchmark.** Measure deposit, HAZ, fusion line and substrate separately in controlled **3.5% NaCl**, then in flowing/biologically active natural seawater with pit-depth and fatigue assessment; matched wrought **C71500** controls are still needed. No retrieved study establishes an **as-deposited, single-stream, ≤500-W** NAB/Cu–Ni repair with seawater resistance at least that of wrought 70–30 Cu–Ni. (iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 7-9, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 119-122, dobson2024corrosionmechanismsand pages 203-208)
