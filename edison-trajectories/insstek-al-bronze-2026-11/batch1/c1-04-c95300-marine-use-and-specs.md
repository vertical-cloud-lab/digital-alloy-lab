# Nickel-free C95300 for seawater-wetted parts and propellers

**Conclusion.** Cast UNS C95300 is a nickel-free Cu–Al–Fe aluminum bronze that has been tested in chloride solution; that is not evidence that it is a class-approved propeller alloy or that its **as-deposited** corrosion resistance equals wrought C71500. The retrieved class-propeller composition ranges exclude Cu–10Al–1Fe, and I found no documented installation of a C95300 ship propeller. Nickel-free aluminum bronze can be a candidate for seawater-wetted components, but its suitability depends on microstructure, flow and selective attack; the evidence does not establish a general equivalence to NAB or 70–30 Cu–Ni. (klassert2007copperandcopper–nickel pages 4-6, metin2022%35 pages 1-3, metin2022%35 pages 3-5, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 48-55, laque1969deteriorationofmetals pages 13-16)

## 1. Propeller rules: what is actually accepted?

- **ISO 484 versus material approval — [CITE] [GAP].** ISO 484 is invoked for **propeller dimensional/tolerance classes**; material chemistry, mechanical requirements and certification are separate class-rule matters. ISO 484 compliance does not itself approve C95300. I did not retrieve authoritative ISO 484, current class-rule, ASTM B148 or MIL-B-24480 text, so these should not be presented as verified clauses of those documents. *Saeed, M.,* *Investigations on Inner Structures of the Propeller Blade Referring Additive Manufacturing* (2025 thesis; DOI not identified). (saeed2025investigationsoninner pages 26-30)

- **Germanischer Lloyd propeller groups — [CITE] [NUMBERS].** Klassert, A., and Tikana, L., *Copper and Copper–Nickel Alloys—An Overview* (2007; DOI not identified), reproduces class propeller-alloy ranges: **CU1**, zinc-rich manganese bronze, Zn 35–40, Al 0.5–3, Fe 0.5–2.5, Ni ≤1; **CU2**, nickel–manganese bronze, Zn 33–38, Al 0.5–2, Fe 0.5–2.5, Ni 2.5–8; **CU3**, nickel–aluminum bronze, Al 7–11, Fe 2–6, Ni **3–6**, Mn 0.5–4, exemplified by CuAl10Ni5Fe5-C/CC333G; **CU4**, manganese–aluminum bronze, Al 6.5–9, Fe 2–5, Ni **1.5–3**, Mn 8–20, exemplified by CuMn11Al8Fe3Ni3-C/CC212E. Copper is the balance. These are *this source’s class ranges*, not verified universal requirements of every society. C95300’s approximately 10 Al/1 Fe/essentially zero Ni meets **none** of the four listed chemistries: CU1/CU2 require substantial Zn; CU3/CU4 require Ni and more Fe. (klassert2007copperandcopper–nickel pages 4-6, metin2022%35 pages 3-5)

- **Propeller mechanical context — [CITE] [NUMBERS].** Ni, Z., *Design of a Blade-Hub Joint for a Built-Up Propeller* (2022 master’s thesis; DOI not identified), attributes to DNV minimum yield/UTS of **245/590 MPa for CU3** and **275/630 MPa for CU4**. These are not C95300 specifications. I found no verified rule routinely accepting nickel-free Cu–10Al–1Fe for a certified propeller; an individually approved alternative cannot be ruled out. ASTM B148 identifies cast-alloy grades, rather than demonstrating class propeller acceptance; the asserted MIL-B-24480/C95800 linkage could not be independently checked in the retrieved primary standard. (ni2022masterthesisdesigna pages 28-32, klassert2007copperandcopper–nickel pages 4-6)

## 3. What the seawater and deposition experiments actually show

1. **C95300 against another nickel-free cast bronze — [CITE] [NUMBERS].** Metin *et al.* (2022), **DOI: 10.35193/bseufbd.1134737**: induction melted and **cast as-cast**, with measured **C95300 Cu balance/Al 10.105/Fe 0.983/Ni 0.003** and **C95200 Cu balance/Al 8.678/Fe 2.510/Ni 0.006**. In **static 3.5 wt% NaCl**, 72-h repeatedly removed/cleaned immersion specimens and polarization at **1 mV/s, Eoc ±0.25 V, saturated calomel reference, n=3** found C95300’s 72-h mass loss and corrosion rate **higher** than C95200’s (see cropped Fig. 5); Ecorr was **−0.118 versus −0.276 V_SCE**, respectively. The article prints its purported *icorr* values **23.3 versus 8.39 with the impossible unit “V”**: retain the directional result but do not treat those figures as validated current densities. C95300 had coarser grains and more β-region material; no measured phase fractions, selective-attack depths or natural-seawater exposures were reported. Because **Al and Fe both differ**, this is not a controlled nickel-effect experiment. (metin2022%35 pages 1-3, metin2022%35 pages 3-5, metin2022%35 pages 5-8, metin2022%35 media 6aa2a225)

2. **Closest same-exposure cross-family benchmark, with a crucial grade caveat — [CITE] [NUMBERS].** Lewis, R. O., and LaQue, F., *Evaluation of Copper-Nickel Alloys for OTEC Applications* (1981), **DOI: 10.2172/6255887**, reproduces **INCO unpublished** one-year **0.6 m/s flowing-seawater** data for uncoupled samples: **C70600 30.5, C71500 20.3, “Al Bronze” 43.2, and “Ni Al Bronze” 150 µm/year**. The two bronzes’ **grades, compositions, processing conditions and specimen histories are unstated**; neither value is a measured C95300 or C95800 rate. Here C71500 was lowest, but the reported NAB value shows why an unqualified universal assertion that NAB always corrodes more slowly than nickel-free bronze—or vice versa—is unsupportable. The report concerns OTEC; the table does not establish a Navy-funded trial. At different seawater velocities or galvanic area ratios, performance may change. (lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 48-55, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 19-21)

3. **Cast NAB under flow, erosion and cavitation — [CITE] [NUMBERS] [NAVY].** Barik, R. C., Wharton, J. A., Wood, R. J. K., Tan, K. S., and Stokes, K. R., “Erosion and Erosion–Corrosion Performance of Cast and Thermally Sprayed Nickel–Aluminium Bronze,” *Wear* **259** (2005), 230–242, **DOI: 10.1016/j.wear.2005.02.033**, tested **cast NAB** approximately **Cu balance/Al 9/Ni 5/Fe 4.5/Mn 1.10** against **HVOF-sprayed** coatings, **not C95300 or Cu–Ni**. Slurry tests used **3.5% NaCl + 3 wt% silica**, mean sand sizes **135 or 235 µm**, **3.1–6.0 m/s**, normal impact, **5 h**; cast-NAB hardness was approximately **235 HV**. The authors report flow-corrosion rates **0.5–0.6 mm/year at 3–5 m/s**, rising at higher velocities, and better cast-NAB erosion performance than sprayed NAB under their conditions. These flow-dependent losses are not quiet-seawater penetration rates or a direct C95300 cavitation ranking. The paper acknowledges UK **Defence Science and Technology Laboratory** support; [NAVY] here denotes defense-supported marine relevance, **not U.S.-Navy approval**. LaQue (1969), **DOI: 10.1016/0029-8018(69)90031-6**, reports that approximately **9–10 Al/5 Ni** NAB in propeller-simulation testing had about **fivefold** the erosion resistance of manganese bronze; that comparison is **not** C95300 versus NAB. (barik2005erosionanderosion–corrosion pages 3-6, barik2005erosionanderosion–corrosion pages 7-8, barik2005erosionanderosion–corrosion pages 2-3, barik2005erosionanderosion–corrosion pages 12-13, laque1969deteriorationofmetals pages 13-16)

4. **Additive NAB is microstructure-dependent, not a proxy for DMT C95300 — [CITE] [NUMBERS] [GAP].** Shahriari, A., Chalasani, D., Amirkhiz, B. S., and Mohammadi, M., “Corrosion Performance of Wire Arc Additively Manufactured NAB Alloy,” *npj Materials Degradation* **7** (2023), **DOI: 10.1038/s41529-023-00405-x**: **GMAW-WAAM** nickel-containing NAB from **ERCuNiAl wire**, compared with as-cast NAB; exact measured bulk wt% was not recovered. Naturally aerated **3.5 wt% NaCl, 23 ± 2 °C**, Ag/AgCl reference, **1800-s OCP stabilization**, EIS/CPP at **0.166 mV/s**; as-built and cast samples were immersed **96 h**, heat-treated specimens **24 h**. As-built WAAM had the **lowest *icorr*** despite a less-noble potential of approximately **−0.293 V_Ag/AgCl**, versus approximately **−0.222 V** for the others. WAAM heat treatments **350 °C/2 h**, **550 °C/4 h** and **675 °C/6 h** yielded approximately **4.4, 6.6 and 8.0 × 10⁻⁶ A/cm²**, respectively. The corrosion explanation involves α versus cathodic κ intermetallics; it does **not** imply that removing Ni, or heat treating C95300, would have the same effect. Unequal immersion times also limit simplistic rate comparisons. (shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 1-2, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 2-2, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 14-14, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 12-14)

5. **Laser repair of an actual NAB propeller, but with Ni-rich powder — [CITE] [NUMBERS] [NAVY] [GAP].** Iatan, G., Cuculea, D., Ardelean, G., Stanciu, E., and Pascu, A., “Optimization of Pulsed Laser Cladding for Reconditioning of Ni–Al–Bronze (NAB) Marine Propeller,” *Materials* **18** (2025), 4301, **DOI: 10.3390/ma18184301**: **pulsed coaxial-powder laser cladding**, not InssTek DMT, of **Cu 62/Ni 38 powder**, **45–75 µm, 3.75 g/min**, onto a worn propeller substrate measured **Cu 80.80/Ni 6.00/Al 5.49/Fe 4.15/Mn 3.55**. The authors used a TruPulse 556, reported **16–18 cm/min** scanning, **3-ms/42-Hz pulses**, **165 °C** preheat for overlapped tracks, an optimized **10.8-J pulse** condition and up to **48% dilution**; a verified beam spot size, bulk density, oxygen content and tensile properties are not reported in the retrieved results. On polished top surfaces, electrochemical tests in **3.5 wt% NaCl**, saturated calomel reference, **1 cm²**, **1 mV/s**, **n=3**, gave extrapolated corrosion rates **0.58 ± 0.03 mm/year for substrate** and **0.30 ± 0.04, 0.24 ± 0.03, 0.16 ± 0.03, 0.10 ± 0.04 mm/year** for coatings 1–4. Peak hardness was **304 HV0.2** for coating 3; coating 4 was **263 HV0.2**. Neither **actual repaired-propeller service nor cavitation performance** was demonstrated; importantly, **Cu–38Ni is not C71500**, and the rates are not long-term natural-seawater gravimetric rates. [NAVY] denotes a *marine propeller application*, not identified Navy sponsorship. (iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 4-7, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 2-4, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 14-16, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 7-9, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 13-14)

6. **Wrought 70–30 Cu–Ni has its own environmental limit — [CITE] [NUMBERS].** Gao, X., and Liu, M., “Corrosion Behavior of High-Strength C71500 Copper-Nickel Alloy in Simulated Seawater with High Concentration of Sulfide,” *Materials* **15** (2022), 8513, **DOI: 10.3390/ma15238513**: **C71500 Cu balance/Ni 30.54/Fe 0.80/Mn 0.93** in **3.5 wt% NaCl plus 0.5 wt% Na₂S**, **pH 8.20, 35 ± 2 °C**, static ASTM G31-based immersion. Corrosion rate was approximately **0.15 mm/year initially** and **0.09 mm/year after 168 h**. This is **sulfide-polluted synthetic chloride**, *not ASTM D1141 substitute seawater or a clean-seawater C71500 benchmark*. It illustrates that even the target alloy’s resistance depends on exposure. (gao2022corrosionbehaviorof pages 2-4, gao2022corrosionbehaviorof pages 1-2)

**Directly unanswered [GAP] [INSSTEK].** No retrieved publication independently demonstrates **pre-alloyed, nickel-free C95300 made by blown-powder laser DED/InssTek DMT**, much less dense, crack-free as-deposited C95300 on both itself and cast NAB, with an as-deposited seawater comparison to **wrought C70600 and C71500**, measured dealuminification depth, cavitation loss, tensile directionality and powder oxygen. Thus the database entry establishes reported process availability and UTS only; it does not establish the proposed corrosion target. No reliable cross-alloy ranking of C95300 versus **C95800 specifically** can be extracted from the cited experiments. (metin2022%35 pages 1-3, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 48-55, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 16-16, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 12-14)

## Top 5 things the PI should know

1. **Do not call C95300 a class-standard propeller bronze:** the retrieved CU1–CU4 chemistry excludes nickel-free Cu–10Al–1Fe; ISO 484 concerns tolerances, not material approval. (klassert2007copperandcopper–nickel pages 4-6, saeed2025investigationsoninner pages 26-30)
2. **Do not infer seawater qualification from the InssTek application or tensile figures:** neither a propeller installation nor a C95300 DMT corrosion test was established. (metin2022%35 pages 1-3, iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 16-16)
3. **The direct C95300 warning is experimental:** as-cast C95300 lost more mass than C95200 over 72 h in 3.5% NaCl, and its higher-Al/lower-Fe microstructure differed; the study cannot isolate nickel or predict deposited performance. (metin2022%35 pages 3-5, metin2022%35 pages 5-8, metin2022%35 media 6aa2a225)
4. **Use C71500 as a matched-test control, not a literature-derived pass threshold:** the one-year flowing-seawater comparator has an unidentified aluminum-bronze grade, while short polarization-derived rates and high-flow losses measure different phenomena. (iatan2025optimizationofpulsed pages 13-14, lewis1981evaluationofcoppernickel pages 48-55, gao2022corrosionbehaviorof pages 2-4)
5. **Prioritize as-deposited phase and local-attack measurements alongside density:** quantify β′/β/γ₂ and Al-rich selective attack, substrate-interface dilution, flowing-seawater erosion/cavitation, and matched cast-C95800/wrought-C70600/C71500 coupons before claiming propeller suitability. This is a research recommendation, not a demonstrated C95300 performance result. (metin2022%35 pages 3-5, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 1-2, barik2005erosionanderosion–corrosion pages 7-8, laque1969deteriorationofmetals pages 13-16)
