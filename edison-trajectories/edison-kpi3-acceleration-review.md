Material strategy: Al-Zr and Al-Mn-Cr-Zr families PRIMARY (~80%), Cu-Cr-Zr COMPLEMENT (~20% stretch for Be-Cu replacement). AlSi10Mg is benchmark only.

KPI UNDER REVIEW: "AI Acceleration Factor" — iterations to reach performance threshold vs baselines (random, expert DOE, standard BO, BOFN). Phase I target: ≥3× fewer iterations than random, ≥1.5× vs standard BO. Phase II target: ≥5× vs random. Pre-registered benchmark with ≥20 seeds, bootstrap CI. Minimum meaningful effect EF≥1.2.

1. Are the acceleration factor targets (3× vs random, 1.5× vs BO) realistic for a real experimental SDL campaign with ~50-80 compositions? What acceleration factors have been demonstrated in published SDL campaigns? Reference BIRDSHOT/ULTIMATE (Hastings et al. 2024, Arroyave ARPA-E report), Kusne et al. (2020), Stach et al. # Critical Literature-Grounded Review of the FLAIME "AI Acceleration Factor" KPI

## 1. Are the Acceleration Factor Targets (3× vs Random, 1.5× vs BO) Realistic?

**Assessment: The Phase I target of ≥3× vs random is conservative and well-supported; the ≥1.5× vs standard BO target is more uncertain but plausible given BOFN's structural advantages.**

A comprehensive benchmarking review by Adesiji et al. (2026) establishes that across published SDL campaigns, the median acceleration factor (AF) versus random search is approximately 6, with a range spanning 1.3 to 100 (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 10-11, adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 1-4). The FLAIME Phase I target of ≥3× versus random thus falls below this cross-study median and should be achievable in a campaign with 50–80 compositions, provided the design space has meaningful property contrast and the optimization dimensionality is moderate (AF tends to increase with dimensionality) (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 15-18, adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 11-15).

Several prominent SDL campaigns provide concrete reference points. Kusne et al. (2020) demonstrated a 10× reduction in experiments with the CAMEO system, discovering the best composition in 19 iterations versus 177 grid measurements (kusne2020ontheflyclosedloopmaterials pages 3-5, kusne2020ontheflyclosedloopmaterials pages 1-2). Liang et al. (2025), using the follow-on AMASE system with real-time CALPHAD integration, achieved a 6-fold reduction in experiments for autonomous phase-diagram mapping (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3). The BIRDSHOT program (Hastings et al. 2024) completed five iterative design-make-test-learn loops and identified a multi-objective Pareto set while sampling only 0.15% of the feasible design space (hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 1-2, mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 5-7), while a related refractory alloy campaign demonstrated a 54% improvement in Pareto hypervolume after just two batch iterations of 24 alloys each (paramore2025twoshotoptimizationof pages 1-3, paramore2025twoshotoptimizationof pages 16-18). Other reported AFs include MacLeod et al. (Ada, AF=100 for qEHVI vs random in simulation) (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 10-11), AI-EDISON (~8× in 25 vs 200 iterations) (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 7-10), and Bateni/SmartDope (AF=37.5) (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 7-10).

The ≥1.5× target for BOFN versus standard BO is more novel and harder to calibrate from the literature because most published benchmarks compare BO against random or grid baselines rather than advanced BO against advanced BO (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 10-11). Astudillo and Frazier (2021) report that BOFN "dramatically outperforms standard Bayesian optimization methods" with improvements ranging from ~5% to several orders of magnitude depending on problem structure (astudillo2021bayesianoptimizationof pages 1-2, astudillo2021bayesianoptimizationof pages 9-11). The largest gains occur when the overall input dimensionality is high but individual network nodes have low-dimensional inputs (astudillo2021bayesianoptimizationof pages 9-11). For FLAIME's function-network structure (CALPHAD → atomization → LPBF → tensile testing), the intermediate observations should provide substantial information, making a 1.5× advantage plausible. However, the 5% improvement end of the BOFN range suggests that on some problem instances, the advantage may be modest and fall below 1.5×.

The Phase II target of ≥5× versus random remains well within the published AF range but will require the campaign to scale beyond 80 compositions and benefit from accumulated learning.

## 2. Is the 4-Baseline Design Sufficient?

**Assessment: The 4-baseline design is well-structured but should include a fifth baseline to isolate the mineral-burden contribution.**

The proposed baselines—random, expert DOE, standard BO, and BOFN—span an appropriate range from uninformed search through increasingly sophisticated model-based approaches. Adesiji et al. (2026) recommend benchmarking SDL algorithms against at least random sampling as a universal reference, which is consistent with this design (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 1-4, adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 4-7). Volk and Abolhasani (2024) further emphasize the value of direct algorithm benchmarking with replicates, including comparisons to grid search, DOE, and human baselines (volk2024performancemetricsto pages 2-3).

However, a critical gap is the absence of a **"BOFN without mineral-burden objective" baseline**. FLAIME's novelty claim rests on two elements: (a) the BOFN architecture exploiting function-network structure, and (b) the inclusion of a mineral-burden objective. Without a fifth baseline—BOFN optimizing only for mechanical performance without the mineral-burden objective—it will be impossible to disentangle whether observed improvements stem from the function-network structure (BOFN vs standard BO) or from the multi-objective formulation incorporating supply-chain risk. This concern is reinforced by Mulukutla et al. (2024), who demonstrated supply-risk-aware alloy design and showed that incorporating supply risk meaningfully changes the Pareto front (hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 1-2). A "BOFN-performance-only" ablation baseline would make the KPI far more scientifically interpretable.

Additionally, because FLAIME uses CALPHAD as the first node in the function network, a "CALPHAD-informed standard BO" baseline (standard BO initialized with or augmented by CALPHAD predictions, but without the function-network structure) would help isolate the contribution of BOFN's network-aware acquisition function from the general benefit of physics-informed priors. The BIRDSHOT program demonstrated that domain knowledge and initialization strategies significantly impact BO convergence (mulukutla2024illustratinganeffective pages 5-7).

## 3. Is ≥20 Seeds Adequate for Statistical Power?

**Assessment: 20 seeds is defensible and above common practice (5 seeds) but below high-rigor standards. The bootstrap CI approach adds credibility.**

The BO benchmarking literature shows wide variation in replicate counts. Stuckner et al. (2026) used only 5 random seeds per setting in their NASA BO benchmarking study (stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 1-6), while Daulton et al. (2022) used 20 replications with ±2 standard error confidence intervals (bakshy2022bayesianoptimizationover pages 16-18). Turner et al. (2021), in the authoritative Black-Box Optimization Challenge analysis, escalated from N=10 (feedback leaderboard) to N=30 (final leaderboard) to N=100 (top-20 re-run) replicates per problem, with B=10,000 bootstrap replications for rank uncertainty (turner2021bayesianoptimizationis pages 6-8, turner2021bayesianoptimizationis pages 8-11). MacLeod et al. (2022) used 1,000 replicates with 1,000,000 pairwise comparisons for their Ada benchmarking (macleod2022aselfdrivinglaboratory pages 8-8).

Mattos et al. (2021) recommend Bayesian hierarchical models for analyzing optimization benchmarks rather than fixed seed-count thresholds, emphasizing that adequate replication depends on effect size, variance, and model structure (mattos2021statisticalmodelsfor pages 14-15). Given FLAIME's minimum meaningful effect of EF ≥ 1.2, 20 seeds may provide adequate power for detecting a 3× improvement over random (a large effect) but could be underpowered for detecting the 1.5× BOFN-vs-BO improvement, which is a smaller effect relative to inter-seed variance.

**Recommendation**: Retain ≥20 seeds as the minimum but: (a) conduct a prospective power analysis on the in-silico surrogate before locking the protocol, (b) escalate to ≥30 seeds if Phase I in-silico benchmarking reveals high inter-seed variance, and (c) adopt the bootstrap methodology with B ≥ 1,000 replications as Turner et al. (2021) demonstrated (turner2021bayesianoptimizationis pages 6-8).

## 4. Is the Enhancement Factor / Acceleration Factor Formulation Well-Defined?

**Assessment: The AF/EF framework follows established conventions but requires explicit edge-case handling.**

The AF and EF metrics used in SDL benchmarking are well-established. Adesiji et al. (2026) define AF as the ratio of experiments required by a reference campaign to reach a target performance versus the active-learning campaign, and EF as the ratio of best-observed performances at the same experiment count (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 4-7, adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 1-4). MacLeod et al. (2022) formalized both metrics and addressed a critical edge case: when the reference method (random search) never reaches the performance of the active-learning method within the budget, AF becomes undefined; they computed a lower-bound AF by assuming the reference would match performance with one additional sample, and reported AF values only until these lower-bound estimates accounted for more than 25% of comparisons (macleod2022aselfdrivinglaboratory pages 7-8).

The "BO sometimes loses" problem is real but partially addressed by the existing formulation. When random search outperforms BO at a given iteration count, EF < 1, which is straightforward to interpret (macleod2022aselfdrivinglaboratory pages 8-8). For AF, the issue is more nuanced: AF is typically computed at a specific performance threshold, and if BO never reaches that threshold while random does, AF < 1. However, the stochastic nature of both strategies means that across seeds, some random seeds will outperform some BO seeds. The FLAIME proposal's use of bootstrap CI across ≥20 seeds and reporting of median AF rather than individual-seed AF partially mitigates this.

**Key gaps in the formulation**: (a) The proposal should specify whether AF is computed at a fixed performance threshold or at the campaign's best-achieved performance, as Adesiji et al. note that AF is sensitive to this choice (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 11-15). (b) The minimum meaningful effect threshold of EF ≥ 1.2 is reasonable but should be justified with reference to the expected property-space contrast C, which bounds maximum achievable EF (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 4-7, adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 11-15). (c) The proposal should pre-specify how to report results when random outperforms BOFN on some fraction of seeds—e.g., reporting the win rate (fraction of seeds where BOFN outperforms random) alongside median AF.

## 5. Is the Time-Ledger Decomposition (Automation × AI × Throughput) Well-Established?

**Assessment: This decomposition is conceptually appealing and aligned with emerging frameworks, but not yet formally standardized in the literature.**

Volk and Abolhasani (2024) recommend reporting multiple complementary metrics for SDL performance—degree of autonomy, operational lifetime (demonstrated vs theoretical, assisted vs unassisted), throughput (demonstrated vs theoretical), and optimization efficiency—warning that single metrics like optimization rate can be misleading across studies (volk2024performancemetricsto pages 2-3, volk2024performancemetricsto pages 1-2). These metrics form the measurable building blocks that could be composed into a multiplicative decomposition, but Volk and Abolhasani do not propose such a formula explicitly.

Delgado-Licona and Abolhasani (2023) claim SDLs can yield "10–100× reduction of energy and chemical consumption, processing time, risk of operation, and capital investment within a typical discovery cycle" (delgadolicona2023researchaccelerationin pages 1-2) and identify process intensification, AI/ML, and automation hardware as the key contributing factors (delgadolicona2023researchaccelerationin pages 3-3). However, they present this as a qualitative technology roadmap rather than a quantitative decomposition formula (delgadolicona2023researchaccelerationin pages 2-3, delgadolicona2023researchaccelerationin pages 6-6).

Stach et al. (2021) cataloged autonomous experimentation campaigns with varying degrees of acceleration attributable to different system components but did not propose a formal decomposition methodology (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 8-9).

**Assessment**: The automation × AI × throughput decomposition proposed in FLAIME is a novel and useful framing, but it should be presented as a **proposed methodology** rather than a well-established standard. The proposal should: (a) clearly define measurement protocols for each factor independently (e.g., throughput = samples/week with automation vs manual; AI = iteration efficiency from BOFN benchmarking; automation = fraction of human-intervention-free operation time), and (b) address the non-independence of these factors (e.g., automation enables higher throughput, and AI value depends on throughput-limited batch sizes).

## 6. Recommended Improvements to Make the KPI More Defensible

Based on the literature review, the following improvements are recommended:

**Baseline Design Enhancements:**
- Add a 5th baseline: **BOFN without mineral-burden objective** to isolate the contribution of the supply-chain-aware multi-objective formulation.
- Add a 6th baseline: **CALPHAD-informed standard BO** (standard BO with CALPHAD initialization but without function-network structure) to isolate the BOFN architecture's contribution from the general benefit of physics priors.

**Statistical Rigor:**
- Conduct a **prospective power analysis** using CALPHAD-based in-silico surrogates to determine the minimum seed count needed to detect the 1.5× BOFN-vs-BO effect with 80% power.
- Increase the minimum seed count to **≥30** if power analysis indicates 20 is marginal, following Turner et al.'s precedent of escalating from 10 to 30 for robust claims (turner2021bayesianoptimizationis pages 6-8).
- Use bootstrap with **B ≥ 1,000** replications for CI construction, as per Turner et al. (turner2021bayesianoptimizationis pages 6-8).

**Metric Specification:**
- Pre-specify whether AF is computed at a **fixed performance threshold** (e.g., 80% of best-known alloy performance) or at the **campaign's realized best**, as this choice materially affects AF (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 11-15).
- Report the **win rate** (fraction of seeds where BOFN outperforms each baseline) alongside median AF to transparently handle the "BO sometimes loses" problem (macleod2022aselfdrivinglaboratory pages 8-8).
- Report **EF trajectories** as a function of normalized experiment number (n/d, where d is dimensionality), following the framework of Adesiji et al. (2026), who show that EF typically peaks at 10–20 experiments per dimension (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 11-15).
- Specify the **property-space contrast C** for each alloy family and use it as a theoretical upper bound on EF (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 4-7).

**Time-Ledger Decomposition:**
- Frame the automation × AI × throughput decomposition as a **proposed methodology** with clear, independently measurable definitions for each factor.
- Report each factor with uncertainty bounds and explicitly address their non-independence.
- Benchmark each factor against the Volk and Abolhasani (2024) recommended metrics: degree of autonomy, operational lifetime, demonstrated throughput, and optimization efficiency (volk2024performancemetricsto pages 2-3, volk2024performancemetricsto pages 1-2).

**Pre-Registration and Transparency:**
- Pre-register the benchmark protocol, including seed count, AF/EF definitions, performance thresholds, and stopping criteria, before experimental data collection begins. The proposal's mention of pre-registration is commendable and should be followed through with detail.
- Release all benchmarking code and data, following the precedent of Stuckner et al. (2026) (stuckner2026benchmarkingbayesianoptimization pages 1-6) and MacLeod et al. (2022) (macleod2022aselfdrivinglaboratory pages 8-8).

In summary, the FLAIME KPI framework is well-conceived and grounded in emerging SDL benchmarking practice. The 3× target versus random is conservative relative to the literature median of 6× (adesiji2026benchmarkingselfdrivinglabs pages 10-11), the 1.5× target versus standard BO is ambitious but plausible given BOFN's demonstrated advantages (astudillo2021bayesianoptimizationof pages 1-2, astudillo2021bayesianoptimizationof pages 9-11), and the 20-seed pre-registered benchmark with bootstrap CI represents a credible if not maximal level of statistical rigor. The principal recommendations are to add ablation baselines isolating the mineral-burden objective and function-network contributions, conduct prospective power analyses, and provide explicit edge-case handling for the AF/EF metrics.
