## Methodology

1. Extracted all **named individuals** from `metal-am-contacts-worldwide.md` (~950 candidate name strings after regex + stopword filtering).
2. Scoring priority: **(a)** Al-AM specificity (novel-Al LPBF authors, Scalmalloy / 7A77 / Aheadd CP1 / Addalloy teams, powder atomizer operators with PSD-control evidence) ≫ **(b)** FLAIME-relevant process (LPBF > DED > BJ > solid-state) ≫ **(c)** ease of introduction.
3. The Top-75 individuals below are drawn from the highest-relevance subset.

> **Primary vs. secondary scope** (per sgbaird clarification, 2026-04-17): Tiers 1–6 below are the **primary** list — people and institutions who are explicit Al-alloy-AM specialists (authors of novel-Al-LPBF/DED/WAAM papers, teams running Scalmalloy/7A77/Aheadd/Addalloy, Al-atomization operators, etc.). **Tier S** (added at the bottom) is a **secondary** list of capability-holders — institutions that have the metal-AM equipment to *print* Al alloys on demand, even if their published work to date isn't explicitly Al-focused. Tier S is a fallback / equipment-access pool for when the primary targets are unavailable or need to be augmented.

> **Purpose of the ask** (per sgbaird clarifications, 2026-04-17, comments 4264858647 and 4264917235): this list is being built in the context of soliciting **Letters of Collaboration or Access** (per DE-FOA-0003612 §IV.C, pp. 65–66) stating willingness to receive at least one of the newly-discovered (FLAIME-output) Al-alloy powders, run a test print on existing equipment, and ship the printed sample back for characterisation. These are the FOA's formal vehicle for exactly this kind of ask and **do not** require the signer to submit a collaborative application or receive any funding — unfunded partners are explicitly contemplated (FOA p. 83, FAQ p. 6). The FOA imposes a strict two-sentence format and a prohibition on recommendation/endorsement content; the template in this doc has been updated to match. This reframes the targeting in three ways that should be kept in mind when using this list:
>
> 1. **Signing authority — what the FOA actually says vs. what's institutional-norm guidance.** The FOA (DE-FOA-0003612 §IV.C, pp. 65–66) explicitly prescribes signer authority **only for Letters of Commitment** ("must be current, signed, and dated by a person authorized to commit the participating institution to a legally binding agreement"); the same requirement is repeated in FAQ v2 p. 9. For **Letters of Collaboration or Access**, the FOA gives the mandatory 2-sentence wording and closes with `<Collaborator's Name and Signature Block>` but **does not specify a required role, title, or institutional-binding authority for the signer** — neither the FOA nor the FAQ contains any authority clause for Letters of Collaboration or Access. The per-named-contact recommendations in §"Who can sign" below are therefore **institutional-norm / partnerships-office guidance**, not FOA requirements: they reflect how real institutions (national labs, large industrials, universities) typically route outgoing Letters of Collaboration or Access in practice, so that the returned letter is uncontroversial inside the signer's organization. A PI/Lab Director/Group Leader signing for their own lab's machine time is normally sufficient from the FOA's perspective; the "AOR countersign / partnerships-office routing" notes below are about the *signer's* internal approval path, not an FOA rule.
> 2. **The ask is low-cost and finite** — ≥1 powder, 1 test print, sample returned — so targets that would be a stretch for a full collaboration are fully reasonable for a Letter of Collaboration/Access. This *raises the value of cold outreach* to the four flagged gaps (HRL/7A77, APWORKS/Scalmalloy, Constellium CRV/Aheadd CP1, Ames Lab HPGA) — a strictly-formatted two-sentence non-binding letter is a much easier "yes" than a multi-month collab proposal, and these are the highest-value Al-AM entities in the doc.
> 3. **Equipment–powder compatibility matters per-letter, but must be expressed within the FOA's 2-sentence format.** A letter from a lab with only an EOS M290 should describe the collaboration as test-printing "at least one FLAIME-produced Al-alloy powder in the 15–45 µm LPBF PSD band"; a WAAM/DED group "in the 45–150 µm DED PSD band"; a binder-jetting group "in the 2–25 µm fine PSD band". The §8b PSD-splitting question to Valimet's atomization engineers is directly relevant — the feasibility of producing all three PSD bands from one composition determines how many compatible candidates each target has. Keep the letter's descriptive clause generic ("at least one") rather than pinning a specific composition.
>
> A FOA-compliant letter template and a per-named-contact signing-authority table are at the end of this doc (§"Letter of Collaboration or Access — FOA-compliant template" and §"FOA compliance — who can sign").

## Letter of Collaboration or Access — FOA-compliant template

**The FOA's rules (DE-FOA-0003612 §IV.C, pp. 65–66 and p. 83; FAQ v2 p. 6).** Two distinct letter types exist:

- **Letter of Commitment** — required from *every funded partner institution on the application team*, signed by a person authorized to commit the institution to a legally binding agreement, ≤1 page, placed in Appendix 7. **Not** what we are asking for here (test-print partners are not submitting a collaborative application and are not receiving funding).
- **Letter of Collaboration or Access** — the correct vehicle for "will you test-print one of our powders and send it back". Appended to the Project Narrative and placed in Appendix 7, does **not** count against the page limit, may be addressed "To Whom It May Concern:", **must be exactly two sentences**, and **must not** read as a recommendation or endorsement. The FOA specifies the exact wording:

> Dear \<Principal Investigator Name\>:
>
> If your application entitled, "\<Application Name\>," is selected for funding under the RFA Name, it is my intent to collaborate in this research by \<Complete Sentence With a Very Short Description of What the Collaborator Offers to Do or Provide\>.
>
> Thank you for the opportunity to participate.
>
> Sincerely,
>
> \<Collaborator's Name and Signature Block\>

**Concrete fill-in for the test-print ask.** Drop-in second clause for the first sentence, parameterised by the signatory's equipment band:

- **LPBF labs:** "…it is my intent to collaborate in this research by receiving at least one FLAIME-produced aluminium-alloy powder in the 15–45 µm PSD range, printing a test coupon on our \<EOS M290 / Aconity MIDI / Renishaw AM400 / SLM 280 HL / Farsoon FS422M / etc.\> laser powder-bed-fusion system, and returning the as-printed sample to the FLAIME team."
- **DED / WAAM labs:** "…by receiving at least one FLAIME-produced aluminium-alloy powder in the 45–150 µm PSD range, depositing a test build on our \<OPTOMEC LENS / BeAM Modulo / Lincoln-Baker WAAM / Addilan / etc.\> system, and returning the as-deposited sample to the FLAIME team."
- **Binder-jetting labs:** "…by receiving at least one FLAIME-produced aluminium-alloy powder in the 2–25 µm PSD range, printing a test part on our \<ExOne / Desktop Metal Shop / HP Metal Jet\> system and sintering it per our standard Al workflow, and returning the finished sample to the FLAIME team."
- **Cold spray labs:** "…by receiving at least one FLAIME-produced aluminium-alloy powder in our standard cold-spray PSD range, depositing a test coating on our \<system\>, and returning the as-sprayed sample to the FLAIME team."

**Do not** include process/characterisation deliverables, cost-share offers, equipment donations, or endorsement language — any of those can push the letter out of compliance with the FOA's 2-sentence / non-recommendation rule. FLAIME-side characterisation and any returned in-process data are handled outside the letter.
