# Worldwide Metal AM Contacts — Candidates for Validation Printing of FLAIME Novel Al-Alloy Powder

**Purpose:** Comprehensive directory of institutions, companies, and government labs with metal additive manufacturing (LPBF, powder DED, binder jetting) capability who could run validation prints of FLAIME-developed novel Al-alloy powder from scaled-up gas atomization runs.

**Compiled from:** 13 Edison Scientific literature searches (5 gap-fill + 8 primary) and 25+ targeted web searches, with emphasis on authors of recently published novel-Al-alloy LPBF / DED papers.

**Feedstock sizing reminder (for the Valimet question):** LPBF machines typically require 15–45 µm, powder DED typically 45–106 µm (or 45–150 µm), binder jetting typically 5–30 µm. Mid-run PSD changes during a single gas atomization campaign would allow one melt to feed multiple AM modalities.

**Legend:**
- 🌟 = developed a novel custom Al alloy or own proprietary AM-Al IP
- 🔬 = published recent (2020–2026) peer-reviewed work on AM-Al alloys
- 🏭 = operational production-scale metal AM capability
- 📜 = owns or heavily uses powder atomization equipment in-house

### 8b. Key powder characterization question (Valimet follow-up — PSD splitting)

**The exact question sgbaird sent Valimet's atomization engineers (2026-04-16):**

> Quick question: can the particle size distribution be adjusted mid-run during gas atomization (i.e., without paying for a whole additional production day to obtain a different PSD more suited to a specific AM process)? For example, being able to get three distinct batches for a particular composition, just different PSDs:
>
> - **LPBF:** 15–45 µm
> - **DED:** 45–150 µm
> - **Binder jetting:** 2–25 µm (fine) or similar to LPBF range
>
> Or would you recommend straddling the size ranges in terms of atomization parameters and handling this primarily through ad-hoc sieving?

This matters because a single ~100 kg novel-Al-alloy atomization campaign would then seed validation prints across LPBF, DED, and binder jetting partners simultaneously, dramatically multiplying the number of validation-printing organisations we can engage per gas-atomization day.

**Published evidence relevant to the question:**
- **Ames Laboratory, Anderson et al. 2010** ("Highly tuned gas atomization for controlled preparation of coarse powder", Iver Anderson, David Byrd, J. Meyer) — closest published evidence that **close-coupled HPGA parameters (discrete-jet nozzle geometry, gas pressure, slotted trumpet bell pour tube) can be tuned to shift the yield toward coarser fractions** (useful for DED 45–150 µm and larger).
- **VTT Finland, Puukko et al. 2025** (recycling of battle-damaged Al) — reports **Hermiga 75/5VI inert gas atomizer** and documents that as-atomized Al PSD often has to be sieved to meet LPBF (or PBF) cut-offs. Direct supporting evidence for the **"straddle + ad-hoc sieving"** strategy being viable.
- **Ames Lab, Anderson et al. 2018** (AM feedstock powder processing research needs, co-authored with ORNL's Ryan Dehoff) — discusses discrete-jet vs annular nozzle studies as a lever for different AM modalities.

**Practical implication for FLAIME's experimental plan:** whether Valimet's answer is (a) "yes, we can bracket all three windows in one run and split by cyclone/sieving" or (b) "no, you'd book three separate campaign days per composition", fundamentally changes the downstream validation-print logistics and the number of collaborator institutions from this directory we can realistically seed per month.

## 10g. Export-control & international-shipping considerations (Genesis Mission / DOE) ⚠️

**Trigger:** Feedback from Moataz (UK-based prospective collaborator): *"It could be a bit of a hassle shipping powders from the USA to the UK where we are based. Export control is another legal issue you have to handle from your end due to the sensitivity around AM work. You may want to check this prior to any further discussion."*

This is a real and material constraint and should be assessed **per-target** before any international Letter of Collaboration/Access is solicited.

### Regulatory framework (US perspective; FLAIME PI-side)

- **EAR (Export Administration Regulations)** — administered by **BIS/Commerce**. Most metal AM powders are dual-use and fall under EAR rather than ITAR.
- **ITAR (International Traffic in Arms Regulations)** — administered by **State/DDTC**. Triggers if powder/process is on the **USML** (United States Munitions List) or developed under an ITAR-controlled program. Most civil-aerospace and energy AM Al alloys are *not* ITAR; some defense-funded work is.
- **Aluminium AM powder ECCN classification (illustrative — NOT legal advice; FLAIME PI must self-classify each batch):**
  - Most Al-AM powders ship as **EAR99** (no license required for UK) or under low-control ECCNs.
  - **High-purity Al powder** (≥99.99%) and certain particle-size cuts can fall under **ECCN 1C111** (propellant/missile-tech precursors) or **ECCN 1C002** (alloyed materials with specified properties). These DO require a license for many destinations.
  - "Sensitivity around AM work" (Moataz's phrase) often refers to: (a) inherent rocket-propellant utility of fine atomised Al, (b) MTCR-overlay controls that can attach to seemingly civil AM equipment, and (c) "deemed export" risk on technical data shared with foreign nationals during sample exchange.
- **Genesis Mission / DOE-specific overlay (DE-FOA-0003612):** the FOA does not waive export-control law. DOE awards typically require:
  - Notification to the DOE export-control officer for any controlled material/data leaving the US.
  - Disclosure of foreign collaborators in the Foreign Components and "DOE Transparency of Foreign Connections" forms.
  - Compliance with **NSPM-33** research-security plan and **CHIPS & Science Act §10632/10638** foreign-talent disclosures.

### Practical implications for the Top-75 outreach plan

4. **Outbound logistics responsibility:** physical shipment of fine Al powder triggers **IATA Class 4.1 (flammable solid, UN1396 or UN3089)** plus **IMDG/ADR**. Practically, PI should ship via a hazmat-licensed forwarder (FedEx-DG, UPS-DG, DHL-DG); cost ~$300–800/shipment to STA-eligible destinations, plus EEI filing in **AESDirect** for shipments >$2,500 or any licensed item.

   - **(c) Defer the international ask to Phase II** once the alloy is published or otherwise classified as fundamental research (FRE — "fundamental research exclusion," 15 CFR 734.8) and therefore not subject to EAR controls on the technical data side.
