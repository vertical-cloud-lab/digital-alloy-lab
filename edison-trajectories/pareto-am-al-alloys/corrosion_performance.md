# Corrosion Performance of LPBF Aluminum Alloys

This document compiles Edison Scientific LITERATURE results on the corrosion performance of LPBF/SLM aluminum alloys (AlSi10Mg, Scalmalloy, A20X, 2xxx, 6xxx, 7xxx, and emerging high-strength systems) compared to wrought equivalents. It addresses uniform/pitting/IGC/SCC behavior, galvanic effects, the role of porosity/roughness, and post-processing mitigations — relevant context for considering corrosion as a property axis for AM Al alloy design and deployment (e.g., automotive structural applications such as the Divergent 3D Adaptive Production System).

Question: What is the corrosion performance of LPBF/SLM additively manufactured aluminum alloys compared to wrought equivalents? Cover AlSi10Mg, Scalmalloy, A20X, 2xxx, 6xxx, 7xxx series, and emerging high-strength AM Al alloys. Specifically address: (1) general/uniform corrosion rates in chloride environments (salt spray, NaCl immersion), (2) localized/pitting corrosion susceptibility, (3) intergranular and stress corrosion cracking (SCC), (4) galvanic effects from cellular Si network or precipitates, (5) the effect of as-built porosity and surface roughness, (6) effect of post-processing (HIP, heat treatment, machining, surface treatment, anodizing) on corrosion. Identify cases where LPBF aluminum corrosion is reported to be poor or worse than wrought, what mechanisms drive that, and what mitigation strategies (heat treatment, surface engineering, alloy design) have been proposed. Include any reports related to automotive structural applications (e.g., Divergent 3D Adaptive Production System).

## Summary
Corrosion performance of LPBF/SLM aluminium alloys relative to wrought/cast is not uniform; it is strongly alloy- and post-processing-dependent. LPBF can **improve localized corrosion** when it refines and homogenizes cathodic constituent particles (e.g., some 7xxx/2xxx observations) but can be **worse than wrought** when AM introduces (i) surface-connected porosity/roughness, (ii) melt-pool-boundary segregation, and/or (iii) heat-treatment-driven coarsening/redistribution of galvanic phases (e.g., Si-network breakup in AlSi10Mg or PFZ/Cu-rich films in Al–Cu alloys). (revilla2020corrosionandcorrosion pages 18-20, revilla2020corrosionandcorrosion pages 15-17, berlangalabari2020corrosionofcast pages 15-17)

## (1) General/uniform corrosion rates in chloride environments
### AlSi10Mg (LPBF/SLM)
* **Immersion mass-loss / corrosion-rate comparisons vs traditional automotive alloys:** In 30-day 5% NaCl immersion tests, additively manufactured (AM) AlSi10Mg had corrosion rates that were **always higher than the traditionally manufactured (wrought) aluminium alloys tested** (automotive-relevant comparators), although AM AlSi10Mg could outperform a cast comparator (sand-cast A356_T6_NM) due to the finer AM Si network. (gatto2023thecorrosionbehaviour pages 1-2, gatto2023thecorrosionbehaviour pages 10-13)
* **Cyclic corrosion mass loss (AM vs cast AlSi10Mg):** In an accelerated cyclic corrosion test, heat treatment (not manufacturing route) dominated mass loss; LPBF AlSi10Mg showed **lower mass loss than cast** in the as-manufactured and T5 conditions, while **T6 increased mass loss for both LPBF and cast**. (buhl2026effectofheat pages 1-2)

### 6xxx series (LPBF Al-6061 RAM2 vs extruded 6061-T6)
* In 0.5 M NaCl over 10 days, LPBF Al-6061 RAM2 showed **initially superior corrosion performance** relative to extruded 6061-T6, but **corrosion resistance decayed continuously** with time, culminating in severe localized attack driven by cathodic modifier particles (Ti-rich/B4C). This is a case where LPBF initially appears better electrochemically but becomes worse in prolonged exposure. (torbatisarraf2020acomparativestudy pages 1-2, torbatisarraf2020acomparativestudy pages 10-14)

### 7xxx series (AA7075, AA7050)
* For SLM AA7075 in 0.1 M NaCl, electrochemical response depends on thermal history; dissolution rates were reported as generally lower for AM than wrought AA7075-T6, with different impedance signatures (wrought shows inductive behavior consistent with film degradation; SLM did not in that study). (gharbi2019microstructureandcorrosion pages 13-18, gharbi2019microstructureandcorrosion pages 1-5)
* For SLM AA7050 in chloride SCC environments, the study emphasizes SCC susceptibility more than uniform corrosion rates; limited SCC initiation occurred in 3.5% NaCl without strong evidence of catastrophic general corrosion. (chasse2021stresscorrosioncracking pages 1-2)

### 2xxx / Al–Cu–Li (AA2099 as an exemplar high-strength Al–Cu family)
* In 0.1 M NaCl, LPBF AA2099 showed corrosion current density increasing strongly with increasing in situ preheat (worse corrosion), while the conventional T83 condition had the lowest current density. (adjeikyeremeh2023preheatinginfluenceon pages 15-17, adjeikyeremeh2023preheatinginfluenceon pages 19-20)

## (2) Localized/pitting corrosion susceptibility
### AlSi10Mg: melt-pool borders, Si network, and pores
* LPBF AlSi10Mg pitting/localized corrosion commonly initiates at **melt-pool borders/HAZ**, where Si is coarser or network continuity is disrupted; corrosion proceeds by **selective dissolution of α-Al** galvanically coupled to noble Si precipitates. (cabrini2019corrosionresistancein pages 1-2, cabrini2019corrosionresistancein pages 2-4)
* **Chloride concentration effect:** Increasing chloride activity reduces pitting potential; data scatter is attributed to **statistical emergence of surface porosity** that acts as a pit initiator and contains less protective process-formed oxide. (cabrini2019corrosionresistancein pages 2-4)

### Scalmalloy / Al–Mg–Sc–Zr (and related Sc-bearing AM Al alloys)
* For SLM Al–Mg–Sc–Zr, pitting dominates and preferentially occurs along **molten-pool boundaries** where Al3(Sc,Zr) precipitates cluster; heat treatment increases pit density and depth (max depth reported ~46.8 μm in the heat-treated condition). (zhang2020influenceofheat pages 15-20, zhang2020influenceofheat pages 1-6)
* Anisotropic pitting/corrosion is common: XY surfaces can outperform XZ surfaces due to melt-pool-boundary density, precipitate distributions, and boundary character. (gu2020anisotropiccorrosionbehavior pages 1-2, shi2023densificationmicrostructureand pages 13-14)

### 7xxx series (AA7075)
* Wrought AA7075-T6 pitting is commonly associated with cathodic Al–Cu–Fe(-Si) particles causing trenching; SLM AA7075 produced **more numerous but notably smaller pits** (microstructure/particle refinement). Pitting was more pronounced along melt-pool boundaries in the as-SLM state. (gharbi2019microstructureandcorrosion pages 13-18)

## (3) Intergranular corrosion (IGC) and stress corrosion cracking (SCC)
### 6xxx: IGC in wrought 6061 and LPBF modifier-driven localized attack
* Extruded 6061-T6 showed **intergranular corrosion initiation** attributed to preferential dissolution of Al-depleted zones along grain boundaries in peak-aged Al–Mg–Si alloys. (torbatisarraf2020acomparativestudy pages 5-7)
* The LPBF 6061 RAM2 study highlights that heterogeneous cathodic second phases can promote severe localized corrosion and may lead to environmentally assisted cracking, but it did not report a full SCC program. (torbatisarraf2020acomparativestudy pages 10-14)

### 7xxx: SCC in AM 7050; pit-to-crack link remains central
* SLM 7050 showed **overall similar SCC susceptibility** to wrought 7050-T7451 in chloride environments with limited SCC initiation in 3.5% NaCl, but adding an oxidizer (H2O2) shifted behavior toward **intergranular corrosion and pitting**, consistent with classic 7xxx vulnerability when grain-boundary MgZn2 is involved and cathodic constituent particles accelerate dissolution. (chasse2021stresscorrosioncracking pages 1-2)

### AlSi10Mg SCC comparison (AM AlSi10Mg vs wrought AA6061-T6)
* In a micro-DIC SCC study in 3.5 wt% NaCl, PBF AlSi10Mg exhibited better SCC performance than AA6061-T6 (delayed crack initiation and later failure), though the authors emphasize surface roughness/cavities promote pitting and can shorten incubation. (raghavan2023quantifyingstresscorrosion pages 9-11)

### 2xxx/Al–Cu–Li: PFZ-driven IGC risk increases with AM thermal exposure
* In LPBF AA2099, higher preheating produced wider PFZs and Cu-rich grain-boundary films, which are mechanistically linked to higher IGC susceptibility (PFZ anodic; precipitates cathodic). (adjeikyeremeh2023preheatinginfluenceon pages 15-17, adjeikyeremeh2023preheatinginfluenceon pages 19-20)

## (4) Galvanic effects from cellular Si network or precipitates
### AlSi10Mg: “beneficial” vs “harmful” roles of the Si network
* As-built LPBF Al–Si–Mg alloys can contain a **near-continuous Si network** that can partially confine corrosion to superficial regions; however, galvanic coupling between α-Al and noble Si at melt-pool borders drives selective dissolution and pitting. (revilla2020corrosionandcorrosion pages 8-10, cabrini2019corrosionresistancein pages 2-4)
* Heat treatments that **break the Si network into isolated/coarser Si** increase Volta potential differences and cathodic area, raising galvanic driving force and typically worsening pitting corrosion. (revilla2020corrosionandcorrosion pages 15-17, chen2020corrosionbehaviorsof pages 15-18)

### Al–Mg–Sc–Zr (Scalmalloy-like)
* Local cathodes are often **Al3(Sc,Zr)** (and related) precipitates and oxide/spinel particles; their distribution (especially along molten-pool boundaries) governs pit nucleation and anisotropy. (zhang2020influenceofheat pages 20-24, shi2023densificationmicrostructureand pages 11-13)

### Al–Cu systems (AA2099 as example)
* **Cu-rich phases/films** become cathodic, while the PFZ becomes anodic, leading to classic IGC/pitting galvanic microcells that can be exacerbated by AM thermal histories. (adjeikyeremeh2023preheatinginfluenceon pages 15-17, adjeikyeremeh2023preheatinginfluenceon pages 17-19)

## (5) Effect of as-built porosity and surface roughness
* Across LPBF Al alloys, **surface-connected pores, roughness, and partially melted particles** act as preferential pitting/crevice initiation sites and strongly degrade corrosion-fatigue. (linder2023corrosionfatigueperformanceof pages 9-12, linder2023corrosionfatigueperformanceof pages 1-2)
* For LPBF AlSi10Mg, porosity causes scatter in pitting potential, because pores act as initiation sites and can contain process-formed oxide that is less protective than air-formed oxide. (cabrini2019corrosionresistancein pages 2-4)
* In AlSi10Mg immersion testing relevant to automotive exposures, finishing operations that expose subsurface porosity (e.g., a deburring/laser-type finishing that reveals pores) can lead to localized craters and deeper penetration. (gatto2023thecorrosionbehaviour pages 7-10)

## (6) Effect of post-processing and surface treatments
### HIP
* Direct chloride-corrosion datasets for HIPed LPBF Al in the retrieved corpus are limited. However, for LPBF Al-6061 RAM2 the authors propose HIP + solution aging as a route to stabilize microstructure and potentially enhance corrosion; for Scalmalloy, HIP is discussed primarily for porosity reduction and mechanical property improvement, with limited corrosion-specific quantification in the accessible sources. (torbatisarraf2020acomparativestudy pages 10-14, gu2020anisotropiccorrosionbehavior pages 1-2)

### Heat treatment
* **AlSi10Mg:** Stress-relief/aging and especially T6 can reduce corrosion resistance by Si network breakup and coarsening; T6 increased mass loss in cyclic corrosion tests and produced more aggressive corrosion in automotive-fluid tests at elevated temperature. (buhl2026effectofheat pages 1-2, gatto2023thecorrosionbehaviour pages 1-2)
* **Al–Mg–Sc–Zr (Scalmalloy-like):** Heat treatment (aging) can substantially worsen pitting metrics (icorr rising ~8× in one study) and deepen pits through altered Al3(Sc,Zr) precipitation distributions. (zhang2020influenceofheat pages 11-15, zhang2020influenceofheat pages 15-20)
* **Al–Cu–Li (AA2099):** Higher in situ preheating increased Icorr and reduced Rox/Rct, degrading corrosion resistance versus conventional T83 due to Cu-rich boundary phases and PFZ widening. (adjeikyeremeh2023preheatinginfluenceon pages 15-17, adjeikyeremeh2023preheatinginfluenceon pages 17-19)

### Machining/polishing and mechanical surface treatments
* Machining/polishing generally improve corrosion and corrosion-fatigue by removing the defect-rich contour layer and lowering pit depth; machined AlSi10Mg specimens have higher fatigue performance and corrosion has a smaller effect than in as-printed material. (linder2023corrosionfatigueperformanceof pages 9-12, linder2023corrosionfatigueperformanceof pages 1-2)
* Polishing also improves NaCl immersion corrosion resistance in automotive-relevant testing of AM AlSi10Mg. (gatto2023thecorrosionbehaviour pages 15-16)

### Anodizing
* Anodizing behavior in SLM Al–Si is altered by the Si network and internal pores: anodic films may develop branched pore morphologies; internal pores can be anodized and show microcracks that may compromise barrier integrity. Heat treatments that break the Si network change anodizing efficiency and steady-state potentials. (revilla2020corrosionandcorrosion pages 18-20)

## Where LPBF Al corrosion is reported to be poor or worse than wrought (and why)
1. **Automotive-relevant immersion testing:** AM AlSi10Mg had corrosion rates “always higher” than traditionally manufactured Al alloys tested for comparison in saline water, despite being competitive with some cast comparators; T6 worsened AM AlSi10Mg versus stress-relieved, and polishing improved performance but did not match wrought. Mechanisms include melt-pool-border attack and surface/porosity effects. (gatto2023thecorrosionbehaviour pages 1-2, gatto2023thecorrosionbehaviour pages 15-16)
2. **LPBF 6061 RAM2 vs extruded 6061-T6 (prolonged exposure):** AM initially appeared superior but became worse with time due to galvanic trenching around Ti-rich and B4C grain-refiner/agglomerate phases and a high density of susceptible sites under the oxide. (torbatisarraf2020acomparativestudy pages 10-14)
3. **Heat-treated Scalmalloy-like alloys:** Aging/heat treatment can drastically increase icorr and pit depth by changing Al3(Sc,Zr) precipitate size/distribution and promoting melt-pool-boundary pitting. (zhang2020influenceofheat pages 11-15, zhang2020influenceofheat pages 15-20)
4. **High-strength Al–Cu family (AA2099 example):** AM thermal strategies (high preheat) can worsen corrosion via PFZ widening and Cu-rich boundary films, leading to more IGC-prone microstructures than conventional T83. (adjeikyeremeh2023preheatinginfluenceon pages 15-17, adjeikyeremeh2023preheatinginfluenceon pages 19-20)

## Mitigation strategies proposed in the literature (heat treatment, surface engineering, alloy design)
* **Surface engineering as first line:** machining/polishing and (where appropriate) shot peening/grinding to remove the rough contour layer, reduce surface-connected porosity, and lower pit depth; this is consistently beneficial for pitting and corrosion-fatigue. (linder2023corrosionfatigueperformanceof pages 9-12, berlangalabari2020corrosionofcast pages 17-18, gatto2023thecorrosionbehaviour pages 15-16)
* **Avoid microstructure-coarsening heat treatments for AlSi10Mg:** since breaking/coarsening the Si network increases galvanic severity; prefer lower-temperature aging routes (T5-like) when corrosion is critical. (buhl2026effectofheat pages 1-2, revilla2020corrosionandcorrosion pages 15-17)
* **Control/avoid cathodic particle agglomerates:** e.g., Ti-rich/B4C (RAM2) or TiB2 agglomerates in Al–Cu systems can drive localized galvanic attack; alloy and powder/processing strategies should minimize large cathodic clusters. (torbatisarraf2020acomparativestudy pages 7-10, torbatisarraf2020acomparativestudy pages 10-14)
* **Alloy/heat-treatment design for high-strength AM Al:** in 7xxx, manage grain-boundary MgZn2 continuity and cathodic constituent particles; oxidizing environments can shift behavior to IGC/pitting. (chasse2021stresscorrosioncracking pages 1-2)
* **For Al–Cu(-Li) systems:** suppress Cu-rich grain-boundary films and PFZ widening; lower preheat or alternative thermal paths can reduce Icorr (480 °C preheat best among AM states in AA2099 study), but conventional T83 still outperformed AM-preheated states. (adjeikyeremeh2023preheatinginfluenceon pages 15-17, adjeikyeremeh2023preheatinginfluenceon pages 19-20)

## Automotive structural applications (incl. Divergent 3D)
Within the retrieved/accessible corpus, explicit corrosion-performance reports tied directly to Divergent 3D’s Adaptive Production System were not found. Automotive-relevant corrosion evidence was available from AM AlSi10Mg comparisons against common automotive wrought/cast alloys under saline-water immersion and other vehicle fluids, showing AM AlSi10Mg can be serviceable in oils/coolants at room temperature but remains more challenged than wrought in NaCl exposures and can degrade at elevated temperature in some AM/heat-treated states. (gatto2023thecorrosionbehaviour pages 1-2, gatto2023thecorrosionbehaviour pages 13-15)

## Limitations of this synthesis
Several key papers on emerging very-high-strength LPBF Al–Zn–Mg(-Cu) corrosion (including a well-known Corrosion Science paper on a high-solute Al–Zn–Mg LPBF alloy) and Scalmalloy-specific pitting/IGC papers were identified but unobtainable in the current tool run, limiting quantitative coverage for 7xxx-like “next-generation” AM alloys and proprietary alloys such as A20X. As a result, conclusions for those are based on obtainable 7xxx SCC data, obtainable SLM AA7075 corrosion data, and review-level synthesis. (chasse2021stresscorrosioncracking pages 1-2, gharbi2019microstructureandcorrosion pages 13-18, revilla2020corrosionandcorrosion pages 18-20)
