C95500, CuAl10Ni5Fe4, CuAl10Fe3 and similar), for example in an AMAZEMET rePOWDER or another
ultrasonic atomizer? For each report give the feedstock, melt temperature, crucible, nozzle and plate
(sonotrode) materials, frequency and amplitude, atmosphere and oxygen level, yield, particle size
distribution, morphology (satellites, flakes, irregular particles), oxygen content, and any problems

Yes. # Ultrasonic atomization of nickel–aluminium bronze: published precedent

**Yes.** Müller and co-workers reported recycling aluminium-bronze grinding chips by ultrasonic atomization for laser directed-energy deposition (DED) in 2023. A subsequent, accessible study by Uhlmann *et al.* (2024) identifies the chips as **nickel–aluminium bronze CuAl10Ni5Fe5, EN CC333G**, and gives measured properties of ultrasonically atomized powder made from those chips. The 2024 authors describe the 2023 work as using powder from the same grinding chips to produce DED specimens. The 2023 paper itself could not be examined here, so its apparatus settings and results should **not** be assumed identical to those reported in 2024. The accessible experiment identifies an **AMAZEMET AUS 500 Atomizer**, not a rePOWDER unit. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 2-4, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 15-15, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 4-6)

## Direct NAB report: Uhlmann *et al.* (2024)

| Requested item | Documented result |
|---|---|
| Feedstock | Grinding chips collected during ship-propeller manufacture; CuAl10Ni5Fe5 (CC333G). A 125 µm sieve was used to prepare raw-chip material for some analyses. No separate cleaning protocol is specified. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 2-4) |
| Atomizer; melt delivery | AMAZEMET/BluePower **AUS 500**; induction heating to **1300 °C**; melt delivered through a nozzle system at **1.5 bar**. The paper does not specify whether this pressure is gauge or absolute. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 4-6) |
| Crucible; nozzle; vibrating plate | **Crucible and nozzle materials not reported.** The vibrating sonotrode/plate is described as **binder-free woven carbon fibre**. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 4-6, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 10-12) |
| Frequency; amplitude | **40 kHz**; amplitude **not reported**. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 4-6) |
| Atmosphere; chamber oxygen | **Argon grade 5.0** during melting and atomization. A measured chamber oxygen concentration is **not reported**; the gas grade is not such a measurement. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 4-6) |
| Powder yield; sizing | Atomization mass yield and usable-size-fraction yield **not reported**. Powder was sieved through **200 µm mesh** to remove spatters. Dynamic-image-analysis **area-equivalent-diameter** percentiles, Xₐ,₁₀/Xₐ,₅₀/Xₐ,₉₀, were **48/98/174 µm**; these are not laser-diffraction D-values. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 4-6, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 6-7, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation media 7cacbeef) |
| Morphology and defects | Predominantly spherical: mean two-dimensional aspect ratio **0.935**, with **82%** of particles having aspect ratio >0.9. A limited number of satellites and agglomerates occurred, but fewer than in the study’s commercial gas-atomized comparator. Spatters and fine carbon fibres up to approximately **20 µm** were observed; virtually no internal gas inclusions were seen in examined powder cross-sections. No retained chip-shaped particles were observed after remelting. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 6-7, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 7-10, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 10-12, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation media 2cf5986d) |
| Powder oxygen | **0.013 wt.% O (approximately 130 ppm by mass)**, measured by hot-gas extraction; the sieved chips contained **0.073 wt.% (730 ppm)**. The figure labels can be misread: the accompanying text identifies the ultrasonically atomized powder as having the *lowest* measured oxygen, assigning **0.019 wt.%** to the commercial gas-atomized comparator. These are **powder** measurements, not chamber oxygen levels. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 10-12, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation media 57b1d664) |
| Other process problems | The authors identify fibres shed from the hot sonotrode and residual ceramic grinding-abrasive contaminants (ZrO₂, Al₂O₃ or SiO₂); the ultrasonic powder contained fewer abrasive particles than mechanically milled samples. **No nozzle clogging, quantified plate wear, interrupted melt flow, or atomization interruption is documented** in the accessible report. Its warning about possible *DED powder-feed-nozzle* clogging concerns the **ball-milled comparator**, not the ultrasonic atomizer. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 10-12, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 7-10, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 12-13) |

The ultrasonic powder’s Carney-funnel time was **8.62 s/150 g**, angle of repose **13.74°**, and Hausner ratio **1.05**, versus **10.55 s/150 g**, **20.08°**, and **1.12** for the commercial comparator. That comparator is **CuAl9.5Fe, not nickel–aluminium bronze**; its properties should not be presented as a gas-atomized NAB control. The 2024 authors assessed powder suitability but said actual DED powder-feeding and deposited-part properties required further testing. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 2-4, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 10-12, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 12-13, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 13-15)

## Related Cu–Al ultrasonic experiment, not bronze

Singh *et al.* (2026) atomized **Al–33 wt.% Cu**, an **Al-rich** alloy rather than Cu-rich aluminium bronze, using an AMAZEMET system. High-purity elemental feedstocks were melted in a **boron-nitride-coated graphite crucible**, held at **800 °C for five minutes**, and fed onto a **carbon-fibre-composite plate** attached to a **60 kHz** sonotrode. Argon purges were used; the authors describe a pressure ramp from **150 to 850 mbar over 50 s** to control melt flow. They report **80–90 wt.% yield below 300 µm**, with size percentiles **45/91/194 µm** after removal of particles and flakes above 300 µm. Initial spattering occurred before the plate wetted, after which atomization stabilized. **Amplitude, nozzle material, measured chamber oxygen, powder oxygen concentration, and plate-wear rate are not given.** This experiment cannot establish a yield or safe operating window for Cu-rich NAB at 1300 °C. (singh2026synthesisandcharacterization pages 1-3, singh2026synthesisandcharacterization pages 3-7)

## Gas-atomized NAB precedents for laser AM

- **Laser powder-bed fusion:** Shahriari and Mohammadi (2025) explicitly report LPBF specimens made from **gas-atomized NAB powder**: Al **8.5–9.5**, Ni **4–5**, Fe **3.4–4.5**, Mn **0.8–1.5 wt.%**, balance Cu. Their accessible extended abstract gives **no powder PSD, morphology, oxygen content, atomizer setup or powder yield**. (shahriari2025additivelymanufacturednickelaluminium pages 1-2)
- **Powder-fed laser DED:** Saai *et al.* (2025) document **30 kg of gas-atomized NAB powder** for one ship-propeller-hub repair, specifying Cu **79–83.2**, Al **8.5–9.5**, Ni **4–5**, Fe **3.5–4.5**, Mn **0.8–1.5 wt.%**, with minor Si and Pb limits. They report **77% of supplied powder deposited** and **23% overspray**. This is **DED deposition efficiency, not gas- or ultrasonic-atomization yield**; powder PSD and oxygen are not supplied. (saai2025comparativelifecycle pages 6-8, saai2025comparativelifecycle pages 4-6)
- **NAB powder characterization outside laser AM:** Krebs (2016) reports **CuAl10Fe5Ni5** made by electrode-induction-melting **gas atomization in nitrogen** for thermal/cold spraying. As-atomized batches have approximately **d₁₀/d₅₀/d₉₀ = 19/31/48 to 23/36/59 µm**, **350 ± 20 ppm O**, and nearly spherical particles with few satellites. These are useful powder-production benchmarks, **not proof that these particular batches were used for LPBF or DED**. (krebs2016thermischesundkinetisches pages 40-43)

**Bottom line:** There is a direct, quantitatively characterized **ultrasonically atomized NAB** precedent on the AUS 500. The available publication supports its temperature, delivery pressure, carbon-fibre sonotrode, frequency, argon grade and powder properties, but leaves **crucible/nozzle materials, amplitude, measured chamber oxygen, mass yield and operating interruptions unresolved**. The cited 2023 predecessor may contain further information, but its full text was unavailable for verification. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 4-6, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 6-7, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 15-15)
