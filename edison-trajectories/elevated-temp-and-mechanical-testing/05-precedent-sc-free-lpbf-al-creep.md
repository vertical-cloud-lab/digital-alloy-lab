**Question (PRECEDENT):** Has anyone measured creep, stress-rupture or long-exposure (1000 h or more) thermal stability at 250 C or above for a laser powder bed fusion aluminum alloy that contains no scandium, such as Al-Ce, Al-Fe-Zr, Al-Ni-Zr, Al-Mn-Zr or Al-Cu-Mn-Zr alloys?

---

Question: Has anyone measured creep, stress-rupture or long-exposure (1000 h or more) thermal stability at 250 C or above for a laser powder bed fusion aluminum alloy that contains no scandium, such as Al-Ce, Al-Fe-Zr, Al-Ni-Zr, Al-Mn-Zr or Al-Cu-Mn-Zr alloys?

Yes. **Yes.** Published studies report both creep or stress-rupture tests at ≥250 °C and *separate, unstressed* thermal exposures lasting ≥1000 h for scandium-free, laser-powder-bed-fused (LPBF) aluminum alloys. The distinction matters: a 1000 h aging result does not demonstrate 1000 h creep life. (griffiths2020coarseningandcreep pages 1-2, mehta2023advancingnovelalmncrzr pages 7-9, sisco2023heterogeneousphasetransformation pages 5-8, stinehart2023creepresistanceand pages 39-47)

| Alloy / primary report | Eligible exposure or test | Verified finding | Category |
|---|---|---|---|
| Binary Al–10Ce (wt.%), Stinehart et al. (2023) | Tensile creep/stress rupture at 300–350 °C: 300 °C/21 MPa ran **>765 h without rupture**; 325 °C/21 MPa ruptured at 92.17 h; 350 °C/21 MPa at 18.17 h; 350 °C/29 MPa at 6.1 h | Direct rupture-life measurements; the >765 h result is right-censored and **not** evidence of a ≥1000 h creep test | Creep and stress rupture ≥250 °C (stinehart2023creepresistanceand pages 39-47) |
| Al–3.6Mg–1.2Zr and Al–2.9Mg–2.1Zr (wt.%), Griffiths et al. (2020) | Compressive creep at 260 °C for 8 and 168 h | Approximate threshold stress was 40 MPa as fabricated versus 14 MPa after prior aging in the 8 h test; behavior converged after 168 h because grain-boundary Al₃Zr coarsened during testing | Creep ≥250 °C; no ≥1000 h exposure reported (griffiths2020coarseningandcreep pages 1-2) |
| Al–8.6Cu–0.5Mn–0.9Zr (wt.%), Michi et al. (2023) | Tensile creep at 300 °C in as-fabricated and heat-treated conditions | Stress producing a minimum creep rate of 10⁻⁷ s⁻¹ fell from 56 MPa as fabricated to 30 MPa after 400 °C/200 h aging; heat treatment promoted precipitate-free zones and grain-boundary sliding | Creep ≥250 °C; aging was only 200 h (michi2023loadshufflingduring pages 30-33, michi2023loadshufflingduring pages 27-30) |
| Al–0.4Zr–1X (at.%), X = Mn, Cr, V, Mo or W, Ekaputra et al. (2026) | Compressive creep at 400 °C; separate **unstressed** aging at 400 °C for as long as 2016 h | Every ternary alloy was substantially more creep resistant than binary Al–0.4Zr. Mn and Cr precipitated during aging, whereas V, Mo and W remained almost entirely in solution after 2016 h; their room-temperature strengthening contribution remained broadly stable | Creep ≥250 °C **and** unstressed aging ≥1000 h (ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 1-4, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 21-25) |
| Al–10Ce–8Mn (wt.%), Sisco et al. This report did not measure creep | Unstressed thermal stability ≥1000 h only (sisco2023heterogeneousphasetransformation pages 5-8, sisco2023heterogeneousphasetransformation pages 13-18) |
| Al–5.0Mn–0.8Cr–0.6Zr (wt.%, Alloy C), Mehta et al. At 1078 h total, precipitates occupied about 18 area% and included Cr-bearing Al₁₂Mn and Al₆Mn | Unstressed thermal stability ≥1000 h; no creep reported (mehta2023advancingnovelalmncrzr pages 7-9, mehta2024roleofcr pages 3-4) |

The nanoscale α-Al/Al₆Fe cellular structure largely dissolved, spherical metastable Al₆Fe particles grew, and stable plate-shaped θ-Al₁₃Fe₄ precipitated | Unstressed thermal stability ≥1000 h only (wang2022microstructuralvariationsin pages 5-8, wang2022microstructuralvariationsin media a4102c0d) |

(2026) | Tensile creep rupture at 400 °C under 25–40 MPa; approximately 330 h life at 25 MPa | Direct stress-rupture result for an as-printed alloy strengthened by about 14 vol.% α-Al(FeMnNi)Si intermetallic nanophases; the longest separate thermal exposure reported was 168 h, not ≥1000 h | Creep and stress rupture ≥250 °C; no ≥1000 h exposure (li2026strongyetductile pages 6-11, li2026strongyetductile pages 11-16) |


*Table: Primary studies documenting actual creep or rupture testing at ≥250 °C, or unstressed thermal exposure lasting ≥1000 h at ≥250 °C. Creep duration and long-term aging are kept distinct to avoid treating censored or shorter creep tests as 1000-hour demonstrations.*

The clearest **stress-rupture measurements** include LPBF Al–10Ce: in Stinehart’s 2023 study, tensile specimens ruptured at 325 °C/21 MPa after 92.17 h, at 350 °C/21 MPa after 18.17 h, and at 350 °C/29 MPa after 6.1 h. Specimens tested at 300 °C/21 MPa and 300 °C/29 MPa were stopped *without rupture* after more than 765 and 546 h, respectively. The same work also measured compressive creep over 275–400 °C. A different, Sc-free LPBF Al–7.44Si–2.34Fe–1.79Mn–1.12Ni alloy was reported to last approximately 330 h in tensile creep at 400 °C/25 MPa. None of these results establishes a ≥1000 h rupture life. (stinehart2023creepresistanceand pages 31-39, stinehart2023creepresistanceand pages 23-31, stinehart2023creepresistanceand pages 39-47, li2026strongyetductile pages 6-11, li2026strongyetductile pages 11-16)

For the alloy families named in the question, **Al–Cu–Mn–Zr has direct creep data**: Michi and colleagues tested LPBF Al–8.6Cu–0.5Mn–0.9Zr at 300 °C. The stress associated with a minimum creep rate of 10⁻⁷ s⁻¹ was 56 MPa as fabricated, versus 30 MPa after a 400 °C/200 h pretreatment; this is evidence of creep measurement, not of 1000 h thermal stability. Griffiths and colleagues measured compressive creep of two Sc-free LPBF Al–Mg–Zr alloys at 260 °C, including an approximately 168 h test. Ekaputra and colleagues measured compressive creep of LPBF Al–0.4Zr–1X (at.%; X = Mn, Cr, V, Mo or W) at 400 °C; all five ternary compositions outperformed their binary Al–Zr comparator. (michi2023loadshufflingduring pages 30-33, michi2023loadshufflingduring pages 27-30, griffiths2020coarseningandcreep pages 1-2, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 1-4, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 21-25)

The **≥1000 h thermal-stability evidence** is also direct, but its conclusions differ by alloy. LPBF Al–10Ce–8Mn underwent 400 °C/1000 h exposure: the investigators observed decomposition of Al₂₀Mn₂Ce and formation of Al₆Mn, Al₁₂Mn and Al₅₁Mn₇Ce₄; this is measured phase evolution, *not* proof that the initial microstructure remained unchanged. For LPBF Al–5Mn–0.8Cr–0.6Zr (‘alloy C’), hardness fell by approximately 17–18 HV, or 13%, over 350 °C/1000 h; a related study characterized precipitates after 1078 h total at 350 °C. The suggestion that a different, Mg-containing variant would retain 140–150 HV after 1000 h was an **estimate**, not that variant’s measured result. (sisco2023heterogeneousphasetransformation pages 5-8, sisco2023heterogeneousphasetransformation pages 13-18, mehta2023advancingnovelalmncrzr pages 7-9, mehta2024roleofcr pages 3-4)

Two further long exposures broaden the evidence. LPBF Al–15Fe was examined after 300 °C/1000 h: hardness decreased moderately, the initial nanoscale Al/Al₆Fe cellular structure changed, and stable Al₁₃Fe₄ precipitated; the study’s hardness-versus-time figure includes the 1000 h measurement. In LPBF Al–0.4Zr–1X, exposure extended to **2016 h at 400 °C**: Mn and Cr showed precipitation, whereas V, Mo and W remained almost entirely in solution; the authors reported broadly stable room-temperature strengthening contributions from the ternary solutes. These are observations of *unstressed* specimens and should not be reported as creep lives. (wang2022microstructuralvariationsin pages 5-8, wang2022microstructuralvariationsin media a4102c0d, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 1-4, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 21-25)

**Bottom line:** the answer is unambiguously yes, including for Al–Ce, Al–Mn–Cr–Zr, Al–Cu–Mn–Zr and Al–Zr–Mn systems. What is substantially less established by the verified examples is **stress-rupture survival for ≥1000 h** at these temperatures: the cited ≥1000 h results are thermal-aging or microstructural measurements, while the specific rupture tests above ended sooner or were stopped before rupture. (stinehart2023creepresistanceand pages 39-47, mehta2023advancingnovelalmncrzr pages 7-9, sisco2023heterogeneousphasetransformation pages 13-18, ekaputra2026coarseningandcreepbehavior pages 1-4)
