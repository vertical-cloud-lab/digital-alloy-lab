**Question (PRECEDENT):** Has anyone measured the high-strain-rate (100 1/s or more, for example with a split Hopkinson bar) tensile or compressive response of laser powder bed fusion aluminum alloys other than AlSi10Mg, such as Scalmalloy, A20X/A205, AlSi7Mg, Al-Ce or Al-Fe-Zr alloys, including the effect of test temperature?

---

Question: Has anyone measured the high-strain-rate (100 1/s or more, for example with a split Hopkinson bar) tensile or compressive response of laser powder bed fusion aluminum alloys other than AlSi10Mg, such as Scalmalloy, A20X/A205, AlSi7Mg, Al-Ce or Al-Fe-Zr alloys, including the effect of test temperature?

Yes. ## High-strain-rate measurements beyond AlSi10Mg

**Yes.** Published experiments establish high-rate tensile response for LPBF **Scalmalloy**, and compressive response for LPBF **AlSi12** and **Al6061**. A 2026 thesis additionally reports high-rate tension of two modified LPBF Al6061 materials. Of these verified studies, **AlSi12 is the clearest example in which specimen temperature was varied during high-rate testing**: split-Hopkinson compression was performed at room temperature and 200 °C. Ageing a specimen before a room-temperature test is a different experiment. (ponnusamy2018highstrainrate pages 1-3, ponnusamy2018highstrainrate pages 3-7, jakkula2021strainratesensitivity pages 1-2, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 1-6, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 17-21)

The verified experiments are summarized below. “Temperature” means the specimen’s temperature *during loading*, unless explicitly identified as a prior treatment. (ponnusamy2018highstrainrate pages 3-7, jakkula2021strainratesensitivity pages 1-2, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 53-57, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 21-25)

### What the measurements show

- **Scalmalloy (AA5028):** Jakkula and colleagues used an EOS laser-powder-bed system and a split-Hopkinson **tension** bar. Their 2021 conference report and journal paper describe the same experimental programme, not two independent datasets: tests at approximately **1,000 s⁻¹**, with lower-rate comparators, on as-built and **325 °C/4 h pre-aged** material. At the high rate, the as-built and pre-aged ultimate tensile strengths were **342.5 ± 1.6** and **497.0 ± 3.0 MPa**, respectively; failure strains were **29.5 ± 0.4%** and **15.0 ± 2.2%**. All mechanical tests were at **room temperature**. Ultimate strength declined weakly as rate increased, whereas failure strain increased at the highest rate. The authors explicitly caution that their tests cannot disentangle intrinsic rate effects from adiabatic heating and call for experiments that vary *test* temperature at fixed rate. Their target 1,000 s⁻¹ rate was reached **after yield**, an important qualification when interpreting the reported yield stresses. (jakkula2021strainratesensitivity pages 1-2, jakkula2021strainratesensitivity pages 2-5, jakkula2021strainratesensitivity pages 5-7, jakkula2021strainratesensitivitya pages 1-3, jakkula2021strainratesensitivitya pages 3-6, jakkula2021strainratesensitivity media 0830a462)

- **AlSi12:** Ponnusamy and colleagues’ 2018 paper reports split-Hopkinson **compression** of horizontally built SLM cylinders, compared with quasi-static compression at the same specimen temperatures. Maximum dynamic rates were **750 s⁻¹ at room temperature** and **1,500 s⁻¹ at 200 °C**. For the hot test, they heated the **specimen**, leaving the bars at room temperature. They report a *negative* apparent rate effect at room temperature but a *positive* one at 200 °C; their stress–strain plots display those comparisons. Because the two dynamic temperature conditions also used **different maximum rates**, this is evidence of temperature-dependent high-rate response, **not** an isolated measurement of temperature dependence at one fixed high rate. A later paper entitled *Dynamic compressive behaviour of selective laser melted AlSi12 alloy: Effect of elevated temperature and heat treatment* (Ponnusamy et al., *Additive Manufacturing* **36**, 101614, 2020; DOI **10.1016/j.addma.2020.101614**) is another directly relevant lead, but its full text could not be verified here; no additional test temperatures or numerical outcomes are attributed to it. (ponnusamy2018highstrainrate pages 1-3, ponnusamy2018highstrainrate pages 3-7, ponnusamy2018highstrainrate media a89c0989, ponnusamy2018highstrainrate media c5e4830e)

- **Al6061:** Yilmaz and colleagues’ published study, *Mechanical behavior of Al6061 produced by laser powder bed fusion: Effects of heat treatments and strain rates* (*Materials Science and Engineering A* **950**, 149574, 2026; DOI **10.1016/j.msea.2025.149574**), is reproduced as a chapter of Yilmaz’s accessible 2026 thesis. It reports split-Hopkinson **compression at 500–800 and 1,500–1,800 s⁻¹**. At 15% plastic strain and the higher rate, flow stress was **333 ± 6 MPa as-built**, **348 ± 17 MPa direct-aged**, **246 ± 6 MPa annealed**, **238 ± 9 MPa T6**, and **231 ± 5 MPa after HIP**. Each condition had a higher flow stress under dynamic than quasi-static loading. These are comparisons of **prior thermal treatments at room-temperature test conditions**, not high-rate tests performed at the heat-treatment temperatures. The material retained approximately **3.5–5% porosity**, limiting generalization to denser LPBF Al6061. (yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 1-6, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 39-43, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior media 2ccf574d, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 17-21, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 21-25)

- **Modified Al6061:** A separate chapter of that thesis, described there as **submitted for publication**, reports room-temperature split-Hopkinson **tension** on LPBF Al6061 mixed with **0.3 wt% Sc** and on ceramic-inoculated **Al6061–RAM2**. The Sc material reached about **600 s⁻¹**, with **33 ± 8 MPa** ultimate strength and **0.5 ± 0.1%** elongation; severe interconnected porosity caused premature fracture. RAM2 reached **2,700 s⁻¹**, with **239 ± 3 MPa** ultimate strength and **21.0 ± 2.0%** elongation. These formulations are **not Scalmalloy or A205/A20X**, and the thesis does not vary their specimen test temperature. (yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 49-53, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 53-57, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 57-61, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 61-64, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior media 1dc0cfa3)

### Status of the other named alloys

I did **not identify a verified ≥100 s⁻¹ tensile or compressive test on LPBF A20X/A205, AlSi7Mg (A356/A357), an Al–Ce alloy, or Al–Fe–Zr**. This is a statement about the located literature, **not proof that no unpublished or unindexed measurement exists**. The distinction matters because several close-looking results fail the rate criterion: the LPBF **Al–Fe–Zr** report gives room-temperature tensile properties after **400 °C/4 h ageing**, not high-rate tests; LPBF **Al–Ce** literature reports elevated-temperature tensile strength and thermal exposure, but not a verified qualifying Hopkinson test; and the surveyed A356/A357 literature emphasizes tensile, fatigue and shear properties rather than qualifying high-rate uniaxial measurements. A 2025 LPBF **Al–Fe–Cr–X** study actually varied *test* temperature from room temperature to 400 °C, but its compression strain-rate steps were only **10⁻⁵–10⁻² s⁻¹**. Likewise, LPBF Al–Mn–Cr–Zr was tensile-tested up to 573 K, including a rate comparison reaching **1% s⁻¹ = 0.01 s⁻¹**—far below 100 s⁻¹. (pauzon2022anovellaser pages 1-4, liu2024reviewoflaser pages 23-25, pauzon2022anovellaser pages 4-8, baharisambran2025dynamicandstatic pages 4-6, ponnusamy2020mechanicalpropertiesof pages 40-42, ponnusamy2020mechanicalpropertiesof pages 38-40, mehta2024mechanicalpropertiesand pages 4-5)

**Bottom line:** For the specific combination of **LPBF, ≥100 s⁻¹ uniaxial response, and a deliberately varied specimen test temperature**, start with **Ponnusamy et al.’s AlSi12 work**. For room-temperature high-rate constitutive data beyond AlSi10Mg, **Scalmalloy and Al6061** have particularly explicit reported measurements. Do not treat a printing-bed temperature, ageing schedule, or quasi-static elevated-temperature tensile curve as a high-rate temperature study. (ponnusamy2018highstrainrate pages 3-7, jakkula2021strainratesensitivity pages 2-5, yilmaz2026strainratedependentmechanicalbehavior pages 39-43, pauzon2022anovellaser pages 4-8, mehta2024mechanicalpropertiesand pages 4-5)
