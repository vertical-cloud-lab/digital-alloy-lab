### 1. CALPHAD Software Vendors

**Thermo-Calc Software AB** (Stockholm, Sweden) is the most widely cited CALPHAD software provider. Its product suite includes the Thermo-Calc equilibrium calculator, DICTRA diffusion simulation software, and TC-PRISMA for precipitation kinetics, along with extensive commercial thermodynamic and kinetic databases (e.g., TCFE for steels, TCNI for Ni-alloys) (sundman2015opencalphada pages 3-4, cote2020ontheelemental pages 3-4). The software originated from academic research at the Royal Institute of Technology (KTH) in Stockholm and was commercialized as a spinoff company in the early 1990s (cote2020ontheelemental pages 3-4, perrut2015thermodynamicmodelingby pages 10-12). Thermo-Calc is routinely used in high-throughput CALPHAD calculations for alloy design and has been linked with AM process simulation tools such as Simufact Additive for multi-scale microstructure prediction.

**CompuTherm LLC** (Madison, WI, USA) develops the Pandat software platform, which includes PanEngine (for API-level integration), PanOptimizer (for thermodynamic assessment), and PanPrecipitation (for kinetic modeling), as well as proprietary databases such as PanAl for aluminum alloys (sundman2015opencalphada pages 3-4, cote2020ontheelemental pages 3-4, perrut2015thermodynamicmodelingby pages 10-12). CompuTherm has academic origins tied to the University of Wisconsin-Madison and its Pandat software is widely used in alloy design research.

**GTT Technologies / CRCT** (Aachen, Germany / Montreal, Canada) jointly develop FactSage, one of the largest integrated thermochemical computing platforms, along with the ChemApp programmer's library for embedding CALPHAD calculations into process models (sundman2015opencalphada pages 3-4, ende2018applicationsofthermodynamic pages 19-20). FactSage emerged from the merger of ChemSage and F*A*C*T software and has strong academic roots at the Centre for Research in Computational Thermochemistry (CRCT) at École Polytechnique de Montréal and RWTH Aachen University.

**Sente Software Ltd** (Guildford, UK) produces JMatPro, a Java-based materials properties simulation tool that integrates thermodynamic calculations with physical and mechanical property predictions for multi-component alloys (sundman2015opencalphada pages 3-4, cote2020ontheelemental pages 3-4). JMatPro was developed in collaboration with Thermotech and is used extensively in academic and industrial materials research.

**MatCalc** is an academic CALPHAD and precipitation kinetics software developed at TU Wien (Vienna, Austria), and **OpenCalphad** is an open-source thermodynamic software (GNU license) maintained by an international academic consortium, providing the first high-quality multicomponent open-source CALPHAD code (sundman2015opencalphada pages 3-4).

### 2. Materials Informatics and AI Platforms

**Citrine Informatics** (Redwood City, CA, USA) developed the Citrination platform, described as a "massively scalable data-driven modeling platform" that hosts one of the largest collections of open materials data (over three million records) and provides AI-driven sequential learning tools for experiment planning and materials discovery (tassone2019aggregationandstructuring pages 6-8, hill2016materialssciencewith pages 7-8, hill2016materialssciencewith pages 11-11). Citrine works with partners along the materials value chain, including collaborations with DOE national laboratories and Stanford's synchrotron facility (SLAC/SSRL), to accelerate advanced materials discovery (tassone2019aggregationandstructuring pages 6-8, ramakrishna2019materialsinformatics pages 2-4).

**Ansys (Granta Design)** (Cambridge, UK / Canonsburg, PA, USA) provides Granta MI for enterprise materials data management, Granta Selector for materials selection, and Granta EduPack for materials education, all incorporating comprehensive materials databases and AI-enabled analytics (papile2023materialscienceand pages 51-56, papile2023materialscienceand pages 40-43, papile2023materialscienceand pages 49-51). Granta Design, originally a University of Cambridge spinoff, maintains extensive university partnerships with institutions including Politecnico di Milano, University of Southern Denmark (SDU), Chalmers University, Complutense University of Madrid, and Montanuniversität Leoben, among others (papile2023materialscienceand pages 51-56, papile2023materialscienceand pages 40-43, tyler2022teachingengineeringdesign pages 10-13).

### 3. ICME Tools and Alloy Design Services

**QuesTek Innovations LLC** (Evanston, IL, USA) is the premier ICME-based alloy design company. Founded in 1997 as a spinoff from Northwestern University, QuesTek uses its proprietary ICMD® software platform and Materials by Design® methodology to computationally design new alloys (saal2015icmedesignof pages 1-10, kuehmann2011materialsbydesigncomputational pages 1-3). The company has designed and commercialized four Ferrium® steels (licensed to Carpenter Technology) and developed novel alloys for AM, including printable aluminum (QT-7X) and L1₂-strengthened high-temperature aluminum alloys (taskin2019ferrium®steelsand pages 5-6, olson2024genomicmaterialsdesign pages 24-29). QuesTek maintains deep academic ties: its materials models were built on 15 years of research with the Steel Research Group at Northwestern University (kuehmann2000computationaldesignof pages 3-6), and it collaborates with MIT (through a Computational Materials Design class) (olson2024genomicmaterialsdesign pages 24-29), Illinois Institute of Technology (liang2024innovativedesignof pages 7-8), and the CHiMaD Center for Hierarchical Materials Design (a Materials Genome Initiative center). QuesTek has received over 70 SBIR/STTR awards and has participated in DARPA "Open Manufacturing" and over 60 AM-related projects (saal2015icmedesignof pages 1-10, olson2024genomicmaterialsdesign pages 24-29).

### 4. AM Process Simulation Platforms

**Ansys Inc.** (Canonsburg, PA, USA) provides the ANSYS Additive Suite (including Additive Print and Additive Science) for part-scale thermal and mechanical simulation of laser powder bed fusion (LPBF), as well as ANSYS Fluent for melt pool CFD modeling (johnson2024monitoringmodelingand pages 42-44, leon2025areviewof pages 17-18, mukalay2024selectivelasermelting pages 29-30). Ansys maintains an extensive academic licensing program.

**Hexagon (MSC Software)** (Newport Beach, CA, USA) develops Simufact Additive, which supports PBF (DMLS, EBM) and DED process simulation including thermal modeling, distortion prediction, and compensation, with cloud computing capabilities (kim2019investigationtometal pages 3-5, mukalay2024selectivelasermelting pages 26-26). Simufact has been linked with Thermo-Calc for macro-to-micro scale AM modeling.

**Flow Science Inc.** (Santa Fe, NM, USA) develops FLOW-3D AM and FLOW-3D WELD for high-fidelity CFD simulation of melt pool dynamics, powder-bed interactions, phase changes, and Marangoni convection during AM processes (leon2025areviewof pages 18-19, kim2019investigationtometal pages 3-5).

**Altair (Additive Works)** (Troy, MI, USA / Bremen, Germany) provides Amphyon for build process simulation, support structure optimization, and distortion compensation in SLM/DMLS, with integration into the HyperWorks ecosystem (kim2019investigationtometal pages 3-5, mukalay2024selectivelasermelting pages 26-26).

**COMSOL Inc.** (Stockholm, Sweden / Burlington, MA, USA) offers COMSOL Multiphysics, a general-purpose finite element platform widely used in academic AM research for multi-physics modeling of thermal, mechanical, and fluid phenomena at multiple scales (leon2025areviewof pages 18-19, leon2025areviewof pages 17-18).

**Autodesk Inc.** (San Francisco, CA, USA) provides Netfabb for AM design, simulation, and build preparation, supporting both PBF and DED technologies (johnson2024monitoringmodelingand pages 42-44, kim2019investigationtometal pages 3-5).

**AlphaSTAR Corporation** (Long Beach, CA, USA) develops GENOA 3DP, a multi-scale simulation platform for AM that integrates with ABAQUS FEM for progressive failure analysis and property prediction (johnson2024monitoringmodelingand pages 42-44, kim2019investigationtometal pages 3-5).

### Summary

All identified companies maintain some form of university or academic research engagement, ranging from direct spinoff origins (QuesTek from Northwestern, Thermo-Calc from KTH, Granta from Cambridge, CompuTherm from UW-Madison, FactSage from CRCT/GTT academic groups) to broad academic licensing and collaborative research programs. The ecosystem spans the full process–structure–property–performance chain needed for computational alloy design for additive manufacturing, from fundamental thermodynamic databases (CALPHAD), through materials informatics and machine learning (Citrine, Granta), to integrated design platforms (QuesTek ICMD) and multi-scale process simulation tools (Ansys, Simufact, FLOW-3D, COMSOL, and others).
