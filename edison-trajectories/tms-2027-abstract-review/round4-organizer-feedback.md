## Organizer Response

### 1. Verdict — ACCEPT with two minor wording fixes

The revised abstract is stronger than the round-3 version. The explicit 15-element palette replaces the
vague three-alloy enumeration with concrete, auditable specificity — exactly what the committee wants
from an early-stage group establishing credibility. "Deliberately spanning domestically secure and
import-reliant compositions" is honestly constructed: it does *not* claim all 15 elements are
domestically secure, only that the palette contrasts the two categories.

**Supply-chain mapping of the 15 elements (US net import reliance):**

| Element | Status | ~US import reliance | Undercuts "supply-chain-resilient"? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mn | Import-reliant | ~100% | Yes |
| Cr | Import-reliant | ~100% (chromite/ferrochromium) | Yes |
| Zr | Mixed | ~20–50% | Mildly |
| Mg | Mixed | ~50–70% | Mildly |
| Si | Domestically secure | <50% | No |
| Cu | Domestically secure | <50% | No |
| Ti | Domestically secure | <50% | No |
| Fe | Domestically secure | <20% | No |
| Ni | Mixed | ~40–60% | Mildly |
| Ce | Import-reliant | >95% (REE) | **Yes — major** |
| Sc | Import-reliant | 100% | **Yes — major** |
| Li | Mixed / import-reliant | ~50–75% | Yes |
| Er | Import-reliant | >95% (REE) | **Yes — major** |
| Zn | Domestically secure / mixed | ~20–40% | Mildly |
| Sn | Import-reliant | ~75% | Yes |

**Elements that undercut a purely "resilient" framing:** Sc (100% import), Ce and Er (>95% REE import,
China-dominated) are the most problematic; Cr, Mn, and Sn are also import-heavy. But the abstract does
*not* claim these are secure — it says the palette *spans* secure and import-reliant compositions, which
is accurate. Including Sc/Ce/Er is metallurgically defensible (Al₃Sc grain refinement, Ce mischmetal
alloys, Er microalloying) and the point is precisely to evaluate whether performance justifies the
supply-chain risk. **Net: the palette strengthens credibility. No change needed.**

### 2. "(sub-45 μm powder yield)" — reintroduces the round-3 over-claim risk

Published rePOWDER Al–Cu data shows d50 ≈ 65 μm (d10 ≈ 36 μm); Al-matrix composites average 88–170 μm.
"Sub-45 μm powder yield" implies the LPBF cut is the primary product, which the data do not support for
most compositions. Anderson himself framed the 15–45 μm cut as a yield challenge even for optimized gas
atomization (targeting only ~30% yield). **Fix:** "(sub-45 μm powder yield)" → **"(including the sub-45
μm fraction)"** — states the fraction is *characterized*, not that it dominates output.

### 3. LPBF single-track melt-pool component — net positive, needs scope-hedging

Single-track (SST) experiments are an established, low-powder-consumption rapid-screening method in LPBF
alloy development (Marola 2020; Aversa 2019; Piedra 2026). The SST addition *strengthens* the story by
closing the atomization-to-printability loop that Anderson cared about. But "perform LPBF single-track
melt pools … " as a blanket promise across all compositions overextends an early-stage group. **Fix:**
scope it — **"For selected compositions we perform LPBF single-track scans with cross-sectional
melt-pool characterization."**

### 4. Remaining over-claims

- **"Dozens of runs" vs. 15-element scope:** ambitious but plausible on rePOWDER; the abstract wisely
  does not claim full factorial coverage. Acceptable.
- **"Statistical models to guide experimental design":** reads as DOE, not physics-based modeling — no
  over-claim. Fine as worded.
- **"Inform alloy selection for gas-atomization scale-up":** appropriate aspirational-but-honest bridge
  to the symposium's core theme (accepted in round 3).
- **Near-future framing / dropping FLAIME:** both appropriate for an abstract submitted ~12 months out.

### 5. Committee's preferred ≤150-word version

> Iver Anderson's career defined oxygen control and fine-powder yield as major challenges for aluminum
> additive-manufacturing feedstock. Ultrasonic atomization can act as a rapid alloy-screening route to
> laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) feedstock, but this has been largely unstudied for novel,
> supply-chain-resilient aluminum alloys in the US. We assemble a parameter-property dataset from dozens
> of in-house ultrasonic atomization runs on an AMAZEMET rePOWDER system, deliberately spanning
> domestically secure and import-reliant compositions from a palette of 15 non-aluminum alloying
> elements (Mn, Cr, Zr, Mg, Si, Cu, Ti, Fe, Ni, Ce, Sc, Li, Er, Zn, Sn). We vary vibration amplitude,
> melt superheat, and atomization atmosphere, and characterize particle size distribution (including the
> sub-45 μm fraction), sphericity, satellite content, flowability, and oxygen pickup. For selected
> compositions we perform LPBF single-track scans with cross-sectional melt-pool characterization. We
> leverage statistical models to guide experimental design and identify compositions meriting
> gas-atomization scale-up.

**Changes from the submitted version:** (i) "(sub-45 μm powder yield)" → "(including the sub-45 μm
fraction)"; (ii) "perform LPBF single-track melt pools with associated cross-sectional
characterization" → "For selected compositions we perform LPBF single-track scans with cross-sectional
melt-pool characterization"; (iii) "inform composition-process windows, and inform alloy selection for
gas-atomization scale-up" → "identify compositions meriting gas-atomization scale-up" (removes the
redundant double-"inform").

**Final disposition: ACCEPT for oral slot, contingent on adopting the two wording fixes above.**
