### 2. Specificity vs. Risk

**Recommendation: Do NOT add a specific D50 or oxygen ppmw number at this stage.** For an early-stage group, the downside of promising a concrete preliminary result that must then be reproduced at the meeting outweighs the marginal credibility gain. UA parameter–property relationships show meaningful variability even within a single alloy system—for instance, Yankin et al. (2025) documented up to 37.5% variation in PSD span across UA process settings for AlSi12 alone (yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 1-2), and the one published AMAZEMET rePOWDER Al-alloy dataset (Al-10Cu) yielded a d50 of 65 µm, which is coarser than the 15–45 µm LPBF window (ukabhai2025investigationofalcu pages 3-7). Locking in a number now creates risk that it shifts by the time of the presentation, which is more damaging to credibility than staying appropriately general. The current abstract's structure—listing what will be characterized and promising "emerging parameter–property trends"—is the safer and more defensible choice for ProgramMaster submission.

Similarly, replacing "dozens of UA campaigns" with an exact count (e.g., "32 campaigns") would add modest credibility, but only do this if the count is truly final and will not change with additional experimental work before the meeting. If the campaign count is still growing, "dozens" is the prudent phrasing.

### 3. Remaining Over-Claims or Fundamentals Issues

Three items that an expert committee member (particularly one versed in Anderson's gas-atomization work) would still flag:

**(a) "The recoverable 15–45 µm fraction."** This phrasing subtly implies a meaningful yield in the LPBF-grade cut. However, published UA aluminum datasets suggest median particle sizes often fall above 45 µm (e.g., d50 ≈ 65 µm for Al-Cu on rePOWDER) (ukabhai2025investigationofalcu pages 3-7), and the 15–45 µm sub-fraction may be a small tail of the distribution rather than the bulk product. The word "recoverable" is technically defensible but could mislead an Anderson-symposium audience accustomed to thinking about gas-atomization yield optimization. **Fix:** Replace with "the sub-45 µm fraction relevant to LPBF." This is neutral on yield magnitude and avoids any implication of high throughput.

**(b) "Inform subsequent gas-atomization trials."** This is the abstract's weakest logical link. UA does not directly map to gas-atomization process parameters (nozzle pressure, gas-to-melt ratio, etc.). What UA screening realistically does is identify which alloy compositions are worth the cost and time of a full gas-atomization campaign—a role well-supported in the literature (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 1-2, hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 9-11, hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 11-12). Hinrichs et al. (2021) explicitly used UA as a flexible, laboratory-scale alloy development tool before scaling to electrode induction gas atomization (EIGA) for industrial quantities of the same compositions (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 1-2). **Fix:** Replace "inform subsequent gas-atomization trials" with "guide alloy selection for subsequent gas-atomization scale-up." This is precise about what UA actually de-risks.

**(c) UA of aluminum and oxygen pickup.** The oxygen framing ("a known UA limitation to be quantified rather than assumed") is well-phrased and honest. One additional nuance: for aluminum specifically, the issue is compounded by the metal's low density and rapid droplet oxidation requiring substantial ultrasonic amplitudes for continuous atomization (yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 1-2). The abstract handles this correctly by not claiming oxygen will be low—only that it will be measured. No change needed, but be prepared for Q&A on whether UA oxygen levels will be too high for LPBF qualification (typical LPBF-grade AlSi10Mg specs are ≤ 400–600 ppmw O). Anderson's own GARS work demonstrated that atomization atmosphere management is critical for controlling oxide film thickness and chemistry on aluminum powders (anderson2001determiningtherole pages 1-2, anderson2001determiningtherole pages 9-10), so the committee will expect the authors to engage with this point substantively.
