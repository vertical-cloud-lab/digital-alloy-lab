## Established protocols and evaluation criteria for validating Al-alloy powders and builds during LPBF scale-up

This synthesis summarizes how aluminum-alloy LPBF programs typically progress from early parameter screening (e.g., single tracks) to production validation, what coupons/builds are standard at each step, which quality metrics and defect modes are most emphasized for Al alloys, and which ISO/ASTM standards are commonly invoked for qualification.

### (1) Standard process-parameter validation builds

**Single tracks / single scan tracks (SST).** A widely used first step is single-track testing (on bulk or powder bed) and/or repeated tracks to form thin-wall “tin-like” structures. These tests quantify melt pool/track geometry (width, depth, length) and stability, and can directly screen regimes prone to lack of fusion, balling, or keyholing before committing to volumetric coupon campaigns. Pfaff et al. describe this explicitly as the entry point to their structured parameter-development method and report geometric screening criteria (e.g., ratios involving track width/depth/length) used to exclude unstable regimes. (pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 1-3, pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 3-5, pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 10-13, pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 8-10)

**Volumetric DoE coupons (multi-parameter matrices).** After track-based down-selection, iterative DoE (e.g., central composite design) is commonly used to find a volumetric process window for the new alloy/powder, rather than relying solely on volumetric energy density. (pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 1-3, pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 10-13)

**Density cubes/coupons (often ~10 mm cubes).** A standard “bulk” coupon for parameter development is a small cube used for rapid density/porosity screening. Gheysen et al. validate an efficient workflow on AlSi12 that uses SST → cubes → tensile specimens, with density screening by Archimedes and achieved densities >99.3% in the validated window. (gheysen2021efficientoptimizationmethodology pages 1-2)

**Mechanical-test specimens (tensile, orientation sets).** Mechanical coupons (typically tensile) are then built—often in multiple orientations—to demonstrate that the parameter set produces repeatable, application-relevant properties and to quantify anisotropy. Industrial qualification workflows described in the literature emphasize destructive testing of witness coupons (e.g., tensile bars) as part of qualification. (calignano2019ametalpowder pages 11-13, brenner2025improvingverticaldimensional pages 1-6, leon2025areviewof pages 23-25)

### (2) Critical quality metrics and typical targets for Al-alloy LPBF

**Relative density / porosity.** Density is a primary process-window discriminator during scale-up. Literature on Al-Si-Mg reports high-density windows (e.g., “99% dense parts” in one cited window) and uses density/porosity strongly as a go/no-go metric because pores degrade mechanical and fatigue behavior. (trevisan2018studyandcharacterisation pages 87-92, trevisan2018studyandcharacterisation pages 92-95)

A concrete, industry-facing example for **AlSi10Mg** (Nikon/SLM parameter sets) reports minimum relative density targets of **99.9%** for a 30 µm “Precision” parameter set and **99.5%** for 60–90 µm “Prime/Productivity” sets. (brenner2025improvingverticaldimensional pages 1-6, brenner2025improvingverticaldimensional media c7a963d0)

**Surface roughness.** For AlSi10Mg, the same parameter-set documentation reports as-built roughness statistics (Ra/Rz), illustrating that “production” parameter sets can tolerate substantially rougher as-built surfaces than “precision” parameter sets. Example values include **Ra mean 5 µm, Rz mean 64 µm** (Precision) and **Ra mean 23 µm, Rz mean 134 µm** (Prime). (brenner2025improvingverticaldimensional pages 1-6, brenner2025improvingverticaldimensional media c7a963d0)

**Dimensional accuracy / geometric capability.** Dimensional accuracy evaluation can be standardized using ISO/ASTM test artifacts. Brenner & Nedeljkovic-Groha demonstrate use of the **ISO/ASTM 52902 Z-artefact** for AlSi10Mg, measured by structured-light 3D scanning. They show that applying a shrinkage compensation factor reduced **RMSE from >100 µm to <25 µm**, improving achievable ISO tolerance grades (IT 9–11 to IT 5–9) without changing process parameters. (brenner2025improvingverticaldimensional pages 1-6)

### (3) Defect types most important to screen for in Al-alloy LPBF

Across Al alloys, the defect screening emphasis differs somewhat between castable Al-Si(-Mg) alloys and crack-sensitive high-strength wrought alloys.

**Porosity families (dominant for Al-Si-Mg):**
- **Lack-of-fusion porosity** from insufficient overlap/energy input. (trevisan2018studyandcharacterisation pages 92-95, pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 3-5)
- **Keyhole porosity** and melt-pool instability at high energy input / inappropriate scan conditions. (trevisan2018studyandcharacterisation pages 92-95, pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 3-5)
- **Hydrogen/metallurgical porosity** (often spherical), which can be mitigated by powder conditioning such as pre-drying (reported as reducing hydrogen porosity). (trevisan2018studyandcharacterisation pages 92-95)

**Oxide-related defects (aluminum-specific):**
- Oxide films (Al2O3) on powder surfaces can be entrained as inclusions or contribute to irregular porosity; this is repeatedly highlighted as an Al-alloy LPBF challenge. (kumar2021additivemanufacturingof pages 1-5, trevisan2018studyandcharacterisation pages 92-95)

**Solidification cracking / hot tearing (dominant for high-strength Al):**
- For Al2024, Kumar et al. report **hot tearing/hot cracking** increasing at higher energy density (along columnar grain boundaries), while **void formation** is promoted at low energy density. (kumar2021additivemanufacturingof pages 1-5)

**Common inspection modalities used in qualification contexts** include metallography/microscopy (for pore morphology, melt pool boundaries), and industrial NDE such as **CT, ultrasonic testing, dye penetrant**, and optical/blue-light scanning, used alongside destructive witness-coupon testing. (calignano2019ametalpowder pages 11-13, trevisan2018studyandcharacterisation pages 87-92, trevisan2018studyandcharacterisation pages 92-95)

### (4) Typical progression from “single track works” to “validated for production parts”

The most commonly documented progression is a staged funnel:

1. **Powder qualification + machine readiness checks** (incoming verification, preventive-maintenance checklists, and powder storage/handling controls). (calignano2019ametalpowder pages 13-15, juhasz2020inandexsitu pages 95-100)
2. **Single-track/SST screening** to map stable melt-pool regimes and avoid obvious LOF/balling/keyhole conditions; optionally thin-wall or repeated-track geometries to simplify measurement. (pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 1-3, pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 3-5, pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 8-10)
3. **Iterative volumetric DoE** to define a practical process window (power/speed/hatch/layer thickness interactions) for bulk properties. (pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 10-13, pfaff2020anempiricalapproach pages 1-3)
4. **Density cubes/coupons** (often Archimedes density screening) to down-select to high-density settings; Gheysen et al. validate this as part of an efficient AlSi12 campaign. (gheysen2021efficientoptimizationmethodology pages 1-2)
5. **Mechanical coupons (tensile, multiple orientations)** to confirm property capability and repeatability; broader qualification discussions point to ASTM E8 tensile data as a common mechanical-basis element. (leon2025areviewof pages 23-25, calignano2019ametalpowder pages 11-13)
6. **Geometric capability and surface reference artifacts** (e.g., ISO/ASTM 52902 Z-artefact) to quantify and compensate systematic dimensional errors, and to set/verify achievable tolerances. (brenner2025improvingverticaldimensional pages 1-6)
7. **Industrial qualification lock-down**: apply NDE (CT/UT/dye penetrant) and post-process controls (stress relief, support removal, cleaning, finishing, optional HIP) with traceability; repeat across powder batches/systems to demonstrate robustness, and then transition to **part-specific qualification** based on risk and critical features. (calignano2019ametalpowder pages 11-13, wells2019metalliclaserbeam pages 1-12)

### (5) ASTM/ISO standards governing AM qualification (process, parts, artifacts, inspection)

Within the evidence set, the most directly cited and relevant standards families for LPBF qualification include:

- **ISO/ASTM 52930**: IQ/OQ/PQ framework for PBF-LB equipment qualification. (leon2025areviewof pages 23-25)
- **ISO/ASTM 52902**: geometric capability test artifacts (used in AlSi10Mg Z-artefact demonstration). (brenner2025improvingverticaldimensional pages 1-6, leon2025areviewof pages 31-33)
- **ISO/ASTM 52904**: process characteristics and performance for metal PBF (frequently referenced in qualification/standard lists). (leon2025areviewof pages 31-33)
- **ISO/ASTM 52907**: methods to characterize metal powders. (bergaliyeva2024standardizationofrecycled pages 41-43)
- **ISO/ASTM 52936-1**: qualification principles and preparation of test specimens for PBF-LB (listed in standards compendium). (bergaliyeva2024standardizationofrecycled pages 41-43)
- **ISO/ASTM TR 52906**: NDE guidance with intentionally seeded flaws (listed in standards compendium). (bergaliyeva2024standardizationofrecycled pages 41-43)
- **ISO/ASTM 52908**: finished part properties, post-processing, inspection and testing. (leon2025areviewof pages 31-33)
- **ASTM E8**: tensile testing basis commonly used for metallic coupon qualification in LPBF programs. (leon2025areviewof pages 23-25)

In addition, multiple qualification discussions cite **SAE AMS 7003** (LPBF process) and ASTM standards for QA acceleration and post-processing (e.g., ASTM F3626, ASTM F3530) as part of the broader qualification toolchain. (leon2025areviewof pages 23-25, bergaliyeva2024standardizationofrecycled pages 41-43)

### (6) Abbreviated validation protocols for high-throughput alloy/process screening

High-throughput programs commonly replace full tensile/density campaigns (initially) with miniaturized coupons and automated, spatially resolved measurements, then promote only top candidates into full qualification.

**LPBF combinatorial builds (parameter libraries in a single part).** Weaver et al. demonstrate a combinatorial LPBF sample that embeds **20 power/speed combinations** (with repeats) into one build, with high-throughput characterization performed at a single Z-plane using automated optical porosity analysis, EBSD, XRD, and very high replicate counts of nanoindentation and Vickers microhardness. The authors estimate ~**5×** time/cost throughput improvement versus producing many separate “regular” coupons, and show that the library can screen conditions for lack-of-fusion (low power/speed ratios) vs keyholing/spherical porosity (high ratios), thereby functioning as an efficient prescreen before full campaigns. (weaver2021demonstrationofa pages 5-10, weaver2021demonstrationofa pages 14-19, weaver2021demonstrationofa pages 10-14, weaver2021demonstrationofa pages 33-37)

**Miniaturized sample-library mounts with automated positioning.** HT-READ proposes a 32 mm mount containing a ring of **16 discrete 3D-printed samples** designed to simplify automated characterization (XRD/SEM/EDS/EBSD) and accelerate closed-loop alloy screening. (vecchio2021highthroughputrapidexperimental pages 1-4)

**Miniature tensile “mass screening” (not LPBF-specific, but illustrates abbreviated qualification logic).** A high-throughput AM review describes combinatorial AM methods capable of producing **>200 miniature tensile samples** for rapid composition screening (demonstrated in LDED); the same principle—using many small specimens to down-select before full-size qualification—maps to LPBF alloy screening even though laser-parameter retuning remains necessary for each new composition. (liu2024high‐throughputpreparationfor pages 13-13)
