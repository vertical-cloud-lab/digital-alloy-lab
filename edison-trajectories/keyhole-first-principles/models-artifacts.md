| **Kaplan (1994)** | At every wall point, absorbed irradiance balances heat flow into the translating workpiece: $q''_{abs}=q''_{cond}$. The Rosenthal moving-line-source field uses $K_0$ and $Pe'=v/\alpha$; radial differentiation gives conductive flux through $K_1$. The local wall angle is chosen geometrically so projected absorbed intensity equals the local loss. Front and rear walls are marched downward until they intersect. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000007) | Quasi-steady translating source; constant effective properties; liquid–vapour interface at evaporation temperature; front/rear asymmetry arises from upstream conduction; initially direct Fresnel absorption, followed by a second iteration with plasma attenuation and multiple reflections. | Two-dimensional longitudinal profile with independently calculated front and rear walls; no imposed cone or rotational symmetry. | $T_w=T_v$, the evaporation/boiling temperature. | Gaussian-like beam; angle-dependent Fresnel absorption. Beer–Lambert plasma attenuation: $1-e^{-\alpha_{IB}\ell}$. Reflected power is propagated using Fresnel reflectivity and an estimated mean reflection count; direct Fresnel and multiple-reflection absorption dominate, whereas plasma absorption was small for the reported steel case. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000008) | Evaporation establishes the wall temperature and vapour/plasma state, but wall recession and a separate local $\dot mL_v$ sink are not central terms in the profile balance. Vapour thermodynamics, Knudsen-layer effects and plasma flow are treated after the first profile calculation. | Compared with measured steel penetration versus speed at 4 and 10 kW CO₂-laser conditions. Predictions generally lay within experimental scatter, but overpredicted depth at low speed, attributed to underestimated plume damping. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000009) | [10.1088/0022-3727/27/9/002](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/27/9/002) |
| **Lankalapalli, Tu & Gartner (1996)** | Cone radius $a(z)=a_0(1-z/d)$ and $Pe(z)=va(z)/(2\alpha)$. Each thin layer solves $\nabla^2T-(v/\alpha)\,\partial T/\partial x=0$. Conductive power per depth is $P'(z)=k(T_v-T_0)g[Pe(z)]$, and depth follows from $P_i=\int_0^dP'(z)\,dz$. (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012) | Quasi-steady state; complete absorption of the specified incident/absorbed power in the keyhole; constant effective properties; axial conduction $Q_z\ll Q_r$; melt convection neglected; keyhole depth equated to penetration depth. Effective $k$ and $\alpha$ were calibrated from weld data, so the published implementation is not parameter-free. | Imposed right circular cone, represented as stacked cylindrical slices; method can formally accept another prescribed radius–depth law. | Cylindrical wall held at $T_v$. | No predictive Fresnel/ray-tracing calculation; power delivered to the keyhole is taken as absorbed. | Latent heats of fusion and vaporization and explicit evaporation loss are neglected. (pqac-00000013) | Twenty-five power–speed combinations for 1010/1035 steel using a 5 kW CO₂ laser. Fitted values $\alpha=0.08$ cm² s⁻¹ and $k=0.765$ W cm⁻¹ K⁻¹ reproduced trends at 3–5 kW; errors grew when the actual keyhole departed from a cone or absorbed power was uncertain. (pqac-00000010) | [10.1088/0022-3727/29/7/018](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/29/7/018) |
| **Fabbro & Chouf (2000); Fabbro (2010, 2020)** | Piston/drilling law $V_d=k_dI_{abs}$; front-wall stationarity $V_d=v\cos\alpha$; $I_{abs}=I_0A(\alpha)\sin\alpha$, commonly $A(\alpha)=A_0(\sin\alpha)^q$, $q\approx0.1$. For a top-hat spot of diameter $D$, $L=(4A_0k_d/\pi)P/(Dv)$. Later conductive scaling gives $R=R_0/(1+v/V_0)$, with $R$ the aspect ratio. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018) | Stationary inclined front in the beam frame; recoil-driven lateral melt expulsion; uniform incident intensity in the simplest scaling; approximately constant material drilling coefficient; direct front-wall interception dominates low-aspect-ratio keyholes; secondary reflections often neglected. | Inclined planar front or half-cone at high speed/low aspect ratio; cylindrical or semicylindrical cavity used for deeper multireflection regimes. | Not simply imposed at normal boiling point in the fuller piston model: $T_s$ is obtained from energy, evaporation and recoil relations; threshold satisfies $p_r(T_{th})=p_{amb}$. | Fresnel-like $A(\alpha)$; simplest depth law uses constant $A_0$ and neglects multiple reflections. | Fuller model partitions $P_{abs}=P_m+P_{vap}+P_{cond}+P_{kin}$ and derives vapour flux/recoil from $T_s$; simplified scaling absorbs these processes into fitted/estimated $k_d$. (pqac-00000021) | Welding experiments supported $L\propto P/(Dv)$ and front-angle trends. In Cunningham’s LPBF Ti-6Al-4V X-rays, use of nominal 95 and 140 µm beam widths overpredicted depth by about $2\times$; replacing them by effective depression widths near 50 and 100 µm gave a very good fit. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000050, pqac-00000051) | [10.2351/1.521924](https://doi.org/10.2351/1.521924); [10.1088/0022-3727/43/44/445501](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/43/44/445501); [10.3390/app10041487](https://doi.org/10.3390/app10041487) |
| **Steen, Dowden, Davis & Kapadia (1988)** | Steady moving-frame conduction: $\alpha\nabla^2T=v\,\partial T/\partial x$. Temperature is the superposition of Rosenthal point-source, image-point-source and uniform line-source solutions; the line term contains $K_0$. The weld boundary is the isotherm $T=T_m$. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025) | Infinitesimally thin keyhole; constant properties; adiabatic top surface enforced by an image source; no recoil, free-surface dynamics, plume loss or self-consistent optical calculation; source strengths inferred from the weld profile. | Broad, approximately semicircular upper region from the point source plus a narrow, nearly parallel-sided lower region from the line source. | No keyhole-wall temperature condition; the predicted fusion boundary is $T=T_m$. | Absorbed power is divided phenomenologically between point strength $q_p$ and line strength $q_l$; no Fresnel or multiple-reflection optics. | No explicit evaporation or latent-heat sink; phase change is represented only by the melting isotherm. | Calculated profiles agreed reasonably with example weld cross-sections. In two examples, point-source fractions were 11% and 26%, and line-source fractions 64% and 19%; the model missed lower-third taper where line strength evidently decreased. (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000027) | [10.1088/0022-3727/21/8/002](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/21/8/002) |
| **Semak & Matsunawa (1997)** | Local optical/thermal balance: $I_{abs}=A(\alpha)I_{inc}\cos\alpha=-k\nabla_nT+I_v$; approximate evaporation intensity $I_v=A(\alpha)I_{inc}\cos\alpha-k(T_v-T_m)/\sqrt{\alpha/v}$. Mass flux $\dot m''=I_v/L_v$ and recoil $p_r=\dot m''v_T$, with $v_T\simeq\sqrt{\gamma R_sT_v}$. Melt acceleration is approximated by $\partial u_m/\partial t=(p_r-\sigma/r_l)/\rho_m$. (pqac-00000029, pqac-00000031) | Front wall receives the significant beam intensity; Gaussian variation in the travel direction and uniform transverse intensity; negligible beam divergence and plasma refraction; one-dimensional lateral melt ejection; recoil usually exceeds capillary and hydrostatic pressure; evaporated mass is small relative to expelled liquid. | Dynamic two-dimensional front-wall profile; rear wall is not solved with equivalent optical detail. | Surface is approximated near $T_v$, but temperature/recoil are linked through the evaporation balance rather than merely imposed as a geometric isotherm. | Angle-dependent surface absorptivity $A(\alpha)$; no complete cavity ray tracing in the reduced model. | Explicit $\dot m''L_v$ vaporization loss and recoil momentum. Melt displacement is principally by liquid expulsion, not direct removal of all penetrated material as vapour. | Compared qualitatively and dynamically with observed front-wall/hump motion. Instantaneous melt-response assumptions caused discrepancies; introducing a finite melt-flow establishment time was identified as necessary. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000034) | Front-wall dynamics: [10.1088/0022-3727/30/5/013](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/30/5/013); recoil-energy analysis: [10.1088/0022-3727/30/18/008](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/30/18/008) |
| **Gouffé cavity approximation; specular Fresnel ray tracing** | For a diffuse-gray cavity, effective absorptivity/emissivity is $\alpha_{eff}=1-R_{esc}$, where $R_{esc}$ is obtained by summing the geometric series of reflected fractions involving wall reflectivity $\rho$, aperture/interior area ratio $s/S$, and aperture configuration factor $F$. In keyhole ray tracing, each ray deposits $A_F(\theta_j)P_j$ and continues with $P_{j+1}=[1-A_F(\theta_j)]P_j$ until it exits or falls below a cutoff. (pqac-00000059, pqac-00000060) | Gouffé assumes a diffuse-gray, isothermal cavity and angular redistribution; laser-keyhole ray tracing instead normally assumes geometrical optics and specular reflection. Gouffé can be inaccurate for elongated cavities and is not a substitute for directional Fresnel tracing. | Gouffé: idealized cylindrical/conical cavity. Ray tracing: arbitrary discretized 2D or 3D wall; circular cross-sections are often assumed when reconstructing 3D geometry. | Optical submodel only; no wall-temperature closure except through temperature-dependent optical constants if supplied. | Gouffé uses hemispherical wall emissivity/reflectivity. Ray tracing uses complex-index/Drude–Fresnel $R_s,R_p$ at every incidence and accumulates spatially nonuniform absorption over all reflections. (pqac-00000061, pqac-00000063) | None intrinsically; it supplies absorbed heat flux to a separate thermal/evaporation model. | Cavity theory confirms increased apparent absorption through repeated encounters, but Gouffé underestimates emissivity for long tubes. Keyhole calculations show strong front-wall localization and geometry-dependent enhancement; therefore total cavity absorptivity alone does not validate predicted depth. (pqac-00000064) | Gouffé’s original work: no DOI located; cylindrical-cavity assessment: [10.1088/0508-3443/18/8/310](https://doi.org/10.1088/0508-3443/18/8/310); keyhole ray tracing: [10.1088/0022-3727/30/23/006](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/30/23/006), [10.1088/0022-3727/39/21/030](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/39/21/030) |
| **Anisimov (1968); Knight (1979) Knudsen-layer recoil model** | Saturation pressure may be evaluated from Clausius–Clapeyron, $p_{sat}(T_s)=p_{ref}\exp[-L_v/R_s(1/T_s-1/T_{ref})]$. Half-range Maxwellian emission plus Knudsen-layer backflow gives $\dot m''=\phi p_{sat}\sqrt{M/(2\pi R_uT_s)}$. At choked monatomic outflow, $\phi\simeq0.82$, hence approximately 18% back-flux. Recoil is the net normal momentum flux; the commonly used strong-evaporation approximation is $p_r\approx0.54p_{sat}$, not $p_{sat}$ itself. (pqac-00000036, pqac-00000037, pqac-00000043) | Locally planar interface; equilibrium half-Maxwellian emission at the liquid side; a Knudsen layer only a few mean free paths thick; continuum gas outside it; ideal monatomic vapour in the standard 0.82/0.54 limit. Ambient-gas pressure and multicomponent Al–Mg–Si evaporation require a more general Knight-type treatment. | No keyhole geometry; local interfacial closure applied normal to any wall element. | $T_s$ is an input determined by the wall energy balance; recoil varies exponentially with it through $p_{sat}(T_s)$. | None; this is a mass-, momentum- and energy-transfer boundary model, not an optical model. | Explicit outgoing and returning molecular fluxes, latent energy $\dot m''L_v$, and momentum recoil. Jump conditions conserve mass, momentum and energy between the surface and outer Knudsen-layer state. (pqac-00000038, pqac-00000042) | The 18% back-flux and associated recoil coefficient arise from kinetic/Knudsen-layer solutions rather than fitting keyhole depth. They should not be interpreted as universal under atmospheric back-pressure, alloy-selective evaporation or nonchoked flow. | Anisimov (1968): no DOI located; Knight (1979): [10.2514/3.61183](https://doi.org/10.2514/3.61183) |
