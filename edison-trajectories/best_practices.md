# Best Practices

*Edison Scientific LITERATURE_HIGH query*

Question: What are the best practices for writing competitive grant proposals for early-career faculty in materials science and engineering, specifically for:
NSF AM (Advanced Manufacturing), AFOSR YIP, ARO YIP, DARPA YFA (Young Faculty Award), DoD DURIP, NSF CAREER, NSF EAGER, NSF DMR TMRP (Metals & Metallic Nanostructures), NSF MEP (Materials Engineering and Processing)

The research program is at BYU (Brigham Young University) in a new lab called the Vertical Cloud Lab (VCL) focused on digital alloy development using:
- AI/ML-accelerated alloy composition screening and design
- Autonomous powder production via ultrasonic atomization (Amazemet rePOWDER)
- Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) additive manufacturing for rapid alloy validation
- High-throughput characterization (SEM/EDS/EBSD, PIP indentation plastometry)
- Closed-loop integration connecting computational prediction to experimental validation

The PI is an early-career assistant professor. The alloy systems of interest include aluminum alloys, high-entropy alloys, and compositionally complex alloys for structural applications. The lab emphasizes autonomous experimentation, materials informatics, and accelerated materials discovery.

Cover: (1) common mistakes to avoid, (2) how to frame AI/ML + experimental materials research for each program, (3) strategies for early-career PIs without extensive preliminary data, (4) tips for the specific review criteria of each program, (5) how to differentiate from competing proposals in the alloy/AM space.

## 2. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Grant-writing research consistently identifies several universal pitfalls for early-career investigators. For NSF proposals, the most common mistake is treating Broader Impacts as a generic afterthought disconnected from research or institutional context (johriUnknownyearansfcareera pages 9-11, johriUnknownyearansfcareera pages 32-35). Strong CAREER proposals integrate research and education concurrently—for example, by developing a "digital metallurgy" course module that uses VCL data and workflows as teaching tools (johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 32-35). Another frequent error is proposing too many research questions for the timeline; limiting to 3–4 well-scoped questions with clear milestones is essential (johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 9-11).

For writing quality, proposals should avoid dense, journal-style prose. Successful proposals use "energetic, direct and concise" language, bullet points, figures, and visual scaffolding to guide reviewers who may not be domain experts (porter2007whyacademicshave pages 1-3, johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 3-5). Submitting to the wrong NSF program or directorate eliminates proposals immediately—nearly a third of submissions fail for this reason alone—so consulting the program officer beforehand is critical (johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 1-3).

For DoD programs, the most critical mistake is insufficient defense relevance. DARPA proposals must address the Heilmeier Questions and frame the work as a capability leap rather than incremental science (frankUnknownyearhowadvancedresearch pages 3-4, frankUnknownyearhowadvancedresearch pages 6-7). AFOSR and ARO YIP proposals must connect fundamental science to specific service priorities (e.g., Air Force aerospace materials or Army soldier protection), as ARO programs are explicitly aligned to DoD and Army S&T strategic priorities (kelby2016armyresearchofficearo pages 6-8, kiserow2017aroinreview pages 8-10).

For NSF EAGER, the defining mistake is proposing incremental work appropriate for a core program. EAGER is strictly for "high risk, high payoff" exploratory research, evaluated primarily by program officers rather than external panels, and requires explicit justification of why the work is appropriate for this mechanism (wang2011theinteractiveheuristic pages 2-3, wagner2013evaluatingtransformativeresearch pages 3-4).

## 3. How to Frame AI/ML + Experimental Materials Research for Each Program

The VCL's closed-loop integration of computation and experiment is its central value proposition, but each program demands different emphasis:

**NSF Programs (CAREER, AM, DMR TMRP, MEP):** Frame AI/ML as a tool for uncovering *fundamental science*, not merely optimizing processes. For NSF CAREER, the emphasis should be on how the autonomous workflow reveals new understanding of alloy phase formation, solidification behavior, and structure–property relationships, integrated with an education plan (johriUnknownyearansfcareera pages 9-11, johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 32-35). For NSF AM, the scientific questions should center on non-equilibrium solidification physics, process–structure–property (P–S–P) laws, and defect science enabled by combinatorial LPBF methods (pegues2021exploringadditivemanufacturing pages 1-5, sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 40-42, sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 1-3). For NSF DMR TMRP, emphasize physics-informed ML that reveals deep physical insights about metallic systems—electronic structure, configurational entropy, atomic ordering—using representations grounded in materials science (hart2021machinelearningfor pages 2-3, hart2021machinelearningfor pages 1-2). For NSF MEP, focus on the manufacturing science of powder–process–microstructure relationships, including how ultrasonic atomization parameters govern powder characteristics and downstream LPBF processability (moorehead2020highthroughputsynthesisof pages 19-22, sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 1-3).

**DoD Young Investigator Programs:** For AFOSR YIP, frame alloy discovery around Air Force–relevant material needs (high-temperature alloys, lightweight structures for aerospace) and emphasize the speed advantage of the VCL's autonomous screening relative to legacy methods (kelby2016armyresearchofficearo pages 6-8). For ARO YIP, connect to Army priorities such as ballistic protection, high-strain-rate performance, and extreme-environment durability, using indentation plastometry data to demonstrate rapid mechanical property screening (weaver2021demonstrationofa pages 1-5). For both, the work must remain basic research—not applied development.

**DARPA YFA:** This program demands revolutionary framing. The VCL should be positioned as a "molecule-to-capability engine" that eliminates technological surprise by enabling on-demand alloy discovery for urgent national security needs (frankUnknownyearhowadvancedresearch pages 3-4, frankUnknownyearhowadvancedresearch pages 1-3). DARPA values "ARPA hard" problems and a portfolio mentality balancing exploration and exploitation; the proposal should explicitly answer the Heilmeier Questions and include measurable go/no-go metrics (frankUnknownyearhowadvancedresearch pages 13-14, frankUnknownyearhowadvancedresearch pages 14-15).

**NSF EAGER:** Propose the most radical, untested element of the VCL—such as a fully autonomous, no-human-in-the-loop alloy discovery campaign—as a short, high-risk proof of concept. EAGER proposals are brief (5–8 pages) and evaluated by program officers, so the emphasis should be on transformative potential rather than extensive preliminary data (wang2011theinteractiveheuristic pages 2-3, wagner2013evaluatingtransformativeresearch pages 3-4).

**DoD DURIP:** Frame equipment requests as force multipliers that unlock specific new experimental capabilities for ongoing DoD-relevant research, quantifying throughput gains and unique experiments enabled (kelby2016armyresearchofficearo pages 6-8, kiserow2017aroinreview pages 8-10).

**Computational results as preliminary data.** CALPHAD phase diagrams, DFT calculations, and ML screening results cost relatively little to generate and serve as compelling preliminary data. Physics-informed ML platforms can screen millions of candidate compositions, producing printability maps and candidate shortlists that demonstrate feasibility without extensive experimentation (rahman2025reviewmachinelearning pages 1-3, rahman2025reviewmachinelearning pages 25-26, sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 40-42). For the VCL, generating computational printability maps for target Al alloy or HEA systems using CALPHAD-informed deep-learning surrogates is an efficient path to strong preliminary results.

**Phased research design.** Structure proposals with early aims dedicated to validation and pilot demonstrations, followed by expansion in later aims. This "build as you go" approach is especially effective for CAREER proposals, where a realistic 5-year timeline with go/no-go milestones demonstrates maturity and planning (johriUnknownyearansfcareera pages 9-11, dawes2017writingagrantobtaining pages 11-13). For the VCL, Aim 1 could validate the closed-loop workflow on a well-characterized benchmark system (e.g., Al-Si or CoCrFeMnNi) before extending to novel compositions.

**Leveraging collaborations and advisory boards.** Formal collaborations with established researchers in CALPHAD/DFT, AM process modeling, and alloy characterization strengthen feasibility arguments. Advisory boards that include DoD laboratory scientists, industry AM practitioners, and senior academics add credibility and demonstrate broader engagement (johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 5-7, dawes2017writingagrantobtaining pages 3-6, botham2017tensimplerules pages 2-4, botham2017tensimplerules pages 4-5). Letters from these advisors should be detailed, specifying meeting frequency and milestones.

**Small-scale rapid demonstrations.** Even with limited resources, a PI can atomize grams of custom powder, print a single combinatorial LPBF plate, and characterize it with SEM/EDS and indentation within weeks. Such rapid demonstrations—even at small scale—provide powerful feasibility evidence for proposals (weaver2021demonstrationofa pages 1-5, liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3). Autonomous experimentation systems have demonstrated that sampling just a small fraction of parameter space with active learning can yield high-quality results, with reported 6-fold reductions in required experiments (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3) and 23× to 1000× decreases in required samples (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 6-8).

## 5. How to Differentiate from Competing Proposals in the Alloy/AM Space

The alloy design and additive manufacturing research space is increasingly crowded, with many groups pursuing ML-accelerated alloy discovery. Differentiation requires articulating what the VCL uniquely offers:

**Vertical integration from powder to property.** Most competing labs rely on commercially available powders, which constrains composition space exploration. The VCL's ultrasonic atomization capability (Amazemet rePOWDER) enables on-demand production of non-standard compositions, a critical bottleneck that most AM-based alloy screening approaches cannot address (moorehead2020highthroughputsynthesisof pages 19-22, pegues2021exploringadditivemanufacturing pages 25-27). In situ alloying approaches have demonstrated significant time savings—"over two orders of magnitude when using in situ alloying"—but still depend on powder availability (moorehead2020highthroughputsynthesisof pages 19-22).

**Closed-loop autonomy vs. semi-automated workflows.** While many groups perform ML-guided but human-executed experiments, the VCL's aspiration for fully closed-loop autonomy—from computational prediction through powder synthesis, printing, characterization, and model updating—is genuinely distinctive. Real-time experiment–theory closed-loop systems remain rare and have been identified as a frontier capability (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3). The "dynamic modification of physical priors/theory within closed-loop autonomous systems has been largely limited in scope" (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3), so proposing credible advances in this direction represents a genuine scientific contribution.

**High-throughput mechanical property measurement.** Indentation plastometry (PIP) provides rapid, automated mechanical property data that most competing approaches lack. While microhardness is commonly used in HT workflows, full stress-strain behavior from indentation plastometry is a significant upgrade that enables more meaningful property screening (weaver2021demonstrationofa pages 1-5, vecchio2021highthroughputrapidexperimental pages 7-11).

**Focus on aluminum alloys and compositionally complex alloys for structural applications.** While much of the HEA/AM literature focuses on refractory or equiatomic model systems (e.g., CoCrFeMnNi, MoNbTaW), the VCL's emphasis on aluminum-based CCAs for structural applications addresses practical engineering needs and aligns with both commercial and defense interests (pegues2021exploringadditivemanufacturing pages 1-5, moorehead2020highthroughputsynthesisof pages 1-5).

**BYU's unique broader impacts potential.** For NSF proposals in particular, BYU's large undergraduate population and mission-oriented culture provide a distinctive platform for broader impacts through workforce development in materials informatics. Integrating VCL research into undergraduate curricula and capstone projects creates authentic research experiences at scale (johriUnknownyearansfcareera pages 9-11, johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 32-35).

## 6. Program-Specific Review Criteria Tips

**NSF CAREER:** The two standard NSF review criteria—Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts—are supplemented by the expectation that research and education are "concurrent and integrated" throughout the project (johriUnknownyearansfcareera pages 9-11). Reviewers explicitly look for concrete outcomes (theory, curricular materials, assessment instruments, dissemination plans) and evidence that the PI is the right person for this work ("Why me?") (johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 9-11). Dissemination should go beyond publications to include open-source tools, workshops, and community engagement (johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 32-35, johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 5-7).

**DARPA YFA:** The Heilmeier Questions serve as the primary screening tool: What are you trying to do? How is it done today? What is new in your approach? Who cares? What are the risks? What are the midterm and final "exams"? (frankUnknownyearhowadvancedresearch pages 3-4). DARPA values "Goldilocks" problems—neither trivial nor intractable—and accepts that many programs will fail because "the freedom to fail was an essential feature of the ARPA model" (frankUnknownyearhowadvancedresearch pages 13-14, frankUnknownyearhowadvancedresearch pages 1-3).

**NSF EAGER:** Evaluation is primarily by program officers, not external panels. Proposals must be short (5–8 pages) and must explicitly justify suitability for EAGER funding and the high-risk, potentially transformative nature of the work (wang2011theinteractiveheuristic pages 2-3). This is not a mechanism for standard research that merely lacks preliminary data; it supports "preliminary work on untested and innovative ideas" and efforts that could "catalyze rapid and innovative advances" (wagner2013evaluatingtransformativeresearch pages 3-4).

**All NSF programs (AM, DMR TMRP, MEP):** Standard NSF Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts criteria apply. The critical differentiation is scope: AM focuses on fundamental manufacturing science, DMR TMRP on deep materials physics of metals and metallic nanostructures, and MEP on materials processing and manufacturing scalability. Targeting the wrong program is a common and fatal error (johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 1-3).

**DoD YIP programs (AFOSR, ARO):** These programs fund basic research and explicitly seek to attract outstanding young investigators to military-relevant research areas. Proposals are evaluated on scientific merit, relevance to the sponsoring service's mission, and the PI's promise as a researcher. ARO programs are aligned to specific Army S&T campaigns and warfighting challenges (kelby2016armyresearchofficearo pages 6-8, kiserow2017aroinreview pages 8-10). DURIP proposals require demonstrating that the requested equipment directly supports existing or planned DoD-funded research.

## 7. Conclusion

The VCL's integrated AI/ML–experimental alloy development pipeline is well-positioned for competitive funding across multiple agencies, provided the PI tailors each proposal to the specific review culture and criteria of the target program. The universal keys to success are: (1) start early and iterate with feedback from program officers and colleagues (johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 1-3, johriUnknownyearansfcareera pages 1-3); (2) write clearly and concisely with strong visual scaffolding (porter2007whyacademicshave pages 1-3, johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 3-5); (3) limit scope to 3–4 focused research questions with feasible timelines (johriUnknownyearansfcareera pages 9-11, dawes2017writingagrantobtaining pages 11-13); (4) generate computational preliminary data quickly using CALPHAD/ML pipelines (rahman2025reviewmachinelearning pages 1-3, sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 40-42); (5) build a strong advisory board with defense, industry, and academic representation (johriUnknownyearansfcareer pages 5-7, botham2017tensimplerules pages 4-5); and (6) differentiate by emphasizing the VCL's unique vertical integration from custom powder atomization through autonomous closed-loop discovery, a capability that remains rare in the field (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3, stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 6-8).
