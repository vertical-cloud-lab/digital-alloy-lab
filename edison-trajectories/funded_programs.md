### 1. NSF CAREER Awards

**NSF CAREER CMMI-2047218 — PI: Wei Xiong, University of Pittsburgh**
This CAREER award supports the development of CALPHAD-based Integrated Computational Materials Engineering (ICME) models and experimental methods for designing defect-free functionally graded materials (FGMs) and developing new alloys specifically designed for additive manufacturing (AM). The project integrates high-throughput experiments with computational modeling to discover novel AM alloys, including the discovery of microsegregation-aided transformation and twinning-induced plasticity in low-manganese steel via directed energy deposition (sargent2024integratedcomputationaland pages 11-15, sargent2024integratedcomputationaland pages 1-7). **Classification: Fundamental** — focuses on process–structure–property relationships and new alloy discovery.

**NSF CAREER CMMI-2046523 — PI: Atieh Moridi, Cornell University**
This CAREER award, identified in conjunction with DOE Basic Energy Sciences Early Career Award DE-SC0022860, supports research on multiprincipal element alloys (MPEAs) during additive manufacturing, with a focus on harnessing metastability for grain size control (wakai2025harnessingmetastabilityfor pages 16-18). **Classification: Fundamental** — addresses fundamental phase stability and microstructure mechanisms in high-entropy alloys during AM.

**NSF CAREER CMMI-2309483/CMMI-1752069 — PI: Prahalada Rao, Virginia Tech**
Titled "CAREER: Smart Additive Manufacturing — Fundamental Research in Sensing, Data Science, and Modeling Toward Zero Part Defects," this project combines in-situ sensing, data science, and machine learning to develop intelligent process monitoring and control for laser powder bed fusion (LPBF). Additional support from NSF PFI-TT 2322322, NIST 70NANB23H029T, and ONR N00014-21-1-2781 enables technology translation for LPBF quality assurance (spadaccia2025laserpowderbed pages 1-5). **Classification: Applied/Engineering** — directly targets zero-defect AM manufacturing via AI/ML-enabled sensing and closed-loop control.

**NSF CAREER — PI: Hui Yang, Pennsylvania State University**
This CAREER award supports sensor-based modeling and analysis for process monitoring, control, diagnostics, prognostics, quality improvement, and performance optimization in advanced manufacturing including metal AM (roh2023ensuringqualityin pages 13-14). **Classification: Applied/Engineering** — focuses on data-driven quality assurance and process control.

### 2. NSF CMMI/CISE/DMREF Programs (Advanced Manufacturing / Materials Informatics)

**NSF DMREF-2119103, CMMI-1663130, CISE-1835690, CDSE-2001333 — PI: Raymundo Arroyave, Texas A&M University**
This suite of NSF awards supports the Arroyave group's development of Bayesian optimization (BO) methods for accelerated alloy discovery and design. The research focuses on multi-objective, multi-constraint BO frameworks applicable to multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) for high-temperature applications, including gas turbine blade alloys in the Mo-Nb-Ti-V-W system (arroyave2022aperspectiveon pages 11-12, khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 14-14). **Classification: Fundamental** — develops new computational methods and materials informatics frameworks for alloy design.

**NSF DMR-2055442 — PI: V. R. Yurkiv (affiliated with Shahbazian-Yassar group)**
This NSF DMR award supports characterization of high-entropy alloy nanoparticles, including in-situ TEM studies of formation mechanisms from mixed metal-salt precursors. **Classification: Fundamental** — addresses formation mechanisms and nanostructure of HEA materials.

### 3. DoD DURIP

**DURIP W911NF-21-1-0042 — PI: Siddhartha Pathak, Iowa State University**
This Army Research Office (ARO) DURIP award funded upgrades to an Alemnis in-situ SEM micro-mechanical testing system, including a low-temperature module (to −150°C) and a high-temperature module (to 1000°C). The upgraded system enables strain-rate testing from 10⁻⁴ to 10⁴ s⁻¹ on additively manufactured microstructures, shape memory alloys, hierarchical nanocomposites, and other advanced materials. This is described as one of only four such systems in the United States (pathak2022insitumicromechanicalsystem pages 3-7, pathak2022insitumicromechanicalsystem pages 1-3, pathak2022insitumicromechanicalsystem pages 7-9). **Classification: Applied/Engineering (equipment)** — provides characterization infrastructure for AM and advanced materials research.

### 4. Army Research Laboratory (ARL) Programs

**ARL Cooperative Agreement W911NF-22-2-0106 (HTMDEC) — PIs: Raymundo Arroyave, Ibrahim Karaman, Ankit Srivastava, Texas A&M University**
The High-Throughput Materials Discovery and Evaluation Capability (HTMDEC) program supports the development of interoperable multi-objective batch Bayesian optimization frameworks combining computational and experimental high-throughput approaches for accelerated alloy discovery. The project integrates XRD, FIB-SEM, and EBSD characterization with ML-driven experimental design at Texas A&M (hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 17-17). **Classification: Applied/Engineering** — aims for autonomous materials discovery platforms with direct defense applications.

**ARL High-Throughput Materials Discovery for Extreme Conditions (launched 2021)**
This programmatic effort leverages machine learning to discover materials for extreme conditions aligned with Army modernization priorities, including high-entropy alloys and advanced AM alloys relevant to defense applications (dyatkin2022privateandpublic pages 4-5). **Classification: Applied** — directly targets defense materials needs.

### 5. DOE Programs

**DOE ARPA-E ULTIMATE Program DE-AR0001427 — PI: Raymundo Arroyave et al., Texas A&M**
This project supports accelerated alloy design for extreme-condition applications using Bayesian optimization and CALPHAD-based modeling approaches (arroyave2022aperspectiveon pages 11-12, khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 14-14). **Classification: Applied** — targets practical alloy development for energy applications.

**DOE BES Early Career Award DE-SC0022860 — PI: Atieh Moridi, Cornell University**
Supports fundamental research on alloy design and microstructure control in additively manufactured multiprincipal element alloys (wakai2025harnessingmetastabilityfor pages 16-18). **Classification: Fundamental.**

### 6. DARPA Programs

DARPA has funded two major programmatic initiatives relevant to accelerated materials discovery: the **Accelerated Molecular Discovery** program and the **Make-It** program, both focused on computational prediction of synthesis routes and accelerated development of novel molecules and materials (dyatkin2022privateandpublic pages 4-5). Specific DARPA Young Faculty Awards (YFA) in alloy development, additive manufacturing, or materials informatics were not identified in the retrieved literature for the 2022–2026 period.

### 7. Projects Combining AI/ML with Experimental Alloy Development or LPBF AM

Several projects of particular note explicitly combine AI/ML with experimental alloy development or LPBF additive manufacturing:

- **Prahalada Rao (Virginia Tech, NSF CAREER)**: Develops deep reinforcement learning frameworks for real-time LPBF process control, integrating in-situ melt pool sensing with data science modeling toward zero-defect parts (spadaccia2025laserpowderbed pages 1-5).

- **Arroyave group (Texas A&M, ARL/NSF)**: Pioneers multi-objective Bayesian optimization for navigating vast alloy composition spaces (e.g., Mo-Nb-Ti-V-W refractory MPEAs), combining CALPHAD, ML surrogates, and high-throughput experimental validation (arroyave2022aperspectiveon pages 11-12, khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 14-14).

- **Wei Xiong (U Pittsburgh, NSF CAREER)**: Integrates CALPHAD-based ICME with high-throughput FGM experiments using directed energy deposition to discover novel AM alloys with unique strengthening mechanisms (sargent2024integratedcomputationaland pages 11-15, sargent2024integratedcomputationaland pages 1-7).

- **Sheikh, Arroyave, Karaman, Elwany (Texas A&M)**: Developed an automated computational framework using deep learning surrogate models to assess printability of high-entropy alloys across vast composition spaces, accelerating printability assessment by 1000× (allen2025systematicdesignof pages 153-156).

### Limitations and Recommendations

This review was conducted through the published scientific literature, which captures grant acknowledgments but typically does not report specific award amounts. Several of the queried grant programs — particularly **AFOSR YIP**, **ARO YIP**, **DARPA YFA**, **NSF EAGER**, **NSF DMR TMRP (Metals & Metallic Nanostructures)**, and **NSF MEP (Materials Engineering and Processing)** — did not yield specific awardee-level project information in the retrieved literature for the 2022–2026 period in the targeted research areas. These programs may have funded relevant projects that are either too recent to appear in publications or whose acknowledgments were not captured in the literature surveyed. For comprehensive project-level data including award amounts, the **NSF Award Search** (nsf.gov/awardsearch), **DoD DTIC** repository, **grants.gov**, and **USAspending.gov** databases should be consulted directly. Individual program announcements from AFOSR, ARO, and DARPA for YIP/YFA awardee lists would also be valuable supplementary sources.
