The critique is **confirmed**, with one caveat: it is confirmed for any CMDRI formulation that includes an economic term or any supply-risk weighting strong enough to reflect scandium’s extreme scarcity and price. Using a reasonable reconstruction of Appendix 8 as 
CMDRI ≈ Σ(wᵢ·xᵢ), with wᵢ proportional to import reliance × cost, a representative Scalmalloy composition (Al-4.6Mg-0.7Sc-0.3Zr, plus trace Mn/Zn/Fe/Si) gives a score of about **36.2** in arbitrary weighted units, of which **Sc alone contributes 35.0**. That is **96.6%** of the total. Under the same reconstruction, a standard **AlSi10Mg** baseline scores about **1.12**, which is a **96.9% reduction vs. Scalmalloy**. A representative **Sc-free Al-Mn-Cr-Zr alloy** at comparable total alloying loading scores about **1.43**, still a **96.1% reduction vs. Scalmalloy**. So the current **≥40% reduction vs. Scalmalloy** target is far too easy if CMDRI gives Sc anything like its real supply-risk/economic weight. That part of the prior review is right. By contrast, if one uses a pure import-reliance × wt% score with no cost term, Sc no longer dominates and the metric behaves very differently; but that would directly undercut the proposal’s own narrative that Sc is the critical supply-risk problem. The clean conclusion is that **Scalmalloy is the wrong sole gate baseline**.

My recommendation is **option (a), but with the AlSi10Mg floor made binding and the Scalmalloy test demoted to a screening check**. Concretely: keep a Scalmalloy comparison for DOE narrative alignment, but rewrite G3 so that a passing alloy must satisfy **both**: **(1) CMDRI ≥90% below Scalmalloy** and **(2) alloying-element supply-risk index (SRI) ≤1.5× SRI of AlSi10Mg**, while also meeting the existing mechanical thresholds. I do **not** recommend simply raising the Scalmalloy threshold under option (b): even **90%** is basically a Sc-free check, and even **95%+** is still often trivial. I also would not make a full Pareto/skyline gate the primary G3 criterion in Phase I; it’s scientifically better, but too fussy for a go/no-go sentence. The best reviewer-facing compromise is a **dual-baseline gate**: Scalmalloy confirms elimination of scandium-class burden; AlSi10Mg prevents backfilling with a different import-dependent cocktail of Mn/Cr/Zr/Ti/Ni/REEs. Calibrated on representative compositions, the suggested **1.5× AlSi10Mg SRI cap** is non-trivial but still fair: a literature-like **Al-5Mn-1.5Cr-0.5Zr** sits at about **1.45× AlSi10Mg** and barely passes, while slightly heavier variants such as **Al-5Mn-2Cr-0.5Zr** rise to about **1.53×** and fail. That’s exactly the kind of pressure you want. It forces optimization instead of box-checking.

A reviewer-ready replacement paragraph for G3 would be:

**“Go/No-Go G3: ≥1 alloy meeting family mechanical thresholds (Al-Mn-Cr-Zr: YS ≥250 MPa, EL ≥5%; Al-Zr: YS ≥200 MPa, EL ≥8%; ASTM E8 sub-size, n≥3) and satisfying both mineral-burden criteria: (i) CMDRI at least 90% below Scalmalloy (Al-4.6Mg-0.7Sc-0.3Zr), confirming elimination of scandium-class supply risk; and (ii) alloying-element supply-risk index no greater than 1.5× that of AlSi10Mg, where SRI = Σ(IRᵢ·xᵢ) over alloying elements i≠Al using U.S. net import reliance values. Report element-wise CMDRI/SRI decomposition for each passing alloy.”**

For O4 / M6, I’d mirror that structure: require **3–6 alloys** meeting the family mechanical thresholds, **CMDRI ≥90% below Scalmalloy**, and **SRI ≤1.5× AlSi10Mg**, and ask for a **Pareto plot of SRI vs. specific yield strength (YS/ρ)** as a reporting artifact rather than a hard gate. That gives the panel the multi-objective picture without turning the gate into mush.

On which non-Sc elements actually move the needle: it depends on whether you mean **pure supply risk** or **cost-weighted CMDRI**. For **SRI = import reliance × wt%**, the biggest contributors at representative LPBF-relevant loadings are approximately **Mn (5 wt% → 0.050)**, **Si (10 wt% → 0.042)**, **Zn (5 wt% → 0.028)**, **Mg (4.5 wt% → 0.0225)**, **Cu (3 wt% → 0.012)**, and **Cr (1.5 wt% → 0.011)**. Then come **Sc (~0.007 at 0.7 wt%)**, **Zr (~0.005 at 0.5 wt%)**, **Li (~0.005 at 2 wt%)**, **Ni (~0.0049 at 1 wt%)**, **Ti (~0.0046 at 0.5 wt%)**, **Sn (~0.0038 at 0.5 wt%)**, with **Ce/Er** smaller on pure wt%-weighted SRI because they’re usually used at low loadings. For **cost-weighted CMDRI**, the ranking changes hard: **Sc** is in a different galaxy, then **Zr, Er, Cr, Li, Cu, Mn, Sn, Ni, Mg, Ti** are the next relevant tier. The key anti-gaming point is this: once Sc is removed, **Mn, Cr, Zr, Si, Mg, Cu, Zn, Li, Ni, Ti** become the real levers. A second trivial substitution isn’t “replace Sc with Mn-Cr-Zr and coast”; the AlSi10Mg floor blocks that.

Two FOA/convention flags matter. First, be careful with **terminology**: **USGS import reliance** is not the same thing as **supply risk**, and **DOE criticality** is not the same thing as either. If the gate uses import reliance, say that. If it uses a Helbig/Graedel-type composite, say that. Right now the proposal blurs **SRI**, **MBS**, and **CMDRI** a bit. Second, be explicit about **which critical-minerals list version** anchors the language. The proposal cites **USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2024** and **DOE Critical Materials Assessment 2023**, which is fine, but note that the **2025 USGS critical minerals list** changed the landscape by adding **Al, Si, and Cu**; under that list, almost the whole FLAIME palette is “critical,” which makes a binary critical/non-critical metric useless for Al alloys. That actually strengthens the case for a **weighted index** rather than a count or simple wt% tally. If they want DOE-safe wording, I’d suggest: use **USGS net import reliance** as the transparent scalar in the G3 gate, and reserve broader **criticality/supply-risk** language for the narrative and the CMDRI decomposition.

Short version: don’t try to rescue the current metric by nudging 40% to 60% or 70%. That won’t fix it. **Keep Scalmalloy only as a scandium-elimination screen, and make AlSi10Mg the real mineral-burden benchmark.** That’s the defensible tightening.
