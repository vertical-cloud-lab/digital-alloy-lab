Question:

Has anyone deposited ultrasonically atomized metal powder (any alloy;
vibrating sonotrode or plate atomization such as AMAZEMET rePOWDER) by
blown-powder laser directed energy deposition and reported deposit
density, microstructure, or properties, ideally compared with gas-atomized
powder of the same alloy? In particular, has the aluminium bronze powder of
Uhlmann et al. 2024 (Materials 17, 3396) or Mueller et al. 2023 (IOP Conf.
Ser. Mater. Sci. Eng. 1296, 012036) been deposited and tested? Give DOIs.
Exclude publications by Sterling G. Baird.

Question: Has anyone deposited ultrasonically atomized metal powder (any alloy;
vibrating sonotrode or plate atomization such as AMAZEMET rePOWDER) by
blown-powder laser directed energy deposition and reported deposit
density, microstructure, or properties, ideally compared with gas-atomized
powder of the same alloy? In particular, has the aluminium bronze powder of
Uhlmann et al. 2024 (Materials 17, 3396) or Mueller et al. 2023 (IOP Conf.
Ser. Mater. Sci. Eng. 1296, 012036) been deposited and tested? Give DOIs.
Exclude publications by Sterling G. Baird.


Yes. ## Answer

**Yes.** Sonotrode-ultrasonically atomized metal powder has been fed into laser blown-powder directed energy deposition (DED), and at least two accessible studies measure properties of the resulting deposits. **The aluminium-bronze powder was also deposited:** Uhlmann *et al.* explicitly state that their earlier work with Müller *et al.* used powder atomized from the **same ship-propeller grinding chips** to produce DED-LB specimens. I could not access the 2023 Müller article’s full text, however, so I cannot establish from it whether deposit density, microstructure, or mechanical properties were measured. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 2-4, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 15-15)

The following studies distinguish verified *deposit* results from powder-only measurements:

*Table: Confirmed sonotrode/plate-ultrasonically atomized metal feedstocks subsequently processed by laser blown-powder DED, excluding Sterling G. Baird. The table separates measured deposit outcomes from comparisons and evidentiary limitations.*

**The specified bronze papers.** Müller *et al.*, “Using ultrasonic atomization to recycle aluminium bronze chips for additive laser directed energy deposition,” *IOP Conference Series: Materials Science and Engineering* **1296**, 012036 (2023), DOI **[10.1088/1757-899X/1296/1/012036](https://doi.org/10.1088/1757-899X/1296/1/012036)**, is the prior specimen-production study identified by Uhlmann *et al.* Its deposit-test results remain **unverified here**, not demonstrably absent. Uhlmann *et al.*, *Materials* **17**, 3396 (2024), DOI **[10.3390/ma17143396](https://doi.org/10.3390/ma17143396)**, itself measures **powder** size, shape, internal porosity, oxygen and flow—not deposited-part density or properties—and explicitly calls for further tests of DED powder feeding and the mechanical properties of resulting structures. Its gas-atomized powder reference is **CuAl9.5Fe**, whereas its ultrasonically atomized nickel aluminium bronze is **CuAl10Ni5Fe5**: this is neither a same-alloy control nor a comparison of deposited parts. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 2-4, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 13-15, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 7-10)

**Verified deposit-property examples.** Ertugrul, Emdadi and Härtel, DOI **[10.21741/9781644903599-28](https://doi.org/10.21741/9781644903599-28)** (2025), atomized Fe–Al on a plasma-heated ultrasonic sonotrode, then built walls through a coaxial powder-fed laser DED nozzle. Microscopy found columnar grains and cracking in the 900 W wall’s dilution zone but not the 600 W wall; measured wall hardness was **345 ± 23** and **336 ± 7 HV1**, respectively. Their comparison is with wire-arc additive manufacturing, **not gas-atomized Fe–Al**. (ertugrul2025advancementsiniron pages 1-3, ertugrul2025advancementsiniron pages 3-6, ertugrul2025advancementsiniron pages 6-9)

Guo *et al.*, DOI **[10.1088/2631-7990/ae3349](https://doi.org/10.1088/2631-7990/ae3349)** (2026), used AMAZEMET-ultrasonically atomized Al–Ni–Ce–Mn–Fe powder in laser **blown-powder** DED. Both its alloy and the AlSi10Mg reference achieved **>99% deposited-block density** by X-ray CT; the new alloy had ≈**22.1 µm** grains and **<5 µm** eutectic colonies. Indentation-plastometry-derived, **not conventional tensile-test**, estimates were **191 ± 26 MPa** yield strength and **421 ± 17 MPa** ultimate strength, versus **112 ± 16** and **279 ± 21 MPa** for AlSi10Mg printed under the same conditions. That is a useful deposit-property comparison but **not the same alloy made by gas versus ultrasonic atomization**. The paper’s Figure 2 shows the compared deposited microstructures and property curves. (guo2026mechanicalandin pages 3-4, guo2026mechanicalandin pages 4-5, guo2026mechanicalandin pages 8-9, guo2026mechanicalandin pages 6-8, guo2026mechanicalandin media 81ef2ffe, guo2026mechanicalandin media 0066b9ac)

**Bottom line:** deposition of the specified recycled bronze is documented through Uhlmann’s explicit citation to Müller 2023; quantitative post-deposition testing of *that bronze* cannot be confirmed from the accessible text. I found **no verified, same-alloy, gas-atomized-versus-sonotrode-ultrasonically-atomized comparison in which both powders were laser blown-powder DED deposited and their resulting part properties compared**. This is a limit of the located and accessible literature, not proof that no such study exists. (uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 2-4, uhlmann2024acomparativeevaluation pages 13-15, ertugrul2025advancementsiniron pages 6-9, guo2026mechanicalandin pages 8-9)
