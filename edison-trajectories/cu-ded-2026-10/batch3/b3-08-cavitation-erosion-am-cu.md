# Cavitation and flowing-seawater evidence for NAB and Cu–Ni

**Scope and interpretation.** Direct, numerically verifiable comparisons are strongest for *cast* propeller bronzes before and after surface modification, not for single-stream, pre-alloyed-powder DED repairs. Laser **surface melting** remelts an existing casting without adding feedstock; friction-stir processing and hot-isostatically pressed (HIP) powder are likewise not DED. Unless stated otherwise, reported salt solutions are **3.5 wt% NaCl, not natural seawater or ASTM D1141 substitute seawater**. Losses from different cavitation rigs, durations and media should not be compared as though they were one standardized ranking. (tang2004improvementincavitation pages 1-3, song2013comparisonofcorrosion pages 4-8, fayanas2025nickelaluminiumbronze pages 1-2, krella2023degradationandprotection pages 4-6)

## Itemized evidence

**1. Laser-remelted propeller manganese–nickel–aluminium bronze (MAB) versus cast NAB and MAB.** C. H. Tang, F. T. Cheng and H. C. Man, “Improvement in cavitation erosion resistance of a copper-based propeller alloy by laser surface melting,” *Surface & Coatings Technology* **182** (2004) 300–307, DOI **10.1016/j.surfcoat.2003.08.048**. **Process:** laser surface melting of cast propeller-blade MAB, *not* laser cladding; cast NAB is a separate control. **Measured wt%:** MAB Cu 75.6, Mn 10.8, Al 7.8, Fe 3.6, Ni 2.2; NAB Cu 81.2, Mn 1.3, Al 9.0, Fe 4.2, Ni 4.3. **Test:** ASTM G32-92-conforming indirect vibratory cavitation, 20 kHz, 100 µm peak-to-peak, 0.5 mm horn stand-off, **3.5 wt% NaCl**, 23 °C, 6 h; weights every 30 min. The optimal 1-kW, 2-mm-spot, 35-mm/s treatment gave 333 versus 160 HV0.2 for untreated MAB; untreated NAB was 159 HV0.2. Reported reciprocal mean-depth-erosion-rate resistances were **161.1, 27.7 and 73.2 h/mm** for melted MAB, cast MAB and cast NAB, respectively: **5.8×** and **2.2×** improvements in this specific test. Cast κ particles and α/β interfaces initiated damage; the few-micrometre, predominantly bcc-β remelted layer first damaged at grain-boundary triple junctions. Surface-damage images were examined as early as 30 s; **a numerical incubation time and porosity percentage were not reported**. **[CITE] [NUMBERS] [NAVY: propeller application] [GAP: remelting is not deposited repair].** (tang2004improvementincavitation pages 1-3, tang2004improvementincavitation pages 3-5, tang2004improvementincavitation pages 5-8)

**2. Distinguishing mechanical erosion from corrosion-assisted cavitation in the same propeller alloy.** C. H. Tang, F. T. Cheng and H. C. Man, “Effect of laser surface melting on the corrosion and cavitation erosion behaviors of a manganese–nickel–aluminium bronze,” *Materials Science and Engineering A* **373** (2004) 195–203, DOI **10.1016/j.msea.2004.01.016**. **Process/composition:** untreated cast versus laser-surface-melted Cu–10.8Mn–7.8Al–3.6Fe–2.2Ni wt% MAB. **Test:** G32-92-conforming indirect cavitation, 20 kHz, 100 µm peak-to-peak, 0.5 mm stand-off, 23 °C, **12 h**; deionized water for nominal mechanical erosion and 3.5 wt% NaCl for combined erosion–corrosion. Separately, static corrosion was measured over 12 h in aerated 3.5 wt% NaCl. Untreated versus optimal melt rates, respectively, were **2,116 versus 311 µg cm⁻² h⁻¹** in deionized water, **2,284 versus 431 µg cm⁻² h⁻¹** under saline cavitation, and **31.85 versus 16.59 µg cm⁻² h⁻¹** under static immersion. Polarization gave **icorr 9.52 versus 4.26 µA cm⁻²** and **Ecorr −372 versus −290 mV/SCE**; surface hardness was **160 versus 333 HV0.2**. Measured α grains of the cast metal were approximately **43.7 µm**, versus **4.2 µm** in the laser-melted β-rich layer. Crucially, the estimated interaction contribution **S/T rose from 6.0% to 24.1%** for optimal melting even while *total* saline loss fell 5.30×; minimizing hardness-independent static current does not measure saline cavitation performance. These **12-h mass-loss rates must not be confused with item 1’s 6-h reciprocal depth-rate values**. **[CITE] [NUMBERS] [NAVY: propeller application] [GAP: not additive deposition].** (tang2004effectoflaser pages 2-4, tang2004effectoflaser pages 6-7, tang2004effectoflaser pages 7-8)

**3. Laser surface *alloying*, a particularly important crack-risk counterexample.** C. H. Tang, F. T. Cheng and H. C. Man, “Laser surface alloying of a marine propeller bronze using aluminium powder: Part I: Microstructural analysis and cavitation erosion study,” *Surface & Coatings Technology* **200** (2006) 2602–2609, DOI **10.1016/j.surfcoat.2004.12.021**. **Process:** a **preplaced ≈4-µm Al powder** layer was laser-alloyed into cast Cu–10.8Mn–7.8Al–3.6Fe–2.2Ni wt% MAB, increasing local Al to ≈10.5 wt%; this is **neither pre-alloyed powder nor blown-powder DED**. **Test:** G32-92-conforming indirect vibration, 20 kHz, 100 µm peak-to-peak, 0.5 mm stand-off, **deionized water at 23 °C**, **12 h**. Appropriate processing yielded a roughly **1-mm**, apparently crack-/pore-free, bcc-rich alloyed layer, ≈**400 HK**, and approximately **30–32×** the untreated bronze’s erosion resistance. **Low fluence/high scan speed produced surface cracks**; high fluence increased dilution and reduced hardness. There is **no demonstrated 30× result in seawater**, and preplaced elemental Al directly differs from the proposed single-prealloyed-powder route. **[CITE] [NUMBERS] [NAVY: propeller repair motivation] [NOVELTY-RISK: compositional surface modification for cavitation, but not the proposed feedstock/process] [GAP: saline performance of the 30× condition].** (tang2006lasersurfacealloying pages 4-6, tang2006lasersurfacealloying pages 2-4, tang2006lasersurfacealloying pages 1-2)

**4. Cast propeller NAB under simultaneous electrochemistry and cavitation.** J. Basumatary, M. Nie and R. J. K. Wood, “The synergistic effects of cavitation erosion–corrosion in ship propeller materials,” *Journal of Bio- and Tribo-Corrosion* **1** (2015) article 12, DOI **10.1007/s40735-015-0012-1**. **Process:** cast NAB, Cu balance, Al **9.39**, Ni **4.73**, Fe **4.53**, Mn **1.07 wt%**, compared with duplex stainless steel—not an AM bronze. **Test:** indirect ultrasonic vibration, 20 kHz, **80 µm** peak-to-peak, **2 mm** stand-off, 17 ± 0.5 °C, **1 h**; 5 L stagnant distilled water versus **3.5 wt% NaCl at open-circuit potential**, including 10 min before cavitation. Gravimetric NAB loss was **1.00 versus 1.48 mg** per 1-h specimen, respectively; optical volume loss was **0.115 versus 0.169 mm³**, and calculated erosion–corrosion synergy **73.6%** of total loss. NAB hardness, ultimate strength and elongation were **170 HV, 650 MPa and 18%**. Caution: the synergy calculation assigns an observed **−0.61-mg mass change** during nominal pure corrosion to C, while distilled water is assumed to isolate erosion; neither assumption makes the 73.6% a universal material constant. This 1-h test provides **no measured incubation duration or stabilized erosion rate**. **[CITE] [NUMBERS] [NAVY: ship propellers] [GAP: no AM comparator].** (basumatary2015thesynergisticeffects pages 2-4, basumatary2015thesynergisticeffects pages 4-6, basumatary2015thesynergisticeffects pages 9-11)

**5. A 16–20-h cast-NAB microstructure/defect control.** Q. N. Song, Y. G. Zheng, S. L. Jiang, D. R. Ni and Z. Y. Ma, “Comparison of Corrosion and Cavitation Erosion Behaviors Between the As-Cast and Friction-Stir-Processed Nickel Aluminum Bronze,” *Corrosion* **69** (2013) 1111–1121, DOI **10.5006/0984**. **Process:** cast versus friction-stir processed (**not AM**) C95800, Cu balance, **9.18 Al, 4.49 Ni, 4.06 Fe, 1.03 Mn wt%**. **Test:** indirect 20-kHz, **60-µm amplitude**, 0.5-mm stand-off, ≈20 °C; distilled water or **3.5 wt% NaCl**. After 10 days in NaCl, processed NAB’s EIS arc diameter was about **one order of magnitude greater**, despite small differences in short-time polarization/EIS under cavitation. **[CITE] [NUMBERS] [NAVY: propeller relevance] [GAP: AM pores and fusion boundaries remain untested here].** (song2013comparisonofcorrosion pages 4-8, song2013comparisonofcorrosion pages 8-12, song2013comparisonofcorrosion pages 12-15)

Quantitative deposit porosity and cavitation rates are **not reported**. **[CITE] [NUMBERS] [NOVELTY-RISK: thermal history/phase/corrosion linkage already studied in arc AM] [GAP: pre-alloyed powder, interface repair and cavitation].** (huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 5-7, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 4-5, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 7-10, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 10-13)

## Selecting a mid-cost network node

**First choice: a controlled 6–16-h indirect ASTM G32-style test in 3.5 wt% NaCl, paired with deionized-water runs, on machined as-deposited coupons and matched cast NAB/wrought C71500.** Record mass loss at multiple intervals, the measured incubation interval *if one exists*, area-normalized post-incubation slope, 3D maximum pit depth, OCP under horn-on/off conditions and cross-sectional κ/β′/pore attack. Tang establishes a 6–12-h saline mass-loss protocol; Song demonstrates that **one hour may miss the first ≈5 h of low-loss behavior**. It is a defensible **mid-cost experimental-design recommendation**, not proof that a 6-h result predicts 30-day immersion. The exposed area, temperature, stand-off and vibration amplitude must be fixed across alloys; ideally use natural/D1141 seawater for a smaller validation subset rather than relabeling NaCl. (tang2004improvementincavitation pages 3-5, tang2004effectoflaser pages 2-4, song2013comparisonofcorrosion pages 4-8, song2013comparisonofcorrosion pages 8-12, krella2023degradationandprotection pages 4-6)

**Second choice, especially for pipe/propeller flow-film questions:** a several-hour **rotating-cylinder or jet-in-slit flowing-saline test**, with controlled dissolved oxygen, velocity and initial film age, measuring impedance/current and maximum local attack depth. The jet-in-slit method demonstrably distinguishes shear-dominated from turbulent regions in **1 h**, but its published 1 wt% CuCl₂ result **cannot be used as a seawater acceptance threshold**; repeat in the actual chosen seawater medium. **ASTM G134** specifies *cavitating-jet* erosion and is an appropriate downstream, higher-apparatus-cost validation of propeller hydrodynamics, not interchangeable with jet-in-slit or a generic high-pressure impinging jet. **ASTM G73** concerns *liquid-impingement erosion*; no verifiable NAB-versus-DED/C71500 G73 numbers were located, and neither G73 nor sand-slurry impact is evidence of seawater cavitation resistance. (yabuki2008breakawaypropertiesof pages 1-2, yabuki2008breakawaypropertiesof pages 2-3, yabuki2008breakawaypropertiesof pages 3-5, krella2023degradationandprotection pages 4-6, krella2023degradationandprotection pages 6-8)

**Qualification gap:** no verified retrieved experiment jointly reports **single-feed pre-alloyed powder size/lot Hall flow/apparent density/oxygen, density and crack acceptance on both cast NAB and wrought Cu–Ni repairs, and matched *as-deposited* 30-day seawater corrosion plus cavitation/jet damage versus wrought C71500**. Other than Zhao’s reported powder size/oxygen, those requested powder-lot numbers are therefore *not available* in the cited comparison, rather than safely fillable from adjacent AM studies. Specific Navy/DoD grant support was **not verified** for the listed papers; [NAVY] above denotes a documented marine/ship-propeller application unless explicitly noted otherwise. (zhao2022microstructureandmechanical pages 2-4, fayanas2025nickelaluminiumbronze pages 1-2, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 4-5, zhao2022microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2)

## Top 5 things the PI should know

1. **Address Xu (2024) and Cai (2025) before writing novelty claims.** Published DED-NAB and WAAM-NAB cavitation titles already exist; establish distinctions in **single pre-alloyed feedstock, both repair interfaces, lower Ni, as-deposited seawater performance and linked cost-aware modeling**, subject to checking those primary papers. (lu2024effectsofsurface pages 17-18, zhao2022microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2)
2. **Use saline cavitation, not hardness or one-hour icorr alone, as an intermediate functional discriminator.** Laser-remelted MAB improved total 12-h saline cavitation loss **2,284→431 µg cm⁻² h⁻¹** while its *fractional* erosion–corrosion synergy increased; short-time electrochemistry also missed much of Song’s cast/FSP separation. (tang2004effectoflaser pages 2-4, tang2004effectoflaser pages 7-8, song2013comparisonofcorrosion pages 12-15)
3. **Do not make a jet-in-slit CuCl₂ threshold a seawater specification.** The 70/30 alloy’s **1 versus 3 m/s** local breakaway in an oxidizing CuCl₂ accelerant demonstrates sensitivity to turbulence, **not** C71500 performance in natural seawater. (yabuki2008breakawaypropertiesof pages 1-2, yabuki2008breakawaypropertiesof pages 2-3, yabuki2008breakawaypropertiesof pages 3-5)
4. **Model and measure the deposited phase/defect state, not just equilibrium phase labels.** Cast κ/α interfaces, retained β′ where present, AM heat-input-driven grain and κ coarsening, and individual lack-of-fusion defects are materially distinct failure pathways; Zhao’s pre-alloyed-powder cladding still supplies **no cavitation or seawater qualification**. (song2013comparisonofcorrosion pages 8-12, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 5-7, huo2025modulatingheatinput pages 7-10, zhao2022microstructureandmechanical pages 4-6)
5. **Preserve a true as-deposited, matched-medium benchmark against wrought C71500 and cast NAB.** Post-annealed powder-metallurgy slurry data, distilled-water cavitation, and cast-versus-remelted propeller results are useful mechanisms and cost-node precedents, but none establishes the required **single-stream DED repair** meeting 30-day seawater and flow-assisted damage targets.
