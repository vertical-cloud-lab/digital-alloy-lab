Question:

Has anyone published seawater or NaCl corrosion tests of a copper-nickel
alloy (90-10 C70600, 70-30 C71500, or a Cu-Ni-Fe-Mn variant) deposited by
blown-powder laser directed energy deposition, laser metal deposition, or
laser cladding (not wire-arc, not laser powder bed fusion)? A draft proposal
states: "We found no corrosion study of copper-nickel deposited by
blown-powder laser DED." Is that statement accurate as of October 2026? Give
any counterexamples with DOIs, including laser-clad Cu-Ni coatings on steel
tested in seawater, and suggest a safer wording if needed. Exclude
publications by Sterling G. Baird.

Question: Has anyone published seawater or NaCl corrosion tests of a copper-nickel
alloy (90-10 C70600, 70-30 C71500, or a Cu-Ni-Fe-Mn variant) deposited by
blown-powder laser directed energy deposition, laser metal deposition, or
laser cladding (not wire-arc, not laser powder bed fusion)? A draft proposal
states: "We found no corrosion study of copper-nickel deposited by
blown-powder laser DED." Is that statement accurate as of October 2026? Give
any counterexamples with DOIs, including laser-clad Cu-Ni coatings on steel
tested in seawater, and suggest a safer wording if needed. Exclude
publications by Sterling G. Baird.


Yes. **No.** As of October 2026, “We found no corrosion study of copper-nickel deposited by blown-powder laser DED” is too broad. A direct counterexample is S. Bhattacharya, G. P. Dinda, A. K. Dasgupta, H. Natu, B. Dutta, and J. Mazumder, “Microstructural evolution and mechanical, and corrosion property evaluation of Cu–30Ni alloy formed by Direct Metal Deposition process,” *Journal of Alloys and Compounds* **509** (2011), 6364–6373, **DOI: 10.1016/j.jallcom.2011.03.091**. A later paper describes this study as laser-clad Cu–30Ni deposited both directly and onto wrought C71500, with corrosion performance compared against wrought C71500. This is a substantially closer precedent than wire-arc or powder-bed fabrication. The original article was not obtainable in this search, however, so its **corrosion electrolyte and powder-delivery details could not be independently checked**; those particulars should be confirmed in the primary paper before describing it specifically as a seawater or NaCl test. None of the cited authors is Sterling G. Baird. (wenkun2024influenceofalloying pages 1-4, wenkun2024influenceofalloying pages 9-10)

A related, but **compositionally distinct**, counterexample is Q. Wu and J. Zhang, “Corrosion behavior of laser-clad Cu-9Ni-6Sn coating,” *Surface and Coatings Technology* **349** (2018), 1117–1129, **DOI: 10.1016/j.surfcoat.2018.07.012**. It establishes published corrosion work on a laser-clad Cu–Ni-containing coating, **not** on C70600, C71500, or a Cu–Ni–Fe–Mn variant. The available evidence does not verify that its electrolyte was seawater/NaCl, so it should not be presented as a confirmed seawater counterexample. (wenkun2024influenceofalloying pages 1-4, wenkun2024influenceofalloying pages 9-10)

**Steel coatings and seawater:** The retrieved sources did **not** establish a qualifying, non-Baird *laser-clad C70600/C71500 or Cu–Ni–Fe–Mn coating on steel tested in seawater* with a verified DOI. In particular, Zhou *et al.*, **DOI: 10.7512/j.issn.1001-2303.2024.11.02**, tested Cu–Ni-based coatings on X80 steel in 3.5% NaCl, but deposited them using **TIG arc and flux-cored wire**, not a laser; it is therefore **not** an eligible counterexample. Nor should a Cu–Ni–Sn coating, nickel–aluminum bronze, or a Cu-containing high-entropy alloy be relabeled as 90/10 or 70/30 cupronickel. (wenkun2024influenceofalloying pages 1-4, wenkun2024influenceofalloying pages 4-9)

**Safer proposal wording:** “Prior laser directed-energy-deposition work has evaluated the corrosion of Cu–30Ni, and corrosion of laser-clad Cu–Ni-containing coatings has also been reported. However, we have not identified a directly comparable study that systematically evaluates seawater corrosion of *our specified powder-fed laser-DED C70600/C71500 [or Cu–Ni–Fe–Mn] build and processing conditions* against wrought material.” The remaining gap is the **specified composition, geometry, process conditions, and seawater-testing comparison**, not an absence of all Cu–Ni laser-deposition corrosion research. (wenkun2024influenceofalloying pages 1-4, wenkun2024influenceofalloying pages 9-10, nemani2024advancementsinadditive pages 102-105)
