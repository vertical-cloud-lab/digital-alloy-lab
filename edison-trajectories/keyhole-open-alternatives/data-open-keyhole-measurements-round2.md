Question: What openly available measurements of keyhole onset thresholds, keyhole depth and keyhole-mode melt-pool dimensions exist for laser powder bed fusion and laser spot or track melting of metals, which could seed an open, curated benchmark for scoring cheap keyhole models? The model for the benchmark is ORCHID, an open repository of CALPHAD assessments with accepted reference values that are used to score models. Context: we use ArroyaveLab's open-source `eagar-tsai` Python package (a conduction-mode Eagar-Tsai travelling Gaussian surface heat source on a semi-infinite solid with constant properties) as a cheap stand-in for the Thermo-Calc Additive Manufacturing (AM) Module when building laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) printability maps for AlSi10Mg and a hypereutectic Al-24Si-0.14Mg (wt%) blend: Gaussian beam with 1/e^2 radius 40-45 um, 75-400 W, 0.3-2.4 m/s, 30 um layer, 100-130 um hatch, 30-150 C build plate. Against the AM Module's maps, eagar-tsai reproduced the lack-of-fusion boundaries (pool depth D <= layer t, and depth at half the hatch <= t) to within about 20 W, but not keyholing. The AM Module flags keyholes with the aspect ratio of the keyhole (vapour cavity) itself, Wk/Dk <= 0.7, and only at 2-4 times the power at which eagar-tsai's pool reaches W/D = 2.5 or its peak temperature reaches Al's boiling point. A conduction model's W/D never drops below about 2 (the semicircle limit), and it has no evaporation cap on peak temperature (eagar-tsai gives 6,400 K where the AM Module gives 3,044 K at 250 W, 0.9 m/s). In keyhole mode (375 W, 0.4 m/s, Al-24Si, 45 um beam radius, absorptivity about 0.38) the AM Module gives a pool 349 um deep, about 460 um wide and 1.47 mm long, which eagar-tsai cannot reach at any absorptivity up to 1. Measured AlSi10Mg single tracks (Solyaev et al. 2025, arXiv:2507.23422; 80 um spot) were in keyhole mode (D/W > 0.75) for P > 220 W and v < 0.8 m/s. We want the best open-source replacement for the AM Module's keyhole prediction: fast enough for printability maps (ideally seconds per point, at most hours for a few hundred points) and validated, preferably on aluminium alloys.

## Openly Available Measurements of Keyhole Onset, Depth, and Melt-Pool Dimensions for Benchmarking Cheap Keyhole Models in LPBF

### 1. In Situ Synchrotron X-ray Imaging Studies of Keyholes

The primary source of direct, time-resolved keyhole measurements is high-speed synchrotron X-ray radiography, predominantly at APS Sector 32-ID-B.

**Cunningham et al. (2019), Science 363, 849–852 (DOI: 10.1126/science.aav4687).** Ti-6Al-4V bare-plate laser melting at APS 32-ID. Reports the transition from shallow depression to stable keyhole to unstable keyhole with pore formation. Keyhole depth versus power and speed is presented, and the porosity onset threshold is identified. Conditions span ~100–500 W, 0.3–1.5 m/s, with beam diameters of tens of micrometres. Data are in plots/supplementary figures, not machine-readable tables. The single most valuable existing source for a keyhole-onset benchmark, though for titanium not aluminium.

**Zhao et al. (2017), Physical Review X 7, 031052 (DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevX.7.031052).** First ultrafast synchrotron radiography of powder-bed laser melting (Ti-6Al-4V, APS 32-ID). Resolves melt-pool dynamics, vapour-depression depth, powder motion, and spatter. Gold open-access with supplementary movies. Limited systematic depth-versus-condition coverage.

**Zhao et al. (2020), Science 370, 1080–1086 (DOI: 10.1126/science.abd1587).** Identified the critical front-wall instability (J-shaped front wall) at the keyhole tip that generates pores, with dynamic cavity depth and front-wall angle data. Supplementary movies available; no curated per-frame cavity-contour table.

**Gan et al. (2021), Nature Communications 12, 2379 (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-021-22704-0).** Assembled dimensionless scaling of conduction-to-keyhole and stable-to-unstable-keyhole transitions from APS radiography of Ti-6Al-4V plus cross-material literature data. Supplementary material tabulates data points. The normalised-enthalpy and Péclet-number framework enables cross-material transfer to aluminium. Highly suitable as a starting set for threshold benchmarks.

**Hojjatzadeh et al. (2019), Nature Communications 10, 3088 (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-019-10973-9).** APS radiography of Ti-6Al-4V tracking keyhole-pore generation, motion, and elimination. Gold open access. Sparse power–speed matrix; useful for porosity-mechanism validation.

**Parab et al. (2018), Scientific Reports 8, 14737 (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-018-33376-0).** Protocol and multi-material demonstration of ultrafast X-ray imaging at APS 32-ID. Gold open access with supplementary videos.

**Aluminium-alloy synchrotron studies.** Direct synchrotron keyhole imaging of Al alloys is growing but rarer than for Ti. Ren et al. studied Al6061 at synchrotron facilities, resolving cavity evolution and hot-cracking. Guo et al. studied Al–Cu alloys. Work at Diamond Light Source and ESRF has imaged AlSi10Mg. However, most Al-alloy data appear only in article figures, not open repositories. The Huang et al. and Wang et al. citations span multiple studies requiring disambiguation before benchmark ingestion.

**Key limitation:** No single, well-curated, openly deposited dataset of keyhole depth versus power and speed exists for any aluminium alloy.

### 2. NIST AM-Bench and Other Open Melt-Pool Datasets

**NIST AM-Bench 2018 (AMB2018-02).** IN625 bare-plate single tracks at 150 and 195 W, 0.4 and 0.8 m/s, ~100 µm beam. Cross-sectional melt-pool width/depth with rigorous metrology. Public-domain data at https://www.nist.gov/ambench (DOI: 10.18434/M32050). Primarily conduction/transition regime.

**NIST AM-Bench 2022.** Expanded alloys (IN625, stainless steels) and modalities. Challenge-specific matrices at https://www.nist.gov/ambench/2022-ambench-challenge-problems.

**NIST AM-Bench 2025.** Latest round at https://www.nist.gov/ambench. Measurement release timing varies by challenge.

**Materials Data Facility (MDF).** https://materialsdatafacility.org/ hosts DOI-backed AM datasets. Suitability depends on whether individual deposits contain calibrated cavity depth and full process metadata.

**MeltpoolNet / Akbari et al.** Literature-aggregated melt-pool geometry database for ML surrogates. Spans steels, Ni, Ti, and some Al alloys. Conflates fusion-zone and vapour-cavity definitions. Available on GitHub.

**King et al. (2014), Applied Physics Letters 104, 214105 (DOI: 10.1063/1.4885147).** Established the normalised-enthalpy criterion for keyhole onset in 316L SS from bare-plate cross-sections (ΔH/h_s ≈ π T_b/T_m).

**Trapp et al. (2017), Applied Physics Letters (DOI: 10.1063/1.4996042).** In-situ absorptivity of 316L SS across conduction-to-keyhole transition, showing the absorptivity jump from ~0.3 to ~0.7. Critical for calibrating absorptivity in cheap models like eagar-tsai.

### 3. AlSi10Mg and Al-Alloy Single-Track Datasets

**Solyaev et al. (2025), arXiv:2507.23422.** AlSi10Mg single tracks with ~80 µm spot. Keyhole mode (D/W > 0.75) for P > 220 W and v < 0.8 m/s. Open arXiv preprint. The most directly relevant dataset for AlSi10Mg benchmarking, though it measures post-solidification fusion-zone depth (not instantaneous cavity depth) and the 80 µm spot differs from the user's 40–45 µm beam.

**Aboulkhair et al. (2014–2016), Materials & Design (DOI: 10.1016/j.matdes.2015.07.065).** AlSi10Mg process windows with density and selected cross-sections. Multi-track density focus limits keyhole-onset precision.

**Read et al. (2015), Materials & Design.** AlSi10Mg factorial process optimisation with density data.

**Key gap:** No openly deposited, machine-readable Al-alloy single-track data with calibrated spot size and absorptivity spans the full conduction-to-keyhole range.

### 4. Existing Benchmarks and ORCHID-like Curation Requirements

**AM-Bench challenge format** (blind predictions → revealed measurements → scoring) is the closest existing analogue to ORCHID. It does not yet include explicit keyhole-onset or keyhole-depth challenges.

**MeltpoolNet** serves as a literature-aggregated training set, not a curated benchmark with accepted reference values and uncertainty.

**What an ORCHID-like keyhole benchmark would need:**
1. **Accepted reference values with uncertainties:** alloy composition, beam radius (1/e² convention), power (after optics), scan speed, substrate temperature, atmosphere, and measured quantity with uncertainty.
2. **Consistent definitions:** distinguish (a) instantaneous vapour-cavity depth (synchrotron), (b) time-averaged cavity depth, (c) post-solidification fusion-zone depth (metallography), and (d) keyhole-onset criterion.
3. **Model-scoring protocol:** RMSE on depth, keyhole/no-keyhole classification accuracy, onset threshold bias, with required reporting of computational cost and absorptivity assumptions.
4. **Machine-readable format:** JSON/CSV with DOI, provenance, and CC-BY or public-domain licence.
5. **Cross-material coverage:** start with Ti-6Al-4V and 316L SS (synchrotron data), extend to Al alloys via metallographic cross-sections.

### Recommendations

1. **Immediate:** Digitise and curate Solyaev et al. (2025) AlSi10Mg data as primary Al-alloy reference set.
2. **Cross-material:** Use Gan et al. (2021) dimensionless scaling to validate cross-material onset transfer.
3. **Absorptivity:** The Trapp et al. (2017) absorptivity jump data provides the functional form for absorptivity at keyhole onset; an aluminium analogue is a critical gap.
4. **Protocol:** Adopt NIST AM-Bench blind-prediction/scoring as procedural template.
5. **Primary gap:** No benchmark-ready, machine-readable keyhole depth vs. P and v dataset exists for any aluminium alloy.
