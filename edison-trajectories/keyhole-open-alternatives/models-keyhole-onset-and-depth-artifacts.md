| Gan keyhole number $Ke$ | One published convention is $\displaystyle Ke=\frac{\eta P}{\pi\rho C_p(T_l-T_0)\sqrt{\alpha v r_0^3}}$; it is proportional to normalized enthalpy. Numerical thresholds such as $Ke\sim6$–10 are convention-dependent and cannot be transferred safely between definitions; use the exact constants and radius convention of the selected implementation | Absorptivity and liquid enthalpy dominate alloy transfer; fitted boundaries aggregate several materials but do not eliminate alloy-specific optical, vapor-pressure, surface-tension or viscosity effects | Multi-alloy laser-melting/welding database, including steels, Ti alloys and aluminium cases; spot scales approximately 25–250 µm across the underlying data | Stronger than normalized enthalpy for screening unstable keyholes and porosity, but a separate calibrated geometry law or CFD model is required for depth, width and length |
| Cunningham incident power-density criterion | $\displaystyle I_0=\frac{P}{\pi r_b^2}$ for a top-hat-equivalent average, or $\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{peak}}=\frac{2P}{\pi w_{1/e^2}^2}$ for a Gaussian peak intensity | Keyhole onset and morphology transition only | Characteristic onset intensity is of order $10^6\ \mathrm{W\,cm^{-2}}$ for the Ti-6Al-4V synchrotron experiments; the boundary also depends on scan speed | Not universal: aluminium’s low single-pass absorptivity and different vapor pressure make direct transfer unreliable; incident and absorbed intensity must be distinguished | Direct high-speed synchrotron X-ray observations on Ti-6Al-4V at LPBF-scale spots and scan speeds | Useful experimental sanity check, but radius/intensity convention alone introduces a factor of two, and a single intensity threshold omits interaction time and thermal diffusivity; it predicts no depth, width or length |
| Fabbro / Fabbro–Chouf penetration models | A schematic reduced balance has the form $\displaystyle d\sim\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{eff}}P}{2\pi k(T_v-T_0)}\left(1+\frac{vd}{2\alpha}\right)^{-1/2}$, solved iteratively; specific Fabbro variants instead close wall inclination, drilling velocity, multiple-reflection absorption and heat loss explicitly | Mean keyhole penetration depth; some variants also return wall angle or effective absorption | No universal constant set: $\eta_{\mathrm{eff}}$, wall-loss coefficient, recoil/drilling closure and characteristic temperature are fitted or selected for the welding configuration | Highly alloy-dependent through optical constants, vapor pressure, latent heat, conductivity and surface tension; effective absorption can change sharply after cavity formation | Validated mainly for deep-penetration laser welding of steels and other engineering alloys, generally at larger spots and lower normalized scan speeds than LPBF | The displayed equation is a dimensional summary, not a unique canonical Fabbro equation.
