## artifact-01

| Model name | Formula | Key threshold/criterion | What it predicts | Known limitation for Al–Si LPBF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hann normalized enthalpy / King criterion ([Hann 2011](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/44/13/135501); [King 2014](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4903166)) | $\displaystyle \frac{\Delta H}{h_s}=\frac{AP}{h_s\sqrt{\pi\alpha v\sigma_0^3}}$, with $h_s$ the volumetric sensible/latent enthalpy under the chosen convention. | King associated keyholing with $\Delta H/h_s\gtrsim \pi T_b/T_m$—approximately 6 for the reported steel case. Radius, enthalpy, and absorptivity conventions must match the source. | Keyhole/vapour-depression **onset only**; no cavity depth, porosity probability, or keyhole-mode pool dimensions. | The threshold was not fitted to Al–Si. Aluminium’s uncertain, state-dependent absorptivity and large conductivity make a fixed-$A$ threshold highly sensitive; powder and substrate enthalpy conventions also differ. |
| Gan keyhole number $Ke$ ([Gan et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-22266-1)) | Often summarized as $\displaystyle Ke=\left(\frac{\Delta H}{h_s}\right)\left(\frac{\sigma_0}{d}\right)^{1/3}$, with a fitted relation $d/\sigma_0=C Ke^a$. The publication’s exact characteristic-length, beam-size, and enthalpy definitions must be used; treating measured $d$ as an input would make the relation implicit rather than predictive. | Approximate regime boundaries commonly reported as $Ke\approx6$ for keyhole onset and $Ke\approx12$ for unstable-keyhole/porosity risk. These are empirical transition bands, not exact universal constants. | Keyhole onset, unstable-keyhole porosity regime, and an empirical **keyhole-depth scaling**; not independently the final melt-pool width or length. | Includes Al 6061 evidence but not AlSi10Mg or Al–24Si. Transfer requires calibrated absorptivity and strict reproduction of the original definitions; depth scatter should not be interpreted as guaranteed Al–Si accuracy. |
| Cunningham absorbed power-density criterion ([Cunningham et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aav4687)) | $\displaystyle I_{\rm abs}\sim\frac{AP}{\pi r_0^2}$, with the numerical value dependent on Gaussian-radius and peak-versus-average-intensity convention. | Vapour depression/keyhole onset occurred at absorbed intensities of order $10^6\ \mathrm{W\,cm^{-2}}$ (roughly 1–3 MW cm$^{-2}$ as a regime-scale summary) for the reported Ti-6Al-4V experiments. | Onset and qualitative instability behaviour observed by in-situ synchrotron radiography; no transferable depth equation. | Ti-6Al-4V threshold cannot be transferred directly to reflective Al–Si; incident versus absorbed intensity and spot-diameter definitions can shift the inferred boundary by factors of order unity. |
| Fabbro cylindrical energy-balance depth model | A simplified scaling is $\displaystyle d\approx\frac{AP}{\pi r_0\rho v\,[c_p(T_b-T_0)+L_m+L_v]}$. More complete Fabbro models add wall conduction, plume/evaporation losses, multiple reflections, and keyhole geometry. | Positive penetration follows when absorbed power exceeds surface-melting and loss terms; there is no single universal onset or porosity constant. | Approximate steady keyhole **penetration depth** and its power/speed trend; simplified forms do not predict width, length, or pore formation reliably. | This expression is a heuristic cylindrical balance, not a universal closed-form Fabbro law. Results are dominated by effective absorptivity, assumed radius, evaporation enthalpy, and wall-loss closure; validation is mainly laser welding rather than Al–Si LPBF. |
| Kaplan pointwise keyhole-profile model ([Kaplan 1994](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/27/9/002)) | At each wall point $z$, $\displaystyle I_{\rm abs}(z)=I_0[r(z)]A_F[\theta(z)]$ and $\displaystyle I_{\rm abs}=q_{\rm cond}+q_{\rm melt}+q_{\rm evap}$; integrating the local slope/inclination relation iteratively from the mouth to the tip yields $r(z)$ and depth. | A self-consistent cavity exists when local Fresnel-absorbed flux can sustain melting/evaporation and the tip boundary condition closes. No universal numerical onset threshold is built in. | Steady keyhole **depth and cavity profile/width**. It does not by itself provide the complete trailing melt-pool width and length or transient keyhole-porosity probability. | Requires optical constants, wall-temperature/evaporation closure, and careful Gaussian-radius convention. Published validation is chiefly welding of steels; powder-layer, transient, and Al–Si validation remain weak. |
| Eagar–Tsai geometric or boiling proxy ([open implementation](https://github.com/ArroyaveLab/eagar-tsai)) | Apply $W/D<2$, equivalently $D/W>0.5$, or $T_{\max}>T_b$ to a conduction-mode Eagar–Tsai temperature field. | Threshold chosen by the printability-map author; some studies use more restrictive $D/W$ values such as 0.75. | Only a heuristic onset flag plus **conduction-mode** pool dimensions; it predicts neither vapour-cavity depth nor keyhole-porosity instability. | A semi-circular cross-section has $W/D=2$; a surface-source conduction solution therefore cannot reproduce a deep keyhole. Without recoil, multiple reflections, evaporation losses, or temperature clamping, it can cross the proxy too early and predict unphysical temperatures far above boiling. |


*Table: Key equations, thresholds, outputs, and transfer limitations for inexpensive onset criteria and semi-analytical keyhole-depth models. Formula conventions must be reproduced exactly before comparing thresholds across studies.*
