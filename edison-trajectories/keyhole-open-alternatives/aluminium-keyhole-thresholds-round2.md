## Keyhole-Onset Conditions and Melt-Pool Dimensions in AlSi10Mg and Hypereutectic Al–Si Alloys: Measurements, Models, and Evidence Gaps

### 1. Measured Conduction-to-Keyhole Transitions for AlSi10Mg

Solyaev et al. (2025, arXiv:2507.23422) reported that AlSi10Mg single tracks with 80 µm spot diameter enter keyhole mode (D/W > 0.75) for P > 220 W and v < 0.8 m/s. King et al. (2014, DOI: 10.1063/1.4883115) established ΔH/h_s ≈ 6 as the keyhole threshold for 316L (D/W > 0.5); transferring to Al requires ~2–4× higher incident power due to Al's higher thermal diffusivity and lower absorptivity. Cunningham et al. (2019, DOI: 10.1126/science.aav4687) showed via synchrotron radiography that a shallow vapor depression precedes deep keyhole formation. Aboulkhair et al. (2014, DOI: 10.1016/j.matdes.2014.05.049) documented keyhole porosity at high energy density in AlSi10Mg but without direct cavity imaging.

### 2. Absorptivity

Trapp et al. (2017, DOI: 10.1016/j.apmt.2017.08.006): Al alloy absorptivity ~0.09–0.15 (conduction) jumping to ~0.3–0.6 (keyhole). Simonds et al. (2020, DOI: 10.1063/5.0018236) confirmed dynamic absorptivity rise. Multiple-reflection models predict A_eff ≈ 1−(1−A_single)^n. Ye et al. (2019, DOI: 10.1016/j.jmapro.2019.04.015) proposed correlations with keyhole aspect ratio.

### 3. Evaporation and Recoil Pressure

Al boils at 2743 K; Mg at 1363 K (preferential Mg loss). Recoil pressure p_r ≈ 0.54·p_sat(T) exceeds capillary pressure (~10–20 kPa) at ~2800–3000 K. Eagar–Tsai's unphysical 6400 K peak temperature reflects the absence of evaporation physics.

### 4. Si Content Effects

Al-24Si vs AlSi10Mg: lower thermal conductivity, lower surface tension, slightly lower keyhole threshold expected (<20% in power). No direct keyhole-depth measurements for hypereutectic Al-Si in LPBF exist—a significant gap.

### 5. Validated Models

CFD: Khairallah et al. (2016, DOI: 10.1016/j.actamat.2016.02.014) for 316L; Bayat et al. (2019, DOI: 10.1016/j.addma.2019.100834) for AlSi10Mg. Semi-analytical: Fabbro (2010, DOI: 10.1088/0022-3727/43/44/445501); Rubenchik et al. (2018, DOI: 10.1016/j.addma.2018.04.028). Typical depth errors 15–30% on steel; AlSi10Mg-specific validation is lacking. Recommended strategy: retain eagar-tsai for LOF, add keyhole-onset classifier + Fabbro-type depth solver with multiple-reflection absorptivity.
