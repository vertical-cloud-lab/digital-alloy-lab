# Landscape Review: VCL Autonomous Alloy Discovery Lab — RAPID+TCT 2026 Update

## 1. Missing Companies Relevant to Autonomous Alloy Discovery

The current landscape of ~62 companies covers the core LPBF OEMs, powder producers, and CALPHAD/AI software vendors well. However, several strategically relevant companies that are likely present at RAPID+TCT 2026 appear absent and should be considered for addition, particularly in the categories of in-situ monitoring, powder production, simulation, and high-throughput characterization. Of highest priority, **Plastometrex** deserves inclusion as a characterization company, given that its profilometry-based indentation plastometry (PIP) technology can extract full stress–strain curves from small samples without conventional tensile specimens and has been validated against tensile testing for AM-specific materials, including anisotropic superalloy components (tang2021profilometrybasedindentationplastometry pages 1-2, tang2021profilometrybasedindentationplastometry pages 2-3, campbell2022indentationplastometryof pages 10-10). This could complement or partially substitute VCL's automated tensile testing stage. **Equispheres** and **Tekna/AP&C** fill gaps in the powder production category alongside Amazemet, particularly for plasma-atomized powders with tight particle size distributions. **Phase3D** and **Divergent Technologies (PrintRite3D)** address the critical monitoring gap — current commercial LPBF systems largely lack closed-loop feedback driven by real-time monitoring (yuhang2025theapplicationof pages 5-7, yuhang2025theapplicationof pages 3-5), and in-situ ML-based monitoring is becoming central to next-generation quality control (fan2025neweratowards pages 42-43, fan2025neweratowards pages 9-10). **Ansys** (and Hexagon/Simufact) should be included in the software/simulation category for process–structure–property simulation capabilities that complement CALPHAD-based optimization workflows.

**Key corrections and clarifications:**

- **6K Additive:** The UniMelt® microwave plasma technology is well-documented in the literature. The process uses microwave plasma rather than high-velocity gases to spheroidize particles, reportedly achieving close to 100% yield within desired PSD ranges while consuming 92% less energy than traditional methods (yadegari2025aluminumlaseradditive pages 8-9). However, the specific **DOE $107M grant figure** was not verifiable through peer-reviewed sources and should be cited to press releases or government records.

- **Velo3D:** The Sapphire platform is consistently referenced in academic reviews as a leading support-free LPBF system (weber2021supportfreemetaladditive pages 9-10). However, the literature cautions that "support-free L-PBF is not yet fully developed" and there are "severe differences in knowledge" between academic understanding and industry claims about support-free capabilities (weber2021supportfreemetaladditive pages 1-3). The **DoD contract amounts ($11.5M + $9.8M)** are not verifiable through academic literature.

- **AddUp:** The FormUp 350 and its connection to the Michelin–Fives joint venture are well-documented. ORNL and Ford used the FormUp 350 for a process capability study in 2019, demonstrating its fine-powder/roller-recoating capabilities and production of complex geometries in maraging steel and Inconel 718 (joslin2023processcapabilitystudy pages 1-7). The description is accurate.

- **Farsoon Technologies:** Confirmed as a top-3 Chinese SLM manufacturer per Wohlers Report rankings, with the FS621M identified as a production-class system (yuhang2025theapplicationof pages 3-5). The "open-platform" characterization is a marketing distinction rather than a peer-reviewed technical term but is directionally accurate.

- **BLT:** Confirmed as a leading Chinese LPBF OEM with multi-laser configurations (ten-laser systems, ~500W per laser, ~600mm build dimensions, ~7 m/s scan speed) (yuhang2025theapplicationof pages 3-5). The DED capability mentioned in the description has limited peer-reviewed documentation; the company's literature presence is strongest in LPBF/SLM.

- **Materialise:** Magics is one of the most widely cited AM software tools in the literature, consistently referenced for build preparation, support generation, and Build Processor OEM integration (tang2021profilometrybasedindentationplastometry pages 2-3). Description is fully accurate.

- **Oqton/3DXpert:** This is a known commercial product, but the peer-reviewed evidence is weak. The "used by Baker Hughes" claim should be cited to commercial sources.

**Highest-priority presentations for VCL:**

**Closed-loop AI for traceable AM** represents the most direct competitive threat. Recent work demonstrates that real-time experiment–theory closed-loop systems such as AMASE can autonomously map phase diagrams using CALPHAD with a six-fold reduction in required experiments (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 11-13, liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3). Similarly, the AutoMAT framework integrates LLMs, automated CALPHAD via TCPython, and AI-guided search to evaluate over 1,000 compositions per day, compressing multi-year discovery campaigns to under two weeks (yang2025automatahierarchical pages 4-7, yang2025automatahierarchical pages 1-4, yang2025automatahierarchical pages 7-10, yang2025automatahierarchical pages 10-13). These systems are architecturally analogous to VCL's CALPHAD+Bayesian optimization approach and represent both the strongest competitive threat and the most natural collaboration space.

**Crack-free printing of gamma prime Ni superalloys** is both a high-threat and high-collaboration topic. Computational materials modeling combined with CALPHAD-based printability screening has demonstrated up to 41× reduction in crack density for high-γ′ superalloys compared to CM247 (yang2025automatahierarchical pages 10-13). This represents an ideal alloy-design challenge for a VCL-type platform, where CALPHAD-guided composition optimization, LPBF printing, and automated mechanical testing form a natural closed-loop workflow.

**PIP for AM qualification (Plastometrex)** is the strongest collaboration opportunity for the test stage. PIP extracts uniaxial stress–strain curves from small indentations (~1 mm diameter) via iterative FEM, with results "entirely consistent with directly-measured [tensile] curves" (tang2021profilometrybasedindentationplastometry pages 1-2). The method requires only ~10×10×5 mm sample blocks rather than full tensile specimens (tang2021profilometrybasedindentationplastometry pages 2-3), and high-temperature PIP has been demonstrated to 800°C (tammpere2024profilometry‐basedindentationplastometry pages 1-2). Integration of PIP into VCL's test stage could dramatically increase throughput by eliminating dogbone specimen machining.

**ML layer-by-layer defect detection** is an essential capability gap for VCL. Current approaches combine CNNs (U-Net, 3D U-Net), multi-modal sensor fusion (thermal + ultrasonic), acoustic emission monitoring, and high-speed optical imaging to achieve real-time defect detection with sub-millisecond response times (fan2025neweratowards pages 42-43, fan2025neweratowards pages 9-10, fan2025neweratowards pages 14-15, fan2025neweratowards pages 15-17). Critically, most commercial LPBF systems still lack closed-loop feedback driven by real-time monitoring (yuhang2025theapplicationof pages 5-7, yuhang2025theapplicationof pages 3-5), suggesting that integrating in-situ ML monitoring into VCL's LPBF stage would be a significant differentiator.

## 4. Emerging Disruptive Technologies for the Autonomous Alloy Discovery Pipeline

Several emerging technologies could fundamentally alter the powder→print→test→optimize pipeline:

**AI/LLM-augmented autonomous discovery platforms** are the most significant disruptor to the optimization stage. AutoMAT's three-tier architecture (LLM ideation → automated CALPHAD simulation → experimental validation) reduced a 43,000-composition study to 3,161 evaluations and discovered alloys with 28.2% yield-strength improvements in under two weeks (yang2025automatahierarchical pages 1-4, yang2025automatahierarchical pages 10-13). Hierarchical multi-agent systems with Bayesian optimization-based schedulers and furnace-aware feedback have reported seven-fold reductions in lab iterations while maintaining ~97% metallurgical feasibility (roy2026hierarchicalmultiagentsystem pages 2-4, roy2026hierarchicalmultiagentsystem pages 4-5). These frameworks threaten to commoditize the CALPHAD+BO approach unless VCL differentiates through its integrated physical infrastructure (atomization→print→test).

**Profilometry-based indentation plastometry** could disrupt the test stage. PIP's ability to generate full stress–strain curves from indent profiles without tensile specimens, combined with automation and ~minute-scale test times (tammpere2024profilometry‐basedindentationplastometry pages 1-2, campbell2022indentationplastometryof pages 1-1), makes it a potential replacement or augmentation for automated tensile testing in high-throughput alloy screening campaigns.

**DED-based high-throughput compositional screening** offers an alternative to LPBF for rapid alloy exploration. DED has been demonstrated for multi-principal element alloy discovery by enabling in-situ compositional gradients and rapid synthesis of diverse compositions within single builds (hao2025autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 19-21). While this differs from LPBF-optimized alloys, it could accelerate the early screening phase before LPBF parameter optimization.

**Additive friction stir deposition (AFSD)** represents a disruptive alternative manufacturing pathway, particularly for alloys like AA7075 that are traditionally unweldable/unprintable via fusion-based AM. AFSD avoids solidification-related defects entirely through solid-state processing, producing refined grain structures and enabling repair/manufacturing of high-strength aluminum alloys that remain challenging for LPBF (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3).

**Centrifugal atomization** technologies, including plasma rotating electrode process (PREP) variants, are gaining attention as alternatives to gas atomization and ultrasonic atomization for producing spherical AM powders with controlled characteristics, particularly for reactive and high-melting-point alloys.

**In-situ ML-based closed-loop process control** could transform the print stage from an open-loop process into an adaptive, self-correcting system. While not yet widely deployed in commercial systems, laboratory demonstrations of real-time keyhole pore detection, acoustic emission monitoring with >98% accuracy, and layer-wise defect segmentation at <4 ms/frame latency indicate that closed-loop print control is approaching practical readiness (fan2025neweratowards pages 42-43, fan2025neweratowards pages 14-15, fan2025neweratowards pages 15-17).

In summary, VCL's strongest strategic position lies in its integrated physical pipeline (powder→print→test), which most purely computational or software-only competitors cannot replicate. The primary risk is that competing autonomous discovery frameworks (AutoMAT, AMASE, multi-agent systems) advance faster in the optimization layer. Priority actions should include: (1) adding Plastometrex/PIP and in-situ monitoring capabilities (Phase3D, PrintRite3D) to the landscape and evaluating them for integration; (2) monitoring the closed-loop AI and multi-agent alloy discovery space closely; and (3) differentiating VCL by emphasizing its unique combination of on-site powder production, LPBF, and automated mechanical testing — a full physical closed loop that purely computational platforms cannot match.
