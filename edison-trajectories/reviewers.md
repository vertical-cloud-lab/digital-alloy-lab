## Detailed Analysis by Field

### 1. AI/ML-Accelerated Alloy Design and Discovery

**Raymundo Arroyave** (Texas A&M University) is a leading figure in Bayesian optimization applied to alloy design within an ICME framework. His group has developed multi-objective, multi-constraint Bayesian optimization methods for the design of refractory multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs), demonstrating constrained optimization over the Mo-Nb-Ti-V-W system for gas turbine blade applications (mcdowell2021gapsandbarriers pages 10-11). He is also recognized as part of the TMS MGI workforce community (mcdowell2021gapsandbarriers pages 10-11).

**Greg B. Olson** (MIT) is a pioneer of computational alloy design and ICME. Recent work with Taheri-Mousavi and Hart demonstrated a hybrid ML-ICME framework for designing additively manufacturable high-strength aluminum alloys, integrating CALPHAD databases, machine learning libraries, and LPBF processing in a full alloy design cycle (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 25-28).

**Gus L.W. Hart** (BYU) and **Stefano Curtarolo** (Duke) have co-authored a highly cited review in *Nature Reviews Materials* on machine learning for alloys, covering the state of the art in ML methods for alloy phase stability, property prediction, and discovery. Curtarolo leads the AFLOW repository for high-throughput computational materials science (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 28-31).

**Tim Mueller** (Johns Hopkins University) works on ML interatomic potentials and alloy thermodynamics and is recognized in the broader ICME/MGI community (mcdowell2021gapsandbarriers pages 10-11).

**Prasanna Balachandran** (University of Virginia) focuses on adaptive design and ML for materials discovery, contributing methods for Bayesian experimental design applied to functional materials (abolhasani2023theriseof pages 1-2).

### 2. Laser Powder Bed Fusion (LPBF) Additive Manufacturing of Metals

**Anthony D. Rollett** (Carnegie Mellon University) is a central figure in metal AM, particularly in understanding process-structure-property relationships. His group has contributed to ML-enabled autonomous characterization of AM powder feedstocks, in-situ X-ray imaging of keyhole dynamics, and automated microstructural image analysis using computer vision (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 28-31).

**Tao Sun** (University of Virginia) leads operando synchrotron X-ray imaging studies of additive manufacturing, providing unprecedented real-time visualization of melt pool dynamics, keyhole formation, pore evolution, and solidification in LPBF and DED processes. His group has developed instrumentation for simultaneous high-speed X-ray and infrared imaging at synchrotron beamlines.

**A. John Hart** (MIT) focuses on AM systems design and process innovation. Recent collaborative work demonstrated ML-based design of additively manufacturable aluminum alloys with thermally stable microstructures, linking computational design directly to LPBF fabrication (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 25-28).

**Lianyi Chen** (University of Wisconsin-Madison) develops in-situ synchrotron X-ray imaging methods for electron beam and powder bed fusion processes, including novel instrumentation for characterizing powder spreading dynamics.

**Sneha Narra** (Carnegie Mellon University) works on AM process qualification, connecting processing parameters to defect structures and fatigue properties in Ti-6Al-4V and other alloys.

**Dan J. Thoma** (University of Wisconsin-Madison) leads directed energy deposition-based high-throughput alloy development, integrating DED with machine learning in closed-loop active learning frameworks. His group demonstrated rapid fabrication and evaluation of multi-principal element alloys through DED combined with active learning (nelaturu2024multiprincipalelementalloy pages 16-20).

### 3. Autonomous Experimentation in Materials Science

**A. Gilad Kusne** (NIST / University of Maryland) is a leader in autonomous materials discovery systems. He developed CAMEO (Closed-loop Autonomous Materials Exploration and Optimization), which integrates Bayesian active learning with synchrotron characterization for on-the-fly phase mapping and property optimization, resulting in the discovery of novel phase-change memory materials. He also developed ANDiE for autonomous neutron diffraction and the multi-agent lab framework (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 1-2).

**Benji Maruyama** (Air Force Research Laboratory) directs the ARES (Autonomous Research System) program at AFRL, which demonstrated autonomous optimization of carbon nanotube growth. His work is foundational for demonstrating the practical utility of robot scientists in materials R&D (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 1-2, lo2024reviewoflowcost pages 1-3).

**Milad Abolhasani** (North Carolina State University) is a pioneer of self-driving laboratories for chemistry and materials science. His group at NC State develops modular microfluidic SDL platforms and has published extensively on SDL performance metrics and roadmaps for implementation (abolhasani2023theriseof pages 1-2).

**Keith A. Brown** (Boston University) focuses on autonomous experimentation for soft materials and mechanical property optimization, contributing to the community perspective on autonomous experimentation systems (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 1-2).

**John M. Gregoire** (Caltech) leads high-throughput and combinatorial synthesis efforts integrated with AI-driven discovery of functional inorganic materials, particularly for energy applications (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 1-2).

**Ichiro Takeuchi** (University of Maryland) is a pioneer of combinatorial materials science, developing thin-film combinatorial libraries and integrating them with autonomous characterization and active learning for phase mapping (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 1-2).

**Eric Stach** (University of Pennsylvania) leads efforts in autonomous electron microscopy and organized the influential community perspective on autonomous experimentation systems for materials development (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 1-2).

### 4. High-Throughput Characterization of Alloys

**Kenneth S. Vecchio** (UC San Diego) developed the High-Throughput Rapid Experimental Alloy Development (HT-READ) platform, which integrates arc melting-based combinatorial synthesis with ML-enabled EBSD analysis and automated characterization for rapid alloy screening.

**Katharine M. Flores** (Washington University in St. Louis) focuses on high-throughput characterization of multi-principal element alloys, including combinatorial synthesis via physical vapor deposition and laser processing, coupled with automated composition-structure-property mapping (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 1-2).

**Jason Hattrick-Simpers** (University of Toronto; formerly NIST) is a key figure in high-throughput alloy screening and autonomous electrochemistry. His work at NIST developed autonomous scanning droplet cell platforms for corrosion-resistant alloy discovery, and he contributed extensively to the community's foundational perspective on autonomous experimentation (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 1-2, nelaturu2024multiprincipalelementalloy pages 16-20).

**Mitra L. Taheri** (Johns Hopkins University) leads high-throughput and high-fidelity characterization of compositionally complex alloys, including multi-element EXAFS analysis and electron microscopy at scale, with applications to corrosion resistance and oxidation behavior (nelaturu2024multiprincipalelementalloy pages 16-20).

**Daniel B. Miracle** (Air Force Research Laboratory) has authored influential reviews on emerging capabilities for high-throughput characterization of structural materials, covering both experimental and computational approaches (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 1-2).

**Peter K. Liaw** (University of Tennessee, Knoxville) is a leader in high-entropy alloy research, contributing to high-throughput CALPHAD-based screening and experimental validation of lightweight HEAs (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 1-2).

**Adrien Couet** (University of Wisconsin-Madison) developed integrated high-throughput research platforms for materials in extreme environments, including nuclear structural materials, supported by ARPA-E and NSF (nelaturu2024multiprincipalelementalloy pages 16-20).

### 5. Materials Informatics and ICME

**David L. McDowell** (Georgia Institute of Technology) is one of the architects of the ICME paradigm and has published extensively on gaps and barriers to adoption of materials informatics tools and workflows. He co-led the Penn State-Georgia Tech Center for Computational Materials Design and the TMS MGI workforce report (mcdowell2021gapsandbarriers pages 10-11).

**Surya R. Kalidindi** (Georgia Institute of Technology) developed the Materials Knowledge Systems (MKS) framework for microstructure quantification using 2-point statistics and principal component analysis, enabling data-driven process-structure-property linkages. He is identified as a key MGI strategist (mcdowell2021gapsandbarriers pages 10-11).

**Zi-Kui Liu** (Penn State University) is a leading figure in CALPHAD-based computational thermodynamics and the zentropy theory. He co-led the Penn State-Georgia Tech CCMD, which was a catalyst for the broader ICME community (mcdowell2021gapsandbarriers pages 10-11).

**Elizabeth A. Holm** (University of Michigan; formerly Carnegie Mellon University) has pioneered the application of computer vision and machine learning to microstructural characterization and analysis, developing methods for image classification, segmentation, and autonomous characterization of AM powder feedstocks (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 28-31).

**Rampi Ramprasad** (Georgia Institute of Technology) is a leader in polymer informatics and ML force fields, recognized as an MGI strategist with broad impact on data-driven materials design workflows (mcdowell2021gapsandbarriers pages 10-11).

**Taylor D. Sparks** (University of Utah) has developed materials informatics methods including best-practice guides for ML in materials science, inverse design approaches, and low-cost "frugal twin" self-driving laboratory concepts for educational and research applications (lo2024reviewoflowcost pages 1-3).

**L. Catherine Brinson** (Duke University) leads NanoMine, a materials data science platform, and has contributed to conceptual frameworks such as the materials-information twin tetrahedra (MITT) that bridge materials science with information science (mcdowell2021gapsandbarriers pages 10-11).

**Brian DeCost** (NIST) has contributed to automated microstructural image analysis, autonomous scanning droplet cell platforms, and ML frameworks for materials discovery. He co-authored several key community perspective papers on autonomous materials science (stach2021autonomousexperimentationsystems pages 1-2, taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 28-31).

## Cross-Cutting Observations

Many of these researchers span multiple fields, reflecting the inherently interdisciplinary nature of accelerated materials discovery. For example, Arroyave bridges AI/ML alloy design with ICME; Kusne and Hattrick-Simpers connect autonomous experimentation with high-throughput characterization; and Rollett and Holm link LPBF AM with materials informatics. Proposals that span these areas should expect reviewers drawn from multiple categories above.

The funding agencies listed (NSF AM, AFOSR YIP, ARO YIP, DARPA YFA, DoD DURIP, NSF CAREER, NSF EAGER, NSF DMR TMRP, NSF MEP) regularly draw from this community for panel review. Researchers at NIST, AFRL, national laboratories (Argonne, Brookhaven, PNNL), and major research universities (Georgia Tech, Carnegie Mellon, MIT, Texas A&M, Penn State, UVA, UCSD, UMD, UW-Madison) form the core reviewer pool.
