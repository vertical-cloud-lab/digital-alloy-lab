# State of the Art in AI/ML-Accelerated Alloy Discovery and Design (2022–2026)

## 1. Machine Learning Approaches for Predicting Alloy Properties from Composition

The field of ML-accelerated alloy property prediction has matured considerably since 2022, with a diverse ecosystem of model architectures now available. The landscape spans classical ML, graph neural networks (GNNs), transformer-based language models, generative models, and ML interatomic potentials (MLIPs), each serving distinct roles in the alloy discovery pipeline.

**Classical ML models** (random forests, support vector machines, XGBoost, LASSO) remain widely used as fast, interpretable baselines for composition-to-property regression and classification. A Bayesian-optimized regression model for bulk modulus of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) achieved R² = 0.969 with MAE = 3.958 GPa, while a glass-forming ability classifier reached F1 = 0.91 and AUC = 0.98 (jung2024predictivemodelingof pages 1-2). Physics-informed frameworks combining random forests trained on experiments with MLIPs trained on DFT have been applied to predict surface energies and Pilling–Bedworth ratios, with some models reporting R² ≈ 0.98 for bulk modulus (golbabaei2025applicationsofmachine pages 44-45). However, these models are constrained by data scarcity, heterogeneous labels, and dependence on hand-crafted descriptor quality (jung2024predictivemodelingof pages 1-2, golbabaei2025applicationsofmachine pages 44-45).

**Graph neural networks** have become foundational for structure-aware property prediction. Crystal Graph Convolutional Neural Networks (CGCNN) achieve DFT-comparable accuracy on formation energy and band gap (lookman2026materialsinformaticsemergence pages 20-22). DeeperGATGNN, which uses group normalization and skip connections to support very deep architectures (up to 30 layers), achieves up to 10% improved accuracy over shallower GNNs (hao2025autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 29-32). The Deep Sets architecture has been applied to HEA elastic property prediction with superior generalizability across composition spaces spanning 14 elements (lookman2026materialsinformaticsemergence pages 20-22).

**Transformer and LLM-based models** represent an emerging frontier. Specialized pretrained transformers such as SteelBERT (DeBERTa-based, 0.18B parameters) predict yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, and elongation for steels with R² ≈ 0.78–0.82 (lookman2026materialsinformaticsemergence pages 24-27). MatSciBERT combined with contrastive learning achieves R² = 0.849 on Ti-alloy ductility with experimental validation (lookman2026materialsinformaticsemergence pages 24-27). Teacher–student strategies where structure-based pretrained models improve composition-based property models (CPMs) have demonstrated a 37.1% accuracy boost on small datasets (hao2025autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 29-32). However, integrating processing and microstructure text data remains challenging, and small, noisy alloy datasets make multi-task learning nontrivial (lookman2026materialsinformaticsemergence pages 24-27).

**Generative models** (VAEs, conditional diffusion-VAE hybrids such as CDVAE, GANs) now enable inverse design of alloy compositions and crystal structures under multi-property constraints (lookman2026materialsinformaticsemergence pages 20-22, rao2024activelearningstrategies pages 2-4). These have been integrated into closed-loop discovery pipelines and process-synergistic active learning frameworks (cai2025aprocesssynergisticactive pages 10-11).

## 2. Bayesian Optimization and Active Learning for Alloy Design

Bayesian optimization (BO) and active learning (AL) have emerged as the dominant paradigms for efficient navigation of combinatorially vast alloy composition spaces. The approach was proposed for materials around 2014 and experimentally validated in 2016 when ultra-low thermal hysteresis NiTi-based shape memory alloys were discovered from ~800,000 candidates (lookman2026materialsinformaticsemergence pages 9-12).

**Multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO)** has been a major advance. Khatamsaz et al. (Texas A&M) demonstrated an entropy-based BO approach that actively learns unknown design constraints for the Mo-Nb-Ti-V-W multi-principal element alloy (MPEA) system, identifying 21 Pareto-optimal alloys satisfying multiple constraints (room-temperature ductility, high-temperature yield strength, low density, high thermal conductivity) much more efficiently than brute force (khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 1-2). The framework incorporates constraint learning to restrict optimization to feasible regions, substantially reducing wasted evaluations.

**Active learning strategies** have been extensively benchmarked for alloy design. Key approaches include dependent strategies (Boltzmann sampling, Alpha rank-based methods) and independent strategies (brute-force exploration followed by exploitation, interactive switching). Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) with Expected Improvement (EI) acquisition functions remains the workhorse, though logarithmic EI has improved numerical stability and convergence in higher dimensions (lookman2026materialsinformaticsemergence pages 12-15, rao2024activelearningstrategies pages 7-8). Maximin-based design strategies have reduced the number of measurements needed to find Pareto-front points by ~30% versus random sampling (rao2024activelearningstrategies pages 13-14).

**Closed-loop demonstrations** have become increasingly sophisticated. PAL 2.0, developed by Priyadarshini et al. (JHU/JHU APL), integrates XGBoost feature selection, a neural-network prior, and a Gaussian process surrogate within BO. Using rapid arc-melting validation, PAL 2.0 synthesized 20 new MPEAs and discovered two compositions with Vickers hardness of 1269 and 1263—increasing the number of known compositions above 1000 hardness from three to eight. It also uncovered a novel silicon+tantalum combination not present among high-hardness training examples (priyadarshini2025machinelearningdrivenclosedloop pages 3-6, priyadarshini2025machinelearningdrivenclosedloop pages 1-3). Closed-loop frameworks have been reported to reduce overall discovery time by over 90% (priyadarshini2025machinelearningdrivenclosedloop pages 3-6).

**Reinforcement learning (RL)** has emerged as an alternative to BO for high-dimensional, combinatorial design spaces, with model-based RL showing more dispersed exploration compared to BO-EI's tendency to concentrate sampling centrally (lookman2026materialsinformaticsemergence pages 15-20).

## 3. High-Throughput Computational Screening Methods

The integration of DFT, CALPHAD, and molecular dynamics through ML-accelerated pipelines has transformed high-throughput alloy screening.

**High-throughput CALPHAD (HT-CALPHAD)** has been established as a cost-effective, credible route for screening alloys, following defined steps of elemental selection, dataset generation, and ranking, with a typical palette of ~57 non-toxic elements (ghassemali2022highthroughputcalphada pages 2-4, ghassemali2022highthroughputcalphada pages 1-2). CALPHAD is particularly effective for phase stability predictions and can be combined with ML, DFT, and empirical models for multi-scale screening workflows (ghassemali2022highthroughputcalphada pages 1-2).

**Universal machine learning interatomic potentials (uMLIPs)** represent perhaps the most transformative advance. M3GNet, CHGNet, MACE, SevenNet, and ORB have been trained on massive DFT datasets and can be applied across the periodic table without retraining (zhu2025acceleratingcalphadbasedphase pages 1-2). Zhu and Arróyave (Texas A&M) demonstrated that integrating these uMLIPs with the Alloy Theoretic Automated Toolkit (ATAT) enables mapping of ML-calculated energies into CALPHAD-compatible thermodynamic descriptions, achieving computational speedups exceeding three orders of magnitude compared to DFT while maintaining acceptable phase-stability accuracy (zhu2025acceleratingcalphadbasedphase pages 1-2). The ORB potential was noted as particularly accurate, approaching viability for high-throughput generation of CALPHAD thermodynamic descriptions for multi-component, multi-phase alloy systems (zhu2025acceleratingcalphadbasedphase pages 1-2). Recent benchmarking shows the EquiformerV2 models achieving DFT-level accuracy with RMSE below 5 meV/atom for energies (zhang2025roadmapforthe pages 42-43).

**Active-learning-driven molecular dynamics** has enabled on-the-fly training of ML potentials during simulations, where epistemic uncertainty triggers selective DFT calculations. This "simulation-verification-update" closed loop captures transient, high-barrier configurations during processes like melting or ionic hopping while avoiding exhaustive DFT sampling (chen2026asurveyof pages 28-30). Large-scale hybrid MC/MD studies using SNAP and MTP have predicted melting points, stacking-fault energies, stress–strain responses, and dislocation migration in HEA supercells containing hundreds of thousands of atoms (zhang2025roadmapforthe pages 42-43).

Key remaining challenges include the need for diverse multi-component training datasets (enhanced chemical complexity increases DFT data requirements), limited transferability for out-of-distribution configurations, and short radial cutoffs that miss long-range mechanical/strain fields (zhang2025roadmapforthe pages 42-43).

## 4. Integration of Computational Predictions with Experimental Validation

The "closing of the loop" between computation and experiment has progressed from ad hoc iteration to increasingly automated, autonomous systems.

**AMASE (Autonomous Materials Search Engine)**, developed by Liang, Takeuchi, and Kusne (University of Maryland/NIST), represents a breakthrough in real-time experiment-theory closed-loop interaction. AMASE couples Bayesian active learning for composition selection with automated XRD and thermal processing, and integrates live CALPHAD thermodynamic modeling via Thermo-Calc that continuously updates computed phase diagrams to drive subsequent experimental choices. Applied to the Sn-Bi thin-film eutectic system, AMASE autonomously mapped the phase diagram in approximately eight hours, achieving a six-fold reduction in required experiments versus exhaustive grid mapping (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3, liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 11-13).

**Hybrid ML-CALPHAD workflows for LPBF** have been demonstrated by Taheri-Mousavi, Hart, and Olson (MIT). Their approach used CALPHAD/ICME coupled with Bayesian optimization to inverse-design an Al-Er-Zr-Y-Yb-Ni alloy across a five-dimensional composition space. The selected composition was manufactured as AM powder and tested via LPBF, matching wrought Al7075 strength without cracking and achieving 50% greater strength than the strongest known printable Al alloy benchmark after 400°C aging (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 1-4).

**Closed-loop superconductor discovery** by Pogue et al. (JHU APL) demonstrated that iterative ML prediction and experimental validation can more than double success rates for superconductor discovery, identifying new superconductors in the Zr-In-Ni system and five re-discovered superconductors through four closed-loop cycles (chen2026asurveyof pages 25-28). Quantitatively, closed-loop frameworks have been shown to reduce required experiments by factors of 3–6× compared to random or grid-based searches (chen2026asurveyof pages 25-28), and computational closed-loop workflows can provide up to 20× acceleration overall through combined task automation, ML surrogatization, and sequential learning (chen2026asurveyof pages 25-28).

## 5. Autonomous/Self-Driving Laboratories for Materials Discovery

Self-driving laboratories (SDLs) have matured from conceptual frameworks to operational platforms. Comprehensive reviews by Tom et al. (Chemical Reviews, 2024) and Abolhasani and Kumacheva (Nature Synthesis, 2023) have established an SDL taxonomy analogous to autonomous driving levels, with most current systems at intermediate autonomy (chen2026asurveyof pages 37-39).

**APEX (Autonomous Alloy Prediction and EXperimentation)** at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory represents the first reported self-driving laboratory specifically designed for alloy discovery. APEX integrates directed-energy deposition for sample fabrication, automated grinding/polishing (including vibration sensors to replicate human "feel"), automated microscopy, hardness indentation, and compression testing. The platform is designed for continuous operation with throughput of approximately 48 samples per day, with all data feeding into a Materials Acceleration Platform (MAP) where ML agents learn from past experiments to propose new alloy candidates (conn2025selfdrivinglabto pages 1-8).

**Materials Acceleration Platforms (MAPs)** more broadly promise to reduce the traditional 10–20-year materials development timeline to 1–2 years by implementing closed-loop systems combining automated robotic experimentation, real-time analytics, and ML-driven experimental planning (hao2025autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 4-7, hao2025autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 1-4). Architectures such as MINERVA use central robot arms linking liquid handling, temperature control, sampling, and inline analytics, while common algorithmic tools include Bayesian optimization, active learning, and quality-diversity evolutionary methods (hao2025autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 4-7, hao2025autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 7-11).

Practical demonstrations have expanded rapidly: robotic BO workflows have reduced experiments to find optimal formulations from 50 to 17 (a 3× improvement), and semi-autonomous platforms have discovered novel 3D-printing formulations in 30 iterations (chen2026asurveyof pages 37-39). Reinforcement learning agents pretrained on offline alloy data can explore higher-value composition regions more efficiently than standard BO-EI (lookman2026materialsinformaticsemergence pages 15-20).

## 6. Closed-Loop Autonomous Pipeline Combining AI Screening, Autonomous Powder Production, LPBF Validation, and Automated Characterization

A fully integrated, autonomous pipeline spanning AI-based compositional screening → autonomous powder production → LPBF printing → automated characterization represents the frontier aspiration for alloy discovery, but no such end-to-end system has been reported in the literature to date. This analysis reveals how such a pipeline compares to and extends existing approaches:

**Existing approaches and their limitations.** Current demonstrations address individual segments of this pipeline. APEX at LLNL uses directed-energy deposition (DED) rather than LPBF for sample fabrication, with automated characterization downstream (conn2025selfdrivinglabto pages 1-8). The MIT hybrid ML-CALPHAD-LPBF workflow demonstrated computational design through LPBF validation but relied on conventional powder atomization and manual sample preparation steps (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 1-4). Hariharan et al. (RWTH Aachen) used extreme high-speed laser application (EHLA) with in-situ alloying and ML to rapidly screen 20 alloy combinations, but this also bypasses the powder production bottleneck rather than automating it (hariharan2025high‐speed3dprinting pages 1-2, hariharan2025high‐speed3dprinting pages 12-13). Reviews have explicitly identified the time-intensive powder atomization step as a key bottleneck limiting the speed of alloy development for LPBF (hariharan2025high‐speed3dprinting pages 1-2).

**Advantages of the proposed pipeline.** A closed-loop autonomous LPBF pipeline would address several critical gaps simultaneously: (i) it would directly validate alloys under the processing conditions (rapid solidification, steep thermal gradients, multiple thermal cycles) for which they are ultimately intended, unlike arc-melting or thin-film screening proxies (liu2024alloydesignfor pages 24-25); (ii) autonomous powder production would eliminate the multi-week powder atomization delay that disconnects computational predictions from experimental feedback (hariharan2025high‐speed3dprinting pages 1-2); (iii) automated characterization would enable high-throughput feedback to ML models, supporting continuous improvement of property predictions; and (iv) alloy-agnostic LPBF process mapping using CNNs has been shown to generalize across materials with up to 96% accuracy, enabling process optimization for novel compositions without exhaustive parameter studies (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 28-31).

**Comparison to existing platforms.** Such a pipeline would represent a significant advance over existing systems in integration scope. AMASE achieves real-time experiment-theory coupling but operates on thin films (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3). APEX achieves autonomous synthesis-to-characterization but uses DED (conn2025selfdrivinglabto pages 1-8). PAL 2.0 achieves closed-loop compositional design but uses manual arc-melting (priyadarshini2025machinelearningdrivenclosedloop pages 3-6). The proposed pipeline would uniquely combine: computational screening (ML + CALPHAD/DFT), physical realization in the target manufacturing modality (LPBF), and automated feedback—all within a single autonomous loop. This would close the "processability gap" that currently exists between computationally predicted compositions and their actual performance when manufactured via AM.

**Key challenges.** Major obstacles include: automating powder atomization with quality control for novel chemistries; developing alloy-agnostic LPBF process maps that generalize to unseen compositions; integrating physics-guided ML with in-situ monitoring for defect detection; managing the high dimensionality of simultaneous composition + process optimization; and standardizing data formats and orchestration protocols across modular subsystems (fan2025neweratowards pages 21-23, hariharan2025high‐speed3dprinting pages 12-13, hao2025autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 7-11).

## Summary

The period 2022–2026 has witnessed a paradigm shift in alloy discovery, from isolated computational or experimental campaigns to increasingly integrated, autonomous pipelines. Key breakthroughs include universal ML interatomic potentials achieving >1000× speedups over DFT (zhu2025acceleratingcalphadbasedphase pages 1-2), transformer-based property prediction models achieving R² > 0.85 for alloy mechanical properties (lookman2026materialsinformaticsemergence pages 24-27), real-time experiment-theory coupling systems like AMASE providing 6× experimental reduction (liang2025realtimeexperimenttheoryclosedloop pages 1-3), and closed-loop discovery platforms like PAL 2.0 uncovering novel high-hardness alloys within three optimization cycles (priyadarshini2025machinelearningdrivenclosedloop pages 3-6). However, a fully autonomous pipeline integrating AI screening, powder production, LPBF validation, and automated characterization remains unrealized. Such a system would represent the next frontier, closing the "processability gap" between computational predictions and manufacturing reality while potentially compressing alloy development timelines from years to weeks.
