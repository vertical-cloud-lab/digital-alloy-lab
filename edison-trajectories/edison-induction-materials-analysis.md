# Autonomous Powder-to-Part Alloy Discovery Platform: Materials Landscape Analysis for DOE Genesis Mission (DE-FOA-0003612, Topic 12F)

## 1. AMAZEMET rePOWDER System Capabilities and Temperature Constraints

The AMAZEMET rePOWDER ultrasonic atomization system offers three melting modules with distinct temperature envelopes. The induction crucible module operates up to approximately 1100–1300°C and has been experimentally used to process Al-10Cu at 1100°C and Mg alloys at ~950°C (1223 K) (ukabhai2025investigationofalcu pages 3-7, ukabhai2025investigationofalcu pages 1-3, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6). The arc melting module can reach temperatures up to 3500°C at the electrode tip, enabling processing of refractory alloys, while the plasma torch module provides an intermediate high-temperature capability for alloys like Ti and Nb C103 (ukabhai2025investigationofalcu pages 1-3, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3). Ultrasonic atomization on the rePOWDER platform has been demonstrated across a remarkably broad alloy space, including Al-10Cu, AlSi12, Al 5754, Mg-Ag variants, AZ31B, pure Cu (from recycled scrap), Nb C103 (Nb-10Hf-1Ti), Ti alloys (from recycled machining chips), Pt-Ru, Inconel 625, Si, W (microgram-scale), Zr-based metallic glass, NiTi, and SS316L (ukabhai2025investigationofalcu pages 1-3, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3, zach2023sphericalpowderscontrol pages 4-4, bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 2-5, bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 1-2). For induction-based processing, the system is ideally suited for low-melting alloys (Al, Mg, Zn, and many Cu alloys), while arc/plasma modules are required for steels, Ti, Ni superalloys, and refractory metals (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3).

**Key observations:** The ~1300°C induction limit encompasses all aluminum alloy families (liquidus 570–660°C), all magnesium alloys (liquidus ~595–650°C), zinc alloys, and—critically—several important copper alloy families. AlSi10Mg and AlSi12 are the most mature LPBF aluminum systems, with LPBF demonstrations showing applications in aerospace weight reduction (35% weight savings in Airbus components) and high-performance structural parts (wang2024areviewon pages 15-18). High-strength 2xxx, 6xxx, and 7xxx aluminum alloys are processable by LPBF when modified with Sc, Zr, Ti, or V grain refiners to suppress hot cracking (wang2024areviewon pages 3-4). Cu-Cr-Zr (liquidus ~1075°C) and Cu-15Ni-8Sn (liquidus ~1150°C) are both within the induction envelope and have been demonstrated in LPBF, making them strong Phase I stretch targets (biffi2024cucrzralloymanufactured pages 4-8, guo2023areviewof pages 1-3).

**The critical finding:** The 1300–1600°C range contains the single most important cluster of AMMTO-priority structural alloys. LPBF 316H stainless steel (~1400°C liquidus) is the named pathfinder material for an ASME 100,000-hour Code Case under the AMMT program (li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 62-64, li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 58-61). Ultrasonic atomization of SS316L has already been demonstrated for closed-loop LPBF recycling, producing highly spherical powder with D50 ~31 µm and improved flowability compared to gas-atomized feedstock (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 2-5, bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 9-11, bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 1-2).

True arc/plasma capability (>1600°C) is required only for Ti alloys (~1660°C), Nb alloys (~2350°C), and W (~3422°C) (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3, zach2023sphericalpowderscontrol pages 4-4, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6).

### 4a. Is this a defensible framing for AMMTO?

**Yes, emphatically.** The AMMT 2025 Roadmap explicitly prioritizes: (i) AI/ML for accelerated qualification and processing-structure-property-performance (P-S-P-P) modeling; (ii) LPBF and DED as the primary AM methods of interest; (iii) integrated material and manufacturing development; and (iv) rapid qualification pathways using risk-informed, data-driven approaches (li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 6-10, li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 72-73, li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 25-27). The proposal's autonomous BOFN framework operating across ultrasonic atomization → LPBF → ASTM E8 testing directly addresses the AMMTO emphasis on "integrated computational approaches" and "data-driven AI/ML to bridge atomistic-to-mesoscale modeling for accelerated design" (li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 25-27). The DOE EERE mission further supports lightweight Al alloys for transportation energy efficiency, where ~10% mass reduction yields ~6% fuel economy improvement (hunt2010linkingtransformationalmaterials pages 15-16, hunt2010linkingtransformationalmaterials pages 23-25).

### 4b. Most compelling Phase II target alloy systems for AMMTO reviewers

In order of likely reviewer impact:

1. **LPBF 316H stainless steel** — The single most defensible Phase II target. It is the named ASME Code Case pathfinder material in the AMMT roadmap, with an active 100,000-hour qualification effort (li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 62-64, li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 58-61). Demonstrating AI-accelerated P-S-P-P relationships for 316H would directly contribute to the most visible AMMTO deliverable.

2. **Alloy 709** — The flagship advanced nuclear structural alloy with ongoing Code Case development for sodium-cooled and advanced reactor applications (li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 32-33, li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 14-16).

3. **Inconel 718** — A widely recognized AM alloy explicitly cited in the AMMT roadmap, with broad relevance to high-temperature energy systems (li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 32-33, li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 14-16).

4. **Hastelloy N** — Directly relevant to molten-salt reactor environments, an area of active DOE investment (li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 32-33).

5. **Ti-6Al-4V** (arc module) — Demonstrates full platform breadth. Ultrasonic atomization of Ti alloys from recycled machining chips has been demonstrated on rePOWDER-class systems, with oxygen pickup of only ~220 ppm and compliance with ASTM F2924 (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6).

### 4c. Framing the Phase I → Phase II technology transfer

The strongest framing positions the **AI/BOFN platform as the invariant asset** and the **heating hardware as the expandable variable**. Specifically:

- **Same AI platform:** The Bayesian optimization framework, data ontology, powder-process-property linkages, and autonomous decision engine developed on Al alloys in Phase I transfer directly to steels and Ni alloys in Phase II. The AMMT roadmap explicitly calls for "integrated computational approaches (CALPHAD, process and microstructure modeling)" and "data-driven AI/ML" for accelerated design (li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 25-27, li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 27-29).

- **Same BOFN framework:** The Build-Observe-Fit-Navigate experimental loop is alloy-agnostic. Phase I establishes the priors, data schemas, and uncertainty quantification methods that Phase II refines via transfer learning across temperature/alloy classes (li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 6-10, li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 72-73).

- **Expanded materials window:** The induction-to-arc upgrade is presented as a planned capability expansion that opens access to the exact alloy families named in AMMTO roadmaps—not a pivot, but a maturation. The circular-economy dimension (ultrasonic atomization enables powder recycling from AM waste) adds a sustainability narrative aligned with DOE goals (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 2-5, bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 1-2, ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3).

## 5. Cu-Based Structural Alloys Within ~1300°C for DOE Energy Missions

Several Cu-based structural alloy systems are comfortably within the ~1300°C induction processing envelope:

**Cu-Cr-Zr (CuCr1Zr):** Liquidus ~1075°C, well within the induction limit. This precipitation-hardened alloy is the baseline structural material for actively cooled plasma-facing components in fusion reactors (ITER, DEMO) and has been used in Tore Supra for >20 years (biffi2024cucrzralloymanufactured pages 4-8, jahns2020additivemanufacturingof pages 1-2). LPBF processing achieves relative densities of 99.3–99.8%, with post-heat-treatment thermal conductivity reaching ~345–350 W/(m·K)—comparable to wrought material (biffi2024cucrzralloymanufactured pages 8-11, biffi2024cucrzralloymanufactured pages 1-4). As-built LPBF CuCrZr exhibits UTS ~230 MPa and elongation ~31%; heat treatment improves UTS to ~265 MPa (biffi2024cucrzralloymanufactured pages 4-8). For the DOE fusion energy mission, demonstrating autonomous AI-driven optimization of Cu-Cr-Zr processing would be highly relevant.

**Cu-15Ni-8Sn (ToughMet 3 / UNS C72900):** Liquidus ~1150°C, within the induction limit. This spinodal-hardening alloy is an established beryllium-copper replacement with UTS values reaching ~593 MPa via LPBF and ~1300 MPa via conventional thermomechanical processing (guo2023areviewof pages 1-3, guo2023areviewof pages 14-16, guo2023areviewof pages 5-7, guo2023areviewof pages 12-14). LPBF has been demonstrated, with rapid cooling producing fine cellular microstructures and subsequent aging precipitating DO22 and L12 ordered phases (guo2023areviewof pages 14-16). The Cu-Be replacement narrative is compelling for DOE manufacturing safety and supply-chain objectives.

**Pure Cu:** Melting point 1085°C, comfortably within the induction range. Ultrasonic atomization of pure Cu from recycled scrap has been demonstrated on the rePOWDER system, achieving ~99.5% purity with good flowability and powder morphology (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6). LPBF of Cu is challenging due to high reflectivity at 1060 nm fiber laser wavelength but has been addressed with high-power lasers and alloying strategies. Copper is critical for energy transition technologies including heat exchangers, busbars, and electrical interconnections (musa2023advancedmanufacturingand pages 30-32).

**Cu bronzes (Ni-Al bronze, phosphor bronze):** Liquidus typically 1000–1040°C, within the induction range. These alloys are relevant to hydropower turbine components, marine hardware, and corrosion-resistant structural applications, all of which are DOE water power and energy-efficiency priorities (musa2023advancedmanufacturingand pages 46-50, musa2023advancedmanufacturingand pages 34-37). Additive manufacturing of nickel-aluminum bronze and tin bronze has been reviewed in the copper AM literature (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 1-3).

**Cu-Ni alloys (Cu-30Ni, Monel-type):** These have liquidus temperatures in the range of ~1240–1350°C, placing them at the upper boundary of the current induction capability but potentially accessible with process optimization. They are relevant to marine, condenser, and corrosion-resistant applications.

## Summary and Recommendations

The Phase I → Phase II strategy is well-structured for the Genesis Mission FOA. The current ~1300°C induction module provides access to a rich and DOE-relevant alloy space (Al families, Mg alloys, and multiple Cu structural alloys) that enables rapid demonstration of the autonomous AI platform. The most important strategic insight from this analysis is that the practical gap between ~1300°C and ~1600°C contains the most AMMTO-relevant alloy families (316H, Alloy 709, Inconel 718, Hastelloy N)—these do not require full arc/plasma capability and represent an intermediate hardware upgrade that opens the door to the AMMT roadmap's highest-priority materials. The proposal should emphasize that the AI/BOFN framework is the durable, transferable asset, while the heating module is a modular hardware variable that progressively expands the accessible materials design space from lightweight structural alloys through nuclear-grade structural steels and superalloys.
