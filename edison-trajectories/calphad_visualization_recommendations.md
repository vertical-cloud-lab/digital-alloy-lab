## 3. Detailed Recommendations by Visualization Category

### 3.1 Screening Funnel Diagram (Highest Priority — Generate Now)

The screening funnel is the single most efficient visualization for communicating the FLAIME pipeline's scale and rigor. In the HT-CALPHAD literature, Zhang et al. (2025) demonstrated a stepwise down-selection from ~160,000 CrCoNiFe compositions to ~1,500 candidates through sequential thermodynamic filters (stacking fault energy, FCC stability, transformation temperature), with counts annotated at each stage (zhang2025investigationoftwiptrip pages 6-9). Hastings et al. (2024) similarly showed reduction from ~53,124 compositions through CALPHAD-based constraints (single-phase FCC stability, solidification range <100 K, thermal conductivity, density) to 2,437 feasible candidates (hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 3-5). Ghassemali and Conway (2022) describe this HT-CALPHAD workflow as having "four distinct steps" from elemental selection through ranking into "islands of selected compositions" (ghassemali2022highthroughputcalphada pages 2-4). Elder et al. (2023) used a "rapid Pareto optimal front approach" as the primary down-selection mechanism across an 11-element refractory MPEA space (elder2023computationaldiscoveryof pages 10-10).

For FLAIME, the funnel should show: **100K+ compositions** → Stage 1 pycalphad screening (phase stability + mineral-burden filter) → **top 1K** → Stage 2 TC-Python Scheil analysis → **top 50–100** → Stage 3 TC AM Module printability → **final experimental candidates**. This directly parallels published approaches while adding the unique mineral-burden filter.

### 3.2 Mineral-Burden vs. Strength Pareto Front (Highest Priority — Generate Now)

This is FLAIME's most distinctive visualization, and our literature review confirms that **no competitor has published a mineral-burden Pareto front for alloy optimization**. Multi-objective Pareto front visualization is well-established in alloy design: Moitzi et al. (2024) used Pareto fronts to visualize strength-vs-ductility trade-offs with color-coded alloying element identifiers and complementary plots of material descriptors along the front (moitzi2024abinitioframework pages 10-11, moitzi2024abinitioframework pages 2-3). Dai et al. (2022) demonstrated pairwise scatter plots with highlighted Pareto frontiers and overlapping contour maps for dual objectives in high-throughput screening (dai2022highthroughputscreeningof pages 8-10). Paraskevas et al. (2019) used 2D and 3D scatter plots with a prominently displayed Pareto front line to visualize trade-offs between dilution and quality in aluminum recycling (paraskevas2019evaluatingthematerial pages 13-17). Burns et al. (2024), in a DOE OSTI report, plotted hardness vs. yield strength to extract Pareto fronts for alloy development (burns2024developmentresultson pages 38-40).

The FLAIME Pareto front should plot MB = Σᵢ wᵢ·xᵢ (x-axis) versus a CALPHAD-derived strength proxy such as predicted L1₂-Al₃Zr volume fraction or solidus temperature (y-axis). Benchmark alloys containing Sc and REE should be annotated to show they occupy high-MB positions. Quantitative supply risk weights from OECD assessments can anchor the MB scoring: Coulomb et al. (2015) report supply risk values of 4.6 for heavy REE, 3.1 for light REE, 2.4 for Mg, 1.1 for Sc, versus only 0.7 for Mn and 1.1 for Cr (coulomb2015criticalmineralstoday pages 47-51). The USGS methodology defines supply risk as "the confluence of three factors: the likelihood of a foreign supply disruption, the dependency of U.S. manufacturers on foreign supplies, and the ability of U.S. manufacturers to withstand a supply disruption" and identified 23 commodities as highest risk (nassar2020evaluatingthemineral pages 1-2). A companion bar chart of these supply risk weights makes the critical minerals rationale immediately legible to non-specialist reviewers.

### 3.3 Phase Diagrams and Scheil Solidification Curves (Generate Now with pycalphad)

Phase diagrams and Scheil solidification curves are canonical CALPHAD outputs that can be generated immediately with pycalphad and freely available TDB databases. Ghassemali and Conway (2022) identified phase diagrams as the foundational visualization in HT-CALPHAD and emphasized that "temperatures, phase fractions, composition and lattice parameters of phases, solutionising window, etc." are the core data used for ranking compositions (ghassemali2022highthroughputcalphada pages 2-4). Bocklund et al. (2020) developed an open-source Scheil-Gulliver implementation built on pycalphad that returns "fractions and compositions of solid and liquid phases at various temperatures" for high-throughput mapping over composition grids (bocklund2020scheilgulliversimulationsfor pages 6-10). This implementation can loop over Al-Zr composition series to produce systematic solidification curves.

For the Al-Zr system specifically, Griffiths (2020) demonstrated ThermoCalc Scheil predictions showing primary Al₃Zr precipitation during solidification, with Gibbs free-energy plots comparing equilibrium D0₂₃-Al₃Zr and metastable L1₂-Al₃Zr stability, and computed nucleation barriers and solute trapping parameters (griffiths2020additivemanufacturingof pages 79-82). Mehta (2023) confirmed that CALPHAD/Scheil approaches for Al-Mn-Cr-Zr alloys were used to guide alloy chemistry selection for PBF-LB processing (mehta2023developmentofhigh pages 39-43).

For preliminary results, use pycalphad with the COST 507 or similar free TDB to compute: (a) the Al-rich corner of the Al-Zr binary phase diagram showing the peritectic topology and Al₃Zr fields, and (b) Scheil solidification curves for 2–3 selected Al-Zr compositions (e.g., Al-0.5Zr, Al-1.0Zr, Al-1.5Zr wt.%) highlighting differences in freezing range and intermetallic onset.

### 3.4 Composition-Property Heatmaps and Feasibility Maps (Generate Now)

Composition-space heatmaps are effective for showing where promising alloys cluster. Zhang et al. (2025) presented SFE maps, isothermal phase diagrams, and T₀ temperature maps across composition space with sequential filtering criteria overlaid (zhang2025investigationoftwiptrip pages 6-9). Sun et al. (2024) developed the MaterialsMap tool, which constructs dense composition grids (42 points per axis, 903 total in a ternary plane), runs equilibrium and Scheil-Gulliver calculations for each, and renders up to eight feasibility maps including deleterious-phase and crack-susceptibility maps (sun2024materialsmapacalphadbased pages 10-15, sun2024materialsmapacalphadbased pages 6-10). Ghassemali and Conway (2022) noted that high-dimensional results are often visualized using 2D/3D plots with color/shape encoding, pairwise element plots, and dimensionality-reduction projections (ghassemali2022highthroughputcalphada pages 4-5).

For FLAIME, generate pseudo-ternary slices in Al-Mn-Cr-Zr space (e.g., fixed Al, varying Mn-Cr-Zr) colored by solidus temperature, freezing range, or L1₂ solvus, with mineral-burden contours overlaid to show low-burden feasible regions.

### 3.5 LPBF Printability Maps (Stage 3 — Describe as Proposed Work)

Printability maps represent the most visually impactful AM-specific output but require Thermo-Calc AM Module capabilities and are best described as proposed work rather than preliminary data. Sheikh et al. (2025) demonstrated printability maps constructed by mapping laser power vs. scan speed (40–400 W, 0.05–3.00 m/s) and classifying each point by defect regime using melt-pool geometry predictions (sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 3-5, sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 40-42, sheikh2024anautomatedcomputational pages 3-4). Their framework applied twelve combinations of defect criteria (lack-of-fusion, balling, keyholing) across three thermal models and defined a "printability index" as the fraction of defect-free processing space (sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 40-42, sheikh2024anautomatedcomputational pages 2-3). Sheikh et al. (2024) further detailed how Eagar-Tsai and Gladush-Smurov models feed melt-pool predictions into color-coded power-vs-speed maps with dashed lines for maximum hatch spacing (sheikh2024anautomatedcomputational pages 14-15). Sargent et al. (2021) showed how the CALPHAD-ICME framework couples LPBF thermal histories with DICTRA solidification calculations to map solidification cracking susceptibility as a function of processing parameters (sargent2024integratedcomputationaland pages 40-45, sargent2021integrationofprocessing pages 10-11).

Even without generating printability maps now, describing this Stage 3 capability with a schematic and citing precedent will strongly appeal to AMMTO reviewers because it demonstrates co-optimization of alloy chemistry and AM processing parameters.

### 3.6 Bayesian Optimization Loop Diagram (Generate Now)

Hastings et al. (2024) provided an effective template for visualizing multi-objective Bayesian optimization loops that couple CALPHAD predictions, experimental synthesis, characterization, and ML-guided next-batch selection (hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 2-3, hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 12-13). Their framework tracked optimization progress via hypervolume plots showing Pareto-front expansion per iteration and used UMAP embeddings colored by properties to show coverage of composition space (hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 39-44). Burns et al. (2024) similarly illustrated a MOBO framework for alloy design in a DOE context (burns2024developmentresultson pages 38-40). The FLAIME loop diagram should show the closed cycle: CALPHAD screening → LPBF parameter selection → printing → tensile testing → Bayesian acquisition function (including MB) → next batch.

## 4. What Makes the Mineral-Burden Pareto Front Unique

Our literature review found no precedent for integrating quantitative supply-risk weights directly into a CALPHAD-based multi-objective alloy optimization loop. While supply risk has been assessed at the mineral commodity level by the USGS (nassar2020evaluatingthemineral pages 1-2, fortier2018draftcriticalmineral pages 9-10) and OECD (coulomb2015criticalmineralstoday pages 47-51, coulomb2015criticalmineralstoday pages 12-14), and Helbig et al. (2020) computed supply risk at the alloy level for Ni superalloys, these assessments are performed retrospectively on existing alloys rather than used as a forward design objective in a CALPHAD screening pipeline. Ferro et al. (2020) proposed a systematic approach for alloy substitution in a critical raw materials perspective, but did not couple it with computational thermodynamic screening. The FLAIME mineral-burden Pareto front thus represents a genuinely novel contribution that merges materials informatics with supply-chain resilience.

## 5. Generating Preliminary Results Now with pycalphad

The following visualizations can be generated immediately using pycalphad with freely available TDB databases (e.g., COST 507 for Al alloys):

1. **Al-Zr binary phase diagram** (Al-rich corner): Compute equilibrium phase boundaries showing the peritectic and Al₃Zr stability fields. Otis and Liu (2017) demonstrated HT phase-diagram computation with pycalphad (liu2022highthroughputcomputational pages 7-11), and the tool supports automated looping over composition grids.

2. **Scheil solidification curves**: Using pycalphad's Scheil module following Bocklund et al. (2020), compute fraction solid vs. temperature for selected Al-Zr compositions to show freezing range and phase formation sequence (bocklund2020scheilgulliversimulationsfor pages 6-10).

3. **Mineral-burden scatter plot**: Compute MB = Σᵢ wᵢ·xᵢ for each of the 100K+ screened compositions and plot against any computed thermodynamic property (solidus, L1₂ solvus, equilibrium phase fraction) to produce a preliminary Pareto front.

4. **Composition-property heatmap**: Sample a ternary or pseudo-ternary slice in Al-Mn-Cr-Zr space and color by a computed metric such as solidus temperature, following the MaterialsMap approach (sun2024materialsmapacalphadbased pages 10-15).

## 6. Summary

For a 5-page DOE Genesis Mission proposal on AI-enabled critical minerals alloy discovery, the three highest-impact figures are: (1) a screening funnel + BO loop schematic showing the FLAIME pipeline at scale, (2) a mineral-burden vs. strength Pareto front with a companion criticality bar chart, and (3) actual preliminary CALPHAD results (Al-Zr phase diagram + Scheil curves + composition heatmap). This combination demonstrates computational capability, critical minerals relevance, and the unique MB objective that distinguishes FLAIME from all known competitors.
