# Improving an LLM-Assisted Grant-Proposal Compliance Framework: A Literature Review and Recommendations

## 1. Retrieval Improvements Beyond BM25

Your current BM25-only retrieval (Recall@5 = 0.867, MRR = 0.733, Hit@1 = 0.600) leaves meaningful room for improvement, particularly on Hit@1/MRR. The 2024–2026 literature consistently demonstrates that **hybrid sparse+dense retrieval with a reranker** is the smallest setup yielding the largest published gains on regulatory and legal corpora.

**Hybrid BM25 + Dense Embeddings.** On the ObliQA regulatory QA dataset (27,869 questions), a BM25+dense hybrid achieved Recall@10 = 0.8333 and MAP@10 = 0.7016, compared to BM25 baseline Recall@10 = 0.7611 and MAP@10 = 0.6237—absolute gains of +7.2% recall and +7.8% MAP (rayo2025ahybridapproach pages 2-3). On a separate regulatory benchmark, hybrid retrieval using BGE-M3, jina-embeddings-v3, or E5-large-v2 combined with BM25 reached Recall@10 ≈ 0.76; adding a bge-reranker-v2-m3 further boosted results to Recall@10 = 0.79 and MAP@10 = 0.66 (umar2025enhancingregulatorycompliance pages 2-4). In the tax-law domain, a Fine-Hybrid system combining BM25 with fine-tuned SBERT via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) achieved Precision@N = 66.0% and Recall@10 = 76.5%, consistently outperforming either method alone (kodri2025finehybridintegrationof pages 6-8, kodri2025finehybridintegrationof pages 1-2).

**Neural Rerankers.** On UK statutory provision-level retrieval (100 expert-validated queries), neural reranking dramatically improved ranking quality: Voyage rerank-2.5 raised nDCG@10 from 0.3117 (dense-only) to 0.4500 and MRR@10 from 0.3984 to 0.5859 in the dense pool (alshehri2026neuralrerankingfor pages 17-20). A distilled open-source ModernBERT cross-encoder (Distilled-Voyage-ModernBERT) approached the proprietary teacher and outperformed other open rerankers (alshehri2026neuralrerankingfor pages 24-26). Cohere rerank-v3.5 placed in the next tier (alshehri2026neuralrerankingfor pages 17-20).

**Embedding Model Selection.** NV-Embed-v2 leads the MTEB leaderboard with an overall score of 69.32 and BEIR retrieval score of 59.36 (lee2025nvembedimprovedtechniques pages 6-8), with MTEB retrieval of 62.8 compared to E5-Mistral's 57.6 (lee2025geminiembeddinggeneralizable pages 7-8). BGE-M3 is a strong multilingual option with lower computational requirements. For a ~600-snippet corpus, domain-specific performance matters more than general benchmarks—the FinMTEB study showed MTEB performance has limited correlation with domain-specific retrieval quality, underscoring the value of evaluating on your own PAPPG queries (zhao2025kalmembeddingv2superiortraining pages 6-8).

**Recommended minimal setup:** BM25 + BGE-M3 (or a comparable dense embedder) fused via RRF, followed by bge-reranker-v2-m3. This requires only adding an embedding index and a reranker call, and should substantially improve your Hit@1 and MRR. ColBERT-v2/ColPali add late-interaction benefits but are overkill for 619 text-only snippets.

## 2. Chunking and Snippet-Schema Improvements

Your corpus is already well-structured with ~500-token chunks, PAPPG-outline breadcrumb paths, RFC-2119 tags, and PDF deep-links. This is an unusually strong starting point. The literature suggests **incremental enrichment rather than re-chunking**.

**Contextual Retrieval (Anthropic-style).** Merola & Singh (2025) evaluated adding LLM-generated context to chunks and found ContextualRankFusion slightly outperformed late chunking (nDCG@5 = 0.317 vs. 0.309 on NFCorpus) while also outperforming traditional retrieval in multiple settings (merola2025reconstructingcontext pages 9-12, merola2025reconstructingcontext pages 12-15). For your corpus, prepending a short (~50-token) LLM-generated contextual summary to each snippet (e.g., "This snippet describes EAGER proposal restrictions under PAPPG §II.F.3…") would improve embedding quality at minimal cost since you have only 619 snippets.

**Structure-Aware Adaptive Chunking.** Albuquerque Júnior et al. (2603) demonstrated that adaptive, document-structure-aware chunking on a corpus including legal documents raised answer correctness from 62–64% to 72% and retrieval completeness from 58 to 68 (junior2603adaptivechunkingoptimizing pages 8-9, junior2603adaptivechunkingoptimizing pages 1-2). Their pipeline converts documents to Markdown and selects chunking strategy per document based on intrinsic metrics. Since your PAPPG snippets are already Markdown-structured, the main opportunity is ensuring block integrity (tables, enumerated lists kept intact) and adding parent-child linkage so that when a child snippet is retrieved, the parent section context is available for the LLM (junior2603adaptivechunkingoptimizing pages 2-3).

**Semantic and Propositional Chunking.** Qu et al. (2025) found semantic chunking often does not justify its computational cost over simpler methods on well-structured documents—fixed-size or structure-based chunking frequently performs comparably (moro2025influenceofchunking pages 21-24). Propositional chunking (Dense X Retrieval) is best suited for long, loosely structured documents rather than your already-small, already-tagged snippets. **Recommendation:** Skip re-chunking; instead add contextual prefixes and parent-child expansion.

## 3. Deontic / Requirement-Extraction Improvements

Your current modal-verb regex (must/shall/may/should) captures surface-level requirement strength. The literature documents substantially richer extraction pipelines.

**LLM-to-Deontic-Logic Pipelines.** Horner et al. (2025) demonstrated a pipeline that segments regulatory text into atomic snippets, extracts Defeasible Deontic Logic rules with operators [O] (obligation), [F] (forbidden), [P] (permitted), and evaluates syntactic/semantic coherence. They compared multiple LLMs including GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet 4, and reasoning models, finding that two-stage pipelines with a dedicated refinement step improved logical consistency (horner2025fromlegaltexts pages 1-3, horner2025fromlegaltexts pages 3-6).

**Structured Compliance Extraction.** COMPACT extracts deontic-temporal components (Subject, Deontic, Action, Object, Temporal_Condition) from contract clauses and organizes them into clause graphs capturing cross-clause dependencies. Training on their ACE benchmark produced +22–43 percentage point gains in multi-clause reasoning (singh2026compactbuildingcompliance pages 1-2). The ATDIE framework (Actor, Trigger, Duty, Implementation, Enforcement) achieved ~61% overall automated coding accuracy on regulatory texts, with systematic failure modes including cross-reference blindness and hedging misinterpretation (yahan2026benchmarkinglargelanguage pages 11-15). ComplianceNLP built a regulatory knowledge graph of 12,847 provisions with 94.7% edge precision and 87.3% recall using LEGAL-BERT extraction plus hybrid dense+sparse retrieval (guo2604compliancenlpknowledgegraphaugmentedrag pages 2-3, guo2604compliancenlpknowledgegraphaugmentedrag pages 1-2).

**REGREACT** demonstrated self-correcting multi-agent pipelines for structured regulatory extraction, decomposing the task across specialized agents rather than prompting a single LLM (ali2604regreactselfcorrectingmultiagent pages 12-14). Galli et al. (2026) reported 93% precision on obligation filtering and >99% accuracy on obligation type classification for the AI Act (ali2604regreactselfcorrectingmultiagent pages 12-14).

**Recommendation for your framework:** Upgrade from regex to a structured extraction prompt that produces JSON frames with fields {actor, modality, condition, exception, PAPPG_citation} for each requirement. Use a two-pass approach: first extract, then validate with a critic agent. This would transform your current flat RFC-2119 tags into machine-readable requirement graphs.

## 4. Eval-Harness Improvements

Your 15-query labelled eval set is a reasonable start but insufficient for confident system-level claims.

**Scaling to 50–100 Queries.** ARES demonstrated that synthetic query generation (using few-shot prompting with an LLM) plus a small human validation set (≥150 annotated datapoints recommended) enables scalable, statistically robust evaluation via prediction-powered inference (PPI) that produces confidence intervals (saadfalcon2024aresanautomated pages 3-5, saadfalcon2024aresanautomated pages 1-2). The practical approach: use an LLM to generate 80–100 candidate queries from your PAPPG snippets, have a domain expert validate relevance labels for 50–100 of them, and use PPI to extrapolate system-level scores.

**Framework Selection.** RAGChecker achieved the strongest correlations with human judgments in meta-evaluation (Pearson ≈ 62 for Overall Assessment), substantially outperforming RAGAS (Pearson ≈ 48) and ARES (Pearson ≈ 20) on the same benchmark (ru2024ragcheckerafinegrained pages 7-8). Deepchecks reported even higher agreement on specific datasets (0.96 vs. RAGAS 0.65 accuracy on RAG-dataset-12000; ROC-AUC 0.86 vs. 0.65 for faithfulness on TRUE) (gerner2026deepchecksevaluatingretrievalaugmented pages 11-15). RAGAS remains the most widely adopted open-source option and is suitable for initial deployment (antal2025evaluatingopensourcellms pages 1-3).

**Evaluating the Generated Review.** For assessing your mock-reviewer output, use claim-level verification: extract each finding as a claim, check entailment against the cited snippet, and flag unsupported claims. RAGChecker's claim-level decomposition approach is directly applicable (ru2024ragcheckerafinegrained pages 1-3). Combine with periodic human spot-checks on a stratified sample.

**Metrics.** Move beyond Recall@k and MRR to include nDCG@k (which rewards graded relevance) and answer faithfulness (whether generated findings are grounded in retrieved snippets). For the review itself, measure precision (are flagged issues real?) and recall (are real issues caught?) against expert-annotated ground truth.

## 5. Mock-Reviewer Playbook Improvements

**Multi-Agent Debate.** The literature strongly supports multi-agent evaluation over single-model judges. Multi-agent debate achieved a Kendall Tau of 0.57 versus 0.52 for single GPT-4 evaluation, and agent-judge frameworks reached near-perfect agreement with majority votes of five human experts (yu2508whenaisjudge pages 7-8). An agent-as-judge whose decisions differed from human majority vote only 0.3% of the time (vs. 31% for a single LLM) demonstrates the potential (yu2508whenaisjudge pages 5-7). A multi-agent meta-judging pipeline showed ~15.6% improvement over raw judgments and ~8.4% over single-agent baselines (li2025leveragingllmsas pages 1-2).

**Persona Conditioning.** Role-specialized agents (e.g., NSF Program Officer, Panel Reviewer, Sponsored-Projects Officer) should evaluate different compliance dimensions. Research shows agents with relevant roles achieved higher correlation with human judgments than single-metric approaches (yu2508whenaisjudge pages 10-11). However, homogeneous model backbones can share blind spots—use diverse model families when possible (yu2508whenaisjudge pages 10-11).

**Known Limitations.** LLM judges exhibit systematic biases including length bias, self-model bias, and vulnerability to adversarial prompts (yu2508whenaisjudge pages 5-7, li2025leveragingllmsas pages 1-2). Critically, LLM-as-judge is "reliable only when provided with a ground truth reference"—without anchoring in specific snippet text, scores become non-discriminative (azher2601limagentsmultiagentllms pages 9-10). Too many agents or excessive feedback rounds introduce noise and can degrade performance (azher2601limagentsmultiagentllms pages 9-10).

**Concrete Patterns:** (1) Require inline citations: every finding must cite a specific snippet ID, and uncitable findings are automatically suppressed. (2) Implement refusal-when-no-evidence: if the retriever returns no relevant snippets for a checklist item, the reviewer should say "insufficient evidence" rather than hallucinate. (3) Use structured JSON output with fields {severity, PAPPG_section, finding, evidence_snippet_id, recommendation}. (4) Add a critic agent that verifies each finding against its cited snippet before output.

## 6. Serving and Integration Improvements

**MCP (Model Context Protocol).** Anthropic's MCP, introduced in late 2024, provides a standardized JSON-RPC interface for exposing tools, resources, and prompts to any compliant LLM (ehtesham2025asurveyof pages 4-6, hou2026modelcontextprotocol pages 2-4). It functions as "a USB-C port for AI applications" (ahmadi2504mcpbridgea pages 1-4). MCP enables dynamic tool discovery and bi-directional communication, supporting not just retrieval but also action execution (hou2026modelcontextprotocol pages 2-4, hou2026modelcontextprotocol pages 4-6). For your snippet store, an MCP server could expose a `search_pappg` tool that any MCP-compatible client (Claude Desktop, Cursor, Copilot) can invoke, making your compliance corpus accessible across development environments.

**MCP vs. RAG Endpoint vs. Fine-Tuning.** MCP is complementary to RAG rather than a replacement—RAG provides passive vector-based retrieval while MCP standardizes the tool interface (hou2026modelcontextprotocol pages 2-4). For a 619-snippet corpus of ~200–500K tokens, fine-tuning is unnecessary and counterproductive (no provenance, no updates without retraining). A raw-markdown-in-git approach works for human reading but lacks programmatic search. **Recommended architecture:** Git-versioned markdown snippets → indexed with hybrid BM25+dense → served via both a REST/RAG endpoint for batch processing and an MCP server for interactive IDE/chat use.

**Security Considerations.** Large-scale studies of MCP servers revealed novel vulnerability categories including tool poisoning (5.5% of 1,899 servers) and general vulnerabilities (7.2%) (hou2026modelcontextprotocol pages 32-34). For a private lab deployment, the risk is lower, but input validation and access controls remain important.

## 7. Sample-Size-of-One Risk

Having exactly one worked example (the EAGER outline) plus one deliberately-broken Project Summary is **far below any published validation threshold** for a compliance-critical tool.

**Published Guidance.** For safety-critical LLM applications, the MedJUDGE framework recommends empirical stabilization: add validators until agreement metrics change by <0.05 per additional reviewer, and meet tiered compound-error thresholds (e.g., <0.10 for moderate-risk, <0.05 for high-risk applications) (li2026ascopingreview pages 22-24, li2026ascopingreview pages 33-37). ARES recommends ≥150 annotated datapoints for statistically robust evaluation (saadfalcon2024aresanautomated pages 3-5).

**Red-Team and Adversarial Testing.** Static, single-turn test suites substantially overestimate robustness; adaptive, multi-turn red-teaming is required (srivastava2602asystematicreview pages 16-19). Multi-evaluator protocols, disclosure of evaluator prompts, human subsamples, and sensitivity analyses are needed—relying on a single LLM judge is insufficient because modest perturbations can substantially change false-negative rates (srivastava2602asystematicreview pages 16-19, srivastava2602asystematicreview pages 19-22). Benchmarks like Behind the Mask (500 camouflaged prompts) reveal failures invisible to lexical filters (srivastava2602asystematicreview pages 16-19).

**Concrete Validation Plan:** (1) Expand to ≥30 proposal excerpts stratified by type (EAGER, RAPID, standard, collaborative, CAREER) × stage (concept outline, project summary, full proposal) × discipline (at least 3 NSF directorates). (2) For each, create known-compliant and known-non-compliant variants. (3) Measure precision (false positive rate) and recall (false negative rate) of the framework's findings against expert labels. (4) Include adversarial cases: proposals that appear compliant but violate subtle PAPPG provisions (e.g., preliminary-data language in EAGER, budget items requiring prior approval). (5) Re-evaluate after each system change.

*Table: This table summarizes the three highest-priority next steps for improving the grant-proposal compliance framework, ranked by expected practical lift versus implementation effort. It highlights which changes are most likely to improve retrieval quality, reduce review errors, and make the system trustworthy enough for real submission support.*

**Priority 1: Add Hybrid Retrieval + Reranker (3–7 days).** This is the single highest-impact change. Your BM25 baseline is solid but leaves Hit@1 at 0.600—meaning 40% of the time the most relevant snippet is not ranked first. Published evidence on regulatory corpora shows hybrid retrieval improves Recall@10 by +7% and MAP@10 by +8% (rayo2025ahybridapproach pages 2-3), and rerankers further boost nDCG@10 by 30–45% relative (alshehri2026neuralrerankingfor pages 17-20). For 619 snippets, a BGE-M3 embedding index fits comfortably in memory, and bge-reranker-v2-m3 adds only a lightweight cross-encoder call on 5–10 candidates. This directly improves the quality of the 3–5 governing snippets surfaced per review.

**Priority 2: Add Contextual Chunk Enrichment + Parent Expansion (2–5 days).** Your chunks already have excellent structure. Adding a one-time LLM pass to prepend ~50 tokens of contextual summary to each snippet (Anthropic contextual retrieval style) improves embedding quality with minimal ongoing cost (merola2025reconstructingcontext pages 9-12, merola2025reconstructingcontext pages 12-15). Implementing parent-child linkage so the LLM receives the parent section when a child snippet is retrieved adds crucial context for nuanced compliance judgments. The adaptive chunking literature confirms structure-aware approaches yield meaningful gains on legal/regulatory text (junior2603adaptivechunkingoptimizing pages 8-9).

**Priority 3: Build a Real Evaluation Harness (5–10 days).** Before trusting the framework for real submissions, expand from 15 queries to 50–100 using LLM-generated queries with human validation, add claim-level faithfulness checking via RAGChecker-style decomposition (ru2024ragcheckerafinegrained pages 7-8, ru2024ragcheckerafinegrained pages 1-3), and include adversarial test cases. ARES's prediction-powered inference approach enables statistically robust evaluation from a modest human annotation budget (saadfalcon2024aresanautomated pages 3-5). This is the critical step that transforms the framework from a promising prototype into a system you can confidently rely on for real NSF submissions.

**Sequencing:** Implement Priority 1 first (immediate retrieval quality gain), then Priority 3 (so you can measure the impact of further changes), then Priority 2 (incremental quality improvement measurable against the new eval harness). The deontic extraction upgrade (Section 3), multi-agent reviewer (Section 5), and MCP serving (Section 6) are valuable but represent second-wave improvements once the foundation is solid.
