## Companies Manufacturing Metal Powder Atomization Equipment for Additive Manufacturing

The metal powder atomization equipment landscape for additive manufacturing (AM) research spans three primary technology categories: ultrasonic atomization, gas atomization, and plasma atomization. This review identifies key companies in each segment, drawing from peer-reviewed literature, patents, and industry supply chain analyses. Notably, some companies are original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) that sell atomization hardware, while others are primarily powder producers that operate proprietary atomization systems and sell only the resulting powders (dawes2015introductiontothe pages 9-11).

### Ultrasonic Atomization Equipment Manufacturers

**Amazemet** (Warsaw, Poland) is a startup/scale-up company that has emerged as a leading developer of ultrasonic atomization equipment for metal powder production. The company's ATO Lab system combines ultrasonic vibration with plasma or arc melting to produce highly spherical metal powders. Patents held by Łukasz Żrodowski and Marcin Bielecki describe ultrasonic atomization methods using cold crucible technology combined with sonotrode vibrations at 20 kHz frequency for producing heavy metal powders for additive manufacturing applications. The ultrasonic plasma atomization (UPA) technique developed at Amazemet produces powders with sphericity indices above 0.90 and narrow particle size distributions (sojoodi2025integrationofcircular pages 15-16, sojoodi2025integrationofcircular pages 14-15).

**Atomising Systems Ltd (ASL)** (Sheffield, UK) is an established equipment manufacturer specializing in atomization of metallic and non-metallic materials via multiple technologies including water atomization, inert gas atomization, ultrasonic atomization, and centrifugal atomization. The company produces laboratory atomizers capable of 1–5 kg/day as well as industrial installations exceeding 5 tonnes/day (radchenko2022requirementsformetal pages 36-40).

### Gas Atomization Equipment Manufacturers

**ALD Vacuum Technologies** (Hanau, Germany; part of AMG Advanced Metallurgical Group N.V., Netherlands) is described in the literature as the world leader in gas atomization equipment manufacturing. The company produces VIGA (Vacuum Induction Gas Atomization) atomizers in laboratory models (VIGA 1-B, VIGA 2 with 1–2 L crucibles) and commercial/industrial models (VIGA-6 at ~40 kg per steel run, VIGA-8 at ~50 kg, up to 250–300 L tundish capacity) producing powders with d50 = 15–60 µm. ALD also manufactures EIGA (Electrode Induction Gas Atomization) systems (EIGA 50 and EIGA 100 models, differentiated by feedstock rod diameter of 50 and 100 mm), which produce powders with d50 = 60–80 µm at ~0.5 kg/s, suitable for reactive metals such as Ti, Zr, and Nb. Sandvik Osprey (Neath, UK; part of Sweden-based Sandvik AB) licensed its atomization technology to ALD in the 1980s (radchenko2022requirementsformetal pages 36-40).

**Phoenix Scientific Industries (PSI)** (Hailsham, UK) is an established manufacturer of the Hermiga series of gas atomizers. Models include the Hermiga 75/3 and 75/5 (3 and 5 kg tundish capacity by steel, respectively) designed for specialty steels and titanium alloys, producing spherical powders with cooling rates of 10³ to 10⁶ K/s and particle sizes from 10 to 100 µm. Larger models include the Hermiga 100/10 VI (10 kg), 100/25 VI, 100/50 V21, 120/100 V21, and 100/200 V21 (up to 200 kg by steel) for medium-series production (radchenko2022requirementsformetal pages 36-40).

**TLS Technik GmbH & Co. Spezialpulver KG** (Bitterfeld, Germany) is an established company specializing in EIGA gas atomization of titanium and other specialty metal powders for AM (dawes2015introductiontothe pages 9-11, mellor2016novelandemerging pages 4-6).

**Arcast Inc.** (Oxford, Maine, USA) manufactures vacuum arc furnaces and gas atomization equipment. Scientific literature references Arcast vacuum arc furnaces (e.g., Arc 200) and gas-atomized powders used in AM research.

In Europe, only three companies—ALD, PSI, and Atomising Systems Ltd—were identified as producing atomizers as commercial products available for sale (radchenko2022requirementsformetal pages 36-40).

Several major established powder producers also operate proprietary gas atomization systems but sell powder rather than equipment. These include **Carpenter Technology Corp** (Philadelphia, PA, USA; founded 1889), **Höganäs AB** (Höganäs, Sweden; founded 1797), **GKN Hoeganaes** (Cinnaminson, NJ, USA), **Sandvik Materials Technology/Osprey** (Neath, Wales, UK; Swedish parent Sandvik), **H.C. Starck** (Goslar, Germany), and **LPW Technology Ltd** (Runcorn, UK) (dawes2015introductiontothe pages 9-11). Additional titanium gas atomization powder producers include **ATI (Allegheny Technologies)** (USA) and **Praxair** (now Linde, USA), as well as **Sumitomo** (Japan) (mellor2016novelandemerging pages 4-6).

### Plasma Atomization Equipment Manufacturers

**AP&C (Advanced Powders & Coatings Inc.)** (Boisbriand, Quebec, Canada), originally a subsidiary of Raymor Industries, now part of Colibrium Additive (formerly GE Additive), uses a wire-fed plasma atomization process. In this approach, a metal wire is fed through converging plasma torches that melt and atomize the material into highly spherical particles. AP&C is one of the pioneering companies in plasma atomization of titanium alloys for AM, and the torch system was originally invented by PyroGenesis (dawes2015introductiontothe pages 3-5, mellor2016novelandemerging pages 4-6, popovich2016metalpowderadditive pages 16-20).

**Tekna Plasma Systems Inc.** (Sherbrooke, Quebec, Canada; founded 1990) is an established manufacturer of RF (radio-frequency) induction plasma systems used for powder spheroidization and atomization. Tekna's systems use powder as feedstock—irregular or non-spherical particles are injected into an RF plasma torch where they are melted and resolidified into spherical form. RF plasma systems (such as Tekna units) can operate under argon or Ar/H₂, which can actively strip oxygen from particle surfaces (dawes2015introductiontothe pages 3-5, sojoodi2025integrationofcircular pages 13-14).

**PyroGenesis Canada Inc.** (Montreal, Quebec, Canada; founded 1991) is an established company that develops plasma atomization torch technology and NexGen plasma atomization systems. PyroGenesis originally developed the plasma torch technology later used by AP&C for wire-fed plasma atomization. Multiple patents by Frédéric Larouche assigned to PyroGenesis/AP&C describe reactive metal powder atomization manufacturing processes using plasma (dawes2015introductiontothe pages 3-5).

**TIMET (Precision Castparts/Berkshire Hathaway)** (Dallas, TX, USA) and **Shaanxi North Steel** (China) are established producers that operate PREP (Plasma Rotating Electrode Process) systems to produce exceptionally spherical and pure titanium powders (mellor2016novelandemerging pages 4-6).

### Summary

The metal powder atomization equipment market for AM research is segmented into a small number of dedicated equipment OEMs and a larger number of integrated powder producers. Gas atomization equipment is dominated by ALD Vacuum Technologies (Germany), PSI (UK), and Atomising Systems Ltd (UK) (radchenko2022requirementsformetal pages 36-40). Plasma atomization and spheroidization equipment is concentrated in Quebec, Canada, with AP&C, Tekna, and PyroGenesis forming a notable cluster (dawes2015introductiontothe pages 3-5). Ultrasonic atomization is an emerging technology led by the startup Amazemet (Poland), with ASL (UK) also offering ultrasonic capabilities (radchenko2022requirementsformetal pages 36-40). The UPA method, combining ultrasonic vibration with plasma melting, represents a particularly promising hybrid approach that can serve both virgin powder production and recycling of AM waste (sojoodi2025integrationofcircular pages 5-6, sojoodi2025integrationofcircular pages 14-15). The landscape continues to evolve rapidly as AM adoption drives increasing demand for high-quality spherical metal powders.
