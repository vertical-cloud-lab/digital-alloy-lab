Question: Has anyone compared predictions of the Thermo-Calc Additive Manufacturing (AM) Module with measured melt-pool cross-sections, porosity or printability windows for 316L stainless steel (or other steels) made by laser powder bed fusion? Give the closest examples with DOIs, the observables compared, the calibrated absorptivity, and how well the module agreed.

Yes. ## Thermo-Calc AM Module versus LPBF measurements in steels

**Yes, but the direct comparisons I could verify are qualitative rather than rigorous validations of 316L porosity or printability windows.** The clearest DOI-bearing steel example compares simulated and imaged melt-pool geometries in M789 maraging steel. I could not verify a study reporting numerical predicted-versus-measured cross-section errors, pore fractions, or printability-window accuracy for **316L using the Thermo-Calc AM Module itself**. (saville2025primingadditivelymanufactured pages 12-17, saville2025primingadditivelymanufactured pages 17-23, sheikh2024anautomatedcomputational pages 1-2)

*Table: Direct AM Module evidence is limited to qualitative or incompletely quantified steel comparisons; the best 316 printability example instead uses Thermo-Calc properties with separate analytical models. The reported 70% calibrated absorptivity belongs to CM247LC nickel superalloy—not steel—and is therefore excluded from the steel rows.*

The **strongest direct comparison** is Saville *et al.*, *Priming additively manufactured cobalt-free maraging steels for improved properties through changes to as-built microstructure*, NIST IR 8582 (2025), **DOI: 10.6028/nist.ir.8582**. They simulated single raster tracks for five M789 LPBF parameter sets with the AM Module and examined corresponding etched/BSE-visible melt-pool cross-sections. The observed variation was broadly consistent with simulated trends—particularly comparable pools for sets A and E—but pools for B–D were difficult to delineate. The authors explicitly call the simulated geometries **estimates for qualitative trends**, not quantitative predictions or an experimentally established fit. Absorptivity and its calibration are **not reported**. XCT separately measured **0.001 vol.% porosity for A and 0.071 vol.% for C**; the module did **not** predict those pore fractions. (saville2025primingadditivelymanufactured pages 9-12, saville2025primingadditivelymanufactured pages 12-17, saville2025primingadditivelymanufactured pages 17-23, saville2025primingadditivelymanufactured media 4b960552, saville2025primingadditivelymanufactured media cb59a453, saville2025primingadditivelymanufactured pages 30-34)

A **closely related low-alloy-steel example** is Vaddamanu’s 2025 Chalmers licentiate work on SSAB AM Engineering martensitic steel. It explicitly uses Thermo-Calc 2025a’s AM Module with *calibrated heat sources* for 30- and 90-µm-layer single-track thermal simulations, while measuring top-layer melt-pool depth and image-based porosity. Experimentally, specimens achieved **at least 99.9% relative density across 75–180 J mm⁻³**. The examined thesis reports **neither a numerical calibrated absorptivity nor a predicted-versus-measured melt-pool error or module-predicted density window**: the measured processing window should not be presented as a validated AM Module prediction. The relevant appended study was identified as a **manuscript**, with no DOI verified for that study. (vaddamanu2025powderbedfusionlaser pages 29-34, vaddamanu2025powderbedfusionlaser pages 34-39, vaddamanu2025powderbedfusionlaser pages 1-7)

**Important distinction for 316 steel:** Sheikh *et al.*, *npj Computational Materials* (2024), **DOI: 10.1038/s41524-024-01436-x**, benchmarks calculated printability maps against published observations that include 316 stainless steel. It obtains material properties from Thermo-Calc but calculates melt pools with **separate Eagar–Tsai and Gladush–Smurov analytical models**, **not** the commercial Thermo-Calc AM Module. Its absorptivity is estimated using Drude theory, not a measured module-calibration value. It is therefore relevant corroboration for *Thermo-Calc-informed* printability modeling, **not** evidence of a validated 316L AM Module window. (sheikh2024anautomatedcomputational pages 14-15, sheikh2024anautomatedcomputational pages 1-2, sheikh2024anautomatedcomputational pages 2-3)

Finally, a **70% calibrated absorptivity** does appear in a Thermo-Calc AM Module LPBF study, but its material is **CM247LC, a nickel-base superalloy**, not 316L or another steel; transferring that value to steel would be unjustified. (hussain2024powderbedfusion–laser pages 25-33)
