Question: Has anyone printed laser powder bed fusion thin-wall, fin or comb specimens of 316L (or another steel or nickel alloy) with different laser power and scan speed on each wall of one part, measured porosity or melt-pool depth in cross-sections of the walls, and compared the results with a printability map from a melt-pool model (Eagar-Tsai, a CALPHAD-based model such as the Thermo-Calc Additive Manufacturing Module, or finite-element simulations)? Give the closest examples with DOIs and what they found.

No. ## Assessment

**I did not find a published experiment that meets all the specified conditions at once:** a *single, connected* LPBF steel or nickel-alloy comb whose individual walls each receive a different laser-power/scan-speed pair, whose wall cross-sections are measured for porosity or melt-pool depth, and whose results are tested against an Eagar–Tsai, Thermo-Calc Additive Manufacturing Module, or finite-element **printability map**. There are, however, unusually close precedents for each part of that workflow. In particular, a wall *colony* is not necessarily a wall-by-wall parameter study, and tracks on one substrate are not built-up walls. (wu2020exploringthefabrication pages 2-4, wu2020exploringthefabrication pages 4-5, thanki2023offaxishighspeedcamerabased pages 7-11, psihoyos2023densitybasedoptimizationof pages 3-6)

| Closest example and DOI | What was actually built and measured | Model comparison, finding, and missing element |
|---|---|---|
| **Wu, Narra & Rollett (2020), Inconel 718**, **10.1007/s00170-020-05827-4** | Connected colonies of multiple thin walls on a **2-mm shared base**, with wall dimensions and processing conditions varied; polished wall sections supplied thickness measurements, and synchrotron micro-CT quantified wall porosity. | A **Rosenthal** melt-pool estimate helped select power/speed settings, and measured results were placed on power–speed process plots with *published* keyhole boundaries—not validated against a newly calculated Eagar–Tsai/FEM map. Minimum IN718 thickness was **107 ± 10 µm**; measured IN718 closed-pore fractions were **0.03% and 0.06%** in vertical and 45° single-bead walls, versus **0.01% and 0.12%** in corresponding rastered walls. The paper does not establish an independently assigned *power–speed pair for every wall* of one colony or a wall-by-wall predicted-versus-observed defect map. (wu2020exploringthefabrication media 4d8a63ca, wu2020exploringthefabrication pages 2-4, wu2020exploringthefabrication pages 4-5, wu2020exploringthefabrication pages 5-7, wu2020exploringthefabrication pages 10-13) |
| **Thanki et al. (2023), 316L**, **10.1007/s00170-023-11075-z** | **Fourteen single tracks on one 316L substrate**, representing seven parameter sets in duplicate—not a multi-wall part. CT classified subsurface defects, and etched track sections measured melt-pool width and depth. | An **AdditiveLab finite-element thermal model** was compared directly with section measurements. At **215 W/900 mm s⁻¹**, experimental/model depths were **149/105 µm**, with no CT-detected defects; at **215 W/225 mm s⁻¹**, depths were **443/336 µm**, with keyhole pores. High-power/high-speed **500 W/2093 mm s⁻¹** tracks were discontinuous despite approximately the same linear energy input as the nominal setting. This is the closest *one-substrate, different-setting, cross-section-plus-FEM* experiment, but its tracks were not walls and the authors did not validate a calculated wall printability map. (thanki2023offaxishighspeedcamerabased pages 7-11, thanki2023offaxishighspeedcamerabased pages 11-15, thanki2023offaxishighspeedcamerabased pages 23-27) |
| **Psihoyos & Lampeas (2023), 316L**, **10.3390/alloys2010004** | No new comb experiment: a predicted map was checked against **published single-/multi-track and sectioned small-cube experiments** with different powers and speeds. | **ANSYS finite-element** melt-pool dimensions and multilayer lack-of-fusion simulations generated power–speed printability maps. For 316L, the initial single-track map correctly placed the examined lack-of-fusion points but misclassified **100 W/500 mm s⁻¹** and **200 W/1000 mm s⁻¹** dense points as lack-of-fusion, and **200 W/500 mm s⁻¹** keyholing as dense. Multilayer porosity simulation enlarged the predicted dense region, improving agreement for dense points; keyhole modeling remained limited. This is the closest explicit **316L FEM-map versus section-observed defects** comparison, but the validation pieces were not connected walls. (psihoyos2023densitybasedoptimizationof pages 8-10, psihoyos2023densitybasedoptimizationof pages 10-13, psihoyos2023densitybasedoptimizationof pages 13-14, psihoyos2023densitybasedoptimizationof pages 14-19, psihoyos2023densitybasedoptimizationof media e4821ff5) |
| **Saunders et al. (2022), 316L**, **10.1007/s40192-022-00276-1** | Different-power/speed **bead-on-plate tracks** were sectioned to measure melt-pool width and depth; there was no built-up comb. | An **Eagar–Tsai analytical solution**, finite-element simulations and experiments trained multifidelity surrogates for melt-pool prediction and defect classification. Their best reported depth prediction was about **11% error**; adding fidelities did **not consistently improve printability classification**, particularly for keyholing. This supplies an Eagar–Tsai-based map methodology and experimental sections, but not wall-specific testing. (saunders2022additivemanufacturingmelt pages 1-2, saunders2022additivemanufacturingmelt pages 4-6, saunders2022additivemanufacturingmelt pages 11-13, saunders2022additivemanufacturingmelt pages 15-16) |
| **Wits et al. (2025), 316L**, **10.23967/simam.2025.012** | Stacked single-track **thin walls** were sectioned; their measured **widths**, not sectional depths or pores, validated an analytical melt-pool calculation. Separate overhanging struts and lattice towers tested geometry transfer. | Predicted wall widths agreed particularly above **0.24 J mm⁻¹** linear energy density. Reduced heat conduction into powder made predicted overhang pools roughly **twice as wide and four times as long**; at high input the conduction model underpredicted widths. The work cites/uses analytical temperature-field approaches, but does not demonstrate differently parameterized walls of one part or a porosity printability-map test. (wits2025analyticallpbfmelt pages 1-3, wits2025analyticallpbfmelt pages 3-7, wits2025analyticallpbfmelt pages 7-9) |
| **Saville et al. (2025), M789 maraging steel**, **10.6028/nist.ir.8582** | One build included specimens made with **five parameter sets** and some thin-walled structures, but reported characterization chiefly concerns **separate 20-mm cubes**, not section-by-section tests of a connected comb. | **Thermo-Calc Additive Manufacturing Module** simulations suggested substantial differences in melt-pool thermal histories; the authors describe the geometries as **qualitative estimates**, not a validated porosity printability map. In contrast to the modeled thermal differences, the examined as-built microstructures changed little across parameter sets. This is the clearest retrieved example using the specifically requested software on steel, but not its proposed wall-map validation. (saville2025primingadditivelymanufactured pages 9-12, saville2025primingadditivelymanufactured pages 12-17) |

A further close **thin-wall porosity** precedent is Mezghani, Nassar & Simpson (2025), **10.1007/s00170-025-15976-z**: they printed **144 separate AlSi10Mg single-track wall samples**, varied power from **80–170 W** and speed from **600–1800 mm s⁻¹**, and measured through-pores by CT. **132** walls survived; double-scanning reduced through-porosity without significantly increasing thickness. Its material is outside the requested steel/nickel group, the walls were separate samples, and its energy/enthalpy correlation was not the specified melt-pool printability-map comparison. (mezghani2025laserbasedpowderbed pages 2-5, mezghani2025laserbasedpowderbed pages 5-8)

**Bottom line:** Connected-wall geometry with pore measurements (Wu), one-substrate parameter sweeps with measured depth and FEM (Thanki), and explicit physics-derived 316L defect maps (Psihoyos; Saunders) are all established **separately**. The precise experiment proposed in the question—*assigning different power and speed to individual walls of the same connected specimen and checking each wall’s section against a melt-pool-derived printability map*—was **not verified in the literature located here**. This is a qualified search finding, not proof that no such report exists. (wu2020exploringthefabrication pages 2-4, thanki2023offaxishighspeedcamerabased pages 7-11, psihoyos2023densitybasedoptimizationof pages 10-13, saunders2022additivemanufacturingmelt pages 4-6)
