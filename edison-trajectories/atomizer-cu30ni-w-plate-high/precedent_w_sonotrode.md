Question: Has anyone used tungsten, a tungsten alloy (W-Re, W-La2O3, W-Ni-Fe or W-Ni-Cu heavy alloy) or a
tungsten-coated carbon or graphite part as an ultrasonic sonotrode, horn tip or atomizing plate in
contact with molten metal, especially copper, nickel or copper-nickel alloys (for example in an
AMAZEMET rePOWDER or another ultrasonic atomizer, or in ultrasonic melt treatment)? If so, report the
melt, temperature, frequency and amplitude, how long the part lasted, how much it eroded or dissolved,
and how much W ended up in the product. If not, give the closest precedents: W or W-coated parts in
contact with molten Cu or Ni alloys (crucibles, nozzles, thermocouple sheaths, casting molds), and Mo
or Nb sonotrodes used with Cu or Ni melts.


Yes. ## Finding

**Yes—but the documented tungsten sonotrode tip was used with a titanium alloy, not a copper or nickel melt.** A patent describes a technical-grade tungsten working tip that supports molten Ti7Al6Nb and transmits ultrasound. I found **no verified report of a tungsten, W–Re, W–La₂O₃, W–Ni–Fe or W–Ni–Cu tip, or a tungsten-coated carbon/graphite tip, operating against molten Cu, Ni or Cu–Ni** with measured service life, erosion and W pickup. This is a limit of the located evidence, not proof that no such equipment has ever been used. (US11938557B2 pages 7-10, US11938557B2 pages 16-18, US20240390977A1 pages 40-42, EP3766610A1 pages 2-8)

| Precedent and evidential status | Melt and conditions | Lifetime, loss and product contamination |
|---|---|---|
| **W tip, explicit patent embodiment:** Zrodowski, *Sonotrode for processing of liquid metals*, US11938557B2. A 20-g, technical-grade W M8 working tip is attached to a Ti6Al4V sonotrode body and contacts the melt. | A **3-g Ti7Al6Nb** charge is melted with a **100-A plasma arc for 30 s**; the arc is then extinguished and ultrasound applied. The assembly’s frequency is **20,200 Hz measured at 297 K**—not a stated frequency measured at melt temperature—and tip amplitude is **10 µm**. Melt temperature and duration of subsequent sonication are **not given**. | **No quantified lifetime, recession, mass loss or W concentration** in the treated alloy. The patent contemplates replacing a tip after degradation, but supplies no wear measurement. This is a disclosed worked example, **not** a published Cu/Ni durability trial. (US11938557B2 pages 7-10, US11938557B2 pages 16-18) |
| **Mo tip, reported rePowder processing:** Goncharov *et al.*, *Metals* **16**, 318 (2026), doi:10.3390/met16030318. | An **AMAZEMET rePowder** with a **molybdenum sonotrode** atomizes an Al–Cr–Cu–Fe–Mn–Ni high-entropy-alloy feedstock prepared from elemental powders and arc-remelted rods. This is a **Cu- and Ni-containing alloy**, not pure Cu or a conventional Cu–Ni alloy. The accessible methods give **no atomization temperature, frequency or amplitude**; temperatures given for later WC–HEA sintering are not melt-atomization temperatures. | **No tip operating life, erosion/dissolution rate or W pickup** reported. The subsequent **WC–12 wt% HEA** mixture contains deliberately added WC and must not be interpreted as W contamination from the Mo sonotrode. (goncharov2026designofcobaltfree pages 2-4) |
| **CuNi3Si atomization, vibrating material unspecified:** Bielecki, US20240390977A1. | The patent expressly reports **CuNi3Si powder made at 35 kHz** using a cooled sonotrode in argon; it gives feedstock **liquidus 1348 K (≈1075 °C)**, **not a measured operating melt temperature**. Its tested apparatus has a **3-mm tungsten arc cathode separate from the vibrating sonotrode/anode**. It reports an experimental hot-end vibration-*velocity* threshold of **0.2 m/s peak**, not the actual displacement amplitude for this CuNi3Si run. | The patent says sonotrodes are exchanged when contaminated by a previous feedstock, but gives **neither tip composition nor service life, erosion or product W assay**. Thus its W electrode cannot establish a W sonotrode or W-derived powder contamination. (US20240390977A1 pages 38-40, US20240390977A1 pages 40-42) |
| **Nb-tip design, not a Cu/Ni performance test:** Kaczynski *et al.*, EP3766610A1. | An atomizing-sonotrode embodiment has a **niobium core pressed into a copper-alloy body**; W and Mo alloys are listed as *alternative* core materials. It describes possible installation for steels and Ti alloys, but gives **no identified Cu/Ni melt run, test temperature, measured frequency or amplitude** for its Nb embodiment. | **No measured life, wear or contamination.** The coating specified on the *coolant-side body* is for protection against cooling-liquid cavitation, not a W coating on graphite exposed to metal. (EP3766610A1 pages 1-2, EP3766610A1 pages 2-8) |
| **Nb immersed-sonotrode experiment, lower-temperature comparator:** Naydenkin *et al.*, *Metals* **7**, 544 (2017), doi:10.3390/met7120544. | **AA6082 aluminum alloy** at **730 °C**, **17.5 kHz**, approximately **30 µm** working amplitude. A niobium conical sonotrode was used for **1 min** of degassing and **2 additional min** after addition of Al–Al₂O₃ master alloy. | Three minutes describes **one treatment schedule, not demonstrated tip life**. Nb wear and pickup are not quantified. The alloy’s minor nominal Cu content does not make this a molten-copper trial. (naydenkin2017influenceofcombined pages 1-3) |

**Closest non-ultrasonic W–melt-contact evidence.** Pure Cu infiltrated W scaffolds at **1250 °C for 10 min** under argon in Han *et al.*; interfacial microscopy found distinct, bonded Cu and W constituents, but neither W loss nor W dissolved in the Cu was measured. Calvo *et al.* similarly infiltrated W microlattices with Cu at **1200 or 1300 °C for 60 min** in vacuum. These are stationary, short-duration composite-fabrication exposures—not qualifying crucible, nozzle, mold, sheath or cavitating-tip lifetime tests. (han2022mechanicalpropertiesof pages 2-5, calvo2018microstructureandprocessing pages 2-3)

Nickel presents a particularly strong **chemical-dissolution warning**. Natanzon *et al.*, *Soviet Powder Metallurgy and Metal Ceramics* **31**, 161–165 (1992), doi:10.1007/BF00794054, measured **50.7 ± 1.4 wt% equilibrium W solubility in stirred liquid Ni at 1525 °C**; W specimens were immersed for **2–4 h** to establish equilibrium. Their rotating-disc tests measured dissolution over **270–720 s**, with solution-rate constants dependent on rotation. This is a deliberately dissolving stationary/rotating W specimen, **not an ultrasonic wear rate or a predicted W concentration in atomized powder**. That paper also cites Eremenko, Minakova and Churakov, “Solubility of tungsten in cupro-nickel melts,” *Poroshkovaya Metallurgiya*, no. 4, 53–58 (1977); I could verify the citation **but not obtain its Cu–Ni-specific numerical results**. (natanzon1992solubilityandkinetics pages 1-2, natanzon1992solubilityandkinetics pages 2-4, natanzon1992solubilityandkinetics pages 4-5)

**Bottom line for the requested metrics:** The titanium-alloy patent supplies the sole located explicit molten-metal-contact **W vibrating-tip** configuration and its frequency/amplitude, but **not its melt temperature, tip life, loss or W pickup**. The Mo/rePowder paper and 35-kHz CuNi3Si patent establish substantially closer **Cu/Ni-containing ultrasonic-processing** precedents, but do **not** establish a W sonotrode or quantify its contamination. I found no defensible numerical W-in-product value or service-life estimate for a W, W-alloy or W-coated-graphite sonotrode in molten Cu/Ni; neither the short stationary Cu exposures nor Ni equilibrium solubility can be substituted for those measurements. (US11938557B2 pages 16-18, goncharov2026designofcobaltfree pages 2-4, US20240390977A1 pages 40-42, han2022mechanicalpropertiesof pages 2-5, natanzon1992solubilityandkinetics pages 1-2)
