# Back-of-envelope estimates: W- vs Mo-coated carbon-fiber plate under Cu-30Ni

Generated by `estimates.py`. Order-of-magnitude only; every input is shown.

## 1. Solubility of W in liquid Cu-30Ni against Mo (both measured at 1300 C)

Cu-30Ni (wt%) has x_Ni = 0.317. At 1300 C the ideal solubility (solid -> liquid, no interactions) is x = 0.101 for W and x = 0.270 for Mo, because W melts 800 K higher.

Measured Mo: 3.7 wt% in Cu-32Ni at 1300 C is x = 0.0242, an excess term RT ln(gamma) = 32 kJ/mol against liquid Mo.

Measured W: Eremenko, Minakova & Churakov 1977 (doi:10.1007/BF00806131), as quoted by the parallel Edison LITERATURE run in ../atomizer-cu30ni-w-plate/, fit W saturation in Cu-Ni at 1300 C as log10(x_W) = -7.011 + 7.770*sqrt(z), z = Ni/(Ni+Cu) atomic, valid for z = 0.054-0.368. For z = 0.317: x_W = 0.0023, **0.68 wt% W**, against x_Mo = 0.0242 (3.7 wt%). x_W / x_Mo = 0.095.

W and Mo have nearly the same molar volume (9.55 vs 9.38 cm3/mol), so that mole-fraction ratio is also the recession ratio: a dense W coat should recede about **10x more slowly** than a Mo coat.

Cross-check without the W measurement (anchored on Mo: W's ideal solubility is 0.37x Mo's, plus 0-15 kJ/mol less favourable excess term) gives 0.8-2.6 wt%, and Edison's LITERATURE_HIGH interpolation gives 1-2 wt%. Both overestimate the measured value.

| W excess vs Mo (kJ/mol) | x_W | W solubility (wt%) | x_W / x_Mo |
|---|---|---|---|
| +0 | 0.0090 | 2.63 | 0.37 |
| +5 | 0.0062 | 1.80 | 0.25 |
| +10 | 0.0042 | 1.23 | 0.17 |
| +15 | 0.0029 | 0.84 | 0.12 |

## 2. How fast the melt eats a dense coat (mass-transfer-limited dissolution)

Recession rate r = k * rho_melt * w_sat / rho_coat. k = D/delta with D ~ 2e-9 m2/s (W in liquid Ni is 2.4e-9 m2/s at 1480-1750 C) and a boundary layer delta of 10-70 um under a thin, ultrasonically agitated film gives k ~ 3e-5 to 1.5e-4 m/s. Under the falling stream (jet ~1.5 m/s, 2 mm, Sc ~ 250) a stagnation-point correlation gives k ~ 5e-4 m/s.

| Coat | Solubility (wt%) | r, film, low (um/min) | r, film, high (um/min) | r, under the stream (um/min) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mo | 3.7 | 52 | 258 | 860 |
| W | 1.5 | 11 | 55 | 184 |
| W | 0.68 | 5.0 | 25 | 84 |

Minutes to cut through a dense coat of a given thickness, using the film-zone range (the spot under the stream goes 3-15x faster):

| Coat | Solubility (wt%) | 25 um | 100 um | 250 um |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mo | 3.7 | 0.1-0.5 | 0.4-1.9 | 1.0-4.8 |
| W | 1.5 | 0.5-2.3 | 1.8-9.0 | 4.5-23 |
| W | 0.68 | 1.0-5.0 | 4.0-20 | 10.0-50 |

Sanity check: a solid Mo sonotrode with ~10 cm2 wetted for 20 min at the film-zone Mo rate loses 11-53 g, i.e. 1-5 wt% of a 1.1 kg charge, bracketing the ~2.6 wt% Mo that the first #219 Edison run attributed to sonotrode erosion in Goncharov 2026 (this run's PRECEDENT task found no erosion figure in that paper's accessible text).

## 3. Thermal-mismatch stress in the coat on cooling (dense coat, in-plane C/C)

sigma = E * (alpha_coat - alpha_CC) * dT / (1 - nu), C/C in-plane CTE 0-1.5e-6/K, coat stress-free at the temperature it was made or last relaxed:

| Coat | Stress-free at | Stress at room temperature (GPa, tensile) |
|---|---|---|
| Mo | 700 C | 1.3-1.8 |
| Mo | 1300 C | 2.5-3.4 |
| W | 700 C | 1.3-1.9 |
| W | 1300 C | 2.5-3.6 |

Both are well above what a W or Mo film can carry without cracking or yielding (roughly 0.3-1 GPa), so a dense coat on in-plane C/C cracks (mud-cracking) or debonds on cooling. W is no better than Mo here, and W is brittle below a few hundred C while Mo keeps some ductility near room temperature.

## 4. Added mass and flexural stiffness of a coated plate

Same coating thickness on both faces of a dense coat; C/C core E = 70 GPa, rho = 1.7 g/cm3. 'Flexural f' is the change in a bending-mode frequency, sqrt(D/m), relative to the bare plate:

| 2 | 100 | +60% | +114% | +26% | +17% | -7.1% |
| 2 | 250 | +150% | +284% | +48% | +31% | -11.5% |
| 3 | 25 | +10% | +19% | +6% | +4% | -1.5% |
| 3 | 100 | +40% | +76% | +20% | +13% | -5.3% |
| 3 | 250 | +100% | +189% | +37% | +24% | -9.6% |

For scale, 3D Lab's sonotrode patent (EP3766610) says the system tolerates only +/-5% detuning and that heating by the melt alone lowers the resonance 3-9%. If the plate is only a tip mass on a much heavier horn the shift is far smaller, so the generator's frequency reading with the new plate fitted is what settles it.

## 5. Capillary infiltration of a porous dip coat

Washburn: t = 2 * eta * L^2 / (gamma * r * cos(theta)), eta = 4.5 mPa s, gamma = 1.3 N/m, L = 100 um:

| Pore radius (um) | Contact angle | Time to cross 100 um |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 10 deg | 0.14 ms |
| 0.5 | 60 deg | 0.28 ms |
| 5 | 10 deg | 0.01 ms |
| 5 | 60 deg | 0.03 ms |

Cu-Ni wets clean W and Mo (Cu infiltration of porous W is how W-Cu composites are made), so a porous slurry coat of either metal is soaked through in milliseconds and puts the melt on the carbon. Only a dense coat can act as a barrier.
