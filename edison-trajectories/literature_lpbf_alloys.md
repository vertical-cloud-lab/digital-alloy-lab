# Novel Alloy Compositions for Laser Powder Bed Fusion Additive Manufacturing: A Literature Review (2022–2026)

## 1. Introduction

Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) is the most commercially developed metal additive manufacturing (AM) process, yet a fundamental mismatch persists: most engineering alloys were designed for conventional casting or forging and do not account for LPBF's extreme processing conditions—cooling rates of 10⁴–10⁶ K/s, steep temperature gradients, and repeated thermal cycling (liu2024alloydesignfor pages 1-3). This mismatch leads to severe cracking, porosity, and residual stresses that limit the range of printable alloys. Recent literature (2022–2026) has increasingly focused on designing alloys specifically tailored for LPBF rather than adapting existing compositions, with strategies organized around crack mitigation, microstructure manipulation, and machine-learning-assisted design (liu2024alloydesignfor pages 1-3, liu2024alloydesignfor pages 3-4).

## 2. Alloy Design Strategies for LPBF Printability

### 2.1 Solidification Cracking Mitigation

Two principal alloy-design strategies have emerged for mitigating solidification cracking in LPBF: **grain refinement** and **eutectic solidification**. Grain refinement is achieved by adding lattice-matched nucleants or solute elements with high growth-restriction factors (e.g., Sc, Zr, Ti, V, Nb, Er) or ceramic nanoparticles (TiC, TiN, TiB₂), which promote a columnar-to-equiaxed transition (CET) and produce fine equiaxed grains with higher cracking resistance (uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 2-4, dixit2022laseradditivemanufacturing pages 18-20). Eutectic solidification is implemented by adding eutectic-forming solutes (Si, Ce, Mg) so that terminal liquid forms late in solidification to feed and backfill cracks, reducing hot-cracking susceptibility (uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 2-4).

For aluminum alloys, a CALPHAD-guided "nucleation-and-feeding co-design" approach has been demonstrated, combining trace grain refiners (V and/or Ti forming Al₃V/Al₃Ti nuclei) with Mg as a eutectic solidification enhancer to reduce freezing range, hot susceptibility index (HSI), and cracking susceptibility coefficients (uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 4-5, uba2025enhancingtheprintability pages 1-2). For nickel-based superalloys, cracking mitigation focuses on controlling γ′-forming elements (Al, Ti, Ta, Nb) to limit the combined content below ~4.5 wt.% and managing grain-boundary chemistry through targeted microalloying with B, Zr, Hf, Sc, and Y (wu2025advancesincrack pages 32-34, wu2025advancesincrack pages 12-14, wu2025advancesincrack pages 17-20). Compositional indices such as the Msac index (Msac = W_Al + 0.5W_Ti + 0.3W_Nb + 0.15W_Ta) and the crack susceptibility coefficient (CSC) have been developed to predict printability (wu2025advancesincrack pages 12-14).

### 2.2 Porosity Control

Process-induced porosity (lack-of-fusion, keyhole, and gas porosity) is controlled through the interplay of composition and process parameters. Increasing laser energy density can raise relative density (e.g., AlSi7Mg from 94.6% to 99.6%), but excessive energy causes vaporization of low-melting-point elements and new porosity (wang2024areviewon pages 5-7). Composition design helps by selecting alloys with favorable solidification behavior—Al-Si alloys with narrow freezing ranges show superior LPBF formability because eutectic liquid promotes feeding and defect-free solidification (dixit2022laseradditivemanufacturing pages 5-7). Hot isostatic pressing (HIP) can close internal pores, though argon-filled pores may re-open during subsequent heat treatment, and HIP cannot address near-surface porosity or blistering (martucci2023ongoingchallengesof pages 17-19).

### 2.3 Residual Stress

Residual stresses arise from LPBF's steep thermal gradients and rapid cooling. Process-level mitigation includes scan-strategy optimization and preheating (up to 1200 °C for Ni superalloys) (adegoke2022influenceoflaser pages 1-2). From a compositional perspective, alloys with lower thermal conductivity (e.g., Zr-modified Al alloys) help stabilize melt-pool dynamics (dixit2022laseradditivemanufacturing pages 18-20). Post-processing stress relief, shot peening, and laser shock peening introduce compressive stresses that improve fatigue performance (dixit2022laseradditivemanufacturing pages 13-15).

## 3. Composition–Process–Structure–Property Relationships

LPBF's extreme thermal conditions produce non-equilibrium microstructures that are strongly composition-dependent. Al-Si alloys form fine cellular structures (~500 nm cells, ~10 µm grains) with Si precipitates at cell boundaries and high dislocation density, yielding elevated as-built strength (dixit2022laseradditivemanufacturing pages 5-7, wang2024areviewon pages 5-7). High-strength Al alloys (6xxx, 7xxx) instead develop long columnar grains aligned with the build direction, increasing susceptibility to solidification cracks—motivating composition modifications (dixit2022laseradditivemanufacturing pages 5-7). In nickel superalloys, LPBF produces columnar dendritic structures with microsegregation of Ti, Nb, and Mo, forming Laves phase and potentially leading to liquation cracking during reheating of previously deposited layers (liu2024alloydesignfor pages 4-6, wu2025advancesincrack pages 3-5). Titanium alloys develop acicular α′ martensite with high residual stress, requiring post-build decomposition into equilibrium α + β phases to balance strength and ductility (dinita2023additivemanufacturingpostprocessing pages 67-69, dinita2023additivemanufacturingpostprocessing pages 66-67).

For high-entropy alloys (HEAs), rapid solidification in LPBF can suppress segregation and stabilize single-phase solid solutions. An AlCoCrNiNb HEA produced by LPBF in-situ alloying achieved a homogeneous FCC structure with peak microhardness of ~970 HV, attributed to rapid solidification and sluggish diffusion (tsai2025explorationofa pages 1-2). CoCrFeMnNi-type systems have been extensively studied computationally, with printability maps identifying Fe₅Co₂₀Cr₄₀Mn₅Ni₃₀ as an optimally printable composition (sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 40-42).

## 4. High-Throughput LPBF Parameter Optimization

### 4.1 Computational High-Throughput Frameworks

A major advance has been the development of fully computational frameworks that co-design alloy chemistry and processing windows. These integrate CALPHAD-derived thermophysical properties with physics-based thermal models (Eagar–Tsai) to predict melt-pool geometry, then apply defect criteria (lack-of-fusion, keyholing, balling) to construct printability maps across power–velocity–hatch space (sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 5-7, sheikh2024anautomatedcomputational pages 1-2, sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 3-5). Deep-learning surrogate models trained on validated computational databases accelerate printability assessment by approximately 1000× without loss of accuracy (sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 1-3). Probabilistic printability maps with uncertainty quantification further enhance alloy ranking (sheikh2024anautomatedcomputational pages 2-3).

### 4.2 Experimental High-Throughput Methods

Experimental high-throughput approaches include fabrication of large arrays (e.g., 100 samples) with varied parameters in a single build, achieving complete screening in approximately 16 hours (islam2022ahighthroughputmethod pages 1-2). Single scan track (SST) screening rapidly maps power–velocity combinations to assess melt-pool stability before committing to full bulk builds (martucci2023ongoingchallengesof pages 8-9). In-situ alloying via blended elemental and pre-alloyed powders enables LPBF to sample many compositions without producing custom powder lots (tsai2025explorationofa pages 1-2). High-speed 3D printing coupled with machine learning can screen 20+ alloy variants (e.g., steel with 0–10 wt.% Al) in a single campaign, extracting process–structure–property relationships via large-area crystallographic analysis and nanoindentation (hariharan2025high‐speed3dprinting pages 1-2).

## 5. In-Situ Monitoring and Quality Control

### 5.1 Sensor Modalities

Current in-situ monitoring for LPBF spans four principal modalities: (i) **optical** methods using co-axial DSLR/high-speed cameras and DIC for surface and powder-bed imaging; (ii) **thermal** methods employing pyrometers, NIR cameras, and dual-wavelength systems for melt-pool temperature estimation; (iii) **acoustic** methods capturing airborne and structure-borne emissions correlated with keyhole formation and porosity; and (iv) **X-ray** synchrotron imaging for real-time observation of subsurface melt-pool dynamics, vapor depression, and pore nucleation (aydogan2024reviewofin pages 20-22, aydogan2024reviewofin pages 4-6, aydogan2024reviewofin pages 11-13). Optical tomography (OT) captures layer-wise thermal emission images, complementing photodiode-based melt-pool monitoring, with OT sensitive to scan speed/hatch deviations and photodiodes sensitive to laser power fluctuations (ero2024insitumonitoringand pages 29-33).

### 5.2 Machine Learning for Defect Detection

Machine learning has been increasingly integrated into LPBF monitoring. Physics-informed features combined with SVMs and multilayer perceptrons achieve true positive rates >90% and false positive rates <1% for void detection (aydogan2024reviewofin pages 11-13). Bayesian classification frameworks using photodiode transient features achieve >90% part-level classification accuracy across parameter sets and can resolve 10–20% bulk parameter shifts and sub-millimeter defects (felix2022insituprocess pages 10-14). Acoustic spectral features in the 20–50 kHz band, trained with CT-labeled data, show promise for keyhole porosity detection (aydogan2024reviewofin pages 11-13).

### 5.3 Sensor Fusion and Closed-Loop Control

Multi-sensor fusion (combining optical, thermal, and acoustic signals) is advocated to overcome individual sensor limitations and enable closed-loop control (aydogan2024reviewofin pages 20-22, aydogan2024reviewofin pages 2-4). However, the field has not yet transitioned from highly sensorized machines to fully intelligent, closed-loop systems—major barriers include massive data volumes, real-time processing requirements, poor interoperability between machines/labs, and the inability of surface sensors to reliably detect subsurface defects (ozel2023areviewon pages 27-29).

## 6. Post-Processing and Heat Treatment Optimization

Post-processing is essential because LPBF as-built microstructures are typically non-equilibrium and contain high residual stresses. For **aluminum alloys**, common strategies include stress relief (270–350 °C, 2–4 h), direct aging (T5), and solutionizing + aging (T6); however, no stress-relief treatment has been found that does not compromise the beneficial as-built fine microstructure (martucci2023ongoingchallengesof pages 17-19). Alloy design approaches (e.g., Sc/Zr additions as in Scalmalloy) enable higher-temperature aging (300–400 °C) that combines stress relief and strengthening in a single step (martucci2023ongoingchallengesof pages 17-19).

For **nickel superalloys** (IN718), multi-step treatments include stress relief (1065 °C, 1.5 h), solutioning (960–1065 °C, 1 h), and double aging (720 °C, 8 h), often combined with HIP for densification. All tested heat treatments for LPBF IN718 produced yield strengths of 1103–1205 MPa and UTS of 1347–1387 MPa above wrought minima, with non-standard treatments achieving the best ductility (~24% elongation) (atabay2022inenvelopeadditivesubtractive pages 1-2, dinita2023additivemanufacturingpostprocessing pages 66-67).

For **titanium alloys**, stress relief + β-annealing (SR+BA) decomposes α′ martensite into basket-weave α + β, achieving the best combination of strength (~920 MPa YS) and fatigue (~600 MPa at 10⁷ cycles), providing a valuable and less expensive alternative to HIP-based treatments (dinita2023additivemanufacturingpostprocessing pages 67-69). For **steels**, stress relief (470–540 °C), homogenization (820–1040 °C), and aging (420–525 °C) cycles are used to control precipitation hardening and residual stress (dinita2023additivemanufacturingpostprocessing pages 63-64).

Surface post-processing—including laser remelting (reducing porosity from 0.77% to 0.036%), shot peening, laser shock peening, and abrasive finishing (roughness from ~7 µm to ~0.155 µm)—complements thermal treatments by introducing compressive residual stresses and improving fatigue resistance (dixit2022laseradditivemanufacturing pages 13-15).

## 7. Alloy Systems Receiving the Most Attention

The literature reveals a clear hierarchy of research attention. **Aluminum alloys** (particularly Al-Si variants such as AlSi10Mg and AlSi12, and high-strength 7xxx/2xxx series) and **nickel-based superalloys** (IN718, IN625, IN738, Alloy 247LC) have received the most attention, driven by strong aerospace and industrial demand (liu2024alloydesignfor pages 1-3, liu2024alloydesignfor pages 3-4). **Titanium alloys** (predominantly Ti-6Al-4V) represent the third most studied system, with emerging interest in alternative α+β compositions such as Ti-6Al-2Sn-4Zr-6Mo (dinita2023additivemanufacturingpostprocessing pages 67-69). **Iron-based alloys** (316L, 17-4 PH, maraging steels) are well-established for LPBF with less active alloy-design research. **High-entropy alloys/multi-principal element alloys** (CoCrFeMnNi, AlCoCrFeNi, and Al-containing variants) represent a rapidly growing frontier, enabled by in-situ alloying and computational screening (li2022reviewmultiprincipalelement pages 27-28, tsai2025explorationofa pages 1-2, sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 42-44).

## 8. Key Unsolved Challenges

Several critical challenges remain:

1. **Incomplete thermophysical databases**: Reliable CALPHAD data for liquid alloys and high-melting subsystems are often missing, limiting predictive alloy design (martucci2023ongoingchallengesof pages 21-23, martucci2023ongoingchallengesof pages 3-4).

2. **Inadequacy of solidification models**: Standard Lever rule and Scheil–Gulliver models make limiting assumptions inappropriate for LPBF's extreme cooling rates (>10⁵ K/s), requiring corrections for back diffusion, coarsening, and undercooling that remain difficult to validate (martucci2023ongoingchallengesof pages 3-4).

3. **Gap between phase prediction and property prediction**: Current CALPHAD-based models can often predict phases but cannot reliably predict resulting microstructure, mechanical properties, or cracking susceptibility, necessitating extensive experimental validation (martucci2023ongoingchallengesof pages 21-23, martucci2023ongoingchallengesof pages 3-4).

4. **Inapplicability of weldability criteria**: Conventional weldability criteria do not reliably predict LPBF printability—elements beneficial in casting (e.g., B in Ni superalloys) can be detrimental in LPBF (liu2024alloydesignfor pages 1-3).

5. **Feedstock production and quality**: Producing high-quality pre-alloyed powders with consistent PSD, morphology, and chemistry for novel compositions remains expensive and time-consuming; oxygen contamination promotes porosity (li2022reviewmultiprincipalelement pages 21-23, roth2024establishingaprocess pages 1-2).

6. **Repeatability and machine transferability**: Many interacting process variables and local effects (part position, gas flow, melt-pool dynamics) challenge build-to-build and machine-to-machine reproducibility; limited transfer learning hinders knowledge transfer between labs (li2022reviewmultiprincipalelement pages 21-23, ozel2023areviewon pages 27-29).

7. **Subsurface defect detection**: Surface-based in-situ sensors cannot reliably detect subsurface porosity and cracks; fully closed-loop control remains unrealized in commercial systems (ozel2023areviewon pages 27-29, aydogan2024reviewofin pages 20-22).

8. **Post-processing trade-offs**: For Al alloys, no stress-relief treatment avoids compromising the as-built fine microstructure; HIP can re-open argon-filled pores; optimizing heat treatments for novel LPBF-specific compositions requires alloy-specific development (martucci2023ongoingchallengesof pages 17-19, dinita2023additivemanufacturingpostprocessing pages 85-87).

## 9. Conclusions

The period 2022–2026 has seen a paradigm shift from adapting legacy alloys to LPBF toward designing alloys specifically for the process. Key enablers include CALPHAD-guided compositional design, machine-learning surrogates for rapid printability assessment, and high-throughput experimental platforms that can screen dozens of compositions in single campaigns. High-throughput and combinatorial approaches offer dramatic acceleration over traditional single-composition methods—achieving up to 1000× computational speedup and completing experimental screening campaigns in hours rather than weeks (sheikh2025highthroughputalloyand pages 1-3, islam2022ahighthroughputmethod pages 1-2). However, these approaches complement rather than replace detailed single-composition studies, which remain essential for validating predictions, understanding defect mechanisms, and qualifying parts for service (islam2022ahighthroughputmethod pages 1-2, martucci2023ongoingchallengesof pages 8-9). Closing the remaining gaps—in thermophysical data, non-equilibrium solidification modeling, subsurface defect detection, and alloy-specific post-processing optimization—will be critical for realizing the full potential of LPBF-tailored alloy development.
