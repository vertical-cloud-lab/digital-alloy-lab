## 5. COMPETITIVE POSITIONING AND PRELIMINARY DATA

### What Reviewers Expect in a Full Proposal

For a full proposal under AMMTO Focus Area F, reviewers will expect substantially more than what suffices for a concept paper. The AMMT 2025 Roadmap explicitly calls for integrated approaches leveraging "advanced characterization, high-throughput and accelerated testing, modeling and simulation, and AI/ML" to "enable the accelerated development, qualification, and demonstration of advanced materials and manufacturing technologies" (li2025advancedmaterialsand pages 72-73). Your proposal must demonstrate that FLAIME is not merely conceptual but operational.

**Minimum preliminary data for competitiveness:**
1. **Atomization proof-of-concept:** Include SEM images and particle size distributions from your existing Al alloy atomization runs. UA literature shows these powders achieve high sphericity and narrow PSD with AM-suitable fractions (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 9-11, yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 2-3, yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 1-2). If you have EDS composition data, include it.
2. **LPBF printability evidence:** If you have printed any coupons from your UA powder at University of Utah, include optical/SEM images of cross-sections and any density measurements. Even negative results (showing why AI-guided optimization is needed) are valuable.
3. **BOFN demonstration:** The retrospective/synthetic BOFN demo is still relevant but insufficient alone for a full proposal. If possible, run BOFN on your existing atomization data (even if limited) to show it can rank compositions and predict feasibility. A figure showing "BOFN-suggested vs. random sampling" efficiency on real or semi-real data is much stronger than a purely synthetic benchmark.
4. **3-composition demo:** The concept of running 3 compositions through the full DAG is excellent for demonstrating workflow integration. If time permits before submission, running even 1–2 compositions through atomize→print→test would provide transformative preliminary data. If not, clearly describe the workflow as "ready to execute Day 1 of Phase I" with supporting evidence of each node's readiness.

**Competitive framing:** Position FLAIME as a "human-in-the-loop self-driving laboratory" specifically designed for alloy-to-qualification pipelines. The SDL literature emphasizes that modern autonomous platforms integrate automated hardware, AI-driven experimental planners (Bayesian optimization, active learning), and orchestration software in closed-loop workflows (hao2025autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 4-7, lee2026towardselfdrivinglaboratory pages 1-2, nematov2025thebrightfuture pages 1-2). FLAIME's 4-node DAG maps directly onto this architecture. Emphasize that your platform is distinctive because it spans from alloy composition to tensile properties in a single integrated loop — most existing SDLs focus on synthesis and characterization without full mechanical testing.

**Al-Ce-X technical positioning:** The Al-Ce system is highly promising for LPBF with a near-eutectic composition at ~10.6 wt% Ce, forming thermally stable Al₁₁Ce₃ intermetallics with excellent coarsening resistance (liu2024reviewoflaser pages 28-29, hesselmann2022effectofprecipitationforming pages 1-3, liu2024reviewoflaser pages 2-4, hesselmann2022effectofprecipitationforming pages 14-15). Published ternary systems (Al-Ce-Mg, Al-Ce-Mn, Al-Ce-Si, Al-Ce-Cu) show a wide property space from ~222 MPa to ~593 MPa UTS depending on alloying additions and LPBF parameters (hesselmann2022effectofprecipitationforming pages 1-3, hesselmann2022effectofprecipitationforming pages 11-14). This makes Al-Ce-X an ideal testbed for AI-guided optimization: the system is compositionally rich enough for meaningful exploration, LPBF-amenable due to the eutectic solidification path, and thermally stable for potential high-temperature applications.
