## Executive summary (DOE posture, 2020–2026; evidence-limited to retrieved acknowledgements)

Across the inspected 2020–2026 literature and DOE/OSTI reports, DOE support for high-performance aluminum alloy development is most visible through EERE (AMO and VTO), with strong coupling to Oak Ridge National Laboratory’s Manufacturing Demonstration Facility (MDF) and propulsion/lightweighting use cases (e.g., Al–Ce–Mg, Al–Ce–Mn, and engine-component demonstrators). (sisco2021microstructureandproperties pages 12-13, wang2025lowmassandhighefficiency pages 70-71)
DOE’s Critical Materials Institute (CMI; an Energy Innovation Hub funded by EERE/AMO) appears as a recurring backbone for Al–Ce alloy design and thermodynamics/CALPHAD work, spanning both journal publications and national-lab collaborations. (moore2020thermodynamicmodelingof pages 20-20)
National labs contribute both through DOE Office of Science user facilities (APS, Molecular Foundry) and through internal Laboratory Directed Research and Development (LDRD), which supports additive-manufacturing microstructure concepts relevant to lightweight Al alloy performance. (henderson2021enhancedthermalcoarsening pages 11-12, martin2021enhancedmechanicalperformance pages 5-6)
ARPA‑E shows up in adjacent “rapid materials design/manufacture” efforts (e.g., ULTIMATE) and in lightweighting materials beyond metals; in the retrieved evidence, explicit ARPA‑E award numbers were more common in OSTI technical reports than in the Al-alloy journal corpus. (alman2024finalscientifictechnicalreport pages 14-14, hu2023superstronglowcostwood pages 1-4)

## Trends (DOE programs, PIs/institutions, new programs, gaps)

### Sub-program emphasis (growing vs. shrinking)
* **EERE AMO + VTO (Propulsion Materials / vehicle lightweighting)**: Multiple acknowledgements tie aluminum alloy development (especially Al–Ce systems and AM alloy processing) to EERE AMO and VTO and to ORNL MDF execution. This indicates sustained DOE-EERE emphasis on manufacturable lightweight alloys for transportation-relevant conditions (including elevated temperature). (sisco2021microstructureandproperties pages 12-13, wang2025lowmassandhighefficiency pages 70-71)
* **EERE AMO via institutes/hubs (REMADE, CMI)**: The REMADE Institute award appears in an Al–Ce casting/alloy sustainability study, while CMI is repeatedly acknowledged as enabling the Al–Ce alloy design ecosystem (thermodynamic modeling; AM-relevant alloy selection). This suggests DOE support that blends alloy performance with supply-chain/critical-materials and circularity framing. (moodispaw2024thebeneficialeffect pages 11-12, moore2020thermodynamicmodelingof pages 20-20)
* **Office of Science enablement via user facilities**: APS and Molecular Foundry contract acknowledgements recur in AM Al–Ce work; this appears as a stable “enabling infrastructure” mode of support rather than a dedicated aluminum-alloy program line in these acknowledgements. (henderson2021enhancedthermalcoarsening pages 11-12)
* **ARPA‑E**: The retrieved evidence shows ARPA‑E presence (ULTIMATE; SCALEUP follow-on), but in this run it is not possible to infer growth/shrinkage quantitatively from acknowledgements alone (award counts are incomplete and not normalized by publication volume). (alman2024finalscientifictechnicalreport pages 14-14, hu2023superstronglowcostwood pages 14-15)

### Dominant institutions and PI clusters (in the extracted corpus)
* **Oak Ridge National Laboratory (UT‑Battelle) / MDF** is central for AM aluminum alloy development in the inspected literature (AM Al–Ce–Mg with EERE AMO/VTO sponsorship and ORNL contract authorship). (sisco2021microstructureandproperties media 09eb7c92)
* **LLNL + Argonne APS + Berkeley Molecular Foundry user-facility ecosystem** appears prominently in AM Al–Ce work (LLNL contract acknowledgements; APS and Molecular Foundry usage), and LLNL LDRD is explicitly acknowledged in at least one AM lightweight Al alloy paper. (henderson2021enhancedthermalcoarsening pages 11-12, martin2021enhancedmechanicalperformance pages 5-6)
* **Named PI evidence** is strongest where the source is an OSTI report rather than a journal paper; e.g., ARPA‑E DE‑AR0001025 explicitly names PI Liangbing Hu (University of Maryland). (hu2023superstronglowcostwood pages 1-4)

### Apparent gaps / proposal opportunities for autonomous closed-loop alloy discovery (FLAIME)
* **Limited explicit linkage to DOE Office of Science (BES/DMSE) awards in Al-alloy acknowledgements**: The extracted Al-alloy journal papers frequently cite user-facility contracts but less often list BES/DMSE award numbers; this suggests an opportunity to position closed-loop alloy discovery as a BES-relevant methodology (autonomous experimentation + theory/ICME) that complements user-facility characterization. (henderson2021enhancedthermalcoarsening pages 11-12)
* **Sparse award-number visibility for AM alloy development programs** in the journal literature (many acknowledgements name program offices but not specific DE-EE award IDs). This suggests that assembling a complete program/PI map will likely require combining literature mining with OSTI award records and agency databases; nevertheless, MDF-centric EERE execution and CMI-centric Al–Ce alloy design are clear anchors for partnership/transition narratives. (sisco2021microstructureandproperties pages 12-13, moore2020thermodynamicmodelingof pages 20-20)
* **Closed-loop discovery gap**: The extracted awards largely fund materials development and demonstration; explicit acknowledgements of autonomy/closed-loop experimentation are not visible in these funding statements, implying a white space to propose “autonomous, closed-loop alloy discovery” as a crosscutting capability accelerating AMO/VTO-relevant alloy insertion.
