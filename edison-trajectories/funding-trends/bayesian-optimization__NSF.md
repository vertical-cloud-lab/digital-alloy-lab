## Executive summary (NSF posture, 2020–2026)
Across 2020–2026 literature on Bayesian optimization (BO), active learning (AL), and multi-objective acquisition functions applied to materials/alloy design and process optimization, NSF support appears concentrated in three buckets: (i) **DMREF/DMR** awards supporting BO/AL for materials discovery and materials-centric surrogate modeling, including constraint-aware alloy design; (ii) **CMMI** awards supporting BO/AL as part of advanced manufacturing and process optimization workflows (including additive manufacturing); and (iii) **infrastructure/centers** (NNCI, Expeditions/CCF, MRI/OAC, XSEDE/ACI) that enable closed-loop and autonomous experimentation platforms and the compute needed to run surrogate models and acquisition optimization (diwale2022bayesianoptimizationfor pages 13-15, khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 14-14, ament2021autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 11-12, adapa2025rapiddevelopmentof pages 22-24, krishna2024adaptiveexplorationand pages 27-31). ## Trends (programs, PIs/institutions, growth signals, gaps)

### 1) Sub-program signals that are growing vs. shrinking (as visible in 2020–2026 acknowledgements)
- **DMREF remains a persistent mechanism** for BO/AL-enabled materials discovery/design, appearing in multiple contexts: noisy materials discovery BO (CMMI-1729304, DMREF) (diwale2022bayesianoptimizationfor pages 13-15), constraint-aware alloy design work (NSF-DMREF-2119103) (khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 14-14, khatamsaz2023aphysicsinformed pages 11-11), and DMREF-labeled DMR awards (NSF-DMR-2118861) supporting iterative materials discovery via GP/BO (kosuri2022machine‐assisteddiscoveryof pages 10-12).
- **CMMI appears consistently in manufacturing/process optimization** and autonomous experimentation for mechanical/material design (CMMI-1661412; CMMI-1825640; plus a second NSF grant number 2119640) (gongora2020abayesianexperimental pages 6-6, adapa2025rapiddevelopmentof pages 22-24). This suggests stable NSF engineering support for BO-enabled closed-loop optimization in manufacturing-adjacent domains.
- **OAC/MRI and other infrastructure-oriented awards appear as enabling “substrate” funding** (e.g., OAC-2320649 for GPU cluster purchase) (jiang2025generativeactivelearning pages 14-14). This is a post-2022 signal (award number in the 2320xxx range) consistent with increased attention to cyberinfrastructure and local compute for data-driven science.
- **Center/facility programs (NNCI, Expeditions) show up repeatedly as foundational support** for autonomous materials platforms (NNCI-2025233; ECCS-2025158; CCF-1522054) (dunlap2023continuousflowsynthesis pages 7-8, ament2021autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 11-12, ament2021autonomousmaterialssynthesis media 733f7346), reflecting sustained NSF investment in shared research infrastructure used by BO/AL-driven workflows.

### 2) Institutions and PI clusters most visible in this extracted set
Because many acknowledgements do **not** explicitly name the PI/institution tied to each award, dominance can only be inferred when the paper provides names or when the award is tied to a facility.
- **Texas A&M / Arróyave group cluster (materials/alloy BO with constraints):** multiple papers on constraint-aware alloy design and physics-informed BO repeatedly acknowledge NSF-CISE-1835690, CDSE-2001333, and NSF-DMREF-2119103 (khatamsaz2023bayesianoptimizationwith pages 14-14, khatamsaz2023aphysicsinformed pages 11-11). These repeated award numbers across multiple papers are a strong signal of a sustained NSF-backed programmatic effort around BO for alloy design with constraints.
- **Princeton cluster (materials discovery + active learning + compute/infrastructure):** Webb-associated work acknowledges NSF-MRI OAC-2320649 for GPU infrastructure (jiang2025generativeactivelearning pages 14-14), and another materials-design paper acknowledges NSF-DMR-2118860 and a DMREF award NSF-DMR-2118861 (kosuri2022machine‐assisteddiscoveryof pages 10-12).
- **Cornell ecosystem (autonomous materials synthesis + facilities/Expeditions):** autonomous materials synthesis via hierarchical active learning acknowledges NSF Expeditions award CCF-1522054 and NNCI-2025233, visibly tied to CHESS/MSN-C and Cornell NanoScale Facility (ament2021autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 11-12, ament2021autonomousmaterialssynthesis media 733f7346).
- **MIT/Harvard NNCI-enabled work** appears through facility and DMR acknowledgements (ECCS-2025158; DMR-2207299) in a multi-objective BO + active learning paper (dunlap2023continuousflowsynthesis pages 7-8).
- **Raed Al Kontar (CAREER + CMMI-FMRG)** indicates NSF support for BO methods relevant to distributed experimentation (CAREER 2144147; CMMI-FMRG 2328010) (kontar2024collaborativeandfederated pages 1-2).

### 3) Newer (launched/visible 2022+) NSF mechanisms funding this topic
- **MRI/OAC compute instrumentation**: NSF-MRI OAC-2320649 is explicitly acknowledged as enabling GPU infrastructure for active-learning-driven materials design workflows (jiang2025generativeactivelearning pages 14-14). This is a concrete post-2022 award-number signal supporting autonomous/ML-driven materials research.
- **CMMI-FMRG** (award 2328010) appears in 2024 for BO theory tied to collaborative/federated experimentation (kontar2024collaborativeandfederated pages 1-2), consistent with NSF interest in foundational methods that could transfer into self-driving labs.

### 4) Visible gaps/opportunities (where NSF support is less explicit in acknowledgements)
- **Direct linkage to specific NSF materials programs beyond DMREF/DMR is often not explicit**: many papers list award numbers without naming a program element (e.g., “NSF grant 2119640”) (adapa2025rapiddevelopmentof pages 22-24), limiting traceability from literature alone.
- **Closed-loop alloy discovery at scale (robotic loop + multi-objective constrained BO) appears funded, but often through a patchwork of awards and infrastructure**, rather than a clearly labeled, dedicated NSF program line in acknowledgements. This suggests an opportunity for proposals to frame autonomous alloy discovery (e.g., Genesis/FLAIME) as a unifying cross-directorate theme bridging **DMR/DMREF + CMMI + OAC**.
- **Network/function-of-network BO and advanced acquisition-function research** shows up via CAREER/CMMI-FMRG in methods venues (kontar2024collaborativeandfederated pages 1-2), but is less frequently co-acknowledged alongside DMREF/DMR materials awards in the extracted set—an opportunity space for stronger NSF cross-program integration between BO theory and alloy-specific autonomous experimentation.

This survey is limited to papers successfully retrieved and whose acknowledgements were accessible in the available text/image chunks. Some highly relevant retrieved papers (e.g., Liang et al. 2021 benchmarking; Priyadarshini et al. 2024 PAL 2.0) did not expose acknowledgements in accessible chunks here, so their NSF awards (if any) could not be extracted without additional full-text access.
