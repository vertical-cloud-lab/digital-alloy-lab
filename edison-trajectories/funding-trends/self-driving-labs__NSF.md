## Trends (NSF programs, PIs/institutions, new programs, gaps)

### 1) Sub-programs/mechanisms that appear to be expanding into SDL/autonomy topics
- **TIP/Directorate for Technology, Innovation and Partnerships (TIP) via POSE (2022+)** appears directly in SDL democratization/infrastructure literature (TIP-2229018) (pelkie2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 17-18). This is qualitatively different from traditional DMR awards: it aligns with scaling, community/platform building, and translation of autonomous lab infrastructure.
- **PREM (DMR) as a partnership mechanism** appears in the same SDL democratization context (DMR-2424949), suggesting NSF is using partnership programs to broaden participation and capacity-building around SDL tooling and workflows (pelkie2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 17-18).
- **MRSEC/MRSEC-associated center funding** is explicitly connected to autonomous experimentation tooling via UT Knoxville Center for Advanced Materials and Manufacturing (DMR-2309083), indicating center-scale mechanisms are being used to underwrite combinatorial libraries and automated workflows that are prerequisites for self-driving materials research (liu2024integrationofscanning pages 11-14).

### 2) Mechanisms that look stable/recurring as “enablers” for autonomy
- **NNCI-related facility/infrastructure awards (ECCS)** are repeatedly acknowledged as prerequisites for building and operating autonomous platforms, e.g., nanofabrication facility support in a self-driving PVD system (ECCS-2025633) and instrumentation facility support (ECCS-1542015) (zheng2025aselfdrivingphysical pages 9-10, sadeghi2025aselfdrivingfluidic pages 10-11). This suggests a persistent NSF role in autonomy through shared facility ecosystems.
- **Cross-directorate compute/physics support (OAC/PHY)** appears in SDL perspective work (PHY-2226511; OAC-2320718), consistent with SDL needs for HPC, workflow orchestration, and data infrastructure (canty2025scienceaccelerationand pages 10-11).

### 3) Dominant institutions / PI clusters in this literature-derived NSF acknowledgement set
Based strictly on the accessible acknowledgements in this corpus (not a comprehensive NSF portfolio analysis):
- **University of Tennessee, Knoxville** appears via MRSEC-linked autonomous microscopy/workflow work (DMR-2309083) (liu2024integrationofscanning pages 11-14).
- **University of Chicago shared facilities ecosystem** appears via DMR-2011854 (MRSEC shared facilities), plus multiple ECCS/CNS awards supporting autonomous PVD platform work (zheng2025aselfdrivingphysical pages 9-10).
- **North Carolina State University / Abolhasani lab-associated SDL work** appears repeatedly with multiple NSF award numbers supporting self-driving fluidic labs and autonomous synthesis in flow (1902702, 1936527, 2315996, 2208406; plus ECCS-1542015 facility support) (epps2021acceleratedaidevelopment pages 10-12, sadeghi2025aselfdrivingfluidic pages 10-11).
- **CAREER-linked autonomy + metals focus** is visible in a dealloying/autonomous characterization context with explicit mention of CAREER and “Metals and Metallic Nanostructures Program” (DMR-1752839) (zhao2022machinelearningfordesigning media 956bfbe5).

### 4) New(er) NSF programs (launched 2022+) visible in this corpus
- **TIP/POSE (TIP-2229018)** is a clear post-2022 mechanism appearing in SDL community infrastructure/democratization literature (pelkie2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 17-18).
- **DMR PREM (DMR-2424949)** appears in the same 2025 SDL democratization perspective (pelkie2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 17-18).

### 5) Visible gaps/opportunities for NSF relative to autonomous closed-loop alloy discovery
- **Alloy-specific, closed-loop autonomous discovery**: within this accessible acknowledgement set, explicitly alloy-centric autonomous loops are underrepresented compared with thin-film/perovskite/nanoparticle/autonomous microscopy and infrastructure support. This suggests an opportunity for NSF programs (e.g., DMR metals programs, CMMI advanced manufacturing, DMREF-like mechanisms) to more explicitly target *closed-loop alloy discovery* integrating synthesis (including AM), characterization, and active learning with reproducibility and data infrastructure.
- **Program labeling in papers**: many awards are given only as numbers (e.g., 1902702, 2315996) without explicit program tags, which makes it difficult (from literature alone) to map to specific NSF solicitations. A proposal could explicitly emphasize program alignment language (e.g., DMR metals, MRSEC, TIP translation, NNCI-enabled autonomy) to improve traceability and program fit in future publications.
