## Trends (NSF × LPBF metals)

### 1) Sub-programs that are growing vs. shrinking (as visible from 2020–2026 acknowledgements)
- **Growing/active: CMMI-centered LPBF sensing, qualification, and control.** Multiple LPBF papers acknowledge a portfolio of NSF awards with CMMI prefixes, and the same LPBF research thread explicitly ties CMMI-2309483/1752069 to understanding causal effects of process parameters and thermal history for in-situ sensing and defect detection. (bevans2025inprocessmonitoringof pages 53-54, riensche2024predictingmicrostructureevolution pages 7-8, riensche2023applicationofhybrid pages 25-29)
- **Growing/active: translation mechanisms via PFI-TT (2022+ visibility).** PFI-TT awards are repeatedly acknowledged and are described as supporting commercialization of a graph-theory thermal simulation approach relevant to LPBF process planning/qualification; newer paired award numbers appear in later papers (PFI-TT 2322322/2044710). (bevans2025inprocessmonitoringof pages 53-54, riensche2024predictingmicrostructureevolution pages 7-8)
- **Stable/enabling: MRI instrumentation for LPBF characterization.** An NSF Major Research Instrumentation award (CMMI-1920245) is cited as partially funding an X-ray CT instrument used in the LPBF research ecosystem; this is characteristic of NSF’s role in enabling measurement/qualification. (riensche2023applicationofhybrid pages 25-29)
- **Materials-science side remains present but appears less frequent in this narrow LPBF corpus.** Explicit DMR and CAREER acknowledgements occur in LPBF microstructure-focused work (e.g., DMR-1748188 and CAREER 1943104), but the dominant acknowledgement pattern in the sampled sensing/qualification/control papers is CMMI-heavy. (welch2021nano‐andmicro‐structures pages 8-9)

### 2) Institutions and PIs dominating NSF-visible funding in this literature sample
- **Prahalada Rao (Virginia Tech; plus collaborators) is the most repeatedly acknowledged PI across multiple LPBF sensing/qualification/control papers**, with a multi-award portfolio spanning CMMI, PFI-TT, ECCS, OIA, and MRI-linked infrastructure. (bevans2025inprocessmonitoringof pages 53-54, riensche2023applicationofhybrid pages 25-29, riensche2024predictingmicrostructureevolution pages 7-8)
- Additional, distinct NSF-supported LPBF clusters visible in acknowledgements include:
  - **Lianyi Chen (University of Wisconsin–Madison)** acknowledged via NSF awards 2002840 and 2011354 in an LPBF synchrotron-focused perspective/review. (sun2020insituoperandosynchrotron pages 11-14)
  - **Saniya LeBlanc (George Washington University)** via **NSF CAREER 1943104** (LPBF processing/microstructure formation in thermoelectric material context). (welch2021nano‐andmicro‐structures pages 8-9)
  - **George Nolas (University of South Florida)** via **NSF DMR-1748188** in the same LPBF microstructure study. (welch2021nano‐andmicro‐structures pages 8-9)
  - **Wing Kam Liu and Gregory Wagner (Northwestern University)** via **NSF CMMI-1934367** supporting early sections of a physics+ML digital twin framework for LPBF. (li2023statisticalparameterizedphysicsbased pages 32-35)
  - **Judy Schneider** via **NSF EAGER CMMI-1841220** in LPBF IN718 microstructure/post-processing work. (schneider2022microstructureevolutionin pages 17-18, schneider2022microstructureevolutionin media 63aea891)

### 3) New NSF mechanisms/programs (launched 2022+) funding LPBF metals (as evidenced here)
- **PFI-TT continuations/new awards** appear explicitly in later (2024–2025) LPBF acknowledgements (PFI-TT 2322322 paired with 2044710), indicating emphasis on commercialization/translation for LPBF-related process modeling tools. (bevans2025inprocessmonitoringof pages 53-54, riensche2024predictingmicrostructureevolution pages 7-8)
- A **newer CMMI award number (CMMI-2309483)** is paired with an earlier CMMI award (1752069) and described as supporting causal understanding of process parameters/thermal history for in-situ sensing and defect detection. (bevans2025inprocessmonitoringof pages 53-54)
- The same LPBF cluster also notes NSF supplements/activities (e.g., INTERN; CMMI Data Science Activities) tied to CMMI-1752069, consistent with increasing NSF emphasis on workforce translation and data-centric approaches in manufacturing topics. (riensche2023applicationofhybrid pages 25-29)

### 4) Visible gaps/opportunities for NSF (from what is *not* strongly evidenced in these acknowledgements)
- **Limited explicit DMREF/CDS&E/AI Institute/ERC visibility in the sampled LPBF papers’ funding sections.** Within this retrieved corpus, acknowledgements skew toward CMMI (engineering/manufacturing), with relatively sparse explicit appearance of NSF programs aimed at materials acceleration (e.g., DMREF) or large institute structures (ERC/AI institutes). This suggests an opportunity space for proposals that frame LPBF as a platform for data/AI-driven materials discovery and autonomous experimentation aligned with NSF cross-cutting programs.
- **Closed-loop alloy discovery (autonomous composition–process co-optimization) is not prominently labeled in the acknowledgements** even when control/monitoring is; the funding language emphasizes qualification/monitoring/control and thermal simulation commercialization, suggesting room for NSF programs to more directly target autonomous alloy/process discovery loops integrating in-situ sensing + adaptive experimentation.
